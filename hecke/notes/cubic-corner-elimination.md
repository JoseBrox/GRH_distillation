# Eliminating the cubic reducible-point rigidity obligation by the corner vanishing

24 November 2026. This is a new exact reduction of the high-dyadic cubic
Selmer problem. It avoids a Rankin--Selberg Euler system for the entire
range in which the already proved residual negative-corner vanishing
holds. No automorphy theorem for the remaining irreducible points, no
characteristic-prime curve exclusion, and no final cubic length bound
are inferred from this elimination.

## 1. The actual integral ring and its tangent

Use the fully marked normalized ordinary ring R of the
[cubic polarized model](cubic-polarized-selmer-model.md) and
[marked point classification](cubic-point-shapes-and-rigidity.md).
The coefficient DVR O has residue field k of characteristic ell>=5.
Over F=Q(omega) the distinguished diagonal is


```math
\operatorname{diag}(a\lambda^{-1},1,a^{-1}\lambda^{-1}),
 \qquad a=\varepsilon^e\rho^N,\quad e\ge1.
```


The torus of normalized conjugations is diag(t,1,t^(-1)).
The weights of matrix-entry displacements are -2,-1,0,1,2.
Both adjacent-entry mark equations are imposed, as in the point
classification. That normalization retains the exact strict
adjacent-root conormal injection at every finite modulus.

Assume the high-dyadic mark condition and the negative-corner
vanishing of [frame cohomology, §3](cubic-frame-cohomology.md).
For example it suffices that the primitive even dyadic factor
of rho has conductor 2^b with


```math
2^b\ge24\ell(\ell+1)^2 .
 \tag{1}
```


The more precise Bernoulli condition there may be used instead,
provided the high-dyadic separating mark is also present. Write
W_j for the residual weight-j adjoint module, and H^1_L(j) for
the actual relative local-condition cohomology.

**Lemma.** The weight -2 part of the relative tangent of R at
the closed residual point is zero.

**Proof.** Such a tangent is a normalized cocycle with coefficients
in the line k E_31. At every finite prime other than ell its local
condition is unramified, by the prime-to-ell inertia-quotient
averaging in the frame note. At ell it is a local coboundary:
the fixed ordinary ordered flag has no lower triangular first-order
entries after local conjugation. Its class therefore belongs to
H^1_L(-2), which is zero by the corner theorem.

It is consequently a global coboundary of a vector X in k E_31.
The fixed mark at complex conjugation requires
c.X-X=0. The action of c on E_31 is negative reversing transpose,
hence -1. Since ell is odd, X=0. The cocycle itself is zero.
No extra freedom from an ordinary flag or eigenbasis occurs:
the separating mark gives the unique prescribed flag by its
ordered distinct residual eigenvalues, as in the construction
of the actual ring. The universal matrix entries generate the
ring, so the normalized derivation is zero. QED.

This is stronger than vanishing of a rational negative-weight
cohomology space. It is vanishing at the *residual* point, for
the exact normalized functor, including the frame.

## 2. Integral elimination of negative-corner generators

The ring R is topologically generated by finitely many actual
matrix-entry displacements, from matrices on a finite set of
generators of the residually finite pro-ell quotient, as in the
polarized model. These displacements are homogeneous and vanish
at the augmentation. The lemma says that the weight -2 space of
the relative cotangent m_R/(m_R^2+pi R) is zero (the tangent
duality convention does not matter for the zero space).
Select a homogeneous basis of this relative cotangent from
those entry displacements, and choose their actual lifts.
There are only weights -1,0,1,2. Topological Nakayama gives a
surjection of complete local rings


```math
O[[z_1,\ldots,z_d]]\twoheadrightarrow R
 \tag{2}
```


with those chosen lifts as variables. This argument is over O,
not after inverting ell: the map is surjective by successive
approximation modulo powers of the maximal ideal, and
completeness. The kernel is stable under weight components,
using the torus action or its finite-degree formal weight
decomposition, exactly as in the parent minimal presentation.
In particular any actual homogeneous entry displacement
admits a homogeneous formal series preimage.

Every monomial of negative weight in (2) must contain a
weight -1 variable, since the other variables have nonnegative
weight. These weight -1 variables are actual adjacent lower
entry displacements. Thus the following is an identity of
closed ideals of the actual ring:


```math
R_{<0}\subset (z_i:\operatorname{wt}z_i=-1)R .
 \tag{3}
```


Here R_{<0} denotes all its negative homogeneous elements;
the ideal is closed because the complete Noetherian local
ring has finitely generated ideals. The argument also gives
R_-2 contained in the square of that ideal. It is not a
claim that the original nonminimal negative-corner equations
are linear over O; (2) eliminates generators in the complete
ring, retaining all integral relations.

The same conclusion is valid in the finite-sum graded algebra
of the minimal ambient presentation used for the parent's
abstract length theorem. Indeed a homogeneous formal series
of negative weight belongs to the displayed ideal already
in that graded algebra, by grouping its monomials by the
first dividing negative variable and factoring it out.
The kernel of its map to R is the homogeneous relation ideal,
as in the parent DC7 argument; this assertion does not need
finiteness of the invariant quotient.

## 3. No two-sided reducible points in the high-dyadic range

**Theorem.** Under (1) and the actual local and marking conditions,
every two-sided point of the normalized cubic frame is absolutely
irreducible on G_F. This holds for local characteristic-zero
DVR points, characteristic-ell DVR curves and algebraic points
of the finite-sum graded quotient. In particular the cubic
outer-block Rankin--Selberg vanishing is not required for the
two-sided point rigidity in this range.

**Proof.** The point classification identifies a two-sided
reducible point as the separate middle line and the irreducible
outer block, with all adjacent upper and lower entries zero.
At such a point every variable of weight -1 in (2) is zero,
since each is an actual lower adjacent entry. By (3), every
negative homogeneous element is zero, including the outer
negative corner. This contradicts the two-sided property.
The proof applies to continuous DVR points and curves by
evaluating the actual complete ring. For algebraic points
of its finite-sum graded quotient use the finite-sum ideal
identity in Section 2, so no preliminary invariant-ring
finiteness is necessary for this exclusion. QED.

A similar argument works for any polarized rank-three frame
with the same marked point classification and residual
negative-corner vanishing, including the parent's rational
frames when that vanishing is supplied. More generally, a
vanishing residual weight eliminates that weight from a
minimal integral presentation; one can use the resulting
weight monoid to rule out support patterns of potential
points. One must still verify that the chosen generators
vanish on the pattern in question.

## 4. Exact effect on the remaining cubic length comparison

The high-dyadic frame has the cohomological defects


```math
h_j-b_j=(0,-1,0,1,1),\qquad j=-2,-1,0,1,2 ,
```


where h_j is the relative Selmer dimension and b_j the relative
obstruction dimension, by the frame-cohomology note. The new
lemma gives n_-2=0 for the minimal generator count; h_-2=0
also gives b_-2=0. Thus no negative-corner generators or
negative-corner defining equations are required in that count.
The remaining weighted presentation target is the parent's
DC5--DC7 with s=n_-2=0.

This removes the outer-block premise from both the generic
rigidity and the characteristic-prime point classification.
The remaining sufficient inputs for the high-dyadic length
theorem are now:

* the actual homogeneous relation-count and finite-invariant-ring
  comparison, in particular exclusion of absolutely irreducible
  characteristic-ell curves (there is no two-sided rank-two
  endpoint to exclude);
* automorphic attachment of every actual irreducible
  characteristic-zero point to the fixed-F GL3 class already
  specified in the [conditional spectrum](cubic-polarized-selmer-model.md);
* the parent's characteristic-zero adjoint-vanishing theorem
  at those attached points, with the exact polarization,
  cyclotomic irreducibility and potentially crystalline local
  conditions checked. The strong irreducibility and local
  conditions were already obtained in the cubic frame;
  the applicability of the cited adjoint theorem is a separate
  check for the attached representation.

If these inputs are supplied, the parent abstract length theorem
and fixed-field Frobenius product need only the Class I
irreducible spectrum. No Kato, Beilinson--Flach or higher-rank
Euler-system theorem for the cross-block tensor is needed
in this range. The finite-modulus conormal injection, rational
negative-weight rigidity and conditional trace-product size
are retained on the actual marked frame.

The argument does not eliminate the lower dyadic or resonant
cases in which the negative corner may have nonzero residual
tangent. A different auxiliary frame, or an independent
cross-block rigidity theorem, is still needed there. Nor does
the proof show that an irreducible characteristic-ell curve
cannot exist. The high-dyadic threshold is a real restriction,
not an estimate uniform over all coefficient primes and labels.

**Scope:** residual tangent vanishing, integral minimal generator
elimination, point-shape exclusion in both characteristics and the
stated conditional reduction are proved. No new quantitative cubic
Selmer length bound or Hecke GRH proof is claimed. The parent
Dirichlet abstract ring proof is a template for the remaining
actual homogeneous relation and invariant-ring comparisons,
not an assertion that its arithmetic hypotheses hold here.
