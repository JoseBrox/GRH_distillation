# Marked point classification and a rank-one Euler-system obstruction

24 November 2026. This note supplies the exact point-shape reduction for
the new polarized cubic model and identifies a genuine obstruction to
one natural proof of reducible-point rigidity. It does not establish
finiteness of the characteristic-prime invariant ring or the required
cross-block Bloch--Kato vanishing.

## 1. Marked point shapes, in either characteristic

Retain the [cubic polarized frame](cubic-polarized-selmer-model.md).
Write its diagonal characters on G_F as


```math
\chi_1=a\lambda^{-1},\qquad \chi_2=1,\qquad
 \chi_3=a^{-1}\lambda^{-1}.
```


Use a coefficient prime ell>=5 and the separating mark x of that note.
We impose both adjacent-entry normalization equations for the difference
r(x)-r(x*) with x*=cx^(-1)c, as in the parent's
[rank-three count](../../paper/shared/rh-dyadic-host-count.tex),
(DC3). This is permissible for the new integral strict-torsion injection:
its representative is already lower-adjacent only, so the upper
equation holds automatically; the lower equation is exactly the one
solved there by an integral coboundary with unit denominator.
We do not infer equivalence of unnormalized higher-order deformation
functors from this observation.

Let r be a point of this normalized ring over a coefficient DVR
in characteristic zero, or a characteristic-ell DVR curve specializing
to the residual diagonal. Pass to an algebraic closure of its fraction
field when testing absolute irreducibility. The polarization gives


```math
r(x^*)=J_3r(x)^{\mathsf t}J_3,\qquad
 r(x)-r(x^*)=d_r\operatorname{diag}(1,0,-1),
```


where d_r reduces to chi_1(x)-chi_3(x), a nonzero residue.
The diagonal entries are d_r,0,-d_r, pairwise distinct because
2 is a unit. Polynomial interpolation in the marked difference
therefore gives all three coordinate idempotents in the algebra
generated by r(G_F), even over the coefficient DVR.

Use directed edges j->i for the nonzero entries r(g)_{ij},
g in G_F, i != j. The polarization and inversion give the
reflection of each edge under reversing transpose: r(cg^(-1)c)
=J_3r(g)^tJ_3. In particular the two upper-adjacent edges occur
together and likewise the two lower-adjacent edges.
The two corner edges are each self-reflected.
Any invariant subspace is a span of coordinate lines, because
the coordinate idempotents act on it; it must be closed under
all these edges. Thus strong connectivity is equivalent to
absolute irreducibility.

Call a point two-sided if it has nonzero off-diagonal entries of
both signs of the torus weights (1,0,-1). Equivalently its actual
entry functions contain a nonzero nonconstant balanced monomial:
if weights b>0 and -a<0 occur, take powers a and b of those
two entries. At any two-sided point precisely the following
possibilities occur:

* If any adjacent edge occurs, the graph is strongly connected.
  Indeed the adjacent pair gives the two successive arrows
  in one direction and the opposite sign supplies either both
  opposite adjacent edges or the corner completing the cycle.
  Hence r|G_F is absolutely irreducible.
* Otherwise both opposite corners occur. Then the middle line
  is invariant and separate, and the outer two-by-two block is
  absolutely irreducible by its coordinate projectors and both
  arrows.

This proves the full two-sided classification for the actual marked
frame. There is no reliance on a generic matrix or on characteristic
zero.

If only one sign occurs, all matrices on G_F are triangular in
the corresponding order. Their three diagonal functions are
characters. Their ratios to the three prescribed diagonal
characters are everywhere unramified: away from ell the inertia
kernel is fixed and the remaining inertia quotient has order prime
to ell; a character on that quotient reducing to the identity is
trivial. At ell, the ordered ordinary flag with distinct residual
marked eigenvalues identifies each triangular constituent by its
residual character and gives its exact prescribed inertia.
Class number one of F kills the three unramified ratios.
This reasoning also works in characteristic ell; it uses
continuous characters, not just equality of rational trace.
For a finite constant coefficient reduction, one can equivalently
first reduce the ratios modulo each power of the maximal ideal.
The trace and characteristic polynomial on G_F of any such
triangular point are therefore exactly those of the basepoint.

In characteristic zero at a two-sided outer-block point the same
middle-character argument gives the middle line 1 and the determinant
of the outer block lambda^(-2). The construction in the polarized
frame note then gives A=lambda^(-1) W_F and W extending to G_Q
with determinant eta=chi_{-3}. In particular this block
description now applies to *every* two-sided reducible point
of this normalization, not an assumed type of point.

For characteristic-ell curves, if the semisimplification of the
fraction-field representation has finite image, its characteristic
polynomials are constant: the eigenvalues of finite-order elements
over the algebraic closure in characteristic ell are roots of unity
of order prime to ell, hence lie in the algebraic closure of the
constant field. The characteristic polynomials have integral
coefficients and specialize injectively on that constant field.
They equal the base characteristic polynomials. The
Brauer--Nesbitt theorem (using characteristic polynomials, not an
unsupported trace criterion in positive characteristic) makes the
semisimplification the sum of the three residual characters.
Neither of the two two-sided shapes has that semisimplification.
Thus the remaining characteristic-prime arithmetic exclusion can
be stated as the exclusion of the two shapes above, or as finite-image
semisimplification for every such curve. The parent rank-three
invariant-ring criterion also requires its actual presentation and
lifting from invariant curves; we have not supplied that step here.

## 2. Why the rank-one tensor Euler system cannot simply be used

At a characteristic-zero outer-block point let W be the normalized
two-dimensional representation above, so det W=eta. Let


```math
B=\operatorname{Ind}_{G_F}^{G_\mathbb Q}\lambda,\qquad
 V=W\otimes B.
```


B is the standard two-dimensional representation of S_3.
Its determinant is eta as well. By Shapiro and the local Mackey
identifications, the new cross-block finite Selmer problem is the
one for W_F tensor lambda (equivalently its conjugate), or V over Q.
The choice of signs in the induction is immaterial. Tate twists
used in the automorphic normalization disappear on the cyclotomic
kernel.

**Proposition.** For every g in G_Q, the dimension of the
one-eigenspace of V(g) over an algebraic closure is even.
In particular no element of G_{Q(mu_{ell^infinity})} has
rank-one quotient V/(g-1)V.

**Proof.** If g in G_F, B(g) has eigenvalues z,z^(-1) with
z a cube root of unity, and det W(g)=1.
If z=1, the tensor is the direct sum of two copies of W(g),
so its fixed dimension is even, including nonsemisimple W(g).
Otherwise z != z^(-1); a fixed vector requires W(g) to have
one of these two eigenvalues, in which case the determinant-one
condition forces both, each simple. The fixed dimension is then
two, and otherwise zero.
If g is outside G_F, B(g) is a transposition with eigenvalues
1,-1. Since det W(g)=-1, if either 1 or -1 is an eigenvalue
of W(g), then both are simple eigenvalues. Again the fixed
dimension is two or zero. These cases exhaust the group.
Kernel and cokernel of g-1 have equal dimension. QED.

Consequently the usual rank-one Euler-system large-image
condition, requiring an element in the cyclotomic kernel with
one-dimensional quotient, is impossible for this particular
tensor. For the general obstruction and its relevance to
Rankin--Selberg Euler systems see E. Studnia, *On obstructions
to the Euler system method for Rankin--Selberg convolutions*,
Res. Number Theory (2026),
https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-026-00718-1 .
This computation is not an appeal to a conjectural implication
from root numbers. It uses the actual two equal determinants.

Passing to G_F separates the two constituents of V and changes
the Euler-system problem. A separate Euler system or theorem over
F for a single constituent might not have this rank-one
obstruction. No such result, with the necessary local and
residual hypotheses uniform in the new points, is proved here.
Also, the obstruction is to this particular standard hypothesis,
not a statement that the required Selmer groups are nonzero
or that no higher-rank or alternative rigidity proof can work.
The parent's Kato theorem for rational modular forms does not
give the desired vanishing over this cubic extension by Shapiro.

**Frontier:** the new point classification reduces the
characteristic-prime and reducible-characteristic-zero
arithmetic tasks. Actual invariant-ring finiteness, attachment
of all relevant points, cross-block finite Selmer vanishing
and the uniform odd-prime length bound remain open.
