# Trace generation and conditional adjoint rigidity of the cubic ring

24 November 2026. Two comparisons on the actual fixed-inertia ring:
its full weight-zero algebra is generated topologically by traces,
and automorphic attachment of an irreducible characteristic-zero
point suffices for normalized tangent rigidity. Neither comparison
proves the attachment or the remaining characteristic-prime
curve exclusion.

## 1. Integral trace generation

Let R be the complete Noetherian local ring of the normalized
polarized frame in
[cubic-high-dyadic-presentation.md](cubic-high-dyadic-presentation.md).
Use its actual homogeneous presentation by matrix-entry displacements,
including the coefficient DVR O. Write B for the full weight-zero
subring and C for the closed O-subalgebra generated by


```math
\operatorname{tr}r(g),\qquad g\in G_F.
```


The closure is in the maximal-ideal topology of R. The assertion
B=C is an identity of rings, without inverting the uniformizer
and without passing to their reduced quotients.

For g in G_F put g*=cg^(-1)c. The polarization and normalization
at c give r(g*)=J_3 r(g)^t J_3. In particular the two adjacent
entries of each weight are exchanged, and each corner is fixed
under reversing transpose. At the separating mark x,


```math
D=r(x)-r(x^*)=dT,\qquad T=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,-1),
```


where d reduces to a nonzero scalar in the residue field. The
marked difference equations and the polarization give precisely
this diagonal identity, not merely the corresponding residual
identity. Since D is a difference of representation matrices,
tr(D^2)/2 lies in C and equals d^2. Fix the lift of its square
root having residue that of d. Hensel iteration in C gives d:
the inverse of a unit whose residue lies in O's residue field
is obtained by the convergent geometric series after multiplying
by a coefficient unit. C is closed and complete for the
induced topology. The unique root in R with that residue is d
because 2d is a unit. Consequently the coordinate projectors


```math
P_1=\frac{D(D+d)}{2d^2},\qquad
 P_2=1-\frac{D^2}{d^2},\qquad
 P_3=\frac{D(D-d)}{2d^2}
```


are C-linear combinations of group matrices (products of group
matrices are again group matrices). The trace of any word in
these projectors and group matrices therefore belongs to C.

For clarity, here is the complete balanced-monomial argument.
The weights of entry displacements are 0, +/-1, +/-2,
from torus weights 1,0,-1. Each balanced monomial is a product
of weight-zero variables and the following elementary balanced
multisets of nonzero weights:


```math
(1,-1),\quad(2,-2),\quad
 (1,1,-2),\quad(-1,-1,2).
```


To see this, successively remove opposite equal weights.
If no such pair remains, the only possible nonempty balanced
multisets use one sign of weight one and the other sign of
weight two; they decompose into the indicated triples.
For the corner-eliminated presentation only the first and last
multisets can occur. The diagonal displacements already lie
in C, since r_ii(g)=tr(P_i r(g)).
Each adjacent entry can, using g* instead of g, be placed
in either adjacent position with the same weight. Opposite
weight-one entries can therefore be written in positions
(1,2) and (2,1). Opposite corners are in positions (1,3)
and (3,1). For the triples place the two adjacent entries
in positions (1,2),(2,3) or (2,1),(3,2), respectively,
and close the cycle with the opposite corner.
In all these cases the product is the trace of the closed
matrix cycle with the coordinate projectors inserted, hence
belongs to C. Every weight-zero variable and every balanced
monomial in the actual entry displacements belongs to C.
The homogeneous presentation expresses every weight-zero
element as the limit of polynomials in such monomials.
This gives B contained in C, and the reverse inclusion follows
from invariance of traces and closedness of B.

The proof works in residue characteristic at least five as
here, or at any odd coefficient prime for this rank-three
marked presentation (only 2 and d are inverted). In the
latter generality use characteristic-polynomial identities
rather than recovering all characteristic coefficients from
traces by division by 3. In the present ell>=5 range
Newton identities do also identify the closed trace algebra
with the characteristic-coefficient algebra.

**Exact consumer.** This gives a smaller target for the
remaining curve and automorphy arguments. It suffices to prove
that the traces of all actual representations of the remaining
irreducible characteristic-prime curves factor through finite
residue-field Hecke algebras at the fixed weights and levels.
Indeed the image of such an algebra in k'[[t]] is a finite
constant field, so all the characteristic polynomials reduce
injectively and equal the distinguished residual polynomials.
Brauer--Nesbitt contradicts irreducibility. This is a sufficient
condition on the actual curve, not a statement merely about
the residual eigenpacket.

A finite O-algebra mapping onto B would also imply B's finiteness
directly. Its characteristic-zero Hecke-point interpretation
must still identify the actual representations and the actual
levels, not merely a common residual system of traces.
The corner-elimination and curve criterion already show that
every remaining curve in the fixed-inertia problem is absolutely
irreducible, so no reducible curve needs to be added to this
consumer. The trace-generation identity does not give such
a finite algebra, and imposes no finiteness on B by itself.

## 2. Local finiteness for actual dual-number lifts

We retain the high-dyadic mark at every v|ell with distinct
residual diagonal values and residual cyclotomic value one.
Let E' be a finite coefficient extension containing the values
of a characteristic-zero point r and let V be its rank-three
representation on G_F. At v|ell its actual ordered flag has
one-dimensional characters delta_1,delta_2,delta_3 of the
prescribed inertia types. These are potentially crystalline:
each is a cyclotomic power times a finite character and
an unramified character. Their exponents in the convention
where epsilon has Hodge--Tate weight -1 are e,0,-e.
For i<j, delta_i/delta_j has strictly negative Hodge--Tate
weight. Moreover


```math
H^0(F_v,(\delta_i/\delta_j)^*(1))=0
```


by the separating mark, since the cyclotomic value reduces to
one and the two diagonal values have distinct residues.

We use the standard local Euler characteristic, local Tate
duality, and Bloch--Kato dimension formula: for a de Rham
representation U all of whose Hodge--Tate weights are negative
and with H^0(F_v,U^*(1))=0,


```math
\begin{aligned}
 \dim H^1(F_v,U)&=[F_v:\mathbf Q_\ell]\dim U+\dim H^0(F_v,U),\\
 \dim H^1_f(F_v,U)&=\dim D_{\rm dR}(U)/\mathrm{Fil}^0+
                         \dim H^0(F_v,U)
                         =\dim H^1(F_v,U).
 \end{aligned}
```


All dimensions are over the coefficient field. Here H^1_f
is the kernel defined using B_cris. This formula can be used
for the potentially crystalline Hom representations at the
original local field; it is not necessary that they already
be crystalline there. In particular we are not assuming
that the residual separating mark survives a further local
extension chosen to kill the finite inertia characters.

First apply the formula to the extension classes for the
ordered flag of V. At each step the Hom representation
between the next graded line and the earlier block has a
filtration by the strictly negative ratios above. It is
potentially crystalline by induction, hence de Rham; its
Tate dual has no invariants by left exactness of H^0 and
the vanishing for the graded ratios. Thus H^1=H^1_f.
Restrict to a common finite extension where the two pieces
are crystalline. The restricted finite class is crystalline
by the B_cris extension criterion. This proves potential
crystallinity of V by induction.

Now let an actual dual-number point lifting r be given,
satisfying the same ordinary equations and inertia labels.
The canonical ordered flag obtained from the separating
mark still exists over E'[u]/u^2. Regard the deformation
as a six-dimensional representation over E'. Its three
successive two-dimensional blocks have scalar characters
delta_i multiplied by an unramified self-extension of the
trivial character: the ordered inertia type is fixed, so
the infinitesimal character is zero on inertia. Each block
is potentially crystalline. Between the earlier blocks and
the next one the Hom representation again has a filtration
by the same strictly negative ratios, now with multiplicity.
The argument above therefore proves that the entire
six-dimensional representation is potentially crystalline.

On a finite local extension where the two copies of V
and their extension are crystalline, the tangent class
in H^1(ad V) becomes a finite class. Restriction followed
by corestriction on the characteristic-zero module
ad V tensor B_cris is multiplication by the extension
degree. Thus restriction there is injective, which proves
that the original tangent already lies in H^1_f(F_v,ad V).
Away from ell the actual inertia kernel has trivial action
and the deformation is trivial on it by its equations.
The remaining finite inertia quotient has prime-to-ell
order; averaging gives the unramified tangent condition.
Restricting a tangent of the polarized representation over
Q to G_F gives exactly these local conditions, which
descend at nonsplit places by the same restriction and
corestriction argument (degree at most two).

This verifies the finite local conditions for actual
tangents. It is stronger than verifying them for the
characteristic-zero representations alone.

## 3. Adjoints after genuine cuspidal attachment

Suppose now that the actual irreducible point V epsilon^(-1)
is attached to a regular algebraic conjugate-self-dual
cuspidal automorphic representation of GL3 over F, with
the polarization of the cubic frame. The scalar Tate twist
does not alter its adjoint. Its multiplier is epsilon^(-2)
on G_F, and the normalized extension over Q acts on adjoint
matrices at c by minus reversing transpose. This is the
polarized adjoint in the theorem below (a scalar change
of the pairing has no effect).

By the full monodromy theorem the representation is
irreducible on G_{F(mu_{ell^infinity})}. Therefore Theorem 6.1
of J. A. Thorne, On the vanishing of adjoint Bloch--Kato Selmer
groups of irreducible automorphic Galois representations,
[arXiv:2207.04925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04925),
Pure Appl. Math. Q. 18 (2022), DOI
[10.4310/pamq.2022.v18.n5.a5](https://doi.org/10.4310/pamq.2022.v18.n5.a5),
applies to this adjoint. The theorem has an irreducibility
hypothesis on the cyclotomic kernel, not a large residual
image hypothesis; its special-case splitting and Iwahori
conditions are part of its own base-change proof, not
additional assumptions of the main theorem.
It gives H^1_f(Q,ad V)=0. For the fixed-determinant adjoint
the trace-zero summand is a direct summand, so the required
cohomology vanishes there as well.

Section 2 places every actual tangent in this vanishing
group. The normalization then leaves precisely the torus
direction: a coboundary primitive is fixed by c and its
commutator with dT is diagonal, forcing it to be a diagonal
multiple of T. At a two-sided irreducible point this torus
direction is nonzero. Thus its normalized characteristic-zero
tangent space has dimension one.

The preceding result is **conditional on actual cuspidal
attachment**. It removes the separate finite-local-condition
and adjoint-vanishing premises of the earlier high-dyadic
length comparison after attachment. It does not prove that
attachment. Nor does the pointwise characteristic-zero
rigidity imply integral finiteness: characteristic-prime
components remain a separate test. Once the actual
irreducible curves have been excluded and the actual
irreducible characteristic-zero points attached, the
homogeneous presentation and fixed-field spectral comparison
supply the previously established conditional length bound.
