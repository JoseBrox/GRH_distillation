# Finite section ranks from one fibre of a projection

## Result and scope

A finite geometric estimate supplies the upper section-rank comparison
used in the RH and Dirichlet GRH component argument. It needs no uniform
Hilbert–Samuel convergence threshold. The proof counts jets at the points
of one general fibre of a projection. Blowing up the base ideal handles
the original linear system without assuming that it is globally generated.

For the parameters used by the source, the resulting relative rank loss is

\[
 \eta\le D\rho+\frac{2D^2+4D}{t}
       \le D\rho+\frac{4D^2}{t}\quad(D\ge2),                 \tag{1}
\]

in place of \(7(D^2/t+D\rho)\). The exact binomial ratio below is better
than either displayed majorant. This is an **additive relative rank
loss**, not a logarithmic loss.

The result supplies a finite rank estimate once the prescribed projective
model, section realization, nefness, birational linear system and degree
comparison are supplied. It does not construct that model, prove the
signed cusp mean, establish the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel remainder, or
close the full geometric proof-map node. In particular, a bound on ranks
alone does not give a bound on arithmetic section degrees.

## 1. Jet detection with an exact degree threshold

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let
\(X\) be an integral projective variety of dimension \(D\ge1\), let
\(A\) be a nef line bundle, and let a finite-dimensional linear system
\(V\subset H^0(X,A)\) give a birational rational map onto a
\(D\)-dimensional projective image of degree \(\delta>0\). Let \(B\)
be any line bundle, and let \(n\ge0\) be an integer such that
\(nA-B\) is nef. Additive notation denotes tensor products of line
bundles. For every integer \(a\ge0\),

\[
 n A^D<(a+1)\delta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{h^0(X,B)\le\delta\binom{a+D}{D}.}                  \tag{2}
\]

The line bundle \(B\) itself need not be nef. More generally, the
argument only requires the mixed intersection inequality
\((\pi^*B)L^{D-1}<(a+1)\delta\), with \(\pi,L\) defined next.

### Resolving only the base ideal

Let \(\mathcal I\subset\mathcal O_X\) be the image of
\(V\otimes A^{-1}\to\mathcal O_X\), and let
\(\pi:Y=\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}X\to X\).
The variety \(Y\) is integral and projective, and
\(\mathcal I\mathcal O_Y=\mathcal O_Y(-E)\) for an effective
Cartier divisor \(E\). The line bundle

\[
                     L=\pi^*A-E
\]

is generated by the lifted sections. Its map is birational onto the
original image, so the projection formula gives \(L^D=\delta\).
Both \(L\) and \(\pi^*A\) are nef. No resolution of singularities
or global smoothness of \(Y\) is required.

A general linear projection of the image to \(\mathbf P^D\) has a
centre disjoint from the image. It therefore gives a morphism
\(f:Y\to\mathbf P^D\) with \(L=f^*\mathcal O(1)\), generically
finite of degree \(\delta\). In characteristic zero it is finite
étale over a nonempty open subset. Choose \(q\) in that subset.
Its fibre \(Q=f^{-1}(q)\) consists of \(\delta\) distinct points,
each regular of dimension \(D\).

### Why one fibre detects every section

Consider evaluation after pullback:

\[
 H^0(X,B)\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{z\in Q}(\pi^*B)_z\otimes
                  \mathcal O_{Y,z}/\mathfrak m_z^{a+1}.      \tag{3}
\]

Suppose a nonzero section \(s\) belongs to its kernel. Its pullback
is nonzero, since \(\pi\) is dominant, and has an effective Cartier
zero divisor \(Z_s\).

The set \(N\subset\mathbf P^D\) over which \(f\) is not finite
is closed of codimension at least two. Indeed, properness makes it
closed, and a positive-dimensional fibre over a generic point of a
divisor would force a proper closed subset of the integral
\(D\)-fold \(Y\) to have dimension at least \(D\). Equivalently,
the precise codimension-one assertion is Stacks, Lemma 33.17.2.
For \(D\ge2\), choose a line \(\ell\) through \(q\) which avoids
\(N\) and is not contained in \(f(Z_s)\). Such lines exist:
the directions meeting \(N\) form a proper closed subset of the
space of directions, while \(f(Z_s)\) is a proper closed subset
of \(\mathbf P^D\). The chosen line may depend on \(s\); the
fibre \(Q\) does not. For \(D=1\), use \(\ell=\mathbf P^1\).

The inverse image of \(\ell\) is finite over \(\ell\). Its
one-dimensional cycle \(C\) is the proper intersection of
\(D-1\) members of \(|L|\), hence has class \(L^{D-1}\).
Every one-dimensional component dominates \(\ell\), so none is
contained in \(Z_s\). Possible zero-dimensional embedded parts do
not enter this cycle. Near each point of \(Q\), the map is étale;
there \(C\) is a smooth curve with multiplicity one. Vanishing
in (3) therefore gives a zero of order at least \(a+1\) on that
curve. Restricting to the normalizations of the curve components
and summing effective zero divisors gives

\[
             (\pi^*B)L^{D-1}\ge(a+1)\delta.                \tag{4}
\]

On the other hand, nefness gives

\[
 (\pi^*B)L^{D-1}
 \le n(\pi^*A)L^{D-1}\le n(\pi^*A)^D=n A^D.              \tag{5}
\]

For the middle inequality when \(D\ge2\), expand

\[
 (\pi^*A)^D-(\pi^*A)L^{D-1}
  =E\,(\pi^*A)\sum_{j=0}^{D-2}(\pi^*A)^{D-2-j}L^j\ge0.
\]

Each term is the intersection of nef line bundles on an effective
cycle. It is nonnegative by approximation by ample classes. For
\(D=1\), the two terms are equal. Equations (4)–(5) contradict
the strict hypothesis in (2), proving injectivity.

Finally, at each regular point \(z\), monomials in \(D\) local
parameters of total degree at most \(a\) form a basis of
\(\mathcal O_{Y,z}/\mathfrak m_z^{a+1}\). This quotient has
dimension \(\binom{a+D}{D}\). Counting (3) proves (2).

The strict inequality cannot be replaced by a weak inequality:
on \(\mathbf P^1\), take \(A=\mathcal O(1)\),
\(B=\mathcal O(a+1)\) and \(n=a+1\). Then \(\delta=1\),
the degree threshold is an equality, and \(h^0(B)=a+2>a+1\).
A section with a zero of order \(a+1\) at the chosen point is
indeed killed by its \(a\)-jet.

## 2. Exact and simplified rank losses

Suppose \(t>D\), \(0\le\rho<1\), and

\[
 \delta\ge(1-\rho)A^D,\qquad n=t+D+3,\qquad
 a=\left\lfloor\frac{t+D+3}{1-\rho}\right\rfloor.
\]

Then \(n A^D\le n\delta/(1-\rho)<(a+1)\delta\).
For any section subspace of rank \(r\le h^0(X,B)\), set

\[
 w=\delta\binom{t-1}{D},\qquad
 \eta=1-\frac{\binom{t-1}{D}}{\binom{a+D}{D}}.             \tag{6}
\]

Equation (2) gives \((1-\eta)r\le w\). To prove (1), write
the ratio in (6) as \(\prod_{j=1}^D(t-j)/(a+j)\). Its \(j\)-th
factor is at least

\[
 \frac{(1-\rho)(t-j)}{t+D+3+j(1-\rho)}=1-e_j,
 \quad 0\le e_j<1,\quad
 e_j\le\rho+\frac{D+3+2j}{t}.
\]

The elementary product inequality
\(1-\prod_j(1-e_j)\le\sum_j e_j\) proves (1).
This remains valid when the displayed majorant exceeds one; the
resulting coarse rank comparison is then simply uninformative.
If a separate lower-rank estimate gives \(w\le r\), and
\(\Theta=D\rho+(2D^2+4D)/t<1\), then the excess-rank parameter
in the raw arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel formula satisfies

\[
             0\le\frac{r-w}{w}\le\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}
                                  \le\frac{\Theta}{1-\Theta}. \tag{7}
\]

This is the precise conversion needed in the signed remainder. It
must not be replaced by \((r-w)/w\le\Theta\).

## 4. References and verification

The elementary proof uses the following standard algebraic-geometric
facts, checked against their primary reference:

- Stacks Project, [Blowing up, tag 01OF](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01OF),
  Lemmas 31.33.4, 31.33.5 and 31.33.13: the base ideal becomes an
  invertible ideal defining an effective Cartier divisor, and the
  blowup is projective.
- Stacks Project, [Generically finite morphisms, tag 0AB5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AB5),
  Lemma 33.17.2: a proper generically finite morphism is finite near
  every codimension-one point of the target.
- Stacks Project, [Numerical intersections, tag 0BEL](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BEL):
  multilinearity, reduction to effective divisor cycles, projection
  and positivity for ample classes. The nef inequalities used above
  follow by continuity from ample perturbations.

The exact checker authenticates the source bytes, checks the rational
binomial inequalities, and certifies injectivity in finite polynomial
models by nonzero minors modulo a prime. It also checks an equality
threshold counterexample. These computations test the formulas and
examples, not the geometric model or the full theorem. The theorem's
proof is the argument above; no Lean compilation or kernel replay is
claimed. Neither RH nor GRH is concluded.
