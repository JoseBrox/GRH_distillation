# The global Galois and period dictionary

This note compares the global representation contract in the pinned source
with the classical Galois representations attached to regular algebraic
polarized cuspidal automorphic representations. It continues the comparison
in [the global input audit](lean-global-inputs.md); the independent audit of
the full local Langlands laws belongs to
[the local correspondence note](classical-local-correspondence.md).

**Result and adoption.** The global automorphic, weight and period-model
dictionary closes by the explicit comparisons below. The same classical
global representation works for every allowed local bridge and tied
period model. The full local correspondence dictionary is now also
proved and independently reviewed. Together with the checked depth-zero
type formula, Good-preimage arguments and transfer to the weaker source
correspondences, this supplies `Row0b IsRecT`. The classical replacement
of the Galois construction is therefore adopted. Its exact logical
dependencies remain explicit in §5; no additional global period-model
hypothesis is retained.

All source citations refer to `jsmorph/rh` at
`ecd09ff9b4aaf6c08f2b5ef17dc49ca9648a77de`. The accompanying
[certificate](../certificates/galois-period-dictionary.json) records checked
Git object hashes, SHA-256 hashes and bounded line ranges. This is a
mathematical comparison, not a new Lean compilation result.

## 1. What the local models actually specify

Write \(p\) for the coefficient characteristic and \(K\) for a completion of
the CM field at a place above \(p\).

| Source object | Mathematical content | Relevant source |
|---|---|---|
| `LocPrime` | One coherent system of period rings and actions for every finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\), with Frobenius, filtration and cyclotomic period | `DataNLoc.lean`, 527–562 |
| `LocBridge` | An embedding of the global algebraic closure into \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\), a dense embedding of the global field into \(K\), and a topological local Galois isomorphism compatible with that embedding | `DataNLoc.lean`, 574–591 |
| `Induces` | The embedding preserves the specified valuation ring | `FiniteDimensionalLetter.lean`, 108–114 |
| `LocTied` | Isomorphisms to the concrete \(B_{\mathrm{cris}}\) and \(B_{\mathrm{dR}}\), preserving the structures listed below | `LocTied.lean`, 11–36 |
| `IsModel` | A representation over a finite coefficient extension whose matrix entries become exactly those of the chosen global representation | `FiniteDimensionalWdSpace.lean`, 187–188 |
| `DdRE` and `FildR` | The embedding eigenspace of de Rham invariants, intersected with the ordinary period filtration | `Statement.lean`, 146–154 |
| `multE` | The dimension difference of successive filtered pieces | `DataN.lean`, 1161–1163 |

The compatibility condition on the local Galois isomorphism is
\(e(g)(j(x))=j(g(x))\), for every element of the global algebraic closure.
Together with density and `Induces`, this identifies it with the usual
restriction to the chosen completion. It is not an extra choice of a
possibly different Galois representation.

The crystalline and de Rham predicates are the usual dimension equalities.
With coefficients in a finite extension \(L/\mathbb Q_p\), the source asks
respectively for

\[
 \dim_L D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V)=[K_0:\mathbb Q_p]\dim_LV,
 \qquad
 \dim_L D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)=[K:\mathbb Q_p]\dim_LV.
\]

Here dimensions over \(L\) include the contribution of the base field; this
is the conventional admissibility condition over \(K_0\otimes L\) or
\(K\otimes L\). The source writes these invariant modules as intersections
of kernels of \(g-1\), which gives precisely the same spaces.

## 2. Transport of the crystalline and de Rham data

Let \(e_B\) and \(e_D\) be the two isomorphisms in `LocTied`. Its fields
`zB` and `zD` identify \(\mathbb Z_p\)-scalars, hence also
\(\mathbb Q_p=\mathbb Z_p[1/p]\)-scalars. Its fields `actB`, `actD`,
`frob` and `fil` give equivariance, compatibility with crystalline
Frobenius, and equality of every de Rham filtration piece.

There are two scalar details worth making explicit.

1. The field `om` identifies the reduction of every algebraic scalar in
   \(B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\). Its image is the usual algebraic scalar: for an
   algebraic element \(x\), apply Hensel uniqueness to its separable
   minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb Q_p\). Two roots in the complete
   local ring \(B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\) with the same residue \(x\) coincide.
   The derivative at that residue is nonzero and therefore a unit. Thus
   \(e_D\) is compatible with \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\)-scalars, not
   just with \(\mathbb Q_p\).
2. The field `ιA`, together with `LocAinf.ιA_teich`, identifies the
   prime-to-\(p\) Teichmüller roots of unity. Every finite unramified
   extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) is generated by such roots. Consequently
   \(e_B\) respects the specified \(\mathbb Q_p^{\mathrm{nr}}\)-scalars.

For a finite coefficient representation \(V\), tensoring with the identity
on \(V\) therefore gives equivariant isomorphisms

\[
 V\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{\mathrm{source}}
   \simeq V\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}B_{\mathrm{cris}},
 \qquad
 V\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\mathrm{source}}
   \simeq V\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}B_{\mathrm{dR}}.
\]

Taking the intersections of the kernels of \(g-1\) preserves these
isomorphisms. The de Rham map also preserves the eigenspaces indexed by
embeddings \(K\hookrightarrow L\), because it respects algebraic scalars,
and it preserves their intersections with every filtered piece. Thus it
preserves crystalline admissibility, de Rham admissibility, every
Hodge–Tate multiplicity and the labeled Hodge–Tate multisets. The argument
applies to every allowed bridge and every tied period model separately.

This is a direct transport argument. No new comparison theorem about
automorphic representations is required to handle these model parameters.

### Finite coefficient models do not add a uniformity hypothesis

The source already proves model independence by placing two finite
coefficient fields in a common finite extension. See
`FiniteDimensionalFilDR.lean`, 218–250: crystalline and de Rham predicates,
potential crystallinity and all labeled multiplicities agree. It also
constructs the jump multiset from the de Rham condition, at 257–260.

For completeness, a continuous representation of a profinite group in
\(\mathrm{GL}_n(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p)\) is defined over a finite
extension. Indeed, there are countably many finite subextensions of
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\). The compact image is covered by its closed
subgroups consisting of matrices over each such extension. The Baire
theorem makes one of these subgroups open. It has finite index, and
adjoining the finitely many matrix entries of a finite set of coset
representatives gives one finite extension containing the entire image.
One can enlarge this field to include the finite coefficient field in the
chosen bridge. This produces the exact matrix model required by
`IsModel`.

The subgroup argument matters: a general compact subset of
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) need not lie in a finite extension. No such
false compactness assertion is used here.

## 3. Semistable periods and the Weil–Deligne representation

The source constructs the semistable period ring as a polynomial ring
over its crystalline period ring. In its variable \(X\),

\[
 g(X)=X+c_p(g)t,\qquad \varphi(X)=pX,\qquad N=-\frac{d}{dX}.
\]

The Kummer cocycle \(c_p(g)\) is defined from a compatible system of
\(p\)-power roots of \(p\), using the same roots of unity as the period
\(t\). The formulas are in `LocalPrimeGaloisActionPeriodT.lean`, 64–65 and
187–229. The source takes the union of the invariant spaces after all
finite local extensions to form \(D_{\mathrm{pst}}\), then imposes the
usual full-rank condition (`EqOfPowEqOneOfNormSubLt.lean`, 252–253 and
358–385).

The polynomial ring, Galois action and monodromy are the presentation in
Berger, *An introduction to the theory of p-adic representations*,
§II.3.3, author PDF p.15. The variable is the logarithm of a compatible
system of roots of \(p\). Consequently
\(\varphi(\log[x])=\log[x^p]=p\log[x]\); see also Berger's 2002 paper,
§2.4, pp.23–24, and the explicit Frobenius formula at the start of §3.1,
p.30. The survey visually prints \(\varphi(Y)=Y^p\) at this point. With
the displayed additive Galois action and \(\varphi(t)=pt\), that formula
would not commute with Galois: the coefficient of \(Y^{p-1}\) in
\((Y+c(g)t)^p\) is nonzero when \(c(g)\ne0\). Thus that printed power
cannot be used for the logarithm variable. The normalization used here
is the directly derived and independently printed \(\varphi(X)=pX\).

### The crystalline inclusion and the period are fixed

One must also identify the source period \(t\), rather than infer it from
the names of the rings. The field `LocTied.incl` characterizes the map
\(B_{\mathrm{cris}}\to B_{\mathrm{dR}}\) by its value on the divided-power
algebra and a boundedness condition on each de Rham quotient. These
conditions determine the usual inclusion uniquely. Here is a precise
way to use the source's existing statement of this elementary completion
argument:

- `CrisIncl.lean`, 20–29, restricts any such map to
  \(A_{\mathrm{cris}}\to B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\).
- `IsCrisP.lean`, 37–42, spells out agreement on the dense divided-power
  algebra and the uniform boundedness condition.
- `CanonicalAcrisToBdRPlusUniqueEquivariantApproxStatement.lean`, 8,
  asserts uniqueness of this restriction. Extending over the two
  localizations defining \(B_{\mathrm{cris}}\) gives uniqueness of the
  original map.

Mathematically, approximate an element of \(A_{\mathrm{cris}}\) modulo
\(p^r\) by an element of the divided-power algebra. In a fixed de Rham
quotient, the difference of two candidate maps lies in \(p^{r-c}\) times
the bounded integral lattice, where \(c\) depends on the quotient but
not on the element. Separatedness, followed by \(r\to\infty\), gives
equality in every quotient, and hence equality in
\(B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\). This explains why the boundedness quantifier is
needed.

Now `LocTied.t` identifies the image of the source period with
\(\log[\varepsilon]\). The crystalline inclusion is injective, so
\(e_B(t)=t_{\varepsilon}\) in \(B_{\mathrm{cris}}\).
Consequently the polynomial extension of \(e_B\), sending \(X\) to the
classical logarithm variable for the same root system, intertwines
Galois, Frobenius and monodromy. Tensoring with \(V\) and taking invariants
over every finite extension therefore identifies the two
\(D_{\mathrm{pst}}\) modules, including all three operators and the
specified unramified scalars.

There is no restriction hidden in the choice of roots. Replacing
\(\varepsilon\) by \(\varepsilon^u\), with
\(u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times\), replaces \(t\) by \(ut\) and the Kummer
cocycle by \(u^{-1}c_p\), leaving their product unchanged. Replacing the
system of roots of \(p\) by its product with
\(\varepsilon^a\) replaces the variable by \(X+at\). This substitution
commutes with Frobenius and monodromy and gives the required Galois
action. Thus the resulting functor is independent of these choices up to
isomorphism.

### The Frobenius exponent and scalar specialization

Let \(q=p^f\) be the size of the residue field, and let \(d(g)\) be the
source Weil degree. It is the arithmetic degree: geometric Frobenius has
degree \(-1\). The source defines

\[
 r_{\mathrm{WD}}(g)=g\,\varphi^{-f d(g)}.
\]

This is `FiniteDimensionalLetter.lean`, 140 and 219–229. Its Galois action
on \(\mathbb Q_p^{\mathrm{nr}}\) is \(\varphi^{f d(g)}\), so the product
is linear over the unramified scalars. Since
\(N\varphi=p\varphi N\), it satisfies

\[
 r_{\mathrm{WD}}(g)N r_{\mathrm{WD}}(g)^{-1}=q^{d(g)}N.
\]

For geometric Frobenius this factor is \(q^{-1}\), exactly the
geometric-Artin convention used by BLGGT. The source proves this formula
at `FiniteDimensionalLetter.lean`, 235–241. Taylor–Yoshida, §1, author
PDF p.4, writes the same construction as \(g\varphi^{fm}\) for an element
over the \(m\)-th power of geometric Frobenius; here \(m=-d(g)\).

The source's `Letter` is simply the scalar specialization

\[
 \bigl(\mathbb C\otimes_L D_{\mathrm{pst}}(V)\bigr)
 \big/\langle cv-\iota(c)v:
             c\in\mathbb Q_p^{\mathrm{nr}}\rangle.
\]

It chooses the same coefficient embedding and unramified embedding as the
classical construction (`FiniteDimensionalLetter.lean`, 19–28;
`FiniteDimensionalWdSpace.lean`, 23–31). Finally, `wdRep` applies
Frobenius semisimplification and leaves \(N\) unchanged, as recorded in
`FiniteDimensionalWdSpace.lean`, 126–136. This is the same operation as
the superscript \(\mathrm{F\!\!\!-ss}\) in the classical theorems.

Finite extensions of the coefficient field commute with all these
constructions: after choosing a finite basis, invariants are computed
coordinate by coordinate. A common finite coefficient extension compares
any two source models; specializing along \(\iota\) gives the same
complex Weil–Deligne isomorphism class. Since Berger's theorem supplies
potential semistability, the default branch in the source definition of
`wdAt` is never used here.

### Places away from the coefficient prime

The source's construction away from \(p\) is also the usual one. Choose
a surjective tame character \(t_p:I_K\to\mathbb Z_p\), arithmetic
Frobenius \(F\), and the nilpotent operator supplied by the monodromy
theorem. Write \(g=F^{d(g)}i(g)\). The source puts

\[
 r_{\mathrm{WD}}(g)=r(g)\exp\bigl(-t_p(i(g))N\bigr)
\]

and then takes Frobenius semisimplification. See
`WeilDeligneParamMonodromyEqZeroIff.lean`, 16–20, 78–107 and 340–354.
These are the standard inverse formulas for
\(r(F^m i)=r_{\mathrm{WD}}(F^m i)\exp(t_p(i)N)\), recalled in
Taylor–Yoshida, §1, author PDF p.3. Existence of monodromy for the
continuous finite coefficient representation makes the default branch
irrelevant; the source uniqueness theorem then identifies `wdOf` with
this usual isomorphism class.

## 4. The automorphic and weight dictionary

The source works with the \(K_\infty\)-finite smooth realization of an
automorphic representation. This is a conventional realization of the
same object used in the classical theorem, not an additional arithmetic
condition.

In detail, `Sm` consists of functions smooth at infinity
(`DifferentiableAtChart.lean`, 296–302). `IsAutForm` imposes left rational
invariance, an open finite-adelic stabilizer, \(K_\infty\)-finiteness,
finiteness under the center of the enveloping algebra, and moderate
growth (`ExpoConjOfIsCsd.lean`, 48–53). The center is expressed by the
Capelli operators. Their coefficients generate that center: under the
Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt filtration their leading symbols are the
coefficients of the ordinary characteristic polynomial, which generate
the invariant polynomials of a matrix. Subtracting a polynomial in the
Capelli coefficients removes the highest degree of any central element;
induction on degree proves the assertion. Evaluating at finitely many
distinct values of the polynomial variable recovers each coefficient, so
the source's use of all these evaluations generates the same algebra.
Its cuspidality condition is the vanishing of the constant
term along every two-block maximal parabolic (87–92). Vanishing along all
proper parabolics follows by taking constant terms successively; the
unipotent quotients involved are compact. Finally, `CuspSub` is a nonzero
irreducible submodule of this cusp-form space, stable under the finite
adeles, \(K_\infty\) and the Lie algebra (209–215). Thus it is precisely
the usual cuspidal automorphic Harish–Chandra module.

The source pairing is explicitly nondegenerate and invariant under all
three actions (`DualIsConjByOne.lean`, 31–35). The pairing between
\(\pi\otimes\psi^{-1}\) and \(\pi^c\) therefore gives

\[
 \pi^c\simeq\pi^\vee\otimes\psi,
 \qquad \psi=\chi\circ N_{E/E^+}\circ\det.
\]

To see this in the smooth realization, the pairing maps the second module
into the smooth admissible dual of the first: finite-adelic and compact
stabilizers are preserved by the invariance equations, as is the
infinitesimal action. The nondegenerate equivariant map between the
irreducible admissible modules is an isomorphism. `PolarizedPair.sign`
requires \(\chi_v(-1)=(-1)^n\) at every real place. These are exactly the
polarization hypotheses of BLGGT, printed pp.536–537. In particular the
totally real character need not be assumed separately algebraic: BLGGT
records that its algebraicity follows in the regular algebraic case.

### Regular algebraicity and the precise shift

At an embedding \(\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\), the source
infinitesimal multiset is characterized by the polynomial through which
the Capelli operators act. On an algebraic highest-weight vector with
weight \(\nu_0\ge\cdots\ge\nu_{n-1}\), the polynomial is

\[
 \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}
 \left(u+\nu_k+\frac{n-1}{2}-k\right).
\]

This is `ContinuousConjFin.lean`, 147–160; the definition of the multiset
is `ExpoConjOfIsCsd.lean`, 275–276. The source's regular algebraicity says
that these \(n\) entries are distinct and belong to
\(\mathbb Z+(n-1)/2\) (591–596). Conversely, order any such multiset
decreasingly, as \(z_0>\cdots>z_{n-1}\), and set
\(\nu_k=z_k-(n-1)/2+k\). The differences between successive \(z_k\)
are positive integers, so \(\nu_k\ge\nu_{k+1}\). This constructs a
dominant integral algebraic highest weight with exactly the required
infinitesimal character. The equivalence with classical regular
algebraicity is therefore explicit in both directions.

BLGGT calls the weight \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) when the infinitesimal
character is that of the **dual** of the algebraic representation of
highest weight \(a\). Thus \(\nu_k=-a_{n-k}\), and the source formula
for the Hodge–Tate multiset becomes

\[
 \left\{\frac{n-1}{2}-z_k:0\le k<n\right\}
   =\{a_i+n-i:1\le i\le n\}.
\]

This is exactly Theorem 2.1.1(3). The filtration convention agrees too:
for the cyclotomic character the de Rham invariant is represented by
\(v\otimes t^{-1}\), which has filtration degree \(-1\). Both sides
therefore assign Hodge–Tate number \(-1\) to the cyclotomic character.

At finite places the source `algNorm` is
\(\pi_v\otimes|\det|_v^{(1-n)/2}\), with the positive real norm and the
displayed exponent (`GaloisRepOfPolarizedCuspidal.lean`, 24–26). This is
the normalization in both BLGGT compatibility theorems.

## 5. Classical theorem inputs and the resulting global contract

The relevant published assertions are:

- Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor, *Potential automorphy and change of
  weight*, Theorem 2.1.1, printed p.538. It provides the global
  representation, compatibility away from \(p\), de Rham representations
  with their labeled weights at places above \(p\), and crystallinity at
  spherical places.
- The same authors, *Local-global compatibility for \(l=p\), II*, Theorem
  1.1, author PDF p.6. Its monodromy dominance assertion has no Iwahori,
  Shin-regularity or odd-prime hypothesis. When the target monodromy is
  zero, dominance forces the source monodromy to be zero too and gives
  the full Frobenius-semisimple comparison needed by the guarded source
  clauses.
- Berger, *Représentations p-adiques et équations différentielles*,
  Theorems 0.6 and 5.19: a de Rham representation is potentially
  semistable.

### Proposition: the global dictionary adds no further hypothesis

The two local hypotheses in the next proposition are now discharged by
[the full local correspondence audit](classical-local-correspondence.md)
and [the depth-zero type comparison](lean-global-inputs.md#the-classical-depth-zero-type-formula).
We leave them visible to distinguish the global argument from its local
dependencies.

Fix the classical local Langlands correspondence, in the geometric-Artin
normalization, at every finite place. Assume its full source local laws
are available, as audited separately, together with the depth-zero type
formula already checked in the global input audit. Then the classical global theorems above
supply the fixed-correspondence version of `Row0b`, with all its period
model and bridge quantifiers. Together with the previously proved
Good-preimage and weak-correspondence transfer lemmas, they supply
`Row0b IsRecT` itself.

**Proof.** Start with any CM field, positive rank, prime, coefficient
isomorphism and regular algebraic polarized cusp module permitted by
`WeightsConjBis.lean`, 69–73. Section 4 makes it a classical regular
algebraic polarized cuspidal representation. Apply BLGGT Theorem 2.1.1
once, choosing its single global continuous semisimple representation
\(r\), integer weight \(w\) and all labeled Hodge–Tate multisets.

Away from the coefficient prime, the classical equality and §3 give the
source equality for the fixed correspondence. It implies both guarded
comparisons and the unconditional dimension assertion in `AwayB`.
The highest-weight computation gives `PropsB.weights`; Theorem 2.1.1(3)
gives its conjugate-weight symmetry with the same integer \(w\).

Now allow an arbitrary prime above the coefficient prime, arbitrary local
bridge, and arbitrary tied period model. A finite coefficient model
exists by §2. The equivariant tensor transport gives the required de Rham
condition and every labeled filtration multiplicity. At a spherical
place, Theorem 2.1.1(4) additionally gives crystallinity. In either of the
guarded `AboveB.nice` cases, Berger gives potential semistability, and
BLGGT's local-global Theorem 1.1 gives monodromy dominance. The target
monodromy is zero by the explicit guard. All target Jordan blocks have
length one, so dominance forces every source block to have length one;
the source monodromy is zero, and the Frobenius-semisimple parameters are
equal. Section 3 identifies this equality with the source `wdAt` equality.
It is stronger than the requested `AwayB` conclusions.

Every choice of local model was made after fixing the same global
\(r,w,\mathrm{HT}\). Thus the universal model quantifier in
`PropsB.above`, 64–67, has its stated order. Finally, the Good-preimage
argument identifies the arbitrary weak local parameter with the
classical one precisely in the guarded cases. Its dimension law handles
the unguarded dimension assertion. This transfers the same global data
to the universal `IsRecT` contract. \(\square\)

The [Good-preimage lemmas](local-correspondence-bridge.md) and
[weak-correspondence transfer](fixed-correspondence-reduction.md) complete
the chain of implications. The current local and global dictionaries
discharge the conditions that those earlier checkpoints recorded as
remaining. No quasi-Riemann hypothesis or Siegel-zero assertion was used
to obtain this replacement. Removing the separate geometric construction
of these Galois representations does not remove the geometric or theta
inputs that have other uses in the full proof.

### Manuscript integration

The conventional theorem and period-independence lemma are integrated
into the manuscript's section on Galois representations and adjoint
vanishing. Its polarization hypothesis only requires the real signs of
the Hecke character to be constant. Multiplication by the quadratic
character of the CM extension, when needed, gives the parity convention
used above without changing the character pulled back by the norm.
Thus this broader formulation also covers every source polarized pair.

The integration preserves the geometric-Artin convention, the twist
\(|\det|^{(1-n)/2}\), the dual highest-weight convention and the
cyclotomic Hodge–Tate number \(-1\). It chooses the global representation
before all local coefficient and period realizations. It asserts
semisimplicity, and the later adjoint-vanishing theorem separately retains
its irreducibility hypothesis on the cyclotomic restriction. The
certificate records the audited manuscript and bibliography hashes and
the ranges inspected; no implication from semisimplicity to
irreducibility is used.
