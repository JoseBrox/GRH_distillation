# The original compact-model integral and signed boundary comparison

## Result and retained arithmetic data

This closes the compact-model mixed-integral and common vertical-curvature
interfaces left in the three preceding boundary packages. Together with
their finite-energy, bounded-cusp and exact metric-variation results, it
proves the **original guarded signed Stokes comparison**. The argument
uses the actual rationally twisted lines and their arithmetic
intersections. It does not replace the signed cusp function by its
positive part or replace the arithmetic limit by an unrelated energy.

The arithmetic variety, its full intersection system, the supplied compact
complex model, the Hodge and cusp data, the tied regularization settings,
and the original smoothness and height hypotheses remain given. Their
construction, the automorphic uniformization and the arithmetic section
remainder are not proved here. In particular this is a completed supplier
inside W10, not completion of W10 or either main theorem.

Fix the original cutoff \(B\). All appearances of \(B\), the reference
metric, the vertical factors and the ample metric below refer to the
same data. The original lower restriction \(B\geq4f+2\) is retained,
although the analytic implication below does not use its numerical value.

## 1. Identifying the supplied compact model

Let \(P\) be the original multihomogeneous arithmetic prime and let
\(Y\) be its complex multiprojective variety, of pure dimension \(n\).
The actual component data include smoothness over \(\mathbf Q\) of
every nonempty rational affine chart. Thus \(Y\) is a smooth projective
complex variety. These are genuine source hypotheses, not consequences
of the name of a model. The chart algebra is the subalgebra generated by
the ratios of the homogeneous coordinates: dehomogenizing the kernel of
the original multicone evaluation gives exactly this algebra. Empty
charts are discarded; smoothness is preserved by extension to
\(\mathbf C\).

The given compact model \(X\) is a compact Hausdorff complex manifold of
dimension \(n\), with a surjection from the homogeneous points of \(Y\).
Its fibres are exactly independent nonzero scalings in the coordinate
blocks, and it has local holomorphic lifts to those points. Sending a
point of \(X\) to the projective class of any such lift therefore gives
a bijection
\[
                         \iota:X\longrightarrow Y.                 \tag{1}
\]
In a projective chart its coordinates are ratios of holomorphic
functions with nonzero denominator, so \(\iota\) is holomorphic.
It is a homeomorphism by compactness. An injective holomorphic map
between complex manifolds of the same dimension has invertible
derivative; applying this locally shows that (1) is biholomorphic
[Lebl, Theorem 1.6.6]. The statement is componentwise and includes
disconnected \(Y\). In dimension zero it is a bijection of finite sets.

A homogeneous positive metric denominator \(h_L\) defines the global
curvature form obtained on local lifts \(s\) as
\(dd^c\log h_L(s)\), with the convention
\(dd^c=(i/\pi)\partial\bar\partial\). Two lifts differ by nonzero
holomorphic coordinate-block factors; their logarithms differ by real
parts of holomorphic functions. Hence the curvature is independent of
the lifts. The original strictly positive denominator \(h_{A'}\)
gives a Kähler form \(\omega\): by (1) the lift of a nonzero tangent
vector is not tangent to the scaling orbit, and the precise source
positivity hypothesis applies to that vector.

## 2. The original sheet integral is the compact-model integral

Choose any of the source's good linear projections of the Segre
embedding to \(\mathbf P^n\). Its centre misses \(Y\). The resulting
map \(q:Y\to\mathbf P^n\) is finite: a fibre is proper and is a closed
subvariety of the affine space obtained by deleting the centre from the
corresponding projective linear fibre, so it is finite. A proper map
with finite fibres is finite. This is the same proper-and-affine-fibre
argument as Stacks Project, Tag 0B1P. Each \(n\)-dimensional component
maps onto \(\mathbf P^n\).

Discard the inverse image of the hyperplane at infinity and the critical
locus. The complex Jacobian is not identically zero on any component:
otherwise the holomorphic constant-rank theorem on a maximal-rank open
set would produce positive-dimensional fibres. These discarded analytic
sets have zero volume. Their images have zero volume too, because a
smooth map is locally Lipschitz in real coordinates of equal dimension.
The same observation permits deletion of any additional chart-null
boundary locus supplied by the regularization. Off the resulting null
set of the base, the fibre consists of finitely many local inverse
holomorphic branches.

Let \(\Omega_i\) be the curvatures of homogeneous metrics, or the
corresponding forms defined by smooth local potentials off the deleted
boundary. Let \(G\) be a measurable function on \(Y\). For each inverse
branch, the actual source lift represents the same projective point.
Changing that lift adds only a pluriharmonic term to each potential.
The source density is exactly the coordinate density of the wedge:
\[
 \Omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\Omega_n
  =n!\left(\frac2\pi\right)^n
       D(H_1,\ldots,H_n)\,dV,                                    \tag{2}
\]
where \(H_i\) are the complex Hessians and \(D\) is the polarized
determinant. The differential normalization was checked separately in
the exact metric-variation package.

Change of variables on the branches, followed by a countable
disjoint refinement, gives first the nonnegative identity
\[
 \int_{\mathbf C^n}\sum_{w\in q^{-1}(y)}
       |\text{the original density at }w|\,dV(y)
       =\int_X |G\,\Omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\Omega_n|.            \tag{3}
\]
Thus, whenever the right side is finite, both signed integrals are
defined in the ordinary absolute-integrability sense and
\[
 \operatorname{ptsIntegralAt}
       (P,q;\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_n;G)
       =\int_X G\,\Omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\Omega_n.              \tag{4}
\]
Every good projection gives the same value, so (4) also holds for the
source's chosen-projection integral. Proving (3) before summing signed
values avoids relying on cancellation between sheets or on the source
convention that a nonintegrable real integral is zero.

All complex components are included. A normalized Segre fibre contains
one point for each projective point over the base; it does not divide
by the number of complex components or by conjugation. When this
integral is subsequently written using the previously verified finite
automorphic sheet cover, its existing covering multiplicities and
stabilizer factors remain exactly those of that cover. No new weights
or factor \(1/2\) are introduced. For \(n=0\), (3)--(4) are simply
finite sums and an empty wedge has value one.

### The stated resolution bridge

The same proof gives the original `InterRes.ResBridgeStatement` for a
**given** analytic logarithmic resolution. Its local holomorphic lifts
are compatible up to scaling; away from the indicated exceptional set
they give a bijection and an immersion onto the original variety.
Their global projective map is proper because the resolution is compact.
Consequently this map is a holomorphic embedding over the complement
of the exceptional set, and the preceding inverse-branch argument
applies there. The definition explicitly makes the exceptional inverse
image chart-null. Its image under the smooth map to the projection
base is also null. No component is contained in that inverse image.

For the bridge's section integrand, the pulled-back logarithmic norm is
locally integrable. The nonzero section is not identically zero on a
component: it is a nonzero divisor on the integral rational variety,
this property survives the flat extension to \(\mathbf C\), and every
resolution component meets the isomorphic open part. Locally
Weierstrass preparation writes its holomorphic coefficient as a unit
times a monic polynomial in one variable. Factoring that polynomial
pointwise, the uniform bound on
\(\int_{|z|<r}|\log|z-a||\,dV(z)\) for bounded \(a\)
proves local integrability by Fubini. The smooth metric and mixed
curvature density do not change it. This proves (3), hence the exact
bridge for section log norms [Lebl, Theorem 6.2.3]. It proves a comparison
on supplied resolution data, not `ResExistsStatement`.

## 3. The actual vertical factors have zero curvature everywhere

The original vertical divisor at an arithmetic prime \(p\) is given
by homogeneous \(f,g\), with the prescribed degree difference.
Its defining unit condition says: at every relevant prime \(W\)
containing \(P\) with \(p\notin W\), there are \(u,v\notin W\)
such that
\[
                              uf-vg\in P.                          \tag{5}
\]
Every complex point lies away from the arithmetic \(p\)-fibre. Thus
the rational section \(f/g\) is represented locally there by the
regular unit \(v/u\). The homogeneous degree and the coordinate
monomial in the original definition of the factor must still be kept.
Extracting the appropriate homogeneous components of (5) gives the
precise source conclusion: near each homogeneous complex point \(z\)
there are homogeneous \(G,H\), with \(H(z)\ne0\), such that
\[
                         V_p(w)=|G(w)/H(w)|.                        \tag{6}
\]
The source positivity gives \(G(z)\ne0\) as well. Equivalently the
source's short local lemma gives a holomorphic function \(T\),
nonzero at \(z\), with \(V_p=|T|\) on the variety near \(z\).

Pull back to any of the supplied holomorphic model lifts and shrink
the neighborhood so that \(T\) remains nonzero. A local holomorphic
logarithm then gives
\[
                     dd^c\log V_p=0.                              \tag{7}
\]
This holds at **every** complex point, including the complex boundary;
there is no divisor-supported curvature current left over. In
particular the product \(V_B\) of the actual retained vertical
factors contributes zero curvature on \(X\).

The proof uses the original unit equations, not merely smoothness or
cancellation in a ratio. A general smooth positive factor need not be
flat, and vanishing curvature off a divisor alone would not exclude
mass on that divisor.

## 4. Fixed positive twists and the same original polynomial

Write \(\theta\) for the curvature of the common reference metric.
The tied regularizations have potentials \(\varphi_t\) and
\(\psi_{t'}\), with limiting potentials \(\varphi\) and \(\psi\).
The already proved boundary packages give
\[
 T=\theta+dd^c\varphi\geq0,\qquad
 \theta+dd^c\psi\geq0,\qquad
                   \psi-\varphi=U/2                               \tag{8}
\]
on the common full-measure interior; \(\psi\) is bounded and
\(\varphi\) has the prescribed logarithmic-logarithmic growth.
They also prove finite energy and full mixed mass, absolute
integrability of \(U\) against every \(T^j\wedge\omega^{n-j}\),
and convergence of both original regularized energies for every fixed
positive correction \(z\omega\).

This use of a positive correction does not assume \(\theta\) itself
is positive. For fixed \(z>0\), a sufficiently small original
regularized potential \(\varphi_{t_0}\) makes
\(\theta+dd^c\varphi_{t_0}+z\omega\) Kähler, by near semipositivity.
Use it as the smooth reference for both energies. The common reference
change cancels in energy differences. Write \(E_z\) for the
unnormalized energy, whose differential is integration against the
Monge--Ampère measure, in this class.

The actual unbounded line \(\mu_t\), at smooth scales, has denominator
\(h_{\rm ref}e^{\varphi_t}V_B\) on the variety. Its curvature is
therefore \(\theta+dd^c\varphi_t\), by (7). The exact metric-locality
theorem identifies each source rational twist with
\(\mu_t^{\otimes q}\otimes(A')^{\otimes p}\). Thus there is one
polynomial, before taking any limits,
\[
 F_t(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{j}z^j
          I\bigl(\mu_t^{[n+1-j]},(A')^{[j]}\bigr),                 \tag{9}
\]
and \(F_t(p/q)\) is exactly the original top intersection divided by
\(q^{n+1}\). Its constant term is exactly the original arithmetic
limit function. The model, \(B\), \(V_B\), \(\mu_t\) and \(A'\)
do not depend on \(p,q\).

For every fixed rational \(z\in(0,1]\), compare the twisted line at
\(t\) with one fixed smooth scale. The exact metric variation, (4)
and the proved energy convergence show that \(F_t(z)\) has a finite
limit. Choose \(n+2\) distinct such rational values. Inverting their
Vandermonde matrix proves coefficientwise convergence of (9), to a
polynomial \(F(z)\). In particular
\[
                           F(0)=\operatorname{arLim}.              \tag{10}
\]
This also proves existence of that limit under the retained analytic
and smoothness data; the original guarded target already assumes its
existence.

For a fixed \(z=p/q\in(0,1]\), let \(G_{t'}(z)\) be the original
bounded corrected top intersection divided by \(q^{n+1}\). Its
denominator is
\((h_{\rm ref}e^{\psi_{t'}}V_B)^q h_{A'}^p\).
The exact source hypothesis supplies smoothness at these positive
\(p\), and the bounded energy convergence supplies its limit
\(G(z)\). No bounded line with \(p=0\), and no bounded polynomial
in \(z\), is assumed. Equations (4), (7) and metric variation give
\[
             G(z)-F(z)=(n+1)\bigl(E_z(\psi)-E_z(\varphi)\bigr).
                                                                    \tag{11}
\]
Indeed the logarithmic ratio contributes \(q\), its \(n\) curvature
factors contribute \(q^n\), and these cancel the original
\(q^{n+1}\). All metrics and finite-component multiplicities are
unchanged.

## 5. The original signed bound

Define the finite polynomials
\[
 d(z)=\int_X(T+z\omega)^n,\qquad
 M(z)=\int_X U\,(T+z\omega)^n.                                    \tag{12}
\]
Their finiteness and their expansions at \(z=0\) follow from the
previously proved full mixed mass and weighted mixed integrability.
For each smooth bounded approximant, (4), (7) and ordinary Stokes
give its original normalized geometric degree as
\(\int_X(\theta+z\omega)^n=d(z)\).

The retained original height inequality consequently gives, after
\(t'\to0\),
\[
                         G(z)\geq(n+1)(a-zK_A)d(z).                \tag{13}
\]
Concavity of finite energy [BBGZ, Proposition 2.1 and Lemma 2.7] gives
\[
 E_z(\psi)-E_z(\varphi)
       \leq\int_X(\psi-\varphi)(T+z\omega)^n
       =\tfrac12 M(z).                                           \tag{14}
\]
Combining (11)--(14) preserves the sign:
\[
                  F(z)\geq(n+1)\bigl((a-zK_A)d(z)-M(z)/2\bigr)
                     \qquad(z\in\mathbf Q\cap(0,1]).             \tag{15}
\]
Let \(z\downarrow0\) through positive rationals. This is a limit of
the already finite polynomials \(F,d,M\), with no interchange of
regularization and a singular integral. By (4) and the actual
potential dictionary, \(d(0)=\deg\mu>0\) and
\(M(0)=\operatorname{ptsIntegral}(U)=\deg\mu\,\operatorname{mean}U\).
Equations (10) and (15) therefore give exactly
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
  \operatorname{arLim}\geq
       (n+1)\left(a-\frac{\operatorname{mean}U}{2}\right)\deg\mu .}
                                                                    \tag{16}
\]
The separate convergence of \(\int U\) against the varying
unbounded regularized measures is unnecessary for this implication.
The argument also covers \(n=0\): energies and intersections are
linear, all model integrals are finite sums, and the same two-point
polynomial interpolation applies.
