# Tied Selmer descent using classical duality

26 November 2026. **Proved, conditional on the auxiliary-prime
construction:** the terminal tied-length estimate and a uniform
bound on the number of descent primes. This note substitutes the
classical finite Greenberg–Wiles formula for the custom module of
relations used in the Sernau–Anthropic proof. It retains the exact
paired-prime constraints and all coefficient lengths. No Lean replay
or independent verification of the prime construction is claimed.

Source: [Sernau–Anthropic, revision 72fead1][source], based on
Alpöge and Claude. See [Count][count] 205–548, [Mirror][mirror]
175–230 and 259–708, [Descent][descent] 67–130, 419–506, 524–715,
and [SelmerData][data] 63–155, 181–233, 537–631. This is a
conventional alternative to those parts, not an assertion that a
theorem with the same name in a library has identical hypotheses.

## 1. Data and the only prime-supply input

Fix a CM field E, complex conjugation c on its chosen algebraic
closure, and G=Gal(Ebar/E). Write h^c=chc^{-1}. Let O be the
integer ring of a finite extension of Q_p with uniformizer pi.
We use ordinary O-lengths here; normalization by the absolute
ramification index can be performed at the end.

Fix M≥1 and N>M, put e=ord_pi(p^M), and fix finitely many
source Selmer data. For one datum of rank n, write rho:G→GL_n(O),
S for its finite allowed set of ramified places (including all
p-places), and W_k=(O/pi^k)^n for 1≤k≤e. There is a fixed
inertial gamma at a place in S away from p, with

    rho(gamma)=zeta I,  rho(gamma^c)=zeta^{-1} I,
    zeta^2-1 in O^×.

In particular gamma has cyclotomic character 1. The matrices
rho(g) span Mat_n(O), as in the source. For each p-place v the
given finitely generated O-module Z_v consists of integral
local cocycles and contains all local coboundaries. The local
condition is that every finite homogeneous linear relation on
Z_v still hold on the restricted cocycle in W_k. Assume

    sum_{v|p} rank_O Z_v ≤ (# {v|p}+r_2(E)) n.

Use the source's local term at an original non-p-place: let
D_{v,k} be the restrictions of all continuous global W_k
cocycles (no local or ramification conditions). Let

    b_v = sup_k max(0, len D_{v,k} - len W_k),

or use the source's larger supremum over all nonzero ideals of O.
For a fixed datum put

    b = sum_{v in S, v not above p} b_v,
    a = sup_{k≥1} len Hom_G(W_k, (W_k^*)^c),

where W_k^* = Hom_ab(W_k, Q/Z)(1) is the finite Cartier dual,
and the superscript c on a representation means precomposition
with g↦g^c. The source hypotheses give finite a and b.
Our proof does not estimate these two quantities for the moving
automorphic data.

A tied prime set J is the source's IsTiedPrimes configuration:
distinct split pairs v,vbar outside S_0 and its conjugate, norms
1 modulo p^N, with specified Frobenius phi_v, tame generator
delta_v, anticyclotomic character and one slope s_{v,i} per
datum. On the conjugate side use (phi_v^c)^{-1} and
(delta_v^c)^{-1}. At the first prime v_0 all slopes are zero.
A nonzero slope for datum i requires rho_i(phi_v) and
rho_i(phi_v^c) to be 1 mod p^M. Keep the source's other
character, norm and level conditions as well. They are needed
for the line-specialization and Stickelberger constructions.

For each datum and k define C_k(J) to be the continuous
W_k-valued cocycles z with z(gamma)=0, the fixed local
relations, unramified off S∪J∪Jbar, and both ties

    z(delta_w) = s_{v,i} z(phi_w)  (w=v,vbar).

The notation on the right uses the conjugate inverse as just
specified. A prospective admissible prime P allows us to form
C_k^P(J) by adding P,Pbar to the ramification set but keeping
only the old ties. Let A_P and B_P on C_k^P(J) be evaluation at
the paired tame generators and paired Frobenius elements,
respectively, with values in W_k^2. Then

    ker A_P = C_k(J).

Indeed the representation is unramified at P,Pbar, and a
cocycle on inertia is an additive homomorphism to a finite
p-group. Wild inertia is killed and the tame pro-p generator
detects all its values. Vanishing at one chosen inertia group
implies vanishing at its conjugates by the cocycle identity.
All these modules are finite by the finite-presentation lemma.

Here is the remaining **prime supply** required by the proof:

* A first singleton configuration exists at every N>M, with
  phi_{v_0} as close to the required g_0 as specified and slopes
  zero.
* For each configuration J, each datum i, each h in H_i below,
  each neighborhood of delta_{v_0}h and each finite set of places
  to avoid, there exists a new admissible P whose Frobenius lies
  in that neighborhood, avoiding the set on both conjugate sides.
  This prime can be appended with arbitrary slope for i and
  zero slope for the other data, preserving every tied-prime
  requirement.

Here H_i is generated by [g,gamma_i] and [g,gamma_i^c] for g∈G.
This is precisely the density property of the source Mirror
175–230; the singleton is Descent 247–290. They depend on the
construction in AuxiliaryPrimes. We leave that construction
as a separate named arithmetic obligation, not a consequence
of the length proof.

## 2. Count via ordinary Selmer duality

Fix a datum and k; abbreviate W=W_k. Let J'=J\{v_0}
and S'=S∪J'∪J'bar. At each v∈S' choose the decomposition
group used in the original local relations (for v∈S), or the
one used in the tie (at an auxiliary prime). Let L_v^Z be the
restrictions of all global continuous W-cocycles satisfying
the fixed local relations and old ties, without the normalization
or unramified condition. Its quotient by local coboundaries is
L_v⊂H^1(E_v,W).

The local coboundaries are indeed in L_v^Z: global
coboundaries satisfy the original local relations by lifting
their defining vector to O^n. At auxiliary inertia they vanish;
at a nonzero slope the Frobenius acts trivially mod pi^k,
and a zero slope requires no such condition. Any local
coboundary is the restriction of the global coboundary on the
same vector. Thus

    len L_v - len H^0(E_v,W) = len L_v^Z - len W.

At v outside S' take the unramified local condition (at complex
places H^1=0). We have the bounds

    len L_v^Z ≤ rank Z_v · k                (v|p),
    len L_v^Z ≤ len W + b_v                 (v∈S, v∤p),
    len L_v^Z ≤ len W                       (v∈J'∪J'bar).

For the p-place bound, regard the set of integral functions as
the dual of the free O-module F on all local coordinate
evaluations. The image of F in Hom_O(Z_v,O) is a submodule
of a finite free module of rank rank Z_v. Hence it is free of
rank ≤rank Z_v. The kernel of this evaluation pairing is the
set of scalar relations on Z_v; they are included among the
matrix relations. Functions annihilating that kernel in O/pi^k
factor through the image, giving the bound. No base-change
equality with the image of Z_v mod pi^k is needed.

The middle bound is the definition of the local term.
For the last bound, if a tied local restriction is zero at
Frobenius, its value at the tame generator vanishes too.
These two values determine a local cocycle: the inertia
restriction is an additive p-primary map, and the unramified
quotient is procyclic. This also works with the inverse
generators on the conjugate side.

Let Sel_L(W) denote the ordinary global Selmer group of
these local conditions, and D=Sel_{L^perp}(W^*) its ordinary
Tate-dual Selmer group. The two global invariant groups
vanish: gamma acts on W and W^* by the scalars zeta and
zeta^{-1} respectively, both with difference from 1 a unit.

The classical finite Greenberg–Wiles formula (Smith,
*Galois cohomology and the Greenberg–Wiles formula*, Thm 1.8,
and local duality Thm 1.3) says

    len Sel_L(W) - len D
      = len H^0(E,W) - len H^0(E,W^*)
        + sum_v (len L_v - len H^0(E_v,W)).

This cardinality formula converts to O-lengths because the
orders of finite O-modules are powers of the same residue
field order; the dual uses O-action by precomposition. At
complex places the sum contributes -r_2(E) len W; outside
S' at finite places both modules are unramified with order
prime to residue characteristic and the unramified condition
has the same length as H^0. The p-place rank bound pays
for the archimedean term. The other local bounds therefore give

    len Sel_L(W) ≤ len D + b.

The map C_k(J)→Sel_L(W) is injective: first-pair ties of
slope zero imply unramifiedness there, and each cohomology
class has at most one cocycle normalized at gamma (a
coboundary zero there is zero). Hence

    len C_k(J) ≤ len D + b.                            (1)

The allowed local conditions may be weaker than imposing the
original global relations, so only this injection, not
equality, is claimed. This replaces the auxiliary relation
module of Count by ordinary finite Tate duality.

## 3. Paired-prime reciprocity and the mirror argument

Let U be the open normal intersection of the kernels of
rho mod p^M and the cyclotomic character mod p^N.
On U both W and W^* are trivial. The group H generated
by the above two families of commutators is normal,
stable under c, and contained in U∩U^c (both scalar
matrices commute with rho(g), and a character kills
commutators). For y a class in D, write y(h) for its
cocycle restricted to U, using a fixed primitive p^N-th
root of unity to identify the Cartier dual there with
Hom_ab(W,Q/Z)[p^N]. These values are independent of the
choice of representative; y|U is additive and G-equivariant,
with action on the dual given by the cyclotomic character
times the inverse action on W.

Restriction of H^1(E,W^*) to U is injective. In fact a
class vanishing on U inflates from G/U. In that quotient
gamma is central and its action on W^* has unit difference
from 1. Normalize a cocycle by subtracting a coboundary
to make its value at gamma zero. Commuting gamma with any
element then makes every value zero (the elementary Sah
argument). Further, a y with y|H=0 vanishes on U: for u∈U,
the commutator [u,gamma] gives

    y(u) = y(gamma u gamma^{-1}) = zeta^{-1} y(u).

Thus y is determined by its values on H.

Suppose a primitive z∈C_k(J) exists (one coordinate of one
value is a unit) and that at every new admissible P,

    B_P z ∈ A_P C_k^P(J).                             (2)

The image z(H) spans W over O. Indeed the cocycle rule,
z(gamma)=0 and scalar action give

    z([g,gamma]) = (1-zeta) z(g).

Its O-span is G-stable because H is normal and acts trivially;
it contains a vector with a unit coordinate, so matrix span
of rho(g) implies it is all of W.

For h∈H and any t∈O choose P with phi_P sufficiently
close to delta_{v_0}h, avoiding S' and the old primes.
The prime supply ensures phi_P and phi_P^c lie in U:
the target and its conjugate lie in U and U is open.
Both z and y vanish at delta_{v_0} and its conjugate
(first-pair ties and unramified dual local conditions).
They are continuous into finite groups, so their values
at phi_P and phi_P^c agree with their values at h and h^c.
By (2) choose z'∈C_k^P(J) whose paired tame values agree
with the paired Frobenius values of z.

Global reciprocity (the sum of invariants of a global
Brauer class is zero), applied to the cup product of t z'
and y, has only two possible nonzero contributions. At
v∈S' it is zero by definition of L_v and L_v^perp,
including for the relaxed z'. Elsewhere both classes
are unramified except at P,Pbar, and outside S' the
annihilator of the unramified condition is unramified
by local Tate duality.

Here is the local sign and scalar calculation at the two
new places. Their residue characteristic is not p, their
norm is 1 mod p^N, and their entire decomposition groups
act trivially on W and W^*. For a normalized tame
generator d acting on a p^N-th root of a uniformizer
by the chosen primitive root, local reciprocity gives,
up to one common cup-order sign, the pairing

    y(phi)(t z'(d)).

To see the formula, split W as an abelian p-group into
cyclic factors of order m|p^N. Via the compatible roots
of unity the corresponding component of z' is a Kummer
class of some u. Its value on d is ord_v(u) mod m
(the unit part of u acquires an m-th root in an
unramified extension). The unramified component of y
evaluated on the local Artin symbol of u is its value on
arithmetic Frobenius times ord_v(u), by the local
reciprocity formula for the Kummer cup product. Summing
over cyclic factors proves the stated formula, with
identical sign at every place. At Pbar the normalized
generator is (d^c)^{-1}: conjugation inverts the chosen
root of unity and conjugates the uniformizer. If the
source's delta_P is d^j in the tame quotient modulo
p^N, its conjugate inverse is likewise the j-th power
of (d^c)^{-1}. Multiplying the reciprocity sum by j
therefore yields the formula with the source's generators.
Use arithmetic Frobenius phi_P^c, not its inverse, in
the second dual evaluation. The tie uses its inverse,
and z((phi_P^c)^{-1})=-z(phi_P^c) because phi_P^c∈U.
Consequently the reciprocity identity is exactly

    y(h)(t z(h)) - y(h^c)(t z(h^c)) = 0.               (3)

For completeness, define the biadditive form on H×H

    F_t(h,h') = y(h')(t z(h)) - y(h^c)(t z(h'^c)).

By (3) its diagonal vanishes; polarization gives
F_t(h,h')+F_t(h',h)=0, in every residue characteristic.
Write h_1=gamma h gamma^{-1}. Equivariance of z|H
and y|U, together with rho(gamma)=zeta I,
rho(gamma^c)=zeta^{-1}I and cyclotomic(gamma)=1, gives

    F_t(h_1,h') = F_{zeta t}(h,h'),
    F_{zeta t}(h',h_1) = F_t(h',h).

Subtract antisymmetry at (h,h') with parameter t from
antisymmetry at (h_1,h') with parameter zeta t.
It follows that F_{(zeta^2-1)t}=0. Since zeta^2-1
is a unit, F_t vanishes identically for every t.

Define eta_y on the spanning values z(h) by

    eta_y(z(h)) = y(h^c).

This extends O-linearly: if sum_j a_j z(h_j)=0,
evaluate sum_j a_j y(h_j^c) on t z(h'^c) and use the
vanishing of F_{a_j t}. The result is zero, and these
test vectors span W. The defining formula and conjugation
equivariance give eta_y∈Hom_G(W,(W^*)^c). Uniqueness
on the spanning set makes y↦eta_y linear; if eta_y=0,
then y|H=0 and hence y=0 by the restriction argument.

This proves len D≤a. Together with (1), it gives the
terminal estimate

    len C_k(J) ≤ a+b                                  (4)

when a primitive z satisfying (2) exists.

## 4. Descent with a finite budget, independent of N

The algebraic tilting lemma is short. Let A,B:C→(O/pi^k)^d
be linear maps from a finite-length module. Form C' as
the sum of all kernels of u A-B as u ranges over O.
Then B(C')⊂A(C'). Choose a free O-module P of rank at
most d mapping onto A(C') (take its inverse image in
O^d), lift P→C', and lift the resulting B-map to an
endomorphism V of P. For u avoiding the roots of the
reduced characteristic polynomial of V, uI-V is
invertible and (uA-B)(C')=A(C'). The entire kernel on C
is contained in C' by construction. Additivity of length
therefore gives

    len ker_C(uA-B)
      = len ker_{C'} A
      ≤ len(ker_C A ∩ B^{-1}(A C)).

At most d residue classes are excluded; zero is one
additional exclusion when a unit u is needed.

Use this lemma at all 1≤k≤e with C=C_k^P(J),
A=A_P, B=B_P and d=2n. If the residue field has more
than 2ne+1 elements, a single unit u works at every
level. Append P with slope u^{-1} for this datum and
zero for all others. At each level its new group is
the kernel of uA-B, with length no larger than the old
one. If some old group fails inclusion (2) at this P
for some z, the intersection is proper at that level,
so the length decreases strictly. All other data have
new slope zero and hence their groups are unchanged.

Start with the singleton supplied in §1. All slopes
are zero, so its groups coincide with the original
normalized, unramified-outside-S groups C_{i,k}(empty).
They are finite independently of N by §1 of the
[finite-presentation note](lsernau-finite-selmer-presentations.md).
Repeat a strict-decrease step whenever one exists.
The sum of lengths over all data and all levels is a
nonnegative integer, and hence the procedure stops
after at most the original sum of lengths. In particular

    |J| ≤ 1 + sum_i sum_{k=1}^e len C_{i,k}(empty).

This number is independent of N, for fixed data and
fixed M,O. It is not asserted to be uniform in those
data. In the terminal configuration every z at every
level satisfies (2) for every admissible new P.

If C_e(J) is nonzero, let pi^k be the largest order
of any of its elements. All its cocycles take values
in W_e[pi^k], identified with W_k by multiplication
by pi^{e-k}. All the equations are homogeneous and
fixed across levels; the identification gives exactly
C_k(J). A cocycle of order pi^k has a unit coordinate
in W_k, so (4) bounds len C_e(J)=len C_k(J).
If C_e(J)=0 the bound holds without a primitive class.

This proves the source's cofinal tied-length bound,
conditional only on the stated prime supply and datum
hypotheses. Release the first pair of ties using the
previous line-witness lemma; divide lengths by the
absolute ramification index, and use the proved
simultaneous torsion specialization. The remaining
arithmetic inputs are the prime construction,
Stickelberger-system witnesses, estimates of a,b
and first-prime determinants for the actual data,
and the projection denominator. This argument is
field-independent within CM fields; it is not by
itself a Hecke GRH theorem.
