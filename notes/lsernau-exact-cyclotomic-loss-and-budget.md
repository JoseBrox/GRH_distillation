# Exact cyclotomic crystalline loss and the remaining arithmetic budget

## Results and scope

There are two new unconditional local results here. First, the actual
crystalline loss of an explicit family of cyclotomic characters is
computed exactly, at every precision and also at \(p=2\). It grows
linearly with the width of the allowed weight interval. Thus the
[bounded-class theorem](lsernau-bounded-class-crystalline-loss.md) cannot
be replaced by a uniformly cheap estimate that remembers only that
interval. Second, for a family whose rational inertia representations
are sums of a fixed finite set of cyclotomic characters, one explicit
integer bounds the total loss over all primes, independently of ranks,
multiplicities, lattices and precision.

These are **not counterexamples to the source's sparse labelled-weight
theorem**. The first family deliberately fills a long weight interval.
The effective upper bound assumes a cyclotomic description of the whole
inertia representation; the actual enlarged automorphic family `Y` is
not known to have that description. Its Hodge--Tate weights alone do
not imply it.

The final sections prove the exact aggregate projection budget that
would suffice in the signed determinant comparison and explain why
qualitative finiteness supplies no diagonal parameter argument. The
effective bound for the actual growing-field family remains open.

## 1. The actual relation lattice of a cyclotomic family

Let \(p\) be a prime, \(G=G_{\mathbb Q_p}\), \(I_p\subset G\)
inertia, and \(\chi:G\to\mathbb Z_p^\times\) the cyclotomic character.
The extension \(\mathbb Q_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/\mathbb Q_p\) is totally
ramified, so \(\chi(I_p)=\chi(G)=\mathbb Z_p^\times\). Put
\[
 (d_p,a_p,\psi)=
 \begin{cases}
   (p-1,1,\chi^{p-1}),&p>2,\\
   (2,3,\chi^2),&p=2.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{1}
\]
Then \(\psi(I_p)=\psi(G)=1+p^{a_p}\mathbb Z_p\). Set
\(q=1+p^{a_p}\), and choose \(\gamma\in I_p\) with
\(\psi(\gamma)=q\). The powers of \(q\) are dense in this image.

For \(N\ge1\), take the crystalline characters

\[
 u_j=\psi^j,\qquad 1\le j\le N.
\]

With \(\mathbb Q_p(1)\) of weight \(-1\), their weights lie in
\([-d_pN,0]\). Use the actual, possibly unsaturated, relation lattice

\[
 A(g)=(\psi(g)^j-1)_{j=1}^N,\qquad
 I_N=\sum_{g\in G}\mathbb Z_p A(g)\subset\mathbb Z_p^N,\qquad
 U=\operatorname{diag}(q,q^2,\ldots,q^N).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]

The action is left multiplication, \(gA(h)=A(gh)-A(g)\).
Let \(P_N(X)=\prod_{j=1}^N(X-q^j)\in\mathbb Z_p[X]\).

**Proposition 1.** There are isomorphisms of integral modules

\[
 I_N\simeq\mathbb Z_p[X]/(P_N),\quad
 f\longmapsto f(U)A(\gamma),\qquad
 (I_N)_G\simeq\mathbb Z_p/(P_N(1)).                        \tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** The columns \(U^iA(\gamma)\), \(0\le i<N\), have determinant

\[
 \prod_{j=1}^N(q^j-1)\prod_{1\le i<j\le N}(q^j-q^i)\ne0.
                                                               \tag{4}
\]

Their span \(J\) is a full-rank lattice and hence is closed in
\(\mathbb Z_p^N\). Since \(P_N\) is monic with unit constant term,
\(U\) and \(U^{-1}\) preserve \(J\). For every integer \(k\),
\(A(\gamma^k)\in J\), using the geometric sum for \(k>0\), and
\(A(g^{-1})=-g^{-1}A(g)\) for negative \(k\). Density of \(q^\mathbb Z\)
and closedness of \(J\) imply \(A(g)\in J\) for every \(g\in G\).
Conversely, \(U^iA(\gamma)=A(\gamma^{i+1})-A(\gamma^i)\in I_N\).
Thus \(I_N=J\).

Division by the monic polynomial \(P_N\) and linear independence in
(4) identify the kernel of \(\mathbb Z_p[X]\to I_N\) exactly with
\((P_N)\). This proves the first assertion integrally, without
saturating \(I_N\).

The subgroup \((U-1)I_N\) is closed: \(U-1\) is invertible rationally,
so its image is a full-rank lattice in \(I_N\). On the finite quotient
\(I_N/(U-1)I_N\), the action factors through \(\psi(G)\), and the dense
subgroup generated by \(q\) acts trivially. The whole group therefore
acts trivially. Hence the subgroup generated by all \((g-1)I_N\) is
exactly \((U-1)I_N\). Under the first isomorphism, imposing \(U=1\)
gives \(\mathbb Z_p[X]/(P_N,X-1)=\mathbb Z_p/(P_N(1))\).
\(\square\)

Both uses of density are in finite free or finite modules with a proved
closed submodule. An abstract nonclosed span is not substituted for
the actual relation lattice.

## 2. Exact loss, including the noncrystalline condition

Take the fixed target \(T=\mathbb Z_p\) with trivial action. Let
\(R_n\) be the cocycles \(c:G\to\mathbb Z_p/p^n\) of the form
\(c=q_0A\), with \(q_0:I_N\to\mathbb Z_p/p^n\) equivariant. Let
\(C_n=\operatorname{red}Z_f^1(\mathbb Q_p,\mathbb Z_p)\).

For this target, \(H_f^1(\mathbb Q_p,\mathbb Q_p)\) consists exactly
of unramified homomorphisms [BK, Example 3.9, p. 359]. Consequently
\(C_n\) consists exactly of the unramified homomorphisms with values
in \(\mathbb Z_p/p^n\): every such homomorphism lifts to an integral
one, and the target has no nonzero coboundaries.

**Theorem 2.** Put

\[
             A_{p,N}=a_pN+v_p(N!).
\]

For every \(n\ge1\),

\[
 R_n\cap C_n=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_p}(R_n/(R_n\cap C_n))
                  =\min(n,A_{p,N}).                       \tag{5}
\]

**Proof.** An equivariant \(q_0\) factors through (3). It is uniquely
specified by \(z=q_0(A(\gamma))\in\mathbb Z_p/p^n\), subject to
\(P_N(1)z=0\). Its cocycle satisfies

\[
 c(\gamma^k)=kz,\qquad
 c(g)=\frac{\log\psi(g)}{\log q}\,z.                      \tag{6}
\]

The first identity follows from the geometric sum and equivariance.
The second follows by continuity and density; the quotient of
logarithms is in \(\mathbb Z_p\). Thus the map from admissible \(z\)
to cocycles is injective. Since \(\gamma\in I_p\), a nonzero \(z\)
gives a ramified cocycle. Equation (6) proves \(R_n\cap C_n=0\),
not merely nonvanishing of a rational extension class.

The elementary lifting-the-exponent identity is

\[
 v_p(q^j-1)=a_p+v_p(j).
\]

It holds for odd \(p\) and \(a_p\ge1\), and for \(p=2\) and
\(a_p\ge2\). Factoring \(P_N(1)\) now gives
\(v_p(P_N(1))=A_{p,N}\). The subgroup of \(\mathbb Z_p/p^n\)
annihilated by this element has length \(\min(n,A_{p,N})\),
which proves (5). \(\square\)

These classes really occur in the torsion-crystalline category used
by the bounded-class theorem. Indeed, the universal extension
\(I_N\oplus\mathbb Z_p\), with cocycle \(A\), embeds in
\(\bigoplus_j\mathbb Z_p(d_pj)\oplus\mathbb Z_p\) by
\((i,z)\mapsto(i+z(1)_j,z)\). Its rational representation is crystalline
in \([-d_pN,0]\). The free module \(I_N\oplus\mathbb Z_p\) is itself
a lattice in that crystalline rational representation, so its reduction
is torsion crystalline. This does not assert that the displayed
embedding remains injective after reduction. Push that reduction out
by \(q_0\), adding the trivial target lattice's reduction as in the
usual pushout; the result is exactly the torsion extension defined
by (6). Subquotient closure proves the assertion.

For a finite coefficient extension \(O/\mathbb Z_p\), with
\(e_O=v_\pi(p)\), the same proof gives the quotient
\(O/(P_N(1))\) and length \(\min(n,e_OA_{p,N})\) at precision
\(\pi^n\). Its limiting **normalized** length is \(A_{p,N}\).
The target is \(O=\mathbb Z_p\otimes O\), still trivial.

### Consequence for bounds using only a weight interval

Let \(c_p(W)\) be the finite length of the universal obstruction module
of the bounded-class theorem for \(K=L=F=\mathbb Q_p\), \(T=\mathbb Z_p\),
and interval \([-W,0]\). Then

\[
 c_p(d_pN)\ge a_pN+v_p(N!).                               \tag{7}
\]

Legendre's identity gives \(v_p(N!)=(N-s_p(N))/(p-1)\), where
\(s_p(N)\) is the base-\(p\) digit sum. In particular

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 c_p((p-1)N)\ge\dfrac{p}{p-1}N-O_p(\log N),&p>2,\\[4pt]
 c_2(2N)\ge4N-s_2(N).&
 \end{array}                                             \tag{8}
\]

Thus even with field and target fixed, the best possible general bound
is at least linear in the interval width. Increasing congruence precision
does not make the loss smaller: (5) increases until it reaches the
positive constant \(A_{p,N}\). As the weight interval grows, the
normalized ramified logarithm in (6) occurs to arbitrarily high precision.
This does not contradict Liu's theorem, which requires one fixed weight
interval for the entire compatible tower.
In particular, at fixed \(p\), no bound \(c_p(W)\le\varepsilon(W)W\)
with \(\varepsilon(W)\to0\) can hold for all weight intervals.

The source's actual classes remember sparse labelled weight sets and
their Frobenius arrangement. They exclude the dense family used here
in the relevant large interval. Equations (7)--(8) are an obstruction
to discarding that information, not an obstruction to the source theorem.

## 3. An effective bound for a sparse cyclotomic inertia spectrum

Here is an explicit case in which a whole enlarged family can be handled
without invariance-matrix Smith computations. Fix a finite set
\(S\subset\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\). At a prime \(p\), allow any finite
family of continuous \(O\)-representations whose rational restrictions
to \(I_p\) are direct sums of the characters \(\chi^s\),
\(s\in S\cup\{0\}\). Allow arbitrary multiplicities, stable lattices
and finite coefficient extensions. The target is the trivial lattice
\(O\). Let \(\kappa_p\) denote the supremum, over precisions and such
families, of the normalized actual crystalline colength.

**Proposition 3.** For odd \(p\),

\[
 \kappa_p\le
       \sum_{s\in S,\ p-1\mid s}(1+v_p(|s|)).             \tag{9}
\]

For \(p=2\),

\[
                  \kappa_2\le1+\sum_{s\in S}(2+v_2(|s|)).\tag{10}
\]

**Proof.** Every relation-respecting cocycle \(c\) vanishes on
\(I_p\cap\ker\chi\), because all the family representations are
trivial there, so \(A(g)=0\). Thus its restriction to inertia factors
through \(\mathbb Z_p^\times\). Its class modulo integral crystalline
cocycles is determined by this restriction, since unramified modular
homomorphisms lift integrally.

For odd \(p\), choose \(\gamma\in I_p\) with
\(\chi(\gamma)=q=\zeta(1+p)\), where \(\zeta\) generates
\(\mu_{p-1}\). The powers of \(q\) are dense in
\(\mathbb Z_p^\times\), so \(c(\gamma)\) determines the restriction.
The polynomial

\[
 Q(X)=(X-1)\prod_{s\in S}(X-q^s)
\]

kills every \(u_y(\gamma)\), even if the integral lattices are not
split. Write \(Q(X)=\sum_j a_jX^j\). Since \(Q(1)=0\),

\[
 \sum_j a_j A(\gamma^j)=0,\qquad
 0=\sum_j a_jc(\gamma^j)=Q'(1)c(\gamma).
\]

Therefore \(\prod_{s\in S}(1-q^s)\) annihilates \(c(\gamma)\).
The valuation of its \(s\)-factor is zero unless \(p-1\mid s\),
and otherwise is \(1+v_p(|s|)\). Evaluation embeds the obstruction
quotient in a rank-one torsion module with this exponent; its
normalized length satisfies (9).

At \(p=2\), use \(\chi(\gamma)=5\). The same polynomial argument
bounds the value on \(1+4\mathbb Z_2\) by the exponent in the sum in
(10). The remaining value on \(-1\) is killed by 2, contributing at
most one unit of normalized length. The two evaluations together
are injective on the inertia restriction. \(\square\)

There is an exact finite-prime certificate for these bounds. Write
\(D=|S|\), \(W=\max(1,\max_{s\in S}|s|)\), and, for \(S\ne\varnothing\),

\[
 Q(S)=2^{1+2D}\prod_{s\in S}\left(
        |s|\prod_{d\mid |s|}(d+1)\right).                 \tag{11}
\]

Take \(Q(\varnothing)=1\). The integer obtained by multiplying the
prime powers in (9)--(10) divides \(Q(S)\): the valuation contributions
divide \(|s|\), and every odd prime with \(p-1\mid s\) occurs among
the factors \(d+1\). If \(S\) is empty, inertia acts trivially on the
entire family, and every relation-respecting cocycle is unramified,
so the loss is zero. Hence

\[
 \sum_p\kappa_p\log p\le\log Q(S)
 \le(1+2D)\log2+D\log W+2D\sqrt W\log(W+1).               \tag{12}
\]

The last inequality uses only the elementary divisor-pairing estimate
\(\tau(n)\le2\sqrt n\), with no prime number theorem. In particular
the estimate is independent of auxiliary-family size and precision.
For a sparse set of weights, it is much smaller than a linear-width
bound even when the largest weight is large.

For scale comparison only, suppose \(x\ge2\), \(L=\log b\ge10^{12}\log x\),
\(D\le x^{100}\), \(W\le2b\), \(m\le2\sqrt x\), \(k\le4x^5\), and
\(f\ge1\). Then (12) is at most \(12x^{100}\sqrt b\,L\), which is
at most

\[
                       \frac{f x b L^3}{mk}.              \tag{13}
\]

Indeed \(mk\le8x^{11/2}\), \(\sqrt b\ge x^{5\cdot10^{11}}\),
and \(96x^{209/2}\le x^{5\cdot10^{11}}L^2\). Thus this particular
effective structural hypothesis has ample numerical room on the source
scales. It has **not** been established for `Y`: being crystalline
with weights in \(S\cup\{0\}\) does not make inertia a direct sum
of those cyclotomic characters. The growing fields also require their
own local restriction and degree accounting.

## 4. The aggregate projection budget is the needed input

Retain the actual factorization and common multipliers of
[one-lift comparison, §§4--5](lsernau-one-lift-comparison.md).
Let \(d>0\) be the common dimension of the good form spaces, \(m\) the
number of lifts, \(k=r+1-m\), \(a=xb\), \(L=\log b\), and

\[
 B_0=\frac{faL^3}{mk},\qquad A=3fmbL^2.
\]

For each lift \(j\), let \(\Lambda_j\ge0\) be the actual all-prime
logarithmic determinant cost of clearing its full projection. Thus the
first comparison factor has height at most \(dA+\Lambda_j\). Using
the individual detected operators, one may take

\[
 \Lambda_j=\sum_p(\log p)\sum_{i=1}^d b_{j,i,p},           \tag{14}
\]

including any class-weight or mixed-pairing factors actually needed.
No maximum over \(i\) is required.

Let \(\mathcal A_j\) be the \(k/2\) main critical pairs in the
three-exchange factorization. For each pair \(\alpha\), suppose the
untwisted squared norm has height \(N_{j,\alpha}-J\), the two sharp
test multipliers have conjugate bounds \(T_{j,\alpha}\), and the
projected pairing has height \(C_{j,\alpha}\ge0\). The sharp
numerator divisibility contributes \(P\), with common direction amount
\(\Delta=J-P\). Retain the proved inverse rank-four squared-ratio
bound 10 and the bound 5 for the other ramified ratios.

The same four-factor identity used in the one-lift chapter gives the
inverse main ratio height

\[
 N_{j,\alpha}+C_{j,\alpha}+2T_{j,\alpha}+10-\Delta.
\]

Assume the already specified norm and sharp-test bounds
\(N_{j,\alpha},T_{j,\alpha}\le B_0\). Define the **one aggregate
projection cost**

\[
 \mathscr A=\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^m\Lambda_j
                  +2\sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_j}
                                      C_{j,\alpha}.       \tag{15}
\]

**Proposition 4.** Under these comparison hypotheses, if the remaining
chain factors have height \(B_{\rm chain}\), the full inverse chain
determinant has height at most

\[
 d\left[B_{\rm chain}+mA+50m^2+mk(3B_0+10)
                        +\mathscr A-mk\Delta\right].     \tag{16}
\]

In particular, the aggregate sufficient condition

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathscr A\le faL^4-mA-50m^2-mk(3B_0+10)
 \quad}                                                   \tag{17}
\]

supplies the source chain bound
\(d(B_{\rm chain}+faL^4-\mathrm{mainAmount})\), with
\(\mathrm{mainAmount}=mk\Delta\). The source has
\(B_{\rm chain}\le b\).

**Proof.** For one lift, sum the first-factor height \(dA+\Lambda_j\)
and \(d\) times the three-exchange height

\[
 50m+2\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_j}
       (N_{j,\alpha}+C_{j,\alpha}+2T_{j,\alpha}+10-\Delta).
\]

There are \(k/2\) main pairs. Bounding \(N,T\) by \(B_0\), summing
over \(j\), and adding \(dB_{\rm chain}\) gives (16), exactly.
The definition of height permits multiplying the common algebraic
multipliers before adding their costs, so this is a bound on the
combined selected-twist family. Substitution of (17) proves the last
assertion. \(\square\)

The source's stronger common bound would give
\(\Lambda_j\le drB_0\) and \(C_{j,\alpha}\le B_0\), hence
\(\mathscr A\le m(r+k)B_0\). Equation (17) instead allows the actual
weighted sum. It does not require a separate source-sized estimate for
every prime, every eigenform or every critical line.

All Selmer, character-detection and local crystalline costs that enter
the projection operators must still be included in (14)--(15).
Qualitative local finiteness makes these quantities finite for fixed
data; it supplies no estimate in (17). No cancellation of a positive
projection denominator is assumed without an identity in the combined
determinant.

## 5. Why changing parameter order does not close the gap

The bounded-class theorem has the quantifiers

\[
 \text{for every fixed }(K,L,T,[a,b]),\quad
 \text{there is a finite }c\text{ valid for all auxiliary families
 and precisions}.
\]

The analytic endpoint needs data for all sufficiently large \(x\),
with \(\log b\ll x^\varepsilon\), and an estimate such as (17).
In addition, admissibility imposes \((\log b)^{100}\le x\).
For fixed \(x\), this puts an upper bound on \(b\). Enlarging \(b\)
changes the permitted Hodge--Tate weights and generally the target;
it does not keep the constant in the fixed-data theorem fixed.

This is a strict logical obstruction to the proposed diagonal inference:
pointwise finiteness of numbers \(c(x,b)\) does not imply that any
admissible \(b\) satisfies \(c(x,b)\le F(x,b)\). For example,
\(c(x,b)=1+\max(0,F(x,b))\) is finite at every fixed datum and violates
every such inequality. This countermodel concerns the implication from
qualitative finiteness, not the values of the actual arithmetic constants.
Equations (7)--(8) give a separate, genuine arithmetic reason why some
dependence on the weight pattern must remain.

The available free auxiliary congruence precision is now harmless for
finiteness, by the bounded-class theorem. It does not multiply the
negative direction term \(mk\Delta\), and it does not divide the
costs in (14)--(15). Equation (5) exhibits this distinction exactly.
An additional fixed-data amplification identity, an effective estimate
for (15), or a proved cancellation would be necessary for a parameter
reordering to replace the quantitative argument. None is asserted here.
