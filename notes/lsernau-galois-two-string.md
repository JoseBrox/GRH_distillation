# Galois attachment by two-string cancellation

**Scope.** This gives an ordinary proof of the all-rank cancellation
and compatibility mechanism in the readable source. In particular,
one need not construct even-rank Galois representations independently
if odd-rank attachment, the two actual theta lifts, and their
spherical local clauses have been proved. The [compact-real lift chapter](lsernau-compact-real-lifts.md) now
supplies the two nonzero lifts using the normalized convergent Rallis
formula and split polynomial transfer. Odd-rank attachment and the
local-global theorem for labelled nonspherical places remain open.

## 1. Two characters with disjoint strings, on the actual CM torus

Let \(E/F\) be the CM extension of the source, and \(T=U(1)\).
Let \(c\) be the CM involution, \(u=|\mu(E)|\) the number of roots
of unity, and \(e_v\) the integral last-entry weight of the definite
automorphic form. Choose a complex place \(v_0\). Take the two
infinite weights
\[
 a_{i,v}=-u\big(\max(0,-e_v)+i\,1_{v=v_0}\big),
 \qquad i=0,1.
\]
They satisfy \(a_{i,v}\le e_v\) and their difference is \(u\)
at \(v_0\) and zero elsewhere. They define characters with trivial
finite integral part on \(T(\mathbb A_\infty)T(\widehat{\mathcal O}_E)\).
These are trivial on its intersection with \(T(E)\): an algebraic
unit all of whose complex absolute values equal 1 is a root of
unity, and the weights are multiples of \(u\).
Consequently they extend to characters \(\mu_i\) of \(T(\mathbb A)\)
trivial on \(T(E)\). For completeness, extend the character from
the product subgroup with \(T(E)\) to the whole abelian group
using divisibility of the circle group. It remains continuous,
since that product subgroup is open and the restriction to it
is continuous. This proves existence without a choice of
independent class-character bases.

These characters have a stronger separation than just different
infinity types. At any split finite place \(w\ne\bar w\), let
\(x=\varpi_w/\bar\varpi_w\) be the norm-one finite idele with
infinite part 1. Then
\[
                       \mu_0(x)\ne\mu_1(x).                \tag{1}
\]
Indeed some positive power of the ideal divisor of \(x\) is
principal, by finiteness of the ideal class group. If
\(\operatorname{div}(x)^h=(\alpha)\), the divisor of
\(\alpha/\bar\alpha\) is \(\operatorname{div}(x)^{2h}\).
Thus \(x^{2h}\gamma\) is finite-integral for the norm-one
rational element \(\gamma=\bar\alpha/\alpha\).
Evaluating \(\mu_0/\mu_1\) on this element gives
\[
 (\mu_0/\mu_1)(x)^{2h}=\tau_0(\gamma)^u ,
\]
where \(\tau_0\) is the CM embedding at \(v_0\).
If the left side is 1, injectivity of \(\tau_0\) implies
\(\gamma^u=1\). But \(\gamma\) has nonzero valuation at
\(w\), a contradiction. This is also valid for rank one.

In the source's convention the algebraic character associated
to \(\mu_i\) and integers \(k,j\) is
\[
 \theta_{i;k,j}(y)=\mu_i(y/\bar y)\upsilon(y)^{-k}
                                      |y|^{j-k/2},
\]
where \(\upsilon\) is the fixed splitting character. For the
same \(k\), the values for \(i=0\) and \(i=1\) at a split
uniformizer are distinct for *every* \(j,j'\).
In fact equality implies equality of phases, after dividing by
complex absolute values, and cancels the common splitting
character. It contradicts (1). The required algebraicity and
avatar convention are those of the original CM Weil data.
Thus the strings used in the source have no common root at
any split finite place, not merely no common global character.

## 2. Two odd-rank lifts produce a common representation

Let \(P_w\) be the monic Hecke polynomial of a fixed definite
eigensystem of positive rank \(n\). Set \(s=n+1\), so \(n+s=2n+1\)
is odd and in the convergent range of the source theta lift.
Use the two characters constructed in Section 1.

The compact-real lift chapter supplies two nonzero
definite theta lifts at rank \(2n+1\) with the exact spherical
Hecke polynomials of the source. After normalization by the
avatar of \(\theta_{i;s,s}\), these polynomials are
\[
 P_w(X)\prod_{a=0}^{s-1}(X-\theta_{i;n+s,s-a}(\varpi_w)).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
Assume the odd-rank attachment theorem and its stated local
clauses for the two lifts. Write \(R_i\) for the normalized
attached representations, and \(\chi_{ia}\) for the avatars
of the added characters in (2). These assumptions match
the two theta-lift inputs of [1], not an application of
a general cuspidal \(GL_n\) theorem to a noncuspidal transfer.

The trace form in the attachment theorem is nondegenerate
on the finite-dimensional algebra generated by the image.
Over \(\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell\) this implies semisimplicity:
any element of the Jacobson radical has zero trace pairing
with every element of the algebra, since their product lies
in the nilpotent radical. The radical vanishes, and modules
over this semisimple algebra are completely reducible.

Frobenius density gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}R_0-\sum_a\chi_{0a}
 =\operatorname{tr}R_1-\sum_a\chi_{1a}.                       \tag{3}
\]
If attachment is expressed by the source's approximation
filter rather than literal equalities of polynomials, use
the limit of the coefficient of degree \(n-1\) of \(P_w\):
monic-product coefficients show that both sides of (3)
are this same limit at every \(g\). The source filter must
have its stated density in every Frobenius neighborhood.
For exact cofinite Frobenius identities the needed density
also has a short proof: in a finite Galois extension over
\(\mathbf Q\) containing \(E\), choose the Frobenius of a
rational prime conjugate to the prescribed restriction
of \(g\in G_E\), by Chebotarev. Choose the prime above it
realizing that restriction. Since the chosen element fixes
\(E\), the prime of \(E\) has residue degree one. Exclude
the finitely many ramified primes and all specified bad
primes. Such a place is split over the CM involution.
Using larger finite quotients gives density in \(G_E\).

The two sets of characters in (3) are disjoint by (1)
and the avatar compatibility at split Frobenius places.
In characteristic zero the characters of pairwise
nonisomorphic irreducible representations are linearly
independent: apply the trace to the primitive central
idempotents of the finite-dimensional semisimple algebra
generated on the sum of the representations.
Hence (3) holds in the group of semisimple representation
classes. Write \([R_i]=C+[\bigoplus_a\chi_{ia}]\).
For each irreducible class, at least one of the two strings
has multiplicity zero in that class. Its multiplicity
in \(C\) is therefore nonnegative. Consequently \(C\)
is the class of an actual semisimple representation \(R\)
of rank \(n\), with
\[
 R_i\simeq R\oplus\bigoplus_a\chi_{ia}.
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
The representation is continuous, as a direct summand of a
continuous finite-dimensional representation. Trace
uniqueness gives uniqueness of the attached semisimple
representation. This is the substantive two-string
cancellation; deleting factors from a single characteristic
polynomial at every \(g\) would not establish (4).

The characteristic polynomials of the direct summands
factor exactly at each element. At a good Frobenius, cancel
the added polynomial from (2) to obtain \(P_w\).
For the approximation-filter version, cancellation does
not degrade the error. Every continuous character of a
compact group has unit absolute value. The monic added
polynomial therefore has Gauss norm 1; Gauss norm is
multiplicative, by dividing polynomials by coefficients
of maximum norm and reducing in the residue field.
Thus a bound for the coefficientwise error after
multiplication by the added polynomial is the same
bound before multiplication. This proves the precise
attachment criterion also in that version.

## 3. Spherical local clauses and the normalized lift trace

A direct summand of an unramified representation is
unramified. A direct summand of a crystalline or potentially
crystalline representation has the corresponding property;
the period functor and its labelled Hodge filtration are
additive for direct sums. Subtracting the character jumps
in (4) gives exactly the original labelled jumps.
At a split spherical place the normalized larger
representation is killed by the polynomial \(P_w S_i\)
for each of the two strings \(S_i\).
This annihilation restricts to the original summand.
The two \(S_i\) are coprime, by the separation at the
*same* split place from Section 1. Choose polynomials
\(u_0,u_1\) with \(u_0S_0+u_1S_1=1\).
Then \(P_w\) kills the original operator. At
\(\ell\) this argument is applied to the linear Weil-pair
operator on the crystalline module, not to ordinary
inertia matrices. This distinction is essential.

Thus, using the nonzero theta lifts and conditional on
odd-rank attachment with spherical clauses,
the even-rank attachment and spherical local
compatibility need no independent automorphic
existence argument. This does not remove the source's
nonspherical base-change and Iwahori compatibility
theorems.

For the actual descent pair in the common chain the
normalized lift polynomial identity is, in reciprocal
notation, the product of the main row polynomial
twisted by \(\psi=\mathrm{invAvatar}(\xi)\) and the
\(t'\) inverse-cyclotomic factors. Once the representations
are attached and that identity has been proved at good
split places, the same trace-density argument gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{lift},\xi}\simeq
   (\psi\otimes R_{\rm row})\ \oplus\
                  \bigoplus_{a=0}^{t'-1}\chi_\ell^{-a}
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
for the normalized semisimple representations.
In the source the row representation comes from the
same untwisted eigensystem; the twist identity is a
comparison on those eigensystems, not just a matching
of dimensions or Hodge weights. If local identities
are initially stated only for trace, apply linear
independence of characters as above. If it is necessary
to recover characteristic polynomials from traces,
Newton identities in characteristic zero applied to
the traces of \(g^j\) give them, including at bad places.

The first \(r\) labelled jumps of the normalized
descents and rank-one critical lift are those recorded
in [the main-summand chapter](lsernau-main-summand-data.md).
In the source normalization the string in (5) has jumps
\(0,1,\ldots,t'-1\) (jumps use the source sign convention).
Direct-sum additivity verifies the cancellation of
this string in the labelled multiset at each embedding.
No independence of reductions of the different
\(\ell\)-adic characters modulo \(\ell\) is needed for
this characteristic-zero argument.

## 4. Nonspherical places: precisely what passes to the row

Away from the coefficient prime, finite-type local
compatibility means inertia has finite image.
If the local-global theorem supplies an annihilating
polynomial for a tame inertia element with root names
given by the residue-field labels, restriction to a
direct summand preserves that annihilation. In
characteristic zero a finite-order matrix is
semisimple, so one may replace repeated roots by
simple roots, provided the finite-inertia hypothesis
has actually been established. In particular at a
twist place where the two types are the row character
\(\psi(g)\) and the trivial string, the polynomial
is \((X-\psi(g))(X-1)\). If \(\psi(g)=1\) it is still
a valid annihilator.

The ordinary descent of the label after a local
unramified extension has no further inertia cost:
the inertia subgroup is unchanged and the tame
character is pulled back by the compatible local
norm. To use this observation, however, one must
first produce the **automorphic** base change,
verify the label's transformation, and obtain
local-global compatibility for its split characters.
These are the precise outstanding inputs of the
source's LabelCompatibility and IwahoriCompatibility
arguments. At a place over \(\ell\) the corresponding
polynomial assertion concerns a potentially crystalline
Weil operator, not the characteristic-zero
inertia action. The proof of finite-type and jumps
there cannot be replaced by the finite-order argument.

Once the actual nonspherical compatibility has been
established on the common eigensystems, apply the
integral primitive-label matrix-span and scalar-inertia
construction in the main-summand chapter. Together
with (5) this supplies the correct row for the
simultaneous small-twist theorem.
