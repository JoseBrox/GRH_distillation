# Kummer relations by one finite Selmer comparison

We prove the strict relation-supply dimension required by
[triangle detection](lsernau-triangle-detection.md), including
the dyadic case. This replaces the source's large split family,
three-place transport of cocycles, and ensuing relation count
by classical finite-coefficient Poitou--Tate duality and
the Greenberg--Wiles formula. The argument supplies actual
relations satisfying the reciprocity condition for every
subsequent family, not just a space of abstract characters.

## 1. Exact local conditions

Let \(E\) be a CM field containing \(\mu_{5040}\), \(F=E^+\),
\(r=[F:\mathbf Q]\ge4\), \(m=\ell^\nu\) with \(\nu\ge1\).
Put \(G=G_F\), \(U=G_{E(\mu_m)}\). Let \(\epsilon\) be the
quadratic character of \(E/F\), and \(\chi\) the \(\ell\)-adic
cyclotomic character. The source signed character is
\(\kappa=\epsilon\chi\); it agrees with \(\chi\) on \(G_E\)
and takes the value 1 at complex conjugations.
Its image is procyclic, including at 2: because \(E\)
contains \(\mu_4\), \(\epsilon\chi\) takes values in
\(1+4\mathbf Z_2\) when \(\ell=2\).
Write \(M=(\mathbf Z/m)(\kappa^{-e})\),
\(D=M^\vee(1)=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mu_m)\), and
\(M_0=M[\ell]\), \(D_0=M_0^\vee(1)\).

Let \(S\) consist of the primes over \(\ell\) and the primes
of \(F\) below the given \(k\) earlier places of exponent \(e\).
As in the source, the latter have rational prime norms, are
distinct below, and are \(\nu\)-split. They split completely
in \(E(\mu_m)/F\): \(E/F\) is unramified at finite places
(the roots of unity of orders 3 and 4 preclude conjugation
in inertia), the residue degree of a prime of rational
prime norm over \(F\) is one, and its Frobenius fixes
\(\mu_m\). In particular the local actions of both \(M\)
and \(D\) are trivial there.

Use the following local conditions on cocycles:
* at \(v\mid\ell\) they vanish on \(G_{F_v}\cap\ker\kappa\);
* at the other places of \(S\) they vanish identically;
* at every real place they vanish on complex conjugation;
* at every finite place outside \(S\) they are unramified.

These are exactly kummerSetting and RelationSetting.cocycles
of the source. The representation is unramified outside
the primes over \(\ell\), since the quadratic extension
is unramified at finite places. All the conditions contain
the local coboundaries: at the first type the character
on the indicated kernel is trivial; at the second type
the entire action is trivial; at real places \(\kappa(c)=1\);
at unramified places the action of inertia is trivial.
Thus these conditions define subgroups of the local
\(H^1\)'s, and a class satisfying them has every cocycle
representative satisfying the original conditions. Use
the same conditions, separately defined, on \(M_0\).
Denote the resulting Selmer structures by \(\mathcal L\)
and \(\mathcal L_0\), respectively. At a finite family
\(P\) of additional primes split in \(U\) and outside \(S\),
replace the unramified condition by the full local group;
write \(\mathcal L(P)\) and \(\mathcal L_0(P)\).
The notation "split in \(U\)" means split completely in
the finite extension fixed by \(U\). The residual
conditions are not assumed to be images of the conditions
at level \(m\); for this proof they are precisely the
conditions on cocycles with values in \(M[\ell]\).

## 2. A residual upper bound with a finite split family

Consider the finite residual dual Selmer group
\(H^1_{\mathcal L_0^\perp}(F,D_0)\). Its restriction to \(U\)
takes values in \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm cont}(U,D_0)\)
because \(U\) acts trivially on \(D_0\); the restriction
does not depend on a coboundary. There is a finite family
\(P\) of primes outside \(S\), split in \(U\), such that
every class in this group whose restrictions at \(P\)
vanish is zero on \(U\). Indeed, for each nonzero restriction
choose an element \(u\in U\) on which it is nonzero.
The finite number of classes gives a finite common
quotient of \(G\) carrying these cocycles, the module
action, and \(G/U\). Chebotarev applied to the class of
\(u\) in that quotient gives a prime split in \(U\)
at which the unramified class is nonzero, choosing the
place above it appropriately. Conjugation preserves
nonvanishing since the action of \(u\) on the module
is trivial. The primes may be chosen distinct and
avoiding any fixed finite set. No uniform size or number
bound on \(P\) is required here: this is an internal
family used only for the dimension argument.

Let \(d=\dim_{\mathbf F_\ell}H^1(G/U,D_0)\).
Inflation-restriction and the choice of \(P\) imply
\[
 \dim H^1_{\mathcal L_0(P)^\perp}(F,D_0)\le d .
 \tag{1}
\]
The quotient \(G/U\) embeds in the product of the group
of order two and the finite image of \(\kappa\bmod m\),
which is cyclic. It is abelian and generated by at most
two elements. Thus \(d\le2\). More precisely, if the
residual character on \(D_0\) is nontrivial, \(d=0\):
commuting a cocycle with a central group element acting
by a scalar \(\ne1\) expresses that cocycle as a
coboundary. If the character is trivial, \(d\le1\)
at odd \(\ell\), and \(d\le2\) at 2. In all cases
\[
 d-\dim H^0(F,D_0)\le1 .
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular this bound involves no class-number
estimate for \(E(\mu_m)\).

Apply the finite Greenberg--Wiles formula to \(M_0\)
and \(\mathcal L_0(P)\). At each real place the term is
\(-1\), since \(M_0\) has dimension one, the action is
trivial, and the local condition is zero. This is also
the term at \(\ell=2\): the real \(H^1\) condition remains
zero, and the formula uses the ordinary \(H^0\) dimension.
At \(v\mid\ell\) an allowed cocycle descends to the
procyclic image of \(\kappa\) on \(G_{F_v}\); its value
at one topological generator determines it. The space
of allowed cocycles therefore has dimension at most
one. After quotienting by the local coboundaries of
dimension \(1-\dim H^0(F_v,M_0)\), the difference
\(\dim L_v-\dim H^0(F_v,M_0)\) is at most zero.
At the \(k\) previous primes this difference is \(-1\)
because the local action is trivial and the condition
is zero. At every \(v\in P\), local \(H^1\) has
dimension two: the prime is away from \(\ell\), its
norm is 1 modulo \(m\), and both unramified and tame
directions are one-dimensional. The difference is \(+1\).
All other terms vanish (unramified condition at an
unramified module away from \(\ell\)).

Write \(Z_P\) for the group of \(M\)-valued global
cocycles with the conditions \(\mathcal L(P)\).
Its \(\ell\)-torsion is exactly the group of allowed
\(M_0\)-valued cocycles. To count them, add the global
coboundary dimension \(1-\dim H^0(F,M_0)\) to the
residual Selmer dimension. Thus the formula, (1), (2)
give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \dim_{\mathbf F_\ell}Z_P[\ell]
 &\le |P|-k-r+1+d-\dim H^0(F,D_0)\\
 &\le |P|-k-r+2 .
 \end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
In particular this is at most \(|P|-k-2\) for \(r\ge4\).
Finiteness of \(Z_P\) and the dual groups follows from
finiteness of finite-module cohomology unramified
outside a fixed finite set, including its coboundaries.

## 3. Interpolation of genuine relations at level \(m\)

Fix the same \(P\) and choose at each of its primes the
source tame generator \(\tau_v\), using the one chosen
primitive \(m\)-th root of unity to identify \(\mu_m\)
with \(\mathbf Z/m\). Put
\[
 B=\{(z(\tau_v))_{v\in P}:z\in Z_P\}\subset M^P,
 \qquad X=(M[\ell])^P.
\]
The map on cocycles to \(B\) factors through classes,
since \(M\) is trivial on inertia there. Moreover it
is exactly the localization map of \(H^1_{\mathcal L(P)}\)
to the direct sum of local full groups modulo their
unramified subgroups: at a split prime of norm 1
modulo \(m\) that quotient is identified with \(M\)
by evaluation on \(\tau_v\). The unramified subgroup
of the dual local \(H^1\) is its own corresponding
annihilator of the primal unramified subgroup.
Its pairing with this quotient is perfect and is
evaluation on the arithmetic Frobenius followed by
evaluation on \(\tau_v\) (a common sign can be fixed
by the order of the cup product). This local assertion
follows from the tame presentation and the normalized
invariant map of local Tate duality. In particular
the same sign convention applies at all split primes
with the chosen root-of-unity convention.

The nested-local-condition form of Poitou--Tate
duality says that the two localization images of the
enlarged primal Selmer group and of the original dual
Selmer group are exact orthogonal complements in
these paired direct sums. Therefore **every** character
of \(M^P/B\) is the evaluation at the Frobenii of a
class in \(H^1_{\mathcal L^\perp}(F,D)\). Here it is
important to apply duality at the full coefficient
level \(m\); a residual dual class need not lift.

For clarity, this form follows already from the
middle exactness of the nine-term Poitou--Tate
sequence. In the finite product of local \(H^1\)'s
for a set containing \(S,P\) and infinity, the
annihilator of the global image is the global dual
image. Restrict the primal image to the product of
the enlarged conditions, then pass to the quotient
by the original conditions. Taking annihilators
gives exactly the claimed dual image: a functional
annihilating the intersection extends to one on the
whole local product annihilating the global image,
and its restriction to the enlarged conditions
vanishes on the original conditions. At a real
place use Tate cohomology in the nine-term sequence
(\(\widehat H^1=H^1\)); the real local condition is
unchanged in the comparison, even at 2.

Choose any cocycle representative \(b\) of a class
so obtained. Its restriction to \(U\) is an
additive continuous homomorphism into \(D\).
Write it as a character of \(M\) with values in
\(\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z\), using the chosen root
of unity, and extend the function by zero outside
\(U\). It is conjugation-equivariant with the
Cartier-dual action \(\chi_m\kappa^e\):
the extra terms in the cocycle conjugation formula
cancel on \(U\), which acts trivially. It is
unramified outside \(S\). For any finite family
of distinct split primes outside \(S\) and any
primal cocycle satisfying the stated conditions
and unramified away from that family and \(S\),
global reciprocity of the cup product pairs it
with \(b\) to zero. At \(S\) and infinity this
follows from orthogonality of the local conditions;
away from the family and \(S\) both classes are
unramified. The remaining contributions are exactly
the Frobenius/tame evaluations above. This proves
the actual relation identity at *all* such families.
Thus the restricted function belongs to the source
RelationSetting.relations with \(T=S\), not merely
a module of unramified homomorphisms.

## 4. Strict dimension and its application to the triangle

The inclusion \(X/(X\cap B)\hookrightarrow M^P/B\)
allows every character of the first group to
extend to the second (finite abelian group duality,
or injectivity of \(\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z\)).
By section 3 these extensions come from genuine
relations off \(S\). Reduction of a character
modulo \(\ell\) records precisely its restriction
to \(M[\ell]\): for the cyclic group \(M\) the
annihilator of \(M[\ell]\) in its character group
is exactly \(\ell\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z)\).
Thus the evaluations of reduced relations at the
Frobenii span the character group of \(X/(X\cap B)\)
on this subspace. It follows that the space of
reductions of relations has dimension at least
\[
 \dim_{\mathbf F_\ell}X/(X\cap B)
 \ge |P|-\dim_{\mathbf F_\ell}Z_P[\ell]
 \ge k+r-2 .
 \tag{4}
\]
For the middle inequality use \(X\cap B=B[\ell]\)
and the elementary inequality
\(\dim B[\ell]=\dim B/\ell B\le
\dim Z_P/\ell Z_P=\dim Z_P[\ell]\) for a finite
abelian \(\ell\)-group quotient. No splitting
of the quotient \(Z_P\to B\) is needed.

On \(G_E\) identify the Cartier dual with
\(\mathbf Z/m\) using the fixed root of unity.
The extended-by-zero homomorphisms are precisely
KummerMaps.IsKummerMap: on \(G_E\cap U\) they give
the same relation evaluations, on \(G_E\setminus U\)
they vanish. Their reductions lose no information
because a relation is already zero off \(U\).
Conjugation by the CM involution acts on these
homomorphisms by \(-1\), since \(\kappa(c)=1\)
and \(\chi_m(c)=-1\). This includes \(m=2\),
where negation is the identity. Equivariance under
\(G_E\) is \(\chi^{e+1}\), as required.

The dimension in (4) exceeds \(k\) for \(r\ge4\).
It supplies the source
exists_card_lt_finrank_kummerReductions, with a
slightly stronger bound, for every \(\nu\) and
all the preceding places of the given exponent.
The finite set \(P\) and the full-level dual
classes are allowed to vary with \(\nu\).
We do not charge their number to the final
triangle: the triangle only uses the strict
inequality to choose one new independent
Kummer map at each step. In fact the resulting
relation can be taken unramified outside the
original \(S\), so \(T=S\) is sufficient.
Combine with the proved triangle detection and
cyclotomic separation estimates. The permitted
Frobenius-set condition, and the remaining
arithmetic selector and length/defect estimates,
still have to be verified at the actual attached
representations.

## References and exact scope

The classical inputs are finite local Tate duality,
the global nine-term Poitou--Tate theorem with
Tate cohomology at real places, the Greenberg--Wiles
formula, finite-module cohomology finiteness, and
Chebotarev. For the duality comparison see
J. S. Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, I.4.10
and Mazur--Rubin, *Kolyvagin Systems*, Theorem 2.3.4
(the finite comparison follows directly from the
nine-term sequence, as in section 3).
For the dimension formula at the prime 2 see
Morgan--Paterson, *On 2-Selmer groups of twists
after quadratic extension*, Theorem 3.4,
[doi:10.1112/jlms.12533](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12533).
The latter's all-place formula retains the ordinary
invariants at real places and applies to finite
residual modules. We do not use the main statistical
results of that paper. These classical inputs are
independent of RH and GRH.
