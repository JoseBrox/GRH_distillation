# Marked-prime charges and the integral chain

This chapter proves the collision comparison, marked-prime charge
identities and the finite algebra of Hecke propagation on the actual
diagram of the source. The local gain budget is unchanged or improved.
It uses the charged-jet theorem and standard Weil normalization already
proved in this repository. The transport of these estimates to the
common kernels, including their exact unramified Hecke-polynomial identity,
is an explicit input below. The exceptional-prime elementary-divisor
estimate and the local double-lift relation at the auxiliary prime are
not proved here.

All source references below refer to
[lsernau/grh-lean@72fead1](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/tree/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src).
Write \(q=q_1\), \(q_0\mid q\), \(\Phi=(\mathbb Z/q)^\times\),
\(f=|\Phi|\), \(m=2^u\), \(s=22u\), and use the source ranks
\(n_j=k+j(t+1)\). Its two types of distinguished embeddings are the
a-places (class 1 modulo \(q_0\)) and the b-places (a subset of class
\(-1\)). Its weight \(w_\tau\) is \(b=a/x\) on the b-places and
\(a/x_1\) elsewhere. Do not confuse this weight with the extra
height-one columns, of number \(a\) on the a-places.

## 1. Collision counting and full marks

If \(p\) is non-split in \(K\), it is prime to \(q\). The partner
is multiplication by \(-p^e\) on \(\Phi\), where the positive exponents
\(e\) are odd at an inert prime, and all positive integers at \(d\).
For a collision both embeddings are a-places. At \(p=d\) there
is no collision, since \(d\equiv q_0/2-1\bmod q_0\) and
\(d^2\equiv1\bmod q_0\), but neither \(-1\) nor \(-d\) is 1
modulo \(q_0\). At an inert prime set
\[
 o_0=\operatorname{ord}_{q_0}(-p),\qquad
 o=\operatorname{ord}_{q}(-p),\qquad r_p=\lceil\log_p m\rceil .
\]
A collision is possible precisely when \(o_0\mid e\), for an odd \(e\).
Thus if \(o_0\) is even, there are no collisions; if \(o_0\) is
odd, the exact *unmarked* collision sum is
\[
 C_p(\varnothing)=|\Phi_a|a\,
       \frac{p^{-o_0}}{1-p^{-2o_0}}.                         \tag{1}
\]
Deleting any long rows only decreases this number. This improves the
geometric-tail estimate \(2fa/p^{o_0}\) in JetExponent.lean.

The exceptional condition is that \(p\) is inert, \(p\not\equiv\pm1
\bmod m\), and \(o_0\) is odd with \(o_0\le r_p\).
In the nonexceptional case, (1) proves
\(m C_p(\varnothing)\le L_p\), where the source loss \(L_p\) is
\(fa(\log b)^2/p\) times \(m\) if \(p\equiv\pm1\bmod m\).
Indeed in the latter case \(C_p\le 2fa/p\); otherwise the
first possible exponent is at least \(r_p+1\), so
\(m C_p\le2fa/p\). Here \((\log b)^2\ge2\).

At an exceptional prime \(u\ge2\) (there are no exceptions for
\(m\le2\)); both \(v_2(p-1)\) and \(v_2(p+1)\) are \(<u\).
The conductor is divisible by \(2^{22u}\).
Since \((-p)^o\equiv1\) modulo this power, the 2-adic lifting
formula, for even \(o\), gives
\[
 v_2(p^o-1)=v_2(p-1)+v_2(p+1)+v_2(o)-1.
\]
The odd-order case is impossible: reduction modulo \(2^u\) would
force \(-p=1\) there, since its group of units is a 2-group.
Consequently \(v_2(o)\ge22u-2(u-1)+1\ge u\), so \(m\mid o\).
This also proves that \(o\) is even.

In the product description of \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\),
the Frobenius at the unramified prime \(p\) is
\((c_K,p)\), where \(c_K\) denotes the nontrivial quadratic
automorphism. Its order is \(o\): when \(o\) is even,
\(\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_q(p))=o\), because the
even powers of \(p\) and \(-p\) coincide, and the two least positive
even exponents giving 1 agree. The full complex conjugation is
\((c_K,-1)\), not in this decomposition group. Indeed its
membership would require \((-p)^i=1\) for an odd \(i\), impossible.
Hence every prime over \(p\) is split in \(E/E^+\) and has norm
\(p^o=z_p^2\), \(z_p=p^{o/2}\), \(o\le f\).

The induced primes \(w(\tau)\) use a fixed coefficient embedding
and the embedding \(\tau\) over \(K\). Two such embeddings induce
the same prime precisely when they differ by an even power of
\(-p\), and conjugate primes when they differ by an odd power.
This follows by intersecting the decomposition group, or its
complex-conjugate coset, with \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\).
A full mark \(\Lambda\) is obtained by choosing exactly one
prime in each conjugate pair meeting the primes induced by
a-places. Both members of each such pair are induced by a-places:
multiplication by \((-p)^{o_0}\) is odd and preserves class 1
modulo \(q_0\). Thus the source's requirement that each chosen prime
itself be induced by an a-place is satisfied.

No marked or conjugate-marked embedding can be a b-place:
its orbit modulo \(q_0\) is contained in the subgroup of odd order
generated by \(-p\), so cannot contain \(-1\), an element of order
two. In particular \(w_\tau=a/x_1\) on both fibers above each marked
pair. Multiplication by \(-p\) identifies these two fibers,
preserving all lift weights \(w_j(\tau)\), since the source
lift weight is \(w_\tau+(j+1/2)\operatorname{gap}\).
This proves the determinant-character balance required below,
including the character value at a marked uniformizer being a
coefficient-prime unit. Finally no collision survives deleting
height-one columns at embeddings inducing \(\Lambda\): the
partner of every unmarked a-place induces a marked prime.

## 2. Exact charged box calculation

At stage \(j\), let \(L_\tau=a\,1_{\tau\in\Phi_a}\), \(c_\tau\)
be the number of columns other than height one and below
\(n_{j+1}\), and \(B_\tau\) their number of boxes. These are
the source's lowColumns and lowBoxes. Let \(S\) consist of
embeddings inducing a marked prime. For a neighbor at
\(\mathfrak l\in\Lambda\), let \(J_\mathfrak l\) be the indices of
its at most \(r_p\) diagonal entries \(p\). The other entries
are 1, and at the conjugate prime use the inverse transpose.
This covers the entire double coset by integral matrices on both
sides (elementary divisor reduction over the unramified DVR).

For row \(i\) at \(\tau\) define \(E_{\tau i}=+1\) when
\(w(\tau)\in\Lambda,\ i\in J_{w(\tau)}\), and \(-1\) when
\(\overline{w(\tau)}\in\Lambda,\ i\in J_{\overline{w(\tau)}}\);
otherwise it is zero. The weight-coordinate embedding uses
\(\bar\tau\), so the image of the diagonal has entries
\(p^{-E_{\tau i}}\). Define the charge of a box at column height
\(h\) to be 1 in exactly the following two cases:
* \(\tau\in S,\ h=1,\ i\notin J_{w(\tau)}\);
* \(\overline{w(\tau)}\in\Lambda,\ i\in J_{\overline{w(\tau)}}\).

All other charges are zero. For every odd partner, a marked
embedding has a conjugate-marked partner with the *same* index
set \(J\). If the direction index is outside \(J\), the marked
height-one box is charged; if inside \(J\), the conjugate box is
charged at every height. This covers both orders of the exempt
pair (height one on either side), which is the charged-jet rule.

At \(\tau\in S\), exponent plus charge is identically 1 on
height one and equals the indicator of \(J\) on the other columns.
At the conjugate fiber exponent plus charge is identically zero.
Each column has distinct row indices, so the total cost is at most
\[
 \sum_{\mathfrak l\in\Lambda}A_\mathfrak l+
                r_p\sum_{\tau\in S}c_\tau,\qquad
 A_\mathfrak l=a\,|\Phi_a\cap w^{-1}(\mathfrak l)|.          \tag{2}
\]
One may replace \(r_p c_\tau\) by the sharper
\(\sum_{h\ne1} \kappa_{\tau,h}\min(h,|J_{w(\tau)}|)\).
The determinant part of the weight contributes zero: at the two
fibers the sums of the last-entry weights agree by Section 1, and
their exponents are respectively \(|J|\) and \(-|J|\).
These statements are exact for every filling, not merely averages.

Use the standard-data charged Schur estimate on the appropriate
matrix at each stage. Assuming the stated kernel transport, the
neighbor kernel multiplies the integral gauge by at most
\[
 p^{\,\sum_{\mathfrak l} A_\mathfrak l+
          r_p\sum_{\tau\in S}c_\tau-D_{p,j}(S)},             \tag{3}
\]
where \(D_{p,j}(S)\) denotes the source divisibilityBound.
In particular the finite sum over all coset representatives of
a neighbor has the *same* bound, not a bound multiplied by their
number: the coefficient norm is ultrametric.

## 3. Compare the actual diagram with the prime sum

Put \(L'_\tau=L_\tau 1_{\tau\notin S}\) and
\(v=v_p(\pi_p)\), with \(v=1\) inert and \(v=1/2\) ramified;
\(\gamma_p=v\sum_{e>0}p^{-e}\) over positive partner exponents.
The preceding charged-jet proof recovers the source estimate
\[
 D_{p,j}(S)=v\sum_{\tau,e>0}\left[
 (L'_\tau/p^e-L'_{\tau'}-c_{\tau'})_+
 +(B_\tau/p^e-B_{\tau'})_+-c_\tau/p^e\right]
 +\gamma_p\sum_{\tau\in S}L_\tau - R_0-R_1 .                \tag{4}
\]
Here \(R_1=f n_{j+1}^3(1+\log_p\max(B_{\rm all},1))\)
is the factorial-loss allowance, \(B_{\rm all}=\sum_\tau(L_\tau+B_\tau)\).
At inert \(p\), \(R_0=0\); at \(p=d\) it is the source
half-weight \(e=0\) deduction. The source's closed-form bound
uses this infinite geometric sum rather than the smaller
finite min-cut cost. Formula (4) is the prescribed lower bound,
not an assertion of equality with the actual valuation.

The diagram estimates in JetExponent.lean follow from the height
\(k\) block of length between \(w_\tau\) and \(w_\tau+\operatorname{gap}\)
and the remaining low columns, at most gap per height:
\[
 k w_\tau\le B_\tau\le k w_\tau+\Delta,\qquad
 c_\tau\le w_\tau+\Delta,\qquad \Delta=x^{60}.
\]
We give the exact dependence of the comparison on scale assumptions.
For \(p<x_1\) set \(U=a/(pm)\). The admissible-scale inequalities
used here are
\[
 2(w_\tau+\Delta)\le a/m,\quad
 2v\Delta\lceil\log_p\lceil x_1\rceil\rceil\le U,\quad
 R_1\le fU,\quad(\log b)^2\ge5 .
\]
For \(p=d\) one also uses \(c_\tau\le a/(dm)\) and
\(B_\tau\le a/(dm)\) off the b-places. These are precisely the
size comparisons in the source with the indicated gap and
\(x^{60}\); they remain assumptions on a choice of the
admissible global scales.

The first positive part in (4) differs from
\(1_{\Phi_a}(\,a/p^e-w_{\tau'}\,)_+\) by at most \(\Delta\),
except for two explicit contributions:
* an erased row can be paid for by \(a/p^e\); at \(e=1\),
  the unused part of this payment is at least \(a/x_1\),
  since \(p<x_1\) and every \(w_{\tau'}\ge a/x_1\);
* if both long rows remain, the lost gain is at most \(a/p^e\).

The second positive part dominates
\(k(w_\tau/p^e-w_{\tau'})_+-\Delta\), by the two bounds on
\(B_\tau\). The required gain parts are both zero for
\(p^e\ge x_1\); there only the negative \(c_\tau/p^e\) needs
to be retained. Thus summing the two \(\Delta\)-errors costs
at most \(fU\), and the negative term costs at most \(fU\)
because \(\gamma_p c_\tau\le (2/p)c_\tau\le U\).
The erased-row payments sum exactly to the
\(\gamma_p\)-term in (4).

At \(p=d\), the involution \(\tau\mapsto-\tau\) cannot take
an a-place to an a-place, or a b-place to a b-place. The three
terms in the \(e=0\) deduction are
\(\min(L'_\tau,L'_{-\tau}+c_{-\tau})\), \(c_\tau\), and
\(\min(B_\tau,B_{-\tau})\).
Each is at most \(a/(dm)\) by the stated inequalities.
The half-weight correction therefore costs at most \(3fU/2\).
With the factorial allowance, the entire error is at most
\(5fU\). In particular, defining gain \(G_p\) by the actual
prime sum
\[
 G_p/m=v\sum_{\tau,e>0}
 \left[1_{\Phi_a}(\,a/p^e-w_{\tau'}\,)_+
       +k(w_\tau/p^e-w_{\tau'})_+\right],
\]
we obtain
\[
 D_{p,j}(S)\ge G_p/m-v C_p(S)+
      v|\Phi_a\cap S|a/x_1-fa(\log b)^2/(mp).               \tag{5}
\]
The factor \(v\) on the collision sum is retained; at \(p=d\)
the sum is zero. This is the required signed comparison, with
no loss from replacing the precise partner terms by their
worst absolute values. It supplies the combinatorial part of
the source's gain estimate.

## 4. Move the Hecke operators through the whole prefix at once

The essential local theta-transfer input is the following
identity on the actual unramified pair, with its standard
vacuum and twisted characters:
\[
 H_{j+1}(U)C_j
       =R_j(U)C_j H_j(z_p^{t+1}U),\qquad
 H_j(U)=\sum_i(-1)^i Q^{i(i-1)/2}T_{j,i}U^i,\quad Q=p^o .
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
Here \(C_j\) denotes the one-lift map on spherical vectors
at the marked prime; \(R_j(0)=1\), \(R_j\) is integral,
and is independent of the other finite tensor indices.
In the source \(R_j=\prod_{\nu=0}^{t}
(1-Q^\nu z_p^{n_j}\chi_j U)\); the character \(\chi_j\)
is a unit by the balance in Section 1. We do not assume
that the resulting local maps are invertible.
The remaining kernel obligation is to establish (6) with
the global normalization; its coefficient algebra is as follows.

For the composite \(C^{(h)}=C_{h-1}\cdots C_0\), induction,
with ordinary polynomial substitution, yields
\[
 H_h(U) C^{(h)} =
 R^{(h)}(U) C^{(h)} H_0(z_p^{h(t+1)}U),
\quad
 R^{(h)}=\prod_{j=0}^{h-1}R_j(z_p^{(h-1-j)(t+1)}U).
\]
All coefficients of \(R^{(h)}\) are integral and its constant
coefficient is 1. The formal inverse has integral coefficients
by the coefficient recursion, including at all small primes.
Extracting coefficient \(i\) proves
\[
 p^{E(h,i)} C^{(h)}T_{0,i}
   =\sum_{i'\le i}a_{i,i'} T_{h,i'} C^{(h)},\qquad
 E(h,i)=\frac{o}{2}\bigl(h(t+1)i+i(i-1)\bigr),
 \quad v_p(a_{i,i'})\ge0 .                                \tag{7}
\]
More precisely \(a_{i,i'}=(-1)^{i+i'}Q^{i'(i'-1)/2}
[U^{i-i'}](R^{(h)})^{-1}\). There is no accumulated
factorial or repeated-rank loss. The same coefficients work
on every finite tensor term.

In particular for \(h\le m,\ i=r_p\),
\[
 E(h,r_p)\le \tfrac12 f r_p(n_m+r_p)
                 =\tfrac12\operatorname{transferExponent}(p).
\]
This saves at least half of the transfer allowance in the
source. The uniform smaller exponent can be used in its
place, or the exact stage exponent \(E(j+1,r_p)\) retained.

Indeed put \(Y=\prod_{\mathfrak l\in\Lambda}
p^{A_\mathfrak l}T_{0,r_p}^{(\mathfrak l)}\).
By target equivariance, moving \(Y\) through a prefix
of length \(j+1\) changes the datum at stage \(j\) by the
combination of target Hecke operators in (7), multiplied
at each prime by \(p^{A_\mathfrak l-E(j+1,r_p)}\).
Because the datum is spherical at the marked primes, these
operators commute there with the source action and the
target integral compact group. Operators at distinct primes
commute. Thus the changed tensors retain the joint
invariance, and induction from stage 0 proves that using
one modified datum at every stage gives \(C^{(m)}Y^m\).
In each term there is at most one neighbor at each marked
prime, so (2)--(3), ultrametric summation and the scalar
factor show a gauge loss at stage \(j\) of at most
\[
 |\Lambda|E(j+1,r_p)+r_p\sum_{\tau\in S}c_\tau-D_{p,j}(S).
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
This is the precise signed charge comparison for the modified
chain. The large \(A_\mathfrak l\)-cost cancels. No polynomial
identity is used on nonspherical source vectors: the needed
spherical property of all intermediate forms is part of (6).

The source scale comparison
\[
 |\Lambda|f r_p(n_m+r_p)+r_p\sum_{\tau\in S}c_\tau
 \le L_p/m+|\Phi_a\cap S|a/x_1
\]
is sufficient for (8); it can now be weakened by replacing
its first term with \(|\Lambda|E(j+1,r_p)\). Together with
\(C_p(S)=0\) and (5), this proves the required exceptional
gain \(G_p-2L_p\) on \(Y^m\) of the integral source.
On the other summand \(p^{\lceil2mfa/p\rceil}\) times the
integral source the unmarked (5) and (1) already give gain
at least \(G_p-L_p\). The same bound \(G_p-2L_p\) therefore
holds on their sum. The missing index estimate for this sum
is exactly the elementary-divisor theorem for the actual
scaled edge operator, not a further charge comparison.

At small nonexceptional primes (5) and Section 1 give
\(G_p-2L_p\) on the whole integral source. Outside those
non-split primes and the auxiliary level prime \(N\), the
all-prime homogeneous derivative estimate on level
\(\delta=\overline{\pi_M}\) at scale \(\bar\delta\) incurs
no loss, since the only remaining fixed denominator
\(\sqrt{-d}\) is a unit. This last application assumes the
actual datum is standard at the indicated level and that
the final restriction preserves the gauge away from \(N\).

## 5. Auxiliary level prime: exact weight-degree colength

The source gauge at \(N\) uses two base points \(d_L,d_\vee\)
and the corresponding integral modules \(M_L,M_\vee\).
Let \(d\) be the all-conjugate-side diagonal
\(\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,N)\) at the relevant primes.
Then \(d_L=d\,d_\vee\), and the source level is contained in
the group preserving both transported lattices.
At a finite class representative \(c\), use the simultaneous open
condition
\[
 r_c d_\vee\in K_N,\qquad d^{-1}r_c d_L\in K_N,
\]
where the first compact neighborhood can be shrunk to
\(K_N\cap dK_Nd^{-1}\); rational weak approximation
via the Cayley transform supplies \(r_c=\gamma_c c\).
The two points commute with \(d\). Transport at each
coefficient embedding now gives a standard value lattice
for \(M_\vee\) and the diagonal-weight lattice for \(M_L\).
The rational change of representative acts by the prescribed
Weyl representation, and the level group preserves the
respective gauges. Stabilizers are trivial at the source
principal level, so class values are arbitrary.

Write \(D_X\) for the dimension of the source and
\(B_X=\sum_{\tau,h<k} h\kappa_{\tau,h}\) for the total
polynomial degree of its Weyl module. The determinant twist
is zero at \(X_0\). On each Weyl basis coordinate the
diagonal acts by \(N^{\sum_\tau m_{\tau,\mathrm{last}}}\),
where \(m\) counts the occurrences of the last index.
All \(k\) index weights have equal total multiplicity,
because permutation matrices conjugate the corresponding
diagonal cocharacters. Their sum is \(D_X B_X\).
It follows that the normalized valuation of the index
is exactly
\[
 \lambda_N=D_X B_X/k.                                      \tag{9}
\]
This is stronger than the estimate using the upper bound
\(B_X\le fa\), and also applies after restricting to the
ramified subspace as an upper bound for its induced index.

For the operator \(U_N\) of the source, its summands have
the explicit form right translation by \(d_\vee h d_L^{-1}\)
times a root of unity, with \(h\in K_N\) (the integral
last-row/column matrix), and all summands are integral on
the input gauge when read at \(d_\vee\).
In fact \(g d_\vee h=(g d_\vee h d_L^{-1}) d_L\);
the norm is unchanged by \(h\), and the coefficients have
unit norm. Thus, once the source-level equivariance of
this operator is established, \(U_N M_L\subset M_\vee\).
The standard integral module \(M_0\) is contained in \(M_\vee\)
by polynomiality and the diagonal exponents of \(d_\vee^{-1}\).

There is a general exact pullback argument: if \(M_L\subset
M_\vee\) has index valuation \(\lambda_N\), start with
\(F_0=M_0\cap M_L\), and define
\(F_{i+1}=\{v\in F_i:U_N^{i+1}v\in M_L\}\).
Since \(U_N^{i+1}F_i\subset M_\vee\), the quotient
\(F_i/F_{i+1}\) injects into \(M_\vee/M_L\).
Therefore \(F_i\) has index valuation at most
\((i+1)\lambda_N\) in \(M_0\). In particular the preimage
of \(M_L\) under \(U_N^{m/2}\) has index at most
\((m/2+1)D_X B_X/k\), with the same conclusion for finite
clearing matrices. This follows from elementary divisors
over a finite coefficient DVR, or directly by diagonal
elimination over a valuation ring; the finite-list
interpretation needs no discreteness of the entire
coefficient valuation ring.

The source's auxiliary-prime bound now follows for the
index part whenever \((m/2+1)B_X/k\le mfb\).
What is still needed for the norm part is the precise
local double-lift identity moving \(U_N^{m/2}\) into the
direct last-column data and its commutation factors.
Thus this is not a proof of the whole \(N\)-adic kernel
theorem. It fixes the exact index budget that its direct
kernel estimates need.

## Remaining boundary

The diagram comparison, charge covering and finite propagation
algebra are proved above. The actual local unitary theta
transport, (6), source-level equivariance at \(N\) and its
double-lift identity, the edge elementary-divisor bound and
the full admissible-parameter existence must still be proved.
The two global estimates cannot be replaced by the conditional
local chain bounds in this note.
