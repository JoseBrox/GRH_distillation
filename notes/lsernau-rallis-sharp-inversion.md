# Convergent Siegel–Weil, sharp finite data, and quantitative Rallis inversion

This chapter supplies an ordinary proof of stable-range Rallis
injectivity for the Schwartz data and nonzero real edges in the
source. It invokes one classical theorem, the convergent
Siegel–Weil formula, with its exact range and measures, rather
than reconstructing the induction and Hecke maximum principle of
the formal proof. It does not give a real edge for every required
parameter or prove the separate derivative sharpness theorem.

Write \(E/F\) for the source CM extension, \(S,T\) for pointed
Hermitian spaces of ranks \(n,M\), and \(G=U(S)(\mathbb A_F)\),
\(G'=U(T)(\mathbb A_F)\). Assume \(M>2n\).
Write \(\Omega(g,h)\) for the source's twisted Weil action,
\(\theta\) for its normalized theta functional, and \([G]\)
for its quotient by rational points with the source Haar
measure. At finite places assume the source hypotheses:
the extension is unramified or split, the normalized lattice
is self-dual, and \(E\) contains the primitive \(5040\)-th
roots of unity. The local additive character, signed
translations, and \(\upsilon\) are those of the
[standard normalization](lsernau-weil-normalization.md).

## 1. The classical input and its exact matching

We use Y. Liu, *Arithmetic theta lifting and L-derivatives
for unitary groups, I*, Algebra & Number Theory 5 (2011),
pp. 849–921, [Theorem 2.1(1), p. 859](https://msp.org/ant/2011/5-7/ant-v5-n7-p01-p.pdf).
The theorem is stated for the split skew-Hermitian group
of dimension \(2r\) and the Hermitian group of dimension
\(M\), for the ordinary Schwartz–Bruhat space on \(T^r\).
With probability quotient measure on the latter group, if
\(M>2r\), its theta integral equals the absolutely
convergent Siegel Eisenstein series at \(s=(M-r)/2\).
The section at this point is \((\omega(g)\phi)(0)\),
with splitting character restricting to
\(\eta_{E/F}^{M}\) on the real-base ideles.
There is no factor two in this range.

Apply it with the split double \(D=S\oplus S^{-}\),
\(r=n\), and the same \(T\). Multiplication of the
split Hermitian form by the source purely imaginary
scalar gives the skew-Hermitian form in the theorem;
the trace symplectic form is the one in the source hom
space. Choose the additive character in the theorem
accordingly. A rational change of hyperbolic basis
identifies this form with the standard split form.
For the chosen hyperbolic splitting \(P\), set
\[
       \phi_f(X)=\operatorname{mixedValue}_{P}(X)f.
\]
Here \(X\) is in rational target coordinates (using
the pointed-space change of coordinates if necessary).
The mixed mean along the isotropic half of the hom
space is exactly evaluation at \(X=0\). The full
mixed summation theorem of
[nonstandard Weil transport](lsernau-nonstandard-weil-transport.md)
gives \(\theta(f)=\sum_{X\in T(E)^n}\phi_f(X)\).
Thus there is no independent scalar in the theta sum
and the value-at-zero functional.

The source twist \(\operatorname{splitWeil}\) on the
double uses \(\upsilon^{n}(\det h)\) on the target.
By the mixed zero-term law, its target action is
\(\phi(X)\mapsto\phi(h^{-1}X)\), with no character.
The source mixed Levi law for \(a\in GL_n(\mathbb A_E)\)
is
\[
      \phi(X)\mapsto|\det a|_E^{M/2}
                         \upsilon(\det a)^M\phi(Xa).
\]
Indeed the inverse of the source \(\operatorname{spaceChar}(1,M)\)
is \(\upsilon^M\). This character restricts to
\(\eta_{E/F}^M\) on the base ideles. These are precisely
the substitutions and Levi factor of the cited theorem
(after the indicated rational basis change). The radical
acts by the moment-matrix character from the same
Heisenberg commutator and has scalar one at zero, by
the parabolic calculation in the transport chapter.
For clarity the representations cannot differ by an
additional Weyl-element phase: on the same irreducible
Heisenberg representation any two lifts of the action
differ by a scalar character. The ratio character is
trivial on the Siegel parabolic and on the target group.
It is also trivial on every conjugate of the parabolic.
The split unitary group is generated by the parabolic
and the opposite root groups, by the relative Bruhat
decomposition (the rank-one interchange is a product
of three unipotent matrices in the hyperbolic plane).
This holds also at split finite places, where the group
is general linear; the restricted product and continuity
then give the adelic assertion. In particular the Weyl
operator and the value-at-zero section have the
normalization of the classical theorem.

At the special point the section evaluated at a rational
isometry of \(D\) depends only on its Lagrangian: the
parabolic multiplier at a rational Levi is one by the
product formula and the Hecke character's rational
invariance. Witt extension gives a bijection of the
rational parabolic cosets with rational Lagrangians,
each counted once. Rational substitution of mixed
means identifies that section with the source zero term
\(Z_L(f)\) along that Lagrangian \(L\). We obtain
\[
 \int_{[G']} \theta(\operatorname{splitWeil}(1,h)f)\,dh
       =\operatorname{vol}[G']\sum_{L}Z_L(f),                 \tag{1}
\]
absolutely on both sides, for every Schwartz datum.
For the polynomial Fock vectors times finite data in
this chapter the mixed function is Schwartz: the
real model is a finite combination of polynomials
times Gaussians under linear metaplectic changes of
polarization, and at the finite places the Fourier
transforms of locally constant compactly supported
functions have the same property.

This use of the classical theorem supplies the needed
Schwartz identity, not the stronger summability of
operator norms on the source integrable-coefficient
Banach completion. That stronger assertion is not
required for the fixed Schwartz data below.

## 2. Absolute convergence in the doubling integral

The classical input yields the uniformity needed here
by a closed graph argument on a fixed Schwartz space.
Fix a finite-dimensional space of finite test functions
and the Fréchet Schwartz space at infinity. Their
tensor product is a Fréchet space \(\mathcal E\).
For each adelic \(h\), the map
\[
 f\longmapsto \theta(\operatorname{splitWeil}(1,h)f)
\]
is continuous on \(\mathcal E\): in the mixed model
this is a lattice sum of a Schwartz function with a
fixed bounded-denominator finite support. The sum
converges by a polynomially weighted supremum seminorm
of order larger than the real lattice dimension.
Each \(Z_L\) is also continuous, by a fixed change
of polarization and evaluation at zero.
By (1), the two linear maps
\[
 \mathcal E\longrightarrow L^1([G']),\qquad
 \mathcal E\longrightarrow\ell^1(\{L\})
\]
whose values are respectively the above function and
\((Z_L(f))_L\), are defined on all of \(\mathcal E\).
Their graphs are closed: convergence in \(\mathcal E\)
implies convergence at every fixed \(h\) and \(L\);
convergence in \(L^1\) gives pointwise convergence along
a subsequence almost everywhere, and convergence in
\(\ell^1\) gives convergence at every coordinate.
The closed graph theorem gives bounds by finitely many
Schwartz seminorms. It is important that this argument
does not assert that the \(L^1\) norm of a sum bounds
the sum of \(L^1\) norms of its terms.

For fixed Schwartz data moved by \(g,g'\) in the two
source factors, the seminorms of the doubled datum
are bounded by powers of the two source heights.
In detail, apply the rational first-minimum reduction
of [graded period unfolding](lsernau-graded-period-unfolding.md),
Section 1. The rational substitutions leave the absolute
theta integral and Lagrangian sum invariant (permutation
of rational Lagrangians). The compact part of the
reduction moves the finite datum through finitely many
vectors, by smoothness and compactness. Their doubles
lie in one fixed finite-dimensional finite-data space.
At infinity we use the equivalent Schwartz seminorms
given by \(L^2\) norms of words of bounded length in
coordinate multiplications and derivatives. Under a
Weil operator, each of those linear operators is
conjugated to a linear combination of the same finite
set of operators by the underlying symplectic matrix.
Unitarity on \(L^2\) bounds the transformed seminorm
of order \(d\) by a constant times the original
seminorm of order \(d\) and the matrix norm to power
\(d\). The tensor/flip operations are continuous for
these seminorms. Each real factor in the reduction
has uniformly bounded matrix norm, and their number is
\(O(\log H)\), giving a polynomial bound in \(H\).
This proves the required majorant
\[
        C_f H_S(g)^A H_S(g')^A                              \tag{2}
\]
both for the absolute theta integral of the doubled
datum and for its sum of absolute zero terms.
For definite \(S\), no height argument is needed:
the rational quotient is compact.

The doubled datum \(d(f',f)\) is the tensor of \(f\)
with the conjugate opposite-source datum of \(f'\).
Multiply the latter by the fixed unit phase obtained
from theta uniqueness, so
\(\theta_D(d(f',f))=\overline{\theta(f')}\theta(f)\).
The covariance of the opposite-source conjugation
follows from matching the Heisenberg operators and
their nonzero vacuum overlaps; its finite coordinates
are changed by the norm-minus-one scalar, and the
conjugation reverses the central character. In
particular the two target determinant characters
cancel in the product of the two original kernels;
the opposite-source Weil correction is
\(\upsilon(\det h)^n\) on the double. This is just
the twist \(\operatorname{splitWeil}\) of Section 1.
The identity for \(d\) therefore also holds with the
first and second data moved by \(g,g'\) and with
simultaneous target action \(h\). This is the
normalization of Doubling.lean, 61–76, 157–177,
362–395; the unit phase has been absorbed only once.

If \(S\) is definite, every rational Lagrangian of \(D\)
is a graph \(\{(\delta^{-1}x,-x):x\in S\}\) of a unique
rational isometry \(\delta\). Indeed its projection
onto the second factor is injective since the kernel
would be isotropic in \(S\), and dimension makes it
bijective. More generally, if \(f\) has primitive
full-column-rank support in the split Schrödinger
model at one finite place, all nongraph terms vanish.
To see this, a nongraph has a nonzero vector in its
intersection with the first factor. The Lagrangian
zero term is invariant under the Heisenberg translations
associated with maps pairing with that vector.
In the split Schrödinger model these translations,
at the chosen split place, act by modulations on
the corresponding column evaluation. Orthogonality
of additive characters forces support on matrices
annihilating that vector. If \(f\) has full column rank
on its support, the projection is zero. This argument
is unchanged by invertible source moves; at a fixed
level the orthogonality is an ordinary finite character
sum. These are the two source cleanness conditions.

At a graph the zero term is particularly simple.
It is the probability mean over maps from \(D\)
vanishing on that graph. Such maps have blocks
\((X,X\delta^{-1})\), with the appropriate order
of the factors in the doubled datum. Transport the
opposite-source block back to \(S\) and change the
rational matrix variable. The probability mean is
the mean-square-normalized data pairing of the two
original theta functions (by the Heisenberg norm
identity). Rational substitution by \(\delta\) has
no scalar. Thus, in the source order of \(f,f'\),
the result for moved data is exactly
\[
 \left\langle f',\Omega(g'^{-1}\delta g,1)f\right\rangle .
\]
This is the graph calculation of Doubling.lean, 196–238,
239–347, 385–395. For the clean data the graph map
is injective, so (1) is the doubling identity with
each matrix coefficient occurring once.

Let \(y,y'\) be continuous rapidly decreasing automorphic
forms in the source height, or bounded forms on a
definite source. Define
\[
 L_f y(h)=\int_{[G]} y(g)\theta(\Omega(g,h)f)\,dg,\qquad
 k(g)=\langle f',\Omega(g,1)f\rangle .
\]
By (2) and rapid decay the absolute triple integral
and the corresponding absolute sum of graph terms
over \([G]^2\) are finite. Fubini and the measure
identity for unfolding the discrete rational group
therefore give
\[
 \langle L_{f'} y',L_f y\rangle
   =\operatorname{vol}[G']
        \int_G k(u)\langle y',R(u)y\rangle\,du .              \tag{3}
\]
Explicitly put \(u=g'^{-1}\delta g\), sum over \(\delta\)
and integrate \(g\) on \([G]\); the rational translates
tile \(G\) and \(y(\delta g)=y(g)\). Integrating \(g'\)
gives the Petersson coefficient on the right.
All measures are the source quotient and ambient Haar
measures, with counting measure on the rational group.
Taking \(f'=f,y'=y\) also proves square integrability
of the lift; no regularized pairing is being used.

## 3. Sharp finite datum at a split level

Use a modulus supported at split places, choosing one
place of each conjugate pair. Let \(K_0\) be the integral
compact and let \(J\subset K_0\) contain the principal
level of the modulus. In the local Schrödinger model
the finite vector \(F_\Psi\) is specified by a nonzero
function \(\Psi\) of an \(M\times n\) matrix modulo the
modulus. Off its support the datum is the vacuum.
Assume that a fixed \(n\times M\) matrix \(A\) has
\(AX\in\overline J\) on the support of \(\Psi\), and
that the twisted right action of \(J\) fixes \(\Psi\).
These conditions concern the actual finite function,
not its average.

Every matrix on the support is integral with an integral
left inverse: its reduction has a left inverse because
\(AX\) is invertible, and a unit minor supplies one over
the local integers. If \(X,Xg\) are both primitive,
then \(g\) and \(g^{-1}\) are integral, by applying
their respective left inverses. When \(g\) is integral
and both reductions are on the support, their images
under \(A\) give \(\bar g=(AX)^{-1}(AXg)\in\bar J\).
As \(J\) contains the principal kernel, \(g\in J\).
The local matrix coefficient is an integral of
\(\overline{\Psi(X)}\Psi(Xg)\), multiplied by a Weil
scalar. Thus it vanishes off \(J\) among the elements
integral away from the chosen primes and their conjugates.
On \(J\) it is exactly the squared data norm, by
the stabilizer condition. This proves the sharpness
coefficient and cleanness at a nonempty modulus.
For empty modulus cleanness follows from definiteness.
The exact mass, with each integral matrix box of
probability measure one, is
\[
         c=\frac{\|F_\Psi\|^2}{[K_0:J]},\qquad
 \|F_\Psi\|^2=\frac{\sum_X|\Psi(X)|^2}
                         {|\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m|^{Mn}} .
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
For a prescribed principal level with trivial determinant
character there, the indicator of \(\binom{I_n}{0}\)
modulo the modulus is an explicit instance, with
\(A=(I_n\ 0)\). No choice of a local L-function
normalizing factor is involved.

## 4. A sharp finite datum for an arbitrary open subgroup

For the definite-standard lifts in Galois attachment
we require an open subgroup \(H\) of the finite unitary
group, with a tame-trivial twisting character \(b\).
Assume \(M\ge2n\) and use the finite integral norm-minus-one
unit \(\epsilon\), supplied by the local norm maps.
Take the matrix
\[
            X=\begin{pmatrix}I_n\\\epsilon I_n\\0\end{pmatrix},
                 \qquad X^*X=0 .
\]
Choose finitely many conjugate pairs and fixed places
\(B\) so that \(K_0\) away from \(B\) lies in \(H\),
and omit every pair whose whole maximal compact lies
in \(H\). Openness gives a nonzero rational integer
\(N\) such that the principal level modulo \(N\)
lies in \(H\). Put \(z=N^{-1}\) on \(B\), zero
elsewhere, and let \(S_0\) be the set where \(z\) is
not integral. Translate the unit vacuum by the
signed Heisenberg operator of \(zX\), giving \(F\).
It has norm one and is a vacuum off \(S_0\).

Here is a direct proof of its coefficient support.
For the matrix lattice \(\Lambda=\widehat{\mathcal O}_E^{M\times n}\)
the translated vacua are eigenvectors for Heisenberg
translations by \(\Lambda\); two different characters
are orthogonal. The vectors \(F,\Omega(g,1)F\) have
these eigencharacter descriptions for \(\Lambda\) and
\(\Lambda g^{-1}\), respectively. Nonzero overlap
forces their characters to agree on the intersection,
so the difference of their translating vectors lies
in its symplectic dual, \(\Lambda+\Lambda g^{-1}\).
This dual-intersection identity follows from self-duality
and the usual annihilator identity for two compact open
additive subgroups. In coordinates this is precisely
\[
        zX(g^{-1}-I)+U g^{-1}\in\Lambda
                 \quad\text{for some }U\in\Lambda.           \tag{5}
\]
The use of signed translations is legitimate: on the
intersection the two vacuum-fixed splittings agree,
since the lattice signs are preserved by the unitary
action. Alternatively (5) follows by projecting onto
the signed lattice invariants twice, as in the
finite vacuum-coefficient calculation.

Let \(D=g^{-1}-I\). The identity rows of \(X\) in (5)
give \((zI+U_0)D\) integral, where \(U_0\) is an
integral \(n\times n\) matrix. At a place of \(S_0\),
\(I+z^{-1}U_0\) is an integral unit matrix by reduction,
so \(zD\) is integral there. At every other place
assume \(g\) is integral, as in the sharpness assertion;
then \(D\) and \(zD\) are integral there as well.
Thus \(g\in K_0\) and \(zD\) is integral everywhere.
The restriction of \(g^{-1}\) to \(B\) is congruent
to the identity modulo \(N\); its complementary
restriction is in \(H\) by the choice of \(B\).
Consequently \(g\in H\). Furthermore
\((zX)^*zXD=0\); the displacement \(zXD\) is integral,
and the signed translation composition phase is one.
The compact fixes the vacuum and the tame-trivial
determinant twist is one there. Hence \(g\) fixes \(F\).
Taking \(J=H\cap K_0\cap\operatorname{Stab}(F)\) now
gives an open compact subgroup and exactly the
coefficient formula of Section 3, with mass
\(c=1/[K_0:J]>0\). The original form fixed by \(H\)
is fixed by \(J\). Every place whose maximal compact
was in \(H\) is excluded from \(S_0\), and the
datum is spherical there. All data used in this
construction are actual vectors in the finite part.

## 5. Lattice descent and absolute summability of the vacuum series

The finite coefficient of a unit vacuum, on \(M\)
copies of the source lattice, is
\[
 a(g)=\big(b(\det g)\Delta_f(g)^M\big)^{-1},\qquad
 |\Delta_f(g)|^2=[\Lambda_0:\Lambda_0\cap g^{-1}\Lambda_0].
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
Here \(\Lambda_0=\widehat{\mathcal O}_E^n\).
This is the finite overlap formula of the source;
the absolute-value identity is also the common
self-dual-lattice projection computation in the
standard normalization. At a set \(A\) of places
where the twisting character is unramified, \(a\)
is bi-invariant under the integral compact. The
vacuum series \(R_A\) on bounded functions fixed by
that compact is the sum of \(a(g)R(g)\) over its
right cosets, supported on \(A\) and conjugates.
We give a direct quantitative proof of inversion.

Locally let \(L\) and \(M_1\ne L\) be self-dual
lattices. At an inert unramified place write \(q\)
for the residue cardinality and \(\pi\) for a base
uniformizer. There exists \(x\in M_1\cap\pi^{-1}L
\setminus L\), by reducing a denominator (self-duality
excludes strict inclusion in \(L\)). Put
\[
   L_0=\{v\in L:h(x,v)\text{ integral}\},\qquad L_1=L_0+Ox .
\]
The linear residue test has codimension one since
\(\pi x\) is primitive and \(L\) self-dual. The norm
of \(x\) is integral, and \(\pi x\in L_0\).
The lattice \(L_1\) is integral and has the same
covolume as \(L\), since both
\([L:L_0]\) and \([L_1:L_0]\) equal \(q\).
It is therefore self-dual. Since
\(M_1\cap L\subset L_0\) and \(x\in M_1\),
\(M_1\cap L_1\) contains \((M_1\cap L)+Ox\),
giving a gain of at least \(q\) in the intersection
index. Each integral unimodular lattice over an
unramified quadratic local extension is isometric
to the standard one: the nondegenerate finite
Hermitian form has a unit-norm vector, lifted and
rescaled using surjectivity of the unit norm, and
orthogonal splitting gives the claim inductively.
This works at residue characteristic two by
nondegeneracy of the residue trace pairing, without
division by two in the lattice.

For each line of \(\pi x\bmod \pi L\) there are at
most \(\sqrt q\) possible \(L_1\). Fix one admissible
\(x\); any other representative of that line is
\(x+v\) after unit rescaling, \(v\in L\).
The condition that its norm is integral is the
trace-zero condition on \(\pi h(x,v)\bmod\pi\).
This residue distinguishes the cosets of \(L_0\),
and the trace-zero subgroup of the residue field
has order \(\sqrt q\). Representatives differing
by \(L_0\) give the same \(L_1\). Thus the number of
neighbors is bounded by
\((q^n-1)\sqrt q/(q-1)\).
At a split pair the lattices at the two places are
mutual duals and the local group is \(GL_n\).
If there is a denominator at a chosen component,
enlarge \(L\) there by \(Ox\), and shrink the
conjugate lattice to the dual. The conjugate
intersection with \(M_1\) is contained in this
shrunken lattice, since \(x\in M_1\) on the first
component. Hence the index in the product improves
by at least \(q\). If one component has no
denominator but differs from \(L\), use its dual
component. There is one such neighbor per residue
line and choice of component. All these split
lattices are isometric to the standard pair.

Choose one transporting element for every neighbor
of the standard lattice, inverting it if needed.
Their left actions on cosets are bijective. The
integer index in (6) is the same as
\([g\Lambda_0:g\Lambda_0\cap\Lambda_0]\).
The preceding construction gives, at every
nonidentity coset, a step from a fixed finite
list at some place that strictly decreases this
index by a factor of at least \(q\).
This descends to the identity coset. For a chosen
deterministic descent rule the sequence of step
labels determines the original coset, by applying
their inverses to the identity. In particular the
sum of absolute coefficients is bounded by the
sum over all finite words in the step labels.

Put
\[
 t_w=\frac{q_w^n-1}{q_w-1}\,q_w^{-M/2}
          \begin{cases}1& w\ne\bar w,\\
                         \sqrt{q_w}&w=\bar w .
          \end{cases}
\]
Since \(M\ge2n+1\), these weights are at most
\(1/((q_w-1)\sqrt{q_w})\) in the split case, and
\(1/(q_w-1)\) otherwise. Each residue field has
at least four elements (third and fourth roots
of unity exclude sizes two and three).
For an orbit \(v\) of conjugation set \(t_v=\sum_{w\in v}t_w\).
Then \(t_v\le1/3\). Also \(\sum_v t_v<\infty\):
the split weights are \(O(q_w^{-3/2})\); at a nonsplit
place \(q_w\ge p^2\), where \(p\) is the residue
characteristic, since the local extension over the
real base is inert unramified. There are at most
\([E:\mathbb Q]\) places above each \(p\).
The sum converges by comparison with the corresponding
Dirichlet series (or with the convergent sum over
rational integers).

Over one orbit the word bound gives
\[
  \sum_{gK_v}|a(g)|\le\frac1{1-t_v},\qquad
           \|R_v-I\|\le r_v:=\frac{t_v}{1-t_v}\le\frac12 .
                                                               \tag{7}
\]
The operator acts on the Banach space of bounded
functions right invariant under the integral compact
at all selected places. Bi-invariance of its
coefficient makes that space stable, and disjoint
local operations commute. The same bound works on
the corresponding closed invariant \(L^2\) subspace
of the automorphic quotient, because right translations
are isometries. We obtain in either norm
\[
 \|R_v^{-1}\|\le\frac{1-t_v}{1-2t_v}.
\]
The sum of the \(r_v\) is finite. The product of the
\(R_v\)'s over any set of orbits converges in operator
norm, since the products of \(1+r_v\) are bounded
and the tails tend to the identity in norm. The
same applies to their inverse product because
\(\|R_v^{-1}-I\|\le r_v/(1-r_v)\le2r_v\).
Multiplication is continuous in operator norm, so
these products are inverse. Absolute summability
of the restricted-product cosets follows from the
finite products of \((1-t_v)^{-1}\); local
multiplicativity of (6) and absolute convergence
identify the limiting product with the actual
vacuum series \(R_A\).

In particular this strengthens the mere nonzero
criterion of the source to the explicit lower bound
\[
       \|R_A y\|\ge
             \left(\prod_{v\subset A\cup\bar A}
                     \frac{1-2t_v}{1-t_v}\right)\|y\| .
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
The factor is strictly positive. All the estimates
are independent of the particular form or eigensystem.
No assertion of a field-independent lower bound
is made. Since the coefficients are bi-invariant,
the real type is unchanged by \(R_A\), and continuity,
boundedness and automorphy are preserved in the
supremum model by uniform convergence. In the
\(L^2\) model the corresponding isotypic closed
subspace is invariant.

## 6. The integrated lift

Use a sharp finite datum \(F\) of mass \(c>0\), at
level \(J\subset K_0\), and a polynomial real Fock
vector \(v\) forming an actual real edge from the
source type to the target type. At the bad places
its coefficient is \(\|F\|^2 1_J\); away from them
it is (6). The finite Haar mass of \(J\) relative
to \(K_0\) is \([K_0:J]^{-1}\). Hence partitioning
the finite integral in (3) into local compact cosets,
and using invariance of \(y\) at \(J\), gives
\[
 \langle L_{v\otimes F}y',L_{v\otimes F}y\rangle
 =\operatorname{vol}[G']\,c\!
   \int_{G_\infty}\! k_v(x)
        \langle y', R_A y(\,\cdot\, x)\rangle\,dx .            \tag{9}
\]
Here \(A\) is the complement of the bad places,
and \(k_v(x)=\langle v,\Omega_\infty(x,1)v\rangle\).
Absolute convergence is already supplied by (3);
the finite convolution commutes with real translations.
The source real edge condition states that on the
specified source type this integral equals the
nonzero scalar
\[
      Z_v=\int_{G_\infty}
        k_v(x)\,\overline{e(x)}/e(1)\,dx
\]
times the Petersson product. Thus the exact Rallis
formula on that type is
\[
 \boxed{\ \langle Ly',Ly\rangle
     =\operatorname{vol}[G']\,c Z_v\langle y',R_A y\rangle\ }.
                                                               \tag{10}
\]
In particular \(Ly=0\) implies \(y=0\), because one
may take \(y'=R_Ay\) and use (8). For the actual
compact real source in Galois attachment, Schur
orthogonality gives the edge scalar directly:
if the matrix coefficient of \(v\) on the indicated
irreducible type is nonzero, its convolution with
the contragredient coefficient has positive trace
on that type, and the matching rank-one edge
gives its positive scalar. Nonvanishing of the
specific edge for the source parameters is still
