# Finite Shintani labels and the selected Frobenius coset

This supplies the special local matching data for squarefree Green labels in
[specific Hecke transfer](lsernau-specific-hecke-transfer.md). We use classical
finite-group character theory, not local Langlands for p-adic groups or the
existence of automorphic Galois representations.

## Classical input and its precise scope

Use Green's cuspidal character parametrization and the finite-field
Shintani theorem in the following form.

* T. Shintani, "Two remarks on irreducible characters of finite general
  linear groups", J. Math. Soc. Japan 28 (1976), 396–414.
  [Published full paper](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/28/2/28_2_396/_pdf).
  Lemmas 2-8 and 2-9 give compatibility with finite parabolic induction.
* C. Bonnafé, "On a theorem of Shintani", J. Algebra 218 (1999), 229–245,
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/jabr.1998.7797),
  [published full text](https://imag.umontpellier.fr/~bonnafe/Travaux/shintani.pdf),
  Theorem 4.3.1(a). The extension on the
  cyclic semidirect product can be chosen with positive Shintani descent,
  without a residual minus sign.
* A. J. Silberger and E.-W. Zink, "Explicit Shintani base change and the
  Macdonald correspondence for characters of GL_n(k)", J. Algebra 319
  (2008), 4147–4176, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2008.02.010),
  equation (1.1): finite-field base change under Macdonald's
  parametrization is restriction of tame Weil–Deligne inertia-equivalence
  parameters along the corresponding unramified local extension.
  In Green's language, the regular-character orbits split under the
  new Frobenius, and the partition on each resulting orbit is unchanged.

The full text of Bonnafé's Theorem 4.3.1(a) and Shintani's induction
Lemmas 2-8 and 2-9 was inspected for the product-type addendum.
The bibliography and explicit parametrization equation (1.1) in the
third paper were checked against its publisher's abstract. We invoke
these classical theorems, not a reconstruction of their proofs. The
third statement is the explicit parametrization theorem, which is
stronger than mere existence of the Shintani bijection. No Ramanujan
bound is used.

Let k have q elements, k' have q^p elements, with p prime. Use the same
faithful multiplicative character T of a common algebraic closure of k
for both fields. The source label c is monic, squarefree and has nonzero
constant term; put n = deg c. Its character Xi_c on GL_n(k) is the
parabolic-induction product of the Green cuspidals tau_P for the monic
irreducible factors P of c. See TauFunctions.lean (thetaExponent,
thetaChar, greenTau, labelChar, IsLabel); LiftingGreenCharacters.lean,
653–end. In the Green parametrization each participating Frobenius orbit
has partition (1). The sign in tau_P is the positive-character convention
of the source; these are actual cuspidal characters and the product over
distinct orbits is irreducible. This chapter treats the squarefree
factor. The [product-type addendum](lsernau-shintani-product-types.md)
supplies its parabolic product with all repeated determinant blocks
in the source; it retains their ranks and gives a genuine positive
selected-coset extension.

## Exact character transport, with the source normalization

For an irreducible P of degree d and root x in F_{q^d}, choose j so that

    T(x) = exp(2 pi i j/(q^d-1)).

The source regular character on F_{q^d}^* is T^j on that field.
Changing the chosen j by a multiple of q^d-1 does not change this
restriction. Replacing x by x^{q^a} replaces the character by its
Frobenius conjugate. Conversely injectivity of T and the exact orbit
length of x imply that the character orbit has length d: its q^b
translate is equal to it exactly when (q^d-1) divides j(q^b-1),
equivalently x^{q^b}=x.

Put s=gcd(p,d) and L=lcm(p,d). The q-orbit of x splits into s
q^p-orbits of length d/s. These are exactly the roots of the
irreducible factors of P over k'. For a representative x_a=x^{q^a}
of such an orbit, the field generated by k' and x_a is F_{q^L}.
The exponent prescribed by the source in that field is

    j'_a = j q^a (q^L-1)/(q^d-1)  mod (q^L-1).

Indeed this is obtained by writing the same T(x_a) relative to the
primitive complex (q^L-1)-st root of unity. Consequently on F_{q^L}^*

    T^{j'_a}(y) = T^{j q^a}(N_{F_{q^L}/F_{q^d}} y).

This establishes norm pullback with the very same faithful character,
not just equality of the lengths of the orbits. The new character is
regular for k' by the same injectivity argument. Distinct original
q-orbits remain disjoint after splitting. This covers p dividing d,
p not dividing d, the linear factor X-1, and residue characteristic p.

Under Macdonald's tame inertia-equivalence parametrization, a
single-box orbit is the Frobenius cycle of precisely this regular
character, with zero monodromy on it. Restricting the tame parameter
along the unramified extension replaces Frobenius by its p-th power:
the s resulting cycles carry the characters computed above, still
with the single-box partition. Therefore the explicit finite-field
Shintani theorem identifies the lift of Xi_c with Xi_{c viewed over k'}.
This identification is insensitive to the uniform choice of using
arithmetic Frobenius or its inverse for indexing orbits.

## Only one coset is needed

Write phi for q-power Frobenius on k', H=GL_n(k'), G=GL_n(k).
The positive Shintani extension theorem gives an actual character
S of H semidirect <phi> whose restriction to H is Xi_{c/k'} and
whose value at (a,phi) is Xi_c(b), where the cyclic norm
a phi(a) ... phi^{p-1}(a) is conjugate to b in G after extension
of scalars. In particular this holds if the norm is literally b.
We require no assertion about the values of this same extension on
the other generating cosets phi^j. The source DescendsTo definition
in CosetDescent.lean asks for all nonidentity cosets in prime degree;
the proof of isLocalMatching_shintani, however, chooses phi to be
the arithmetic residue Frobenius and uses DescendsTo only at phi.
Thus this weaker, single-coset theorem suffices for the automorphic
transfer, although it does not independently reconstruct the full
abstract Lift Nat statement in that library.

At a source split CM place w inert in the prime-degree step, let u
be its unique extension. Use the hypotheses that mu is trivial on
the whole local multiplicative group, the step is unramified there,
and the local residue degree is p. Choose sigma in Gal(E'/E) lifting
phi. Let K,K' be the two maximal compacts and L,L' their reduction
kernels. Their pullbacks agree, reduction is equivariant for sigma,
and the upstairs maximal compact has split norm-one cocycles by
integral unramified descent. These are the compact levels already
constructed in global norm roots.

Use functions supported on the compacts:

    f(g)  = conjugate(Xi_c(reduce(g))),
    f'(a) = conjugate(S(reduce(a),phi)).

They are bi-invariant for L,L', and f' is invariant under twisted
K'-conjugation by the class-function property of S. The compact
indicator root for a supported characteristic polynomial supplies
exactly the same root for these functions. Indeed both supports are
contained in the indicator supports, and the quotient support
condition follows from that of the indicator. If the root quotient
has norm equal to a base-changed matrix, the indicator matching
first shows that either both arguments are integral or neither is.
In the integral case reduction carries the local norm to the
semidirect product p-th power. The single-coset identity just proved
then gives the matching values, and in the nonintegral case both
values vanish. This is IsLocalMatching with one matching function
and an empty family of distinguished local eigenvalue operators.

## Trace and output label

Here are the positivity and absorption checks, so that the matching
is of actual vectors and not merely abstract characters. On a
finite-dimensional space of L-invariants stable under K, f acts
by the finite group-algebra element

    B = sum_{g in G} conjugate(Xi_c(g)) rho(g).

By character orthogonality this is |G|/Xi_c(1) times the
orthogonal isotypic projector. A vector having the label is fixed
by L and is not killed by B, so the trace of B is strictly positive
in any such space containing that vector. The same argument applies
if the space is obtained by intersecting with a complementary
spherical generalized eigenspace; it remains stable under K.
This is the required HasNonzeroTraceAround.

For the upstairs group-algebra element B' with coefficients
conjugate(S(a,phi)), let Pi be the representation of the semidirect
product having character S. Then its coefficient on the selected
coset is tr(Pi(a)Pi(phi)), a linear combination of matrix
coefficients of the restriction Pi|_H. Schur orthogonality implies
that B' kills every irreducible H-representation absent from Pi|_H.
Since Pi|_H has character Xi_{c/k'}, the image of B' lies in that
isotypic component. Thus a nonzero vector in the image of B'
has the required label at u. In particular the twisted spectral
extraction of the specific transfer chapter produces an actual
weak base-change eigenform with that label.

This supplies the squarefree finite-character and local matching
inputs to labelled automorphic transfer. The
[product-type addendum](lsernau-shintani-product-types.md) extends it
to the full prescribed character, including repeated determinant
blocks, and proves trace positivity and absorption without requiring
the product to be irreducible. The [split attachment chapter](lsernau-split-galois-attachment.md)
supplies spherical Galois attachment, and the linked Iwahori chapters
handle dominant matching and sorted extraction. Their global
descent and crystalline assertions are separate from finite
Shintani transfer.
