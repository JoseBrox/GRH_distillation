# Construction of the tied rows and their squarefree coefficient

The finite algebra below constructs the actual matrices of tied rows
used by the first-order congruence argument, including the character
matrices and their assembly. Its hypotheses are the arithmetic
Selmer-length, local-defect, character-triangle and detection bounds,
and the trace-ideal relations on the prescribed enlarged eigensystem
family. It does not infer those bounds from mere finiteness of Selmer
groups. In particular the integral automorphic action of the final
coefficient still needs the auxiliary-level operator calculation.

Source: the readable
[lsernau/grh-lean@72fead1](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/tree/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/Congruences/FirstOrder).
The construction is a direct elementary-divisor proof of the finite
algebra in RibetMatrices.lean (blob

## 1. Integral tied-row module

Let \(O\) be a coefficient DVR with fraction field \(K\),
uniformizer \(\varpi\), and valuation \(v(\varpi)=1\).
Lengths and valuations in this chapter are in uniformizer
units; divide by \(v(\ell)\) when using the source's normalized
lengths. Let \(G\) be the Galois group, and \(u_x\), \(x\in Y\),
the finite family of integral representations on the actual
enlarged level. On the distinguished lifts \(x\in\mathcal X\)
assume the already normalized decomposition
\[
 u_x=\rho_x\oplus\bigoplus_{e=0}^{t-1}\xi_e,\qquad \xi_0=1 .
\]
These are the block lattices, not merely their residual traces.
We work over \(R=O[X_j:j\in J]\) and \(\Lambda=R[G]\).
Fix the scalar \(P=\ell^\nu\ne0\), the two inertia/Frobenius
pairs \((\tau_j,\phi_j),(\tau'_j,\phi'_j)\) at each auxiliary
index, and the source tie polynomials \(\epsilon_j\) with
\(\epsilon_j(\xi_e(\phi_j))\equiv1\bmod P\) and the corresponding
primed congruence.

The \(O\)-span of \((u_x(g)-1)_x\) is a finitely generated
torsion-free submodule of a finite free module. Choose finitely
many \(g\)'s spanning it, together with the prescribed tame
generators \(\delta_v\) and detecting elements \(g\in s\).
Retain separate generator indices even if some elements agree.
Denote the list by \(g_i\), indexed by \(I=W\sqcup s_0\sqcup s\).
For each \(g\), choose integral coordinates \(z(g)\) in this
list, satisfying
\[
 (u_x(g)-1)_x=\sum_i z_i(g)(u_x(g_i)-1)_x .
\]
No basis theorem about a noncommutative group algebra is needed.

Write \([g]=g-1\), \(I_{\rm aug}=\ker(\Lambda\to R)\),
\(\mathfrak k=\bigcap_x\ker u_x\). At the distinguished lift
\(\pi\), take \(\rho_\pi(\gamma)=y1\), \(y-1\in O^\times\),
and \(\xi_e(\gamma)=1\) for all \(e\).
Put \(n=(y-1)^{-1}[\gamma]\) and
\[
 b_0=n+(1-n)[a].
\]
Here \(a\) separates the cyclotomic characters. The source tie
left ideal is the sum of \(\mathfrak k\), \(P I_{\rm aug}\),
the left ideals generated by the two ties at every \(j\),
and \(I_{\rm aug}b_0\). The tied rows \(N\subset\Lambda^I\)
are its inverse image under
\[
 r\longmapsto \sum_i r_i[g_i].
\]
This specifies a module over the actual polynomial group
algebra, not an auxiliary commutative relation module.

In particular \(N\) contains the following rows (write
\(z(g)\) for the row of constant group-algebra entries):
* \(z(gh)-z(g)-g z(h)\), \(z(g_i)-e_i\), and every
  \(\sum_g c_g z(g)\) for which the corresponding linear
  combination of the family \((u_x(g)-1)_x\) vanishes;
* \(P e_i\), and the two rows \(z(\tau)-X_j\epsilon_j(\phi)z(\phi)\);
* \([g]((y-1)^{-1}z(\gamma)+(1-n)z(a))\) for every \(g\).

For the first rows use the representation cocycle identity for
\(u_x-1\). For the last row the row-value of the expression in
parentheses equals \(b_0\) modulo \(\mathfrak k\).
If \(o_v(T)(T-1)\) kills \(u_x(\delta_v)\) on every member
of the family, then the row with only entry \(o_v(\delta_v)\)
at the index \(\delta_v\) also belongs to \(N\).
All of these assertions follow directly by applying the
row-value map and reducing by \(\mathfrak k\).

## 2. The main block: one exact dual-lattice calculation

Let \(r=\dim\rho_\pi\). Assume the matrices \(\rho_\pi(g)\)
span \(M_r(O)\), as required by the source's main summand
hypothesis. Specialize \(X=0\) and act by \(\rho_\pi\)
on \(N\). Its image is a left \(M_r(O)\)-submodule of
\(M_r(O)^I\). Multiplying by matrix units shows that it is
exactly the matrices all of whose flattened rows lie in one
\(O\)-submodule \(L\subset O^{r|I|}\): each row can be isolated
and moved to any row index.

Let \(w=(w_i)\), \(w_i\in(K/O)^r\), annihilate this lattice.
Define \(c_w(g)=\sum_i z_i(g)w_i\).
The first group of relations in Section 1 makes \(c_w\) a
cocycle for \(\rho_\pi\), makes \(c_w(g_i)=w_i\), and enforces
all the source linear relations on \(u_x-1\).
The tie rows at \(X=0\) force \(c_w(\tau_j)=c_w(\tau'_j)=0\).
The last row for \(g=\gamma\) specializes to \(z(\gamma)1\),
since \(\rho_\pi(n)=1\); hence \(c_w(\gamma)=0\).
Consequently the annihilator is injected by \(w\mapsto c_w\)
into the prescribed tied-cocycle module. This injection does
not require \(z(g_i)=e_i\) before passing to the row quotient.

If that module has length at most \(b_0^{\rm len}<\infty\),
then by Smith normal form \(L\) is full rank and
\[
 \operatorname{length}_O(O^{r|I|}/L)
 =\operatorname{length}_O\operatorname{Hom}_O
                 (O^{r|I|}/L,K/O)\le b_0^{\rm len}.
\]
Indeed any free quotient summand would give a copy of \(K/O\)
in the dual of infinite length. Arrange an \(O\)-basis of
\(L\) into \(|I|\) successive groups of \(r\) rows. By the
matrix-unit property every such group lifts to one actual
row of \(N\). These lifts form a square matrix \(B_0\) over
\(\Lambda\), with
\[
 v\det(\rho_\pi(B_0)|_{X=0})\le b_0^{\rm len}.               \tag{1}
\]
The determinant here and throughout is of the flattened
matrix. In particular there is no factor \(r\) multiplying
the length. This constructs the actual main rows once
the arithmetic tied-length bound is supplied, rather than
assuming their existence.

## 3. Rank-one triangle and Fitting determinants

We first give a general elementary calculation of the
Fitting cost used for a character. For \(N'\subset R^s\)
let \(F(N')\) be the ideal generated by determinants of
square matrices with rows in \(N'\).
For a new row \(v\) satisfying \(h v\in N'\), multilinearity
and the vanishing of determinants with two equal rows give
\[
 h F(N'+Rv)\subset F(N').
\]
For several rows use induction. More generally let the
images of a family of constant-coordinate rows in \(R^s/N'\)
generate a finite \(O\)-module \(M'\) killed by \(P\).
Choose lifts of its elementary-divisor generators.
The enlarged Fitting ideal, multiplied by
\(\varpi^{\operatorname{length}_O M'}\), lies in \(F(N')\).
This uses the same single-row calculation for each cyclic
summand. The statement applies to an infinite family of rows
in a finite \(O\)-span, since the generated module is finite.

Suppose \(\Phi:O^I\to R^s\) is \(O\)-linear, with
\(P\Phi(O^I)\subset N'\), and the images of \(\Phi z(g)\)
for \(g\in s\) equal the respective unit rows modulo \(N'\).
Let \(Z\) consist of the functions \(c(g)=\lambda(z(g))\)
where \(\lambda:O^I\to O/P\) vanishes on \(\Phi^{-1}N'\).
The annihilator duality used here is finite DVR duality:
\(O/P\) identifies with the \(P\)-torsion of \(K/O\);
it separates all points of a finite \(P\)-torsion module.
Assume the two tie relations
\(\Phi z(\tau_j)=X_j\Phi z(\phi_j)\) and the primed
version modulo \(N'\).

For a set of indices \(J_e\) of one character type,
assume \(X_i R^s\subset N'\) for all earlier indices
not in \(J_e\). The source triangle hypothesis is that
there exists a set \(A\subset G\), with \(Z_A\) the
functions of \(Z\) vanishing on \(A\), such that:
1. For \(j\in J_e\), if \(c\in Z_A\) has equal paired
   inertia values for all earlier indices and equal paired
   Frobenius values for the earlier indices in \(J_e\),
   then \(C(c(\tau_j)-c(\tau'_j))=0\).
2. For every submodule \(S\subset Z_A\) vanishing at
   all inertia pairs and equal on the Frobenius pairs of
   \(J_e\), one has
\[
 \operatorname{length}(Z/Z_A)+
 \operatorname{length}(S/S[C])\le b_e .
\]
Here \(C\in O\setminus\{0\}\) and \(S[C]\) is its \(C\)-torsion.
This is exactly the form of HasTriangleCost.

Add the rows \(\Phi z(g)\) for \(g\in A\), obtaining
\(K'\supset N'\). The elementary-divisor cost for this is
at most \(\operatorname{length}(Z/Z_A)\), and in fact equals
it: by finite duality the module generated by the new row
images has dual the image of evaluation on \(A\).
Any ambiguity in the linear form \(\lambda\) giving \(c\)
vanishes on all \(z(g)\), hence contributes no evaluation
image and no cost.

In increasing order \(j\in J_e\) add
\(v_j=\Phi(z(\phi_j)-z(\phi'_j))\).
At the current intermediate module \(T\), all functions
from its annihilator satisfy the equalities in part 1:
the earlier \(v_i\) have been added, and other earlier
\(X_i\) act by zero. Thus every such linear form kills
\(C(z(\tau_j)-z(\tau'_j))\), proving by finite duality
that \(CX_jv_j\in T\). By the single-row Fitting calculation
the cost for this stage is exactly bounded by
\(\prod_{j\in J_e} CX_j\).

Finally specialize all variables to zero, obtaining
\(\bar T\subset O^s\). This quotient is killed by \(P\)
by the unit-row and \(P\Phi\) hypotheses. Its dual injects
into \(Z_A\) via \(\lambda\mapsto c\), because the \(g\in s\)
give the standard unit rows in the quotient.
The resulting functions \(S\) vanish at all inertia pairs
and are equal on the Frobenius pairs of \(J_e\). If
\(M=O^s/\bar T\), then finite duality gives
\(\operatorname{length}(S/S[C])=\operatorname{length}(CM)\)
for this image \(S\), and
\(\operatorname{length}(M/CM)\le |s|v(C)\).
Choose a basis of the full-rank submodule \(\bar T\) and lift
its rows to the final polynomial module \(T\). Its determinant
\(d\in F(T)\) has
\(v(d(0))=\operatorname{length}(M)\).
Multiplying by the costs of the first two stages gives
\[
 \Gamma_e\prod_{j\in J_e}X_j\in F(N'),\qquad
 v(\Gamma_e(0))\le b_e+(|J_e|+|s|)v(C).                    \tag{2}
\]
This derivation of the triangle determinant bound is valid
even when some of the indicated sets are empty. It gives
actual determinant generators, not a rational diagonalization
of the polynomial module.

## 4. Apply to the character rows and assemble them

Act on \(N\) by the scalar character \(\xi_e\), forgetting the
\(\delta_v\)-coordinates. A scalar linear form modulo \(P\)
on constant coordinate rows which kills this image gives a
character cocycle obeying the family relations and vanishing
on the \(\delta_v\).
The last rows in Section 1 give
\[
 (\xi_e(g)-1)((y-1)^{-1}c(\gamma)+c(a))=0 .
\]
In particular if \(c(\gamma)=0\), then
\((\xi_e(g)-1)c(a)=0\). These are the precise extra
character-cocycle conditions in FittingBlock.lean, 279--290.
Assume the arithmetic detection bound: every such function
in any of these modules that vanishes on the prescribed
set \(s\) is zero.

First project the row image onto \(s_0\). An annihilating
form defines such a cocycle zero on \(s\), and therefore
is zero on the generator coordinates of \(s_0\).
Since the image contains \(P R^{s_0}\), finite duality
on its constant rows proves it contains each constant
unit row. Thus for each \(e\) choose tied rows having
the unit vectors on \(s_0\) under \(\xi_e\).
They can be made common to all the characters without a
denominator. If \(v\) already has the required \(s_0\)
unit vector \(e_i\) on the earlier characters, and \(m_{e,j}\)
are the unit rows on the next character, replace it by
\[
 v+\sum_{j\in s_0}((e_i)_j-v_j)m_{e,j}.
\]
On the earlier characters all correction coefficients
evaluate to zero; on the new one they correct the desired
coordinates. Denote the resulting common rows by \(\nu_i\).

For character \(e\) eliminate the \(s_0\) coordinates by
these rows, leaving the \(s\)-row module \(K_e\).
Precisely, apply the character to a row, then subtract
its \(s_0\)-coordinates times the respective character
evaluations of \(\nu_i\), and retain the \(s\)-coordinates.
This defines the map \(\Phi\) on constant-coordinate rows
for (2). Its annihilator still consists of the indicated
character cocycles by Section 1. The generator-coordinate
and \(P\)-row properties give the other hypotheses of (2);
the tie polynomials become 1 modulo \(P\), as required.

Let \(Q=(X_j^2:j\in J)\) in \(R\), and let
\(q_e=Q:(\prod_{\operatorname{ty}(j)<e}X_j)\).
Here the order of indices respects their character types.
Apply (2) with \(N'=K_e+q_eR^s\); earlier variables
outside \(J_e\) act by zero because they lie in \(q_e\).
Multilinearity gives
\(F(K_e+q_eR^s)\subset F(K_e)+q_e\). Hence there are
\(d_e\in F(K_e)\), \(z_e\in q_e\) with
\[
 d_e+z_e=\Gamma_e\prod_{j\in J_e}X_j                        \tag{3}
\]
and (2)'s valuation bound.
Expand \(d_e\) as a finite \(R\)-linear combination of
determinants of matrices \(B_{ei}\) of \(s\)-rows.
Each such row lifts to an actual tied row with zero
\(s_0\)-coordinates on \(\xi_e\), by subtracting the
common rows \(\nu_j\) before applying the character.

Assume that for the source separating element \(a\in G\),
\(C\) is divisible by
\(\prod_{e'\ne e}(\xi_e(a)-\xi_{e'}(a))\ne0\) for all \(e\).
Lagrange's numerator polynomial, multiplied by the
integral quotient of \(C\) by this product, gives
\(q^{(e)}\in O[G]\) evaluating to \(C\) on \(\xi_e\)
and zero on the other characters.
For a tuple of choices \(i=(i_e)\), keep the rows \(\nu_j\)
on \(s_0\), and take each of the \(s\)-rows as the sum of
the chosen lifted rows multiplied on the left by \(q^{(e)}\).
Complete by the \(\delta_v\)-rows from Section 1.
The resulting matrix \(M_i\) has actual tied rows and,
by block triangularity,
\[
 \det(\xi_e(M_i))=c_0\det B_{e,i_e},\qquad
 c_0=C^{|s|}\prod_v o_v(1).
\]
There is no cost for the number of initial generators \(s_0\);
the powers of \(C\) depend only on the detecting set.

## 5. Common Ribet element and exact coefficient

For every lift \(x\in\mathcal X\) the scalar inertia
element \(\gamma_x\) with
\(\rho_x(\gamma_x)=y_x1,\ y_x-1\in O^\times\),
and \(\xi_e(\gamma_x)=1\), gives
\[
 k=\prod_{x\in\mathcal X}\frac{y_x-\gamma_x}{y_x-1}.
\]
Choose any product order. It acts as 0 on every main block
and as 1 on every character, since one factor of each
main-block product vanishes. Put
\(B_i=(1-k)B_0+kM_i\), \(b_1=kq^{(0)}\).
All its rows are tied. On the lifts \(b_1\) is \(C\) on
the trivial character and zero on all other summands;
\(b_0\) is zero on that trivial character.

Now suppose the trace ideal in the source Hecke coefficient
algebra contains the traces against all group elements of
the two ties and the \(P\)-multiples of group elements.
Together with the common-action kernel these are the terms
of the tie ideal other than \(I_{\rm aug}b_0\).
Modulo the common-action kernel, the left ideal generated
by the \([g_i]\) contains the whole augmentation ideal,
by our coordinate choice. Thus the hypotheses of
[the Ribet polynomial construction](lsernau-ribet-polynomial.md)
hold for every \(B_i\). It gives a common element multiplying
every \(\det(1-u_x(g))\), and indeed every determinant of
a linear combination of \(u_x(g)-1\), into the trace ideal.
Take the weighted sum over \(i\), using the product of
the weights in the determinant expansions of the \(d_e\).
On the lifts its value is
\[
 f(x)=C c_0^t\det(\rho_x(B_0))\prod_e d_e.
\]
In the quotient \(R/Q\) we may replace \(d_e\) by the
right side of (3) in increasing order: the product of
earlier variables annihilates \(z_e\). Therefore the
coefficient of \(\prod_jX_j\) equals
\[
 d=C c_0^t\det(\rho_\pi(B_0)|_{X=0})\prod_e\Gamma_e(0)
\]
at \(\pi\), and every proper squarefree coefficient is
zero on every lift. In particular, adding (1)--(2),
\[
 v(d)\le b_0^{\rm len}+\sum_e b_e+
 t\sum_v v(o_v(1))+(2t|s|+|J|+1)v(C).                      \tag{4}
\]
This is the uniform valuation inequality for the actual
common Ribet coefficient (RibetLemma.lean, 593--595).
The proof gives all rows and the squarefree coefficient
without a determinant expansion of the full group algebra.

## Remaining arithmetic obligations

The input bounds on \(b_0^{\rm len}\), the \(b_e\), \(C\),
\(|s|\), and \(|J|\) are still the source arithmetic
Selmer, local-defect, tame relation and prime triangle bounds.
In particular (4) does not imply the source's
\(\ell\)-normalized bound
\[
 v_\ell(d)+2v_\ell(\mathrm{chosenGroupOrder})
 \le \mathrm{sel}+2t'\mathrm{ram}+r'\mathrm{loc}
                  +\mathrm{denominatorError}
\]
without those estimates. Nor have we proved the diamond/Frobenius
operator identities for the trace ideal and the root-chain
condition at the actual auxiliary places. Those supply the
integral action of the extracted coefficient on the desired
projection, in TiedTraces.lean and DenominatorBound.lean.
This is a construction and valuation proof of the tied-row
algebra under its exact arithmetic inputs, not yet a proof
of the individual integral automorphic separators.
