# Smith exponents on finite p-adic windows

**Status: proved auxiliary estimate.** This strengthens the polynomial-matrix
window argument in Jimmie Adriazola’s reconstruction of Alpöge’s proof.
It does not close a theta, Selmer, norm or geometric supplier: application
still requires the actual Gram-polynomial identity and its integral
coefficient and determinant bounds.

The [shared manuscript proof](../paper/shared/padic-smith-window.tex),
[exact verifier](../scripts/check_padic_smith_window.py) and
[finite-check certificate](../certificates/padic-smith-window-check.json)
record the result and its verification scope.

## Source and scope

Adriazola's §12, `sec12_loss.tex:689--794`, gives an elementary window
estimate for the Smith exponents of a matrix of binomial polynomials.
Its key idea is useful: perturb an evaluation without changing its
truncated invariant factors, then cap each determinant-root valuation;
at most one sample per root can be exceptionally close.

The exact source is
[the immutable source file](https://github.com/jimjamzola/RH-Alpoge-Reconstruction/blob/aa9c86bf2a7d29b08575641fecea19a2a603e87c/manuscript/sec12_loss.tex),
labels `w1:lem:trunc`, `w1:lem:binshift`, `w1:lem:closest`,
`w1:thm:window`. Its SHA-256 is
`22929e6fd051373261a10e4c8281b4a1e77e50dd853821a39d0e60918c1744c7`; the relevant source range is lines 689–794.

The stated source theorem is valid after a small repair to its proof:
its first sentence says that finitely many arbitrary \(\mathbb C_p\) coefficients
and roots lie in a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\). Arbitrary \(\mathbb C_p\) elements need
not be algebraic over \(\mathbb Q_p\). The proof below works over its valuation
ring directly and therefore keeps the stated coefficient generality.
This is a repairable proof error, not an obstruction to the method.

The strengthening also:

- removes the unnecessary upper restriction on the window length;
- allows progression steps divisible by p and arbitrary distinct
  sampling points in \(\mathbb Z_p\);
- uses the actual determinant degree (and only roots in the unit disk),
  rather than the worst-case product n Delta;
- retains the exact factorial valuation instead of (Y-1)/(p-1);
- works at p=2 and over ramified coefficient fields without changes.

No currently open theta, Selmer, W08 or W10 supplier is claimed closed
by this general lemma. The required Gram-polynomial identification,
coefficient integrality and leading-coefficient control remain actual
application hypotheses.

## Strengthened theorem

Let \(v\) be the p-adic valuation on \(\mathbb C_p\), normalized by
\(v(p)=1\), and put \(\mathcal O=\{x:v(x)\ge0\}\). Let

\[
 A(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\Delta}C_k\binom zk,
 \qquad C_k\in M_n(\mathcal O),
\]

with \(n\ge1\), \(\Delta\ge0\), and nonzero determinant

\[
 f(z)=\ell\prod_{\rho\in R}(z-\rho).
\]

Roots are counted with multiplicity. Write

\[
 R_+=\{\rho\in R:v(\rho)\ge0\},\qquad s=|R_+|,
\]
\[
 c=v(\ell)+\sum_{v(\rho)<0}v(\rho),\qquad
 L=\lfloor\log_p\max(1,\Delta)\rfloor.
\]

The empty-root conventions apply to nonzero constant determinants.
For distinct \(z_1,\ldots,z_Y\in\mathbb Z_p\) define the collision quantity

\[
 C(Z)=\max_k\sum_{j\ne k}v(z_j-z_k).
\]

At each sample \(A(z_j)\) is integral. Its Smith exponents
\(a_1(j)\le\cdots\le a_n(j)\), including infinity for zero diagonal entries,
are well-defined. For every real \(E>0\), let

\[
 b_E(j)=\#\{i:a_i(j)\ge E\}.
\]

**Theorem.**

\[
 E\sum_{j=1}^Y b_E(j)\le Yc+s\{E+L+C(Z)\}.\tag{SW1}
\]

In particular, if \(z_j=t+dj\) for \(0\le j<Y\), with
\(t\in\mathbb Z_p\), \(0\ne d\in\mathbb Z_p\), and \(a=v_p(d)\), then

\[
 C(Z)=(Y-1)a+v_p((Y-1)!),
\]

and

\[
 \frac1Y\sum_j b_E(j)
 \le\frac sY+\frac{c+s\{L+(Y-1)a+v_p((Y-1)!)\}/Y}{E}.
 \tag{SW2}
\]

The right side can be replaced by its minimum with n. There is no
restriction Y<=p^h. A version not requiring root locations replaces
s by r=deg f and c by v(ell) throughout (SW1)--(SW2). This latter
formula follows from the same proof treating all roots as potentially
exceptional, and is useful when only the determinant's leading
coefficient and degree are known.

### Proof

**Smith reduction over the valuation ring.** For a finite matrix, pick a nonzero entry
of least valuation as pivot. Every other entry divided by it is
integral. Integral invertible row and column operations clear its row
and column, and induction diagonalizes the matrix. The valuation of
the ideal generated by k-by-k minors is the sum of the k smallest
finite diagonal valuations, so these valuations are independent of
choices. This proof does not require O to be discrete or Noetherian.

**Truncated determinant mass.** Suppose integral matrices B and B'
differ entrywise by elements of valuation at least E, where E is
rational. Choose q in C_p with v(q)=E. Their submodules

\[
 B\mathcal O^n+q\mathcal O^n=B'\mathcal O^n+q\mathcal O^n
\]

are equal. The determinant ideal of this finitely generated full-rank
module is generated by maximal minors of [B q I_n]. Smith reduction
shows its valuation is sum_i min(a_i(B),E). Hence

\[
 \sum_i\min(a_i(B),E)=\sum_i\min(a_i(B'),E).\tag{SW3}
\]

This is the only truncation invariant the argument needs. It follows
directly from equality of integral submodules, even over O_C_p.

**Binomial perturbation.** If z is in Z_p and v(y)=E+L with E>0,
then for 1<=k<=Delta the polynomial Vandermonde identity and

\[
 \binom yk=\frac yk\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{y-i}{i}
\]

give v(binom(y,k))=v(y)-v_p(k)>=E, since v_p(i)<=L<v(y).
All binom(z,k) lie in Z_p by continuity and density of nonnegative
integers. Thus

\[
 v(A(z+y)_{ab}-A(z)_{ab})\ge E.\tag{SW4}
\]

For Delta=0 the conclusion is automatic.

**Choose the perturbation.** First take E rational and set S=E+L.
Choose q with v(q)=S. For each root rho with v(z-rho)=S, the
requirement

\[
 v(z+qu-\rho)=\min\{v(z-\rho),S\}
\]

excludes at most one nonzero residue class for u. The residue field of
O_C_p is infinite, so one can choose a unit u avoiding all excluded
classes. With y=qu the equality therefore holds for every root; the
same equality is automatic for roots with v(z-rho)!=S. In particular
f(z+y)!=0. Applying (SW3), (SW4), nonnegative Smith exponents, and the
determinant identity gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E b_E(j)&\le\sum_i\min(a_i(j),E)\le v(f(z_j+y))\\
 &=c+\sum_{\rho\in R_+}\min\{v(z_j-\rho),S\}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{SW5}
\]

For roots outside the unit disk v(z_j-rho)=v(rho)<0, which accounts
for c exactly.

**Sum over samples.** For each rho in R_+, choose k maximizing
v(z_k-rho), allowing infinity. The ultrametric inequality implies

\[
 v(z_j-\rho)\le v(z_j-z_k)\qquad(j\ne k):
\]

otherwise v(z_k-rho)=v(z_j-z_k)<v(z_j-rho), contradicting the
choice of k. The k-th capped term is at most S, while the others
sum to at most C(Z). Summing (SW5) proves (SW1) for rational E.
For real E>0 take rational E_m increasing to E. There are finitely
many Smith exponents, so b_{E_m}(j) tends to b_E(j), and passage to
the limit proves the assertion.

For the progression, fixing k gives

\[
 \sum_{j\ne k}v(z_j-z_k)
 =(Y-1)a+v_p(k!)+v_p((Y-1-k)!).
\]

The integrality of binom(Y-1,k) bounds this by
(Y-1)a+v_p((Y-1)!), and equality occurs at k=0 and k=Y-1.
This proves (SW2). To obtain the version with all r roots, apply the
same closest-sample argument to every root; its bound remains true
for a root of negative valuation. QED.

### Recovering and improving the source theorem

For a unit progression step, L<=h-1 whenever Delta<p^h, and
v_p((Y-1)!)<=(Y-1)/(p-1). Thus the degree-r version gives

\[
 \frac1Y\sum_j b_E(j)\le\frac rY+
 \frac{v(\ell)+r/(p-1)+rL/Y-r/\{Y(p-1)\}}{E}.
\]

Under v(ell)+r/(p-1)<=n Lambda_0 and r<=n Delta this implies
Adriazola's printed bound. It is stronger before those relaxations,
and its proof has no use for the printed assumption Y<=p^h.

The p-divisible-step term is essential: A(z)=z, t=0, d=p^a and
1<=E<=a give b_E(j)=1 at every sample. A unit-step estimate applied
without the added (Y-1)a term would be false for large Y.

The number s of roots in the unit disk is sometimes much smaller than
deg f. For p=2 and A(z)=2z-1, the single root is outside Z_2, c=0,
s=0, so (SW1) correctly gives b_E(j)=0 for every sample and E>0.


## Verification

The reproducible rational verifier passed 2,205 inequalities on 50
polynomial matrices at p=2,3,5,7. It computes Smith valuations from
all determinantal minors and uses exact rational arithmetic. The
cases include singular evaluations, p-divisible steps, windows longer
than the old restriction and fractional thresholds. A separate exact
example shows why omitting the step valuation is false. These finite
checks corroborate the proof; they are not a verification of all
coefficient families or a Lean replay.
