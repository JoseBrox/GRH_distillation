# Completion, remaining work and paper length

## What the present evidence measures

The project has a complete source-import atlas and a complete assignment of
the 46 endpoint fields, within the stated local-source scope. Neither is a
complete theorem-dependency audit or a measure of how much mathematics
remains. Import coverage and human proof completion are different quantities.

The current [eleven-package ledger](../blueprint/full-proof-program.md) has:

| Status | Packages | Share of the eleven packages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Written mathematical argument | W01, W07 | 2/11, about 18% |
| Supplied by an adopted cited theorem and its comparisons | W05 | 1/11, about 9% |
| Partial | W03, W04, W06, W09, W10, W11 | 6/11, about 55% |
| Open compatible construction or global estimate | W02, W08 | 2/11, about 18% |

Thus three packages, about 27%, are marked written or supplied by a cited
input. This is an **unweighted milestone count**, not “27% of a proof.”
The packages have different difficulty, share hypotheses, and are not
independent. A written implication can still require an unresolved input.
The new scale, degree and energy reductions improve several partial
packages without making their substantive suppliers complete.

Ordinary RH has a smaller analytic interface, but the present construction
retains the difficult growing-field, integral, geometric and global-norm
problems. The [RH frontier audit](rh-construction-frontier.md) checks this
against the actual source clauses. Four surviving characters do not make
the integral or geometric problem fixed-dimensional.

## Completion percentages and prompts

There is no defensible numerical estimate of the fraction of the independent
RH or Dirichlet-GRH proof completed. Reporting, for example, “80% proved”
would assign an unsupported size to the missing compatible construction.
Likewise, no finite number of further prompts can currently be forecast as
sufficient to complete either proof. The central estimates may admit a
short replacement, may require substantial additional work, or may expose
an obstruction to this particular reconstruction.

This uncertainty is specific to proof closure. The next research tasks are
concrete and can be completed or rejected one by one:

1. Supply the actual mean-volume and explicit section-rank/surplus estimate
   for the prescribed lattices and metrics. The new short-section argument
   removes a separate negative-height hypothesis.
2. Supply the centered global theta-energy estimate with the actual positive
   normalizers and common target. The new scalar-energy interface avoids
   requiring a separate estimate for every pair of words.
3. Complete the remaining integral congruence, divisibility and compatibility
   requirements and control the combined discrepancy. The final criterion
   permits a weighted average over scales and a fixed local logarithmic
   average before taking the positive part.

A completion forecast should be revisited when those suppliers, rather than
only their acceptance criteria, have been proved. The fact that a prompt can
contain several parallel research investigations makes prompt counts an
especially unstable unit of work.

## A provisional page budget

The last fully integrated checkpoint before these new results had a
seven-page didactic guide, a 21-page analytic companion and a 573-page
working manuscript. These were measured PDF lengths. The long manuscript
preserves detailed arguments and alternatives; it is not a finished short
paper and its length is not a lower bound for one.

For planning the eventual fully argued papers, a **very low-confidence
editorial budget**, conditional on completing and substantially compressing
the present route, is:

| Deliverable | Provisional space budget |
| --- | --- |
| Complete ordinary-RH argument | roughly 100–200 pages, including necessary technical appendices |
| Complete Dirichlet-GRH argument | roughly 150–300 pages, including necessary technical appendices |
| Didactic overview | keep the existing guide within its twelve-page limit; it currently fits in seven |

These ranges are judgments about exposition, not predicted proof lengths or
mathematical bounds. A major replacement of the arithmetic construction
could make them much smaller; unresolved technical details could make them
larger. They are not a reason to omit a necessary argument or hide it as an
unproved lemma. A paper of around twenty pages remains an attractive goal,
but no current reduction justifies predicting a complete proof at that
length. The analytic reduction itself is already much shorter than that;
the missing arithmetic construction determines the final length.
