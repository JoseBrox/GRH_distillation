## 2. Four retained characters can have an arbitrarily large integral index

**Proposition.** Let \(A\) be any finite group of order \(n\ge1\), let
\(Q=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), and give
\(L=\mathbb Z[Q\times A]\) its standard orthonormal basis. Let
\(P=n^{-1}\sum_{a\in A}a\) act by translation in the second factor.
Put \(L_+=L\cap\operatorname{im}P\) and
\(L_-=L\cap\ker P\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{rank}L_+=4,\qquad
 L/(L_+\oplus L_-)\simeq(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^4,
 \qquad [L:L_+\oplus L_-]=n^4.                         \tag{2.1}
\]
For abelian \(A\), \(\operatorname{im}P\) is exactly the sum of the
four sign-character spaces of \(Q\times A\).

**Proof.** Decompose the lattice into its four \(Q\)-fibres. In one
fibre identify \(\mathbb Z[A]\) with \(\mathbb Z^n\). Its invariant
lattice is generated by \(v=(1,\ldots,1)\), and its orthogonal kernel
is the lattice of vectors with coordinate sum zero. The homomorphism
\(z\mapsto\sum z_i\pmod n\) is onto. Its kernel is exactly the sum
of those two lattices: if \(\sum z_i=kn\), then \(z-kv\) has sum zero.
Thus each fibre contributes \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Character
orthogonality identifies the retained rational spaces when \(A\) is
abelian. \(\square\)

For the RH orders \(n=2^e\), (2.1) consists entirely of 2-primary
torsion and its order is \(2^{4e}\). The retained rank is fixed while
the integral index is unbounded. After inverting 2 the defect vanishes;
that rational or odd-prime simplification cannot be substituted for an
integral bound at 2. This is an exact finite-lattice counterexample to a
purported implication, not a claim that the source's actual section
lattice has this particular defect. The actual normalized defect may be
small, but must be bounded.

## 3. An optional comparison with only four analytic characters

The direct route needs a thin estimate because the actual thin terms
remain in its arithmetic identity. There is another exact way to remove
them: compare two constructions whose thin data agree. This trades an
analytic requirement for an additional arithmetic compatibility theorem.

Retain \(G=Q\times A\), the common top set \(T\), orders \(a_t\),
stages \(1\le i\le m\), thick sets, and normalization
\[
 n=|A|,\quad f=2n,\quad d=|S|/n>0,\quad
 k=|T|mbd/2>0.
\]
Fix \(\lambda>1\). Suppose two actual arithmetic balances have the
same host, characters, orders and indexing. At every thin place their
weights agree exactly. At a thick place their weights are respectively
\(v\) and \(\lambda v\), where \(b\le v\le b+r\), and assume all
sampled arguments are at least one. Write their averaged discrepancies
as \(\overline E_1,\overline E_2\), and define
\[
       \Delta_\lambda F(y)=F(y)-\lambda F(y/\lambda).
\]

**Relative transfer.** Under precisely these hypotheses,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac1{|T|}\sum_t\Delta_\lambda D_+(a_t/b)
 \le\frac{\overline E_1-\overline E_2}{k}
     +4C_\Psi\frac rb\frac{\overline a}{b}.
 \quad}                                                   \tag{3.1}
\]
No thin-character bound or weight symmetry is used.

**Proof.** For the positive full kernel in the direct-transfer note,
\[
 K_{g,a}(w)-K_{g,a}(\lambda w)
 =\sum_n\Lambda(n)\kappa_g(n)
       \bigl[(a/n-w)_+-(a/n-\lambda w)_+\bigr]-a\log\lambda.
\]
All nonprincipal constants cancel. For \(z\ge0\), the function
\(h_z(w)=(z-w)_+-(z-\lambda w)_+\) has slopes
\(\lambda-1,-1,0\), so
\(h_z(v)\ge h_z(b)-(v-b)\mathbf1_{\{z\ge b\}}\) for \(v\ge b\).
The full prime coefficients are nonnegative, including at ramified
primes. Therefore the kernel difference at \(v\) is at least its
difference at \(b\), minus \(r\Psi_g(a/b)\).
The two thin contributions cancel exactly. Summing translations before
taking bounds leaves exactly the four sign characters, by the same
orthogonality calculation as the direct transfer. Its normalized prime
error is at most \(4C_\Psi(r/b)(\overline a/b)\). This proves (3.1).
\(\square\)

Thus the analytic expression is
\(\Delta_\lambda G_1-\Delta_\lambda C_{q,d}\). Both companion
arguments must be covered by their actual estimates. If
\(|a_t/b-x|\le1\) and \(x\ge\lambda+1\), the established elementary
derivative bound gives a centering cost at most
\(2(1+\log(x+1))^2\): the factor \(\lambda\) cancels the shorter
distance \(|a_t/b-x|/\lambda\) in the second sample.

This comparison does not follow from two separate upper bounds for the
discrepancies. Even \(\overline E_1\le0\) and
\(\overline E_2\le0\) leave their difference unbounded above: take
\(\overline E_1=0\), \(\overline E_2=-M\). A signed **relative**
geometric estimate is needed, or a suitable lower bound on the second
discrepancy. Also, the unchanged source does not supply two constructions
with identical thin data and scaled thick weights. Its exponential thin
scale depends on its construction parameter. Equation (3.1) is an
optional new sufficient interface, not a completed replacement supplier.

### Why the dilation difference still detects RH

For a fixed \(\lambda>1\), a one-sided eventual bound
\[
 \Delta_\lambda G_1(x)\le C_\delta x^{1/2+\delta}
 \quad(x\ge X_\delta)
 \quad\text{for every }\delta>0
                                                               \tag{3.2}
\]
implies RH, using the standard zero-free boundary of the open critical
strip as in the guide. Indeed, if
\(F(s)=\int_1^\infty G_1(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx\),
then initially for \(\Re s>1\),
\[
 \int_\lambda^\infty\Delta_\lambda G_1(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx
 =(1-\lambda^{1-s})F(s)
       -\int_1^\lambda G_1(x)x^{-s-1}\,dx.                 \tag{3.3}
\]
The last term is entire. At every zeta zero \(\rho\) with
\(0<\Re\rho<1\), the multiplier is nonzero, since
\(|\lambda^{1-\rho}|>1\). There are no real singularities above
\(1/2\), by the same real nonvanishing and pole subtraction used in
the proved one-sided zeta criterion. For \(\theta=1/2+\delta<1\),
apply its nonnegative-Mellin argument to
\(C_\delta x^\theta-\Delta_\lambda G_1(x)\) on the eventual tail.
It forces holomorphy on \(\Re s>\theta\), contradicting any surviving
zero pole there. Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\), and using the zeta
functional equation, proves RH. Thus (3.1) has a genuine analytic target;
its obstacle is the new paired arithmetic construction.

## 4. What can honestly be measured

The import atlas and assignment of all 46 endpoint fields are complete
within their explicitly documented scope. They measure organization and
source coverage. They do not measure the fraction of the mathematical
proof reconstructed, and the fields are not independent equal-sized
tasks. Several analytic implications, finite-character reductions and
local or lattice lemmas are proved; the central compatible construction
and its uniform geometric, integral and global norm bounds remain open.

Consequently no evidence-based numerical completion percentage or finite
number of remaining prompts is available for either the independent RH
proof or the independent Dirichlet-GRH proof. RH has a smaller final
analytic interface, but its present arithmetic supplier shares the hard
construction. A newly found shortcut could greatly reduce it; a difficult
remaining estimate could require substantial further research.

At this checkpoint the compiled guide has 7 pages, the analytic companion
21 pages, and the complete working manuscript 573 pages (measured with
`pdfinfo`, not extrapolated from TeX line counts). The guide's editorial
limit is 12 pages. None of those measurements predicts the length of a
future completed proof: the missing constructions may need substantial
space, and a successful replacement could remove existing material.
The two-page analytic implication is already short; it does not contain
the still-missing arithmetic estimate.
