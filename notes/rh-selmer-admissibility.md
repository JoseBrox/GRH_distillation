# Admissibility from the four small weights

## Result and scope

The admissibility needed by the graded Koszul comparison follows from the
actual four-weight generation, negative cotangent spanning, finite
degree-zero quotient, fixed-point separation, and trace separation.
The proof below reconstructs the point-classification argument using
even-degree subalgebras and elementary field-valued points. It does not
assume a regular sequence, flat deformation ring, or a point-lifting
theorem without checking its hypotheses.

The marked arithmetic model and the stated geometric hypotheses remain
inputs. This closes the admissibility supplier within that model; it
does not prove those arithmetic inputs or a full RH/GRH node.

## 1. Data and hypotheses

Let \(\mathcal O\) be a characteristic-zero discrete valuation ring,
\(F\) its fraction field, and \(K\) an algebraically closed extension
of \(F\). Let \(A=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb Z}A_j\) be an augmented
graded \(\mathcal O\)-algebra, \(x:A\to\mathcal O\), with
\(B=A_0\) Noetherian local. Assume \(A\) is generated over \(B\) by
finitely many homogeneous elements of weights \(-2,-1,1,2\), and
\(x(A_j)=0\) for \(j\ne0\).

Let \(Q\subset A\) be the homogeneous ideal generated by

\[
F_1,\ldots,F_r,F_*\in A_{-1},\quad
G_1,\ldots,G_s\in A_{-2},\quad
E_1,\ldots,E_{r'}\in A_1,\quad
E'_1,\ldots,E'_{s'}\in A_2,\quad Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\in\ker(x|_B).
\]

The assumptions are the following.

1. \(B/(Q\cap B)\) is finite over \(\mathcal O\).
2. For \(I=\ker x\), the classes of the \(F_i,F_*\) span
   \((I/I^2)_{-1}\otimes_{\mathcal O}F\), and the \(G_j\) span
   \((I/I^2)_{-2}\otimes_{\mathcal O}F\).
3. At any \(K\)-point of \(A/Q\) that kills every nonzero-weight
   element and differs from \(x_K\), its degree-zero character cannot
   be shared by both a nontrivial negative-only point and a nontrivial
   positive-only point of \(A/Q\).
4. \(a\in B\), \(x(a)=0\), and \(\xi(a)\ne0\) at every point
   \(\xi:A/Q\to K\) with some nonzero positive-weight value and some
   nonzero negative-weight value.

Here a negative-only point kills every positive-weight element and has
at least one nonzero negative-weight value; positive-only means the
reverse. These are exactly the source's spanning, finite quotient,
fixed-point separation, and trace-separation contracts. Passing from the
saturated cotangent lattice to assumption 2 is legitimate after
inverting the nonzero elements of \(\mathcal O\).

As before, define

\[
N_0=A_{-2},\quad N_1=A_2,\quad
N_2=BF_*^2+A_{-2}^2A_2,\qquad
\mathcal R=\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol d\ge0}
N_0^{d_0}N_1^{d_1}N_2^{d_2}t^{\boldsymbol d}.
\]

Let \(\boldsymbol s\) be the sections
\(F_i^2t_0,G_jt_0,E_k^2t_1,E'_lt_1,F_*^2t_2\), with \(i=1,\ldots,r\).
Write \(I_{\mathrm{irr}}\) for the product of the three coordinate-degree
ideals of \(\mathcal R\).

**Theorem.** For any ideal \(J\subset\mathcal R\) containing the
degree-zero images of all \(Z_i\), there is an integer \(N\ge0\) such
that

\[
\boxed{(\mathfrak m_B\mathcal R I_{\mathrm{irr}})^N
\subset J+(\boldsymbol s,a).} \tag{1}
\]

## 2. The even halves and their points

For every \(n\ge0\),

\[
A_{-2n}=(A_{-2})^n,\qquad A_{2n}=(A_2)^n, \tag{2}
\]

where products denote \(B\)-submodule products. To prove the negative
identity, consider a monomial in the chosen generators of total weight
\(-2n\). Remove pairs of opposite equal weights; their products belong
to \(B\). If both signs remain, their weights are either \(-2,1\)
or \(-1,2\). In the former case, pair two weight-one generators with
one weight-minus-two generator to obtain weight zero; in the latter,
pair two weight-minus-one generators with one weight-two generator.
The remaining monomial is purely negative and has even total weight,
so its weight-minus-one factors pair up. This factors it into a
weight-zero coefficient and \(n\) weight-minus-two blocks. The positive
identity is symmetric.

This also proves that \(A_{-2}\) is generated over \(B\) by the chosen
weight-minus-two generators and products of two chosen weight-minus-one
generators; similarly for \(A_2\). Thus \(N_0,N_1,N_2\) are finite
\(B\)-modules, and \(\mathcal R\) is standard and finitely generated
over the Noetherian ring \(B\).

Put \(\mathfrak d=A_{-2}A_2\subset B\). Suppose a character
\(\sigma:B\to K\) kills \(\mathfrak d\). For any opposite-weight
pair \(u\in A_j,v\in A_{-j}\), \(j\ne0\), (2) gives

\[
(uv)^2\in\mathfrak d^{|j|},\qquad \sigma(uv)=0. \tag{3}
\]

Consequently the map that equals \(\sigma\) in weight zero and is zero
in every other weight is an algebra homomorphism \(\xi_0:A\to K\).

We also need the following precise extension of a half-algebra point.
Let \(V^-=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}A_{-2n}\). Any character
\(\rho^-:V^-\to K\) restricting to \(\sigma\) extends to a point
\(\xi^-:A\to K\) that kills every positive-weight element.
Indeed, it kills the intersection of \(V^-\) with the ideal generated
by all positive-weight elements. For a homogeneous product
\(uv\in A_{-2n}\) with \(v\in A_j\), \(j>0\), (2) shows

\[
u^2v^2\in (A_{-2})^{2n+j}(A_2)^j
\subset \mathfrak d^j(A_{-2})^{2n}.
\]

Its value under \(\rho^-\) is zero, so \(\rho^-(uv)=0\).
Taking homogeneous components proves the assertion for the whole ideal.
The resulting map on the even subalgebra of
\(A^-:=A/(A_{>0})\) extends to \(A^-\), since that ring is finite
integral over its even subalgebra: the finitely many remaining odd
generators each satisfy \(T^2-z^2\). Lying over and algebraic closure
give the extension into \(K\). The same proof works with the signs
reversed. Existing nonzero values in the even half are preserved.

## 3. A relevant Rees prime kills the degree-zero equations

Let \(\mathfrak p\subset\mathcal R\) be a prime containing
\(J+(\boldsymbol s,a)\), with
\(I_{\mathrm{irr}}\not\subset\mathfrak p\), and set
\(P=\mathfrak p\cap B\). Choose \(n_i\in N_i\) with
\(n_it_i\notin\mathfrak p\), for \(i=0,1,2\).
Since \(N_2\subset A_{-2}\), the identity

\[
(F_*^2t_0)(n_2t_2)=(F_*^2t_2)(n_2t_0)
\]

shows that \(F_*^2t_0\in\mathfrak p\). Thus all negative weight-one
equations, including the last one, have their squares killed in the
first Rees direction.

For \(F\in A_{-1}\) among these equations and \(h\in A_1\),

\[
(hF)^2(n_0t_0)=(F^2t_0)(h^2n_0).
\]

Primality gives \(hF\in P\). For \(G\in A_{-2}\) and
\(h\in A_2\), use
\((hG)(n_0t_0)=(Gt_0)(hn_0)\).
The analogous identities with \(n_1t_1\) handle the positive
equations. The \(Z_i\) already lie in \(P\). Projecting any expression
in the homogeneous ideal \(Q\) onto weight zero therefore proves

\[
Q\cap B\subset P. \tag{4}
\]

Assumption 1 makes \(B/P\) finite over \(\mathcal O\). If
\(P\cap\mathcal O\ne0\), this domain is finite over the residue field
of \(\mathcal O\), so it is a field and \(P=\mathfrak m_B\).
Otherwise \(B/P\) has fraction field finite over \(F\), which embeds
in \(K\). We obtain a character \(\sigma:B\to K\) with kernel \(P\).

In this characteristic-zero case there is also a character
\(\theta:\mathcal R\to K\) extending \(\sigma\), killing
\(\mathfrak p\), with \(\theta(n_it_i)\ne0\) for every \(i\).
To check this assertion, localize the domain \(\mathcal R/\mathfrak p\)
at the nonzero elements of \(B/P\), extend that fraction field to
\(K\), and invert the nonzero product \(\prod_i n_it_i\).
The resulting finite-type \(K\)-algebra is nonzero: localization is
injective on the domain and field extension is faithfully flat, so no
power of that product becomes zero. A maximal ideal then has residue
field \(K\) by the weak Nullstellensatz. This gives exactly the stated
\(\theta\), without an unproved lifting of a Rees prime to an \(A\)-point.

## 4. Excluding the characteristic-zero contraction

First suppose \(\sigma(\mathfrak d)\ne0\). The algebra

\[
(A/Q)\otimes_{B,\sigma}K
\]

is nonzero and finite type over \(K\). Its degree-zero summand is
\(K\), by (4), so nonzeroness is explicit. The weak Nullstellensatz
gives a point \(\xi:A/Q\to K\) restricting to \(\sigma\).
Some product of an element of \(A_{-2}\) and an element of \(A_2\)
has nonzero value, so \(\xi\) is two-sided. But
\(\xi(a)=\sigma(a)=0\), contradicting assumption 4.

It remains to consider \(\sigma(\mathfrak d)=0\). By (2), the map
\(f\mapsto ft_0^n\), for \(f\in A_{-2n}\), identifies \(V^-\)
with the first-axis subalgebra of \(\mathcal R\). Restricting
\(\theta\) gives a character \(\rho^-\) of \(V^-\) that is nonzero
on \(n_0\). The extension in section 2 gives a negative-only point
\(\xi^-\) of \(A\). It kills the negative equations because
\(\theta(F_i^2t_0)=\theta(F_*^2t_0)=\theta(G_jt_0)=0\), kills the
positive equations by construction, and kills the \(Z_i\) through
\(\sigma\). Thus it is a point of \(A/Q\). The positive axis similarly
gives a positive-only point \(\xi^+\) of \(A/Q\). Both restrict to
\(\sigma\), as does the fixed point \(\xi_0\) from (3).
The fixed point also kills \(Q\): its nonzero-weight values vanish and
\(\sigma(Z_i)=0\).

If \(\xi_0\ne x_K\), assumption 3 excludes these two points.
If \(\xi_0=x_K\), the negative-only point \(\xi^-\) kills every
nonnegative-weight element of the augmentation ideal. Work over \(F\)
to use assumption 2. In weight \(-1\), every product in \(I_F^2\)
has a factor of nonnegative weight, hence evaluates to zero. Cotangent
spanning by the \(F_i,F_*\), which also evaluate to zero, therefore
forces \(\xi^-(A_{-1})=0\). In weight \(-2\), a product in
\(I_F^2\) either has a nonnegative-weight factor or has two weight
\(-1\) factors. Both possibilities now evaluate to zero. Spanning by
the \(G_j\) forces \(\xi^-(A_{-2})=0\), contradicting its nonzero
value on \(n_0\). This is just a two-degree argument; no formal
smoothness or completed local Nakayama theorem is needed.

Every prime \(\mathfrak p\) containing \(J+(\boldsymbol s,a)\)
therefore contains either \(I_{\mathrm{irr}}\) or
\(\mathfrak m_B\mathcal R\). Consequently
\(\mathfrak m_B\mathcal R I_{\mathrm{irr}}\) lies in the radical of
that ideal. The ring \(\mathcal R\) is Noetherian, so finite generation
of this product ideal gives a single power contained in
\(J+(\boldsymbol s,a)\). This proves (1).
