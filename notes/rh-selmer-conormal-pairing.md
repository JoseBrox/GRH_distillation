# The strict Selmer group embeds in a finite conormal dual

## Result and scope

For a fixed marked deformation model, the lower comparison can be
strengthened from a length inequality to an injective linear map

\[
 S\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(T^-,K/\mathcal O).
 \tag{1}
\]

Here \(S\) is the actual strict Selmer module, \(K\) is the fraction
field of its coefficient discrete valuation ring \(\mathcal O\), and
\(T^-\) is the negative-weight conormal quotient defined below. The
map is canonical **relative to the fixed marking and ambient model**.
It is independent of cohomology representatives, annihilators, local
coboundary choices, integral derivative lifts, and generators of the
equation ideal. No independence under changing the marking or ambient
model is asserted.

This is a complete lower-comparison proof, conditional on the explicitly
stated deformation-model inputs. It does not prove their existence or
the upper graded-intersection estimate. Those suppliers, including the
arithmetic rigidity and automorphy inputs, remain in place. It closes
no additional existing proof-map node by itself.

The important extra conclusions are bounds for every finite torsion
level, the exponent, and each elementary divisor of \(S\). Merely
knowing its total length would not give these conclusions.

## 1. The correct finite module and the order of the quotients

Let \(A=\bigoplus_j A_j\) be an augmented graded \(\mathcal O\)-algebra,
with augmentation \(x:A\to\mathcal O\) zero on nonzero weights. Write
\(I=\ker x\), and define

\[
 I^{(2)}=\{f\in A:\text{some }0\ne a\in\mathcal O
                         \text{ satisfies }af\in I^2\}.
\]

This is a homogeneous ideal contained in \(I\). The module
\(C=I/I^{(2)}\) is the ordinary conormal \(I/I^2\) with its
\(\mathcal O\)-torsion removed. Let \(J\subset I\) be the homogeneous
ideal defining the graded part of the global deformation ring. Set

\[
 C^-=C_{-1},\qquad
 T^-=(I/(I^{(2)}+J))_{-1}
     =C^-/\operatorname{image}(J_{-1}).                 \tag{2}
\]

Assume \(C^-\) is finite free and that the image of \(J_{-1}\)
spans \(C^-\otimes K\). Then \(T^-\) is a finite torsion module.
If \(J\) is generated by homogeneous elements vanishing at the
augmentation, its image in \(C\) is generated by their linear parts:
modulo \(I^{(2)}\), a product \(bf\), with \(f\in J\), equals
\(x(b)f\). Consequently only the equations of weight \(-1\)
contribute to (2). In the prescribed model these are precisely its
\(r+1\) negative equations, and \(\operatorname{rank}C^-=r\).

This quotient is independent of the chosen homogeneous generators and
of coordinates on the fixed augmented pair \((A,J)\). It is also
unchanged by adjoining a graded polynomial variable together with the
equation setting that variable to zero: the new conormal summand and
the new equation cancel. The assertion is not invariance under arbitrary
replacement of the ambient model.

The order in (2) is essential. For \(B=A/J\), its raw conormal is
\(I/(I^2+J)\), and (2) is its weight \(-1\) quotient by the image
of the **ambient** conormal torsion \(I^{(2)}/I^2\). It is not the
torsion-free conormal of \(B\). The new torsion produced by the
equations is exactly what must be retained.

For example, on a free conormal with two generators, equations with
linear coefficient matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\pi^2&\pi&0\\0&\pi^4&\pi^5\end{pmatrix}
\]

give \(T^-\simeq\mathcal O/(\pi)\oplus\mathcal O/(\pi^5)\).
Removing torsion after imposing these equations would erase the entire
quantity being measured.

## 2. The elementary lower comparison

Let the residue field have size \(q\), let \(0\ne a\in\mathcal O\),
and suppose an additive injection associates to each element \(s\) of
an \(\mathcal O\)-module \(M\) an \(\mathcal O\)-linear map
\(D_s:A\to\mathcal O/a\). Assume that each \(D_s\) is the reduction
of an integral derivation at \(x\) supported in weight \(-1\), and
that \(D_s(J)=0\). An integral derivation kills \(I^2\), and then
kills \(I^{(2)}\), because its target \(\mathcal O\) has no torsion.
Thus evaluation defines an additive injection

\[
 M\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(T^-,\mathcal O/a).
 \tag{3}
\]

Injectivity follows because the derivatives vanish in every other
weight. If the original association is linear, (3) is linear as well.
Writing \(a=u\pi^k\) and
\(T^-\simeq\bigoplus_i\mathcal O/(\pi^{c_i})\), cardinalities give

\[
 \operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}M
 \le\sum_i\min(k,c_i)\le\sum_i c_i.                 \tag{4}
\]

Indeed, the Hom group has cardinality
\(q^{\sum_i\min(k,c_i)}\). The injection first makes \(M\) a finite
set, and every finite \(\mathcal O\)-module has cardinality \(q\) to
its length. No linearity of the injection is needed for this numerical
claim. If the conormal quotient has infinite length, the unrefined
length inequality is tautological. This proves the full abstract lower
length contract and its sharper finite-exponent form.

### Why the integral lifts exist in the prescribed model

The ambient completed ring is a formal power-series extension of the
local deformation algebra:

\[
 \widehat A=B[[z_1,\ldots,z_m]].                      \tag{5}
\]

It has a homogeneous presentation, and \(A\) is the algebra of finite
sums of homogeneous elements. The local and global base points agree.
Suppose a modular derivative on the global ring has a compatible
integral derivative \(d_B\) on \(B\), both supported in weight
\(-1\). Choose lifts in \(\mathcal O\) of its values on the new
variables of weight \(-1\), and choose zero for every other weight.
These values and \(d_B\) define the required derivative on (5).

For a presentation \(B=\mathcal O[[u_1,\ldots,u_t]]/J_B\), the
formula on a series \(f\) is simply

\[
 d(f)=\sum_i [u_i]f\,d_B(u_i)
                  +\sum_j[z_j]f\,d(z_j).             \tag{6}
\]

Here \([u_i]f,[z_j]f\in\mathcal O\) are its linear coefficients;
all variables vanish at the augmentation. The product rule follows
from the linear coefficient of a product. The derivative kills the
extended ideal \(J_B\), since it restricts to \(d_B\) and all
relations have augmentation zero. It therefore descends. Equality
after reduction follows on the variables and hence on every first
jet; terms of order at least two are killed. The weight assertion is
immediate from (6). This proof imposes no torsion-free hypothesis on
the local conormal: the local integral derivative is the necessary
input, and its existence is checked next.

## 3. A compatible pairing on strict cohomology

Let \(\Gamma\) be profinite, \(\alpha:\Gamma\to\mathcal O^\times\)
continuous, and

\[
 S=\ker\left(H^1(\Gamma,(K/\mathcal O)(\alpha))
       \longrightarrow\prod_v H^1(\Gamma_v,(K/\mathcal O)(\alpha))
       \right).                                      \tag{7}
\]

Retain the actual marked-model hypotheses. In particular there are
marks \(c,g_1\) with \(c^2=1\), \(\alpha(c)=1\), and
\(\alpha(g_1)-1\in\mathcal O^\times\), and the residue characteristic
is odd. For the local group \(H\), one of the groups in (7), used
in (5), require an element
\(h_0\in H\) with \(\alpha(h_0)-1\in\mathcal O^\times\).
Each prescribed element to be killed belongs to one of the groups in
(7) and has \(\alpha\)-value one. These are actual good-character
conditions, not consequences of abstract Selmer finiteness.

The universal global ring represents the prescribed marked rank-three
deformation problem and has base point \(\rho_0\). Its fixed continuous
quadratic character is \(\eta\), with \(\eta(c)=-1\) and
\(\eta(g_1)=1\), and

\[
 \rho_0(g)=\bigl(\operatorname{diag}(\Psi(g),1,\Psi(g)^{-1}),
                    \eta(g)\bigr),\qquad\Psi=\alpha^{-1},
\]

in \(\mathrm{GL}_3\rtimes\{\pm1\}\), where \(-1\) acts by
\(U\mapsto J_3U^{-t}J_3\) and \(J_3\) reverses the basis. The
local condition contains the base point and is stable under
conjugation by a matrix congruent to one. The ambient model has (5),
maps to this universal ring, and contains homogeneous preimages of
all universal matrix entries. Its weighted coordinates are compatible
with these entries: at the base point, the derivative of each ambient
coordinate is an \(\mathcal O\)-linear combination of entry derivatives
of the same weight. The local coordinate system has the same property.
These model-existence and local
conditions are retained suppliers.

### Unique normalized representatives

Every cohomology class \(s\) has a unique cocycle representative
vanishing at \(g_1\). If \(z\) is any representative, it is

\[
 \nu_s(g)=z(g)-(\alpha(g)-1)
                         (\alpha(g_1)-1)^{-1}z(g_1). \tag{8}
\]

Changing \(z\) by a coboundary changes the two terms equally.
Conversely, a coboundary vanishing at \(g_1\) is zero, since the
displayed scalar is a unit. Hence \(s\mapsto\nu_s\) is an injective
\(\mathcal O\)-linear map, and its formation commutes with coefficient
maps. If \(as=0\), then \(a\nu_s=0\).

Use the coefficient isomorphism

\[
 \iota_a:(K/\mathcal O)[a]\longrightarrow\mathcal O/a,
 \qquad z+\mathcal O\longmapsto az\pmod a.            \tag{9}
\]

This uses a representative \(z\in K\); it is not multiplication
inside \(K/\mathcal O\), which would give zero. Put
\(t_{a,s}=\iota_a\nu_s\), and set

\[
 L(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\t&0&0\\0&-t&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \gamma_{a,s}(g)=L(t_{a,s}(g)).                \tag{10}
\]

Direct multiplication gives
\(\operatorname{Ad}(\rho_0(g))L(t)=L(\alpha(g)t)\), including
the component \(\eta(g)=-1\), because
\(-J_3L(t)^tJ_3=L(t)\). Thus (10) is an adjoint cocycle of
weight \(-1\). Over the square-zero ring
\(\mathcal O\oplus(\mathcal O/a)\varepsilon\), it gives the
deformation \((1+\varepsilon\gamma_{a,s}(g))\rho_0(g)\).

The marking holds: \(\nu_s(c)=0\), since \(2\nu_s(c)=0\), and
\(\nu_s(g_1)=\nu_s(cg_1^{-1}c)=0\). In particular the required
two off-diagonal entries of the difference at \(g_1\) and
\(cg_1^{-1}c\) vanish. At every prescribed killed element, strict
local triviality and \(\alpha(g)=1\) give \(\nu_s(g)=0\).

On \(H\), strictness gives \(\nu_s(h)=(\alpha(h)-1)m\).
Because \(a\nu_s=0\) and \(\alpha(h_0)-1\) is a unit, necessarily
\(am=0\). Thus the same modulus \(a\), with no enlargement,
trivializes this restriction. Let \(m_a=\iota_a(m)\). The local
cocycle is the coboundary of \(-L(m_a)\), with convention
\(X-\operatorname{Ad}(\rho_0(h))X\). Lift \(-L(m_a)\) integrally;
conjugating the integral base point by \(1+\varepsilon Y\) gives
an integral local deformation. Conjugation stability supplies its
point of \(B\), and hence the compatible derivative \(d_B\) used
in (6). Continuity follows from continuity of \(\rho_0\) and the
constant matrix \(Y\).

### Definition and independence of the pairing

Universality gives a unique derivation \(D_{a,s}\) on the global
ring at its base point, obtained from (10). Pull it back to \(A\).
Its entry derivatives have weight \(-1\); the stated coordinate
compatibility gives this same support on the ambient and local
coordinates.
The preceding local construction and (6) show that it is the reduction
of an integral weight \(-1\) derivative. It therefore kills
\(I^{(2)}\); it kills \(J\) because the latter maps to zero in
the global ring. For \(f\in I\) of weight \(-1\), define

\[
 \langle s,[f]\rangle
   =\iota_a^{-1}\bigl(D_{a,s}(f)\bigr)\in K/\mathcal O,
                     \qquad as=0.                   \tag{11}
\]

We have absorbed the ambient-to-global map into the notation for
\(D_{a,s}\). Equation (11) factors through (2).

If \(a\) is replaced by \(ab\), the coefficient map
\(\mathcal O/a\to\mathcal O/(ab)\) is \(u\mapsto bu\), and
\(t_{ab,s}=bt_{a,s}\) under this map. The corresponding square-zero
algebra map sends the first deformation to the second. By the natural
universal property,

\[
 D_{ab,s}=bD_{a,s}\pmod {ab}.                         \tag{12}
\]

Dividing by \(ab\) in (11) gives exactly the old value. Two arbitrary
annihilators are compared through their product. This proves
independence of the annihilator and of a uniformizer. A common
annihilator for two classes, together with (8), (10), and uniqueness
of the universal derivative, proves linearity in \(s\). Linearity
in \([f]\) follows from the derivative.

No local coboundary or integral derivative lift occurs in (11): they
prove only that its fixed modular value kills \(I^{(2)}\). Thus
their possible nonuniqueness causes no ambiguity. Cohomology
representatives disappeared in (8), and equation generators disappeared
in (2). This proves all the claimed independence assertions.

If (11) is zero for a class \(s\), then \(D_{a,s}\) is zero on
\(A_{-1}\). It is already zero on every other weight, by (6).
Every universal matrix entry has a homogeneous preimage in \(A\),
so its derivative is zero. Multiplication by the invertible base
matrix then gives \(\gamma_{a,s}=0\). Since \(L\), \(\iota_a\),
and the normalized cocycle map are injective, \(s=0\). This proves
(1), without choosing unrelated injections at the different levels.

Every continuous cocycle into \(K/\mathcal O\) has finite image,
because \(\Gamma\) is compact and the coefficient module is discrete;
some nonzero \(a\) annihilates that image. Thus (11) is defined on
all of \(S\), without assuming its finiteness in advance.

## 4. Consequences and the remaining upper estimate

If \(T^-\simeq\bigoplus_i\mathcal O/(\pi^{c_i})\), (1) gives

\[
 \operatorname{length}S[\pi^k]\le\sum_i\min(k,c_i),\qquad
 \operatorname{length}S\le\sum_i c_i,\qquad
 \pi^{\max_i c_i}S=0.                                \tag{13}
\]

When \(T^-=0\), the last assertion means \(S=0\). Moreover, the
number of elementary divisors of \(S\) at least \(k\) is at most
the corresponding number for \(T^-\): apply (1) to the residue-field
space \(\pi^{k-1}S[\pi^k]\). Equivalently, the elementary divisor
exponents in decreasing order are bounded term by term, padding with
zeros. This is stronger than either the total-length bound or a family
of independent cardinality inequalities (4).

Choose a basis of \(C^-\), and let \(F\) be the matrix of the
linearized negative equations. The zeroth Fitting ideal of \(T^-\)
is generated by its maximal minors. Over a DVR their least valuation is

\[
 v_\pi\operatorname{Fitt}_0(T^-)=\sum_i c_i.           \tag{14}
\]

The usual empty convention is \(\operatorname{Fitt}_0(0)=\mathcal O\).
For a coefficient prime \(p\), normalized lengths and valuations are
obtained by dividing both sides by the ramification index
\(e(\mathcal O/\mathbf Z_p)\). No residue-degree factor is inserted
into this normalization.

The remaining arithmetic supplier is a bound on **this same** Fitting
ideal by actual admissible trace differences:

\[
 \frac{v_\pi\operatorname{Fitt}_0(T^-)}
      {e(\mathcal O/\mathbf Z_p)}
 \le 2\sum_\rho v_p\bigl(\operatorname{tr}\rho(\sigma)
                   -(\Psi(\sigma)+1+\Psi(\sigma)^{-1})\bigr).
 \tag{15}
\]

The pinned upper proof is a substantive graded-intersection argument.
It squares the weight \(\pm1\) equations, uses three graded Rees
directions, and compares lower and upper Euler characteristics. Its
intermediate factors are \(2^{r+r'}\) and \(2^{r+1+r'}\), leaving
the final factor two. Finiteness of the weight-zero algebra, the
point and tangent conditions, and trace separation are all used.
Nothing in (1)–(14) establishes (15), and no generic determinant
formula is being substituted for it.

The ordinary-RH specialization does not delete these weights in the
existing model. If \(a(j)\ge0\) counts the added global variables of
weight \(j\), the actual construction gives

\[
 r=a(-1)+2,\quad s=a(-2)+1,\quad
 r'=a(1)+3,\quad s'=a(2)+1,\quad m=a(0)+3.
\]

Its local chart dimensions at weights \(-2,-1,0,1,2\) are respectively
\(1,2,3,4,2\). None is zero. This calculation is independent of the
order of the finite character. Even for a real finite character,
\(\Psi=\psi\varepsilon_p^e\) retains its cyclotomic factor; the
marked-model condition expressly requires
\(\Psi(g_1)^2-1\in\mathcal O^\times\). Thus \(\Psi\) and its
inverse are already distinct modulo the coefficient maximal ideal at
the chosen mark. Real-valuedness of \(\psi\) gives no self-inverse
collapse of the two weights. Nor does it remove the third Rees
direction associated with the extra negative equation. A different
construction would require its own proof; no such replacement is
claimed here.
