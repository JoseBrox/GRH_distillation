# Closing the abstract arithmetic length theorem

## Result and scope

The preceding lower and upper calculations required a normalized
negative equation list, a chart with no weight-zero variables, a
chart-nonzero representative of the trace element, and a finite orbit
cover. These requirements follow from the original abstract model
hypotheses by four short arguments. The trace element is changed only
by an equation, so its values on every equation point are preserved.

Together with the existing proofs, this supplies the complete abstract
commutative-algebra length theorem under its original hypotheses.
It does not construct the marked arithmetic deformation model or prove
its geometric and automorphic hypotheses. No full RH or GRH node is
completed by this abstract theorem.

## 1. The original hypotheses

Let \(\mathcal O\) be a characteristic-zero discrete valuation ring,
\(F\) its fraction field, and \(K\) an algebraically closed extension
of \(F\). Let \(A=\bigoplus_jA_j\) be an augmented graded
\(\mathcal O\)-algebra, \(x:A\to\mathcal O\), with \(B=A_0\)
Noetherian local. Assume \(A\) is generated over \(B\) by finitely many
elements of weights \(-2,-1,1,2\), and \(x(A_j)=0\) for \(j\ne0\).
Put

\[
I=\ker x,\qquad
I^{(2)}=\{h\in A:\text{some }0\ne c\in\mathcal O
                     \text{ satisfies }ch\in I^2\},\qquad
T=I/I^{(2)}.
\]

The ideal \(Q\) is generated by \(r+1\) elements \(F_i\) of weight
\(-1\), \(s\) elements \(G_j\) of weight \(-2\), \(r'\) elements
\(E_k\) of weight \(1\), \(s'\) elements \(E'_l\) of weight \(2\),
and \(m\) elements \(Z_i\in B\cap I\).
Assume \(r\ge1\).

The initial formal chart is a graded homomorphism
\(\Phi:A\to F[[z_1,\ldots,z_m,y_1,\ldots,y_n]]\) with constant
coefficient \(x\), where the \(z_i\) have weight zero. The \(y\)
variables have multiplicities

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
\text{weight}&-1&-2&1&2\\ \hline
\text{multiplicity}&r&s&r'+1&s'+1 .
\end{array}
\]

Its linear-part map on \(T\otimes_{\mathcal O}F\) is an isomorphism
onto the space of all linear forms, preserving weights. This states
precisely the original chart's tangent-surjectivity and
saturated-cotangent-injectivity conditions. Assume the classes of the
\(F_i\), \(G_j\), and \(Z_i\) span respectively
\(T_{-1}\otimes F\), \(T_{-2}\otimes F\), and \(T_0\otimes F\).

Retain the remaining hypotheses of the proved comparisons:
\(B/(Q\cap B)\) is finite over \(\mathcal O\); fixed points other
than \(x_K\) cannot share a degree-zero character with both a
nontrivial negative-only and a nontrivial positive-only equation
point; tangent spaces at two-sided \(K\)-points of \(A/Q\) have
dimension at most one; and \(a\in B\), \(x(a)=0\), is nonzero at
every two-sided equation point. All point and tangent assertions are
relative to \(\mathcal O\). The characteristic-zero generic points,
not the special fibre, are used in the orbit argument.

Write

\[
\mathcal C^-=
T_{-1}/\langle[F_i]\rangle_{\mathcal O}.
\]

**Theorem.** The two-sided points of \(A/Q\) have finitely many
\(\mathbb G_m(K)\)-orbits. In fact, restriction to \(B\) gives a
bijection between those orbits and the occurring degree-zero
characters. The module \(\mathcal C^-\) has finite length, and for
every finite set \(U\) of two-sided points meeting all those orbits,

\[
\boxed{\ell_{\mathcal O}\mathcal C^-
 \le 2\sum_{\xi\in U}v_\pi(\xi(a)).} \tag{1}
\]

Here \(v_\pi(\pi)=1\); the usual common ramification normalization
may be applied to both sides. A set \(U\) with exactly one point per
orbit has no repeated degree-zero characters.

## 2. Finite cotangent lattices and Smith normalization

The ring \(A\) is Noetherian because it is finitely generated over
the Noetherian ring \(B\). The ideal \(I\) is therefore finitely
generated, and \(I/I^2\) is finite over \(A/I=\mathcal O\).
The ideal \(I^{(2)}\) is contained in \(I\): if \(ch\in I^2\), then
\(cx(h)=0\), so \(x(h)=0\). It is exactly the inverse image of the
\(\mathcal O\)-torsion in \(I/I^2\). Thus \(T\) is a finite
torsion-free \(\mathcal O\)-module, hence free, and its weight
components are finite free.

The initial chart identifies \(T_{-1}\otimes F\) with the
\(r\)-dimensional space of weight-minus-one linear forms. Therefore
\(T_{-1}\) has rank \(r\). Its submodule
\(L=\langle[F_i]\rangle_{\mathcal O}\) has full rank by the spanning
hypothesis. Smith normal form gives a basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_r\) and
nonnegative integers \(c_i\) such that

\[
L=\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathcal O\pi^{c_i}e_i,\qquad
\ell_{\mathcal O}(T_{-1}/L)=\sum_i c_i. \tag{2}
\]

This normal form can be imposed on the original equation list without
changing its integral span. The map
\(\mathcal O^{r+1}\twoheadrightarrow L\) sending the coordinate
vectors to the \([F_i]\) splits because \(L\) is free. Choose lifts
of the displayed basis \(\pi^{c_i}e_i\) and a generator of the
rank-one kernel. They form a basis of \(\mathcal O^{r+1}\).
Applying this unimodular change to the actual \(F_i\) gives
\(F'_1,\ldots,F'_r,F'_*\) with exactly the same
\(\mathcal O\)-span. Choose homogeneous lifts \(u_i\in A_{-1}\)
of \(e_i\). Then

\[
F'_i-\pi^{c_i}u_i\in I^{(2)},\qquad F'_*\in I^{(2)}. \tag{3}
\]

The ideal \(Q\), all its equation points, every point condition, and
the quotient \(\mathcal C^-\) are unchanged. This is an integral
basis change, not merely an equality after tensoring with \(F\).

## 3. Removing the weight-zero variables

Let \(H_i=\Phi(Z_i)\). These series have weight zero and constant
term zero. Their linear terms span the \(m\) weight-zero variables;
there are exactly \(m\) equations, so the matrix
\(J=(\partial H_i/\partial z_j)(0)\) is invertible over \(F\).
There are no linear \(y\)-terms. Write

\[
H(z,y)=Jz+R(z,y),\qquad R\in(z,y)^2F[[z,y]]^m.
\]

There is a unique vector \(h(y)\in(y)^2F[[y]]^m\) with
\(H(h(y),y)=0\). A coefficient proof suffices. Having chosen the
homogeneous terms of \(h\) below ordinary degree \(d\), its degree
\(d\) term must be

\[
h_d=-J^{-1}[H(h_{<d}(y),y)]_d. \tag{4}
\]

Changing a degree-\(d\) term in the quadratic remainder affects only
higher degrees. Thus (4) recursively gives existence and uniqueness.
Each correction has weight zero: the equations do, and the preceding
corrections do. The case \(m=0\) is the identity substitution.

Substituting \(z=h(y)\) defines a continuous graded homomorphism
\(F[[z,y]]\to F[[y]]\), preserving constant terms and every
nonzero-weight linear coefficient. The new chart
\(\varphi:A\to F[[y]]\) therefore kills all \(Z_i\), has precisely
the four required nonzero weights, and remains surjective on their
linear parts. No injectivity on the original weight-zero cotangent
space is claimed after this cut, and none is needed by the core.

## 4. Preserving point values while making the chart value nonzero

The condition \(\varphi(a)\ne0\) need not hold for the original
element. It can always be arranged within its equation class.
If it already holds, put \(a'=a\). Otherwise the negative cotangent
spanning and \(r\ge1\) imply that at least one \(F'_i\) has nonzero
linear part under \(\varphi\). The positive weight-one chart has
dimension \(r'+1\ge1\), so there is a homogeneous \(g\in A_1\)
with \(\varphi(g)\ne0\). Put

\[
b=gF'_i\in Q\cap B,\qquad a'=a+b. \tag{5}
\]

Since \(F[[y]]\) is a domain,
\(\varphi(a')=\varphi(g)\varphi(F'_i)\ne0\).
Also \(x(b)=0\). Thus \(a'\) has the same augmentation and
\(\xi(a')=\xi(a)\) for every equation point \(\xi\).
In particular its two-sided nonvanishing and all valuation sums are
unchanged. Neither factor in (5) requires a denominator in
\(\mathcal O\). The explicit representative adjustment proves the
chart condition for \(a'\); it does not incorrectly infer it for \(a\)
from point nonvanishing.

## 5. One orbit per degree-zero character

The reduced-slice theorem was proved using only four-weight generation,
the finite degree-zero quotient, and the one-dimensional tangent
condition. Apply it over the algebraically closed characteristic-zero
field \(K\). Every two-sided point has nonzero values in weights
\(-2\) and \(2\): square a nonzero negative-weight value and use
\(A_{-2n}=(A_{-2})^n\), then do the same on the positive side.
Consequently its degree-zero character \(\sigma\) is nonzero on
\(\mathfrak d=A_{-2}A_2\).

For a fixed occurring \(\sigma\), choose \(f\in A_{-2}\),
\(g\in A_2\) with \(\sigma(fg)\ne0\). Both are nonzero at every
point above \(\sigma\). With the convention
\((t\cdot\xi)(u_j)=t^j\xi(u_j)\), choose a square root of \(\xi(f)\)
to scale any such point to \(f=1\). The two choices differ by
\(\mu_2=\{1,-1\}\). The finite reduced slice above \(\sigma\) is
a single \(\mu_2\)-orbit by the earlier parity argument. Hence all
points above \(\sigma\) lie in one \(\mathbb G_m(K)\)-orbit.
Conversely the action fixes \(B\), so distinct characters give
distinct orbits.

Every character factors through the finite-dimensional \(F\)-algebra
\((B/(Q\cap B))\otimes_{\mathcal O}F\), which has only finitely
many homomorphisms into \(K\). Thus the required finite orbit cover
exists, and can be chosen with exactly one representative per
occurring character. This argument does not assume an independent
covering theorem or a geometric quotient theorem.

## 6. Completion and verified correspondence

Apply the already proved abstract core to the normalized equations,
the cut chart \(\varphi\), and the adjusted element \(a'\).
Equations (2)--(5) supply all the hypotheses that were previously
retained at that stage. The lower and upper bounds give

\[
\ell_{\mathcal O}\mathcal C^-=\sum_i c_i
\le2\sum_{\xi\in U}v_\pi(\xi(a'))
=2\sum_{\xi\in U}v_\pi(\xi(a)),
\]

which is (1). Finiteness of \(\mathcal C^-\) was already proved by
Smith normalization. The finite orbit cover was proved in section 5.
The slice refinement gives coefficient one for a single-point slice
and coefficient two for a two-point slice when one representative per
character is used. Determining those types for the marked arithmetic
models is a further question.

This completes the abstract theorem, including its finite-cover and
finite-length assertions. The actual arithmetic construction still
must supply its initial chart, the specified tangent dimensions and
spanning, the finite degree-zero quotient, fixed-point separation,
two-sided tangent bound, and trace element. The conormal Selmer
comparison additionally retains its marked representation and integral
lifting hypotheses.
