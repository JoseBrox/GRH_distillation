# The finite-level Selmer estimates at the original limiting group

## Result and exact frontier

**Classification: closed obligation inside W04.** The two finite-level
Selmer bounds now supply the three cohomological conditions used by the
local presentation theorem, on the **original limiting auxiliary group**.
No uniformity in the normalized point, coefficient field or odd family is
asserted. The construction of the arithmetic tower, its normalized points,
and the patched automorphic modules remains required. No whole proof-map
node is closed by this result.

Fix the original characteristic-prime normalized point, its odd family
\(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_g\), the auxiliary tower \(T\), and the
limiting quotient \(\Lambda\) of that tower. Put
\[
 A=k[[t]],\qquad F=k((t)),\qquad q=|k|,\qquad A_m=A/(t^m),
 \qquad D=n(d+q_{\rm aux}).                                      \tag{1}
\]
The residue field is finite and its characteristic is odd. The original
ordinary bases, the diagonal conditions on the original subgroups
\(\mu_v\), unramified inertia conditions, polarization and coefficient
modules are retained throughout. In particular the second-order module is
\(W_2=\bigoplus_j\epsilon'_j E\oplus\eta O\), acted on by the original
\(R=(1+\sum_j\epsilon_j\kappa_j)\rho\), not by the uncoupled action.

For the supplied tower and limit, the first and second global bounds prove:

1. Every family of first-order integral Selmer cocycles independent modulo
   the original polarized boundaries has at most \(D\) members.
2. For every \(g+1\) tuples \((p_{ij})_{j=1}^g\) of integral even Selmer
   cocycles, there are \(a_i\in A\), not all zero, and an integral
   second-order Selmer cocycle \(\Psi\) such that
   \[
                         \Psi_{\epsilon'_j}=\sum_i a_i p_{ij}.  \tag{2}
   \]
   More precisely, \(a_i=t^{C_2}c_i\) with at least one \(c_i\) a unit,
   where \(C_2\) is exactly the previously proved finite-level constant.
3. Every integral odd Selmer cocycle \(z\) satisfies
   \[
                t^s z-\sum_j b_j\,h^*\kappa_j\in B^1_{\rm odd}
                \quad(s\geq0,\ b_j\in A).                      \tag{3}
   \]

These are the source's `K1`, `K2`, and `K3`. They also give their exact
Laurent versions for the **bounded continuous cocycles** used by the
completed-local-ring argument. Thus the gap between the established
finite-level B1/B2 estimates and those cohomological inputs is removed.
The already proved padded-coordinate presentation and incidence theorem
can now use them. The remaining dimension hypothesis on the automorphic
support is not a consequence of this result.

## 1. Transfer the finite Selmer sets without losing a factor

Here are the precise properties of the original limit used in the proof.
There is a topologically finitely generated profinite group \(P\), with
continuous surjections \(\pi_N:P\to\Gamma_N\) and
\(\pi_\infty:P\to\Gamma_\infty\), and a free ultrafilter \(\mathcal U\)
containing every cofinite set. The maps preserve the index-two subgroup
and the chosen complex conjugation. For every open normal subgroup
\(V\subset P\),
\[
       (\ker\pi_N)V=(\ker\pi_\infty)V
                    \quad\text{for }\mathcal U\text{-almost every }N. \tag{4}
\]
The maps to the base group agree modulo every open normal subgroup there,
uniformly on \(P\), for \(\mathcal U\)-almost every \(N\). The original
local maps and auxiliary Frobenius/inertia elements are likewise close
modulo \(V\): they have lifts to \(P\) differing by an element of \(V\).
These are exactly the `Lim` fields `ker_lim`, `p_lim`, `phi_lim`,
`phiu_lim`, `ph_lim`, and `ta_lim`; they are retained data, not new
Selmer estimates. The source constructs this object from its pro-\(\ell\)
tower. This note does not replace construction of that arithmetic tower.
The defining source is `ThetaCoefficientMapEq` 265--297: finite
generation is line 272, the common quotient maps are 275--282, and
the uniform convergence fields are 287--294. The finite-action equality
uniform on the common cover is derived in
`EventuallyLocallyConstantCompTowerEq` 6--39, so it is not an extra
pointwise-convergence assumption.

Let \(M\) be any of the original finite coefficient modules at a fixed
truncation \(m\). The subgroup \(P^+\) of index two is topologically
finitely generated: choose Schreier generators in the dense subgroup
generated by a finite generating set of \(P\). A continuous cocycle
\(P^+\to M\) is determined by its values on those generators. Indeed,
the cocycle identity determines its values on their abstract subgroup,
and continuity determines the values on its closure. Consequently there
are only finitely many such cocycles.

A locally constant map \(f:P^+\to M\) is constant on cosets of some open
normal subgroup \(V\subset P\) contained in \(P^+\). To see this, cover
the compact group by finitely many cosets contained in fibres of \(f\),
then take the normal core of an open subgroup refining this finite
partition. Thus (4) says that \(f\) factors through \(\Gamma_N^+\) if
and only if it factors through \(\Gamma_\infty^+\), for almost every
\(N\). The original local-map congruences identify its restrictions to
every original local group and the values at the auxiliary elements.

First make the finite coefficient action on the common cover agree with
the limiting action. Then intersect the finitely many almost-everywhere
sets obtained for **all** cocycles with that action. We now have one set
of levels working simultaneously for every finite Selmer cocycle.
Factorization is unique because the quotient maps are surjective.
The cocycle law and polarization transfer through the common cover;
local restrictions agree, so the same ordinary witness matrices and the
same inertia and \(\mu_v\) conditions work on both sides. Hence pullback
to \(P^+\) identifies the original finite Selmer sets bijectively.

The boundary sets agree too: the same allowed skew primitive \(Y\)
gives the same pulled-back boundary because the finite actions agree.
The comparison is therefore an identification of the Selmer quotients,
not just an inequality with an unspecified boundary index.

Intersect these sets with the cofinite set supplied by B1 or B2 at
this \(m\). This proves at the limit, with **the same constants**,
\[
 \#\operatorname{Sel}^1_{\infty,m}
      \leq\#B^1_{\infty,m}\,q^{mD+C_1},                    \tag{5}
\]
and the original second-order lifting assertion with factor \(t^{C_2}\).
There is no exchange of quantifiers: the good auxiliary levels can
depend on \(m\), while \(C_1,C_2\) were fixed before \(m\).

## 2. The integral rank bound, retaining finite congruence defects

Suppose \(z_1,\ldots,z_e\) are integral first-order Selmer cocycles
independent modulo the original integral boundaries. Let \(V\) be the
finite free \(A\)-module of allowed skew primitive matrices. For a
fixed finite generating set \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r\) of
\(\Gamma_\infty^+\), form the finite \(A\)-linear map
\[
 \mathcal F:A^e\oplus V\longrightarrow W_1^r,
 \qquad (c,Y)\longmapsto
        \left(\sum_i c_i z_i(\gamma_j)-dY(\gamma_j)\right)_j. \tag{6}
\]
Its kernel projects to zero in \(A^e\): a continuous cocycle vanishing
on the generators vanishes everywhere, and independence applies.

Smith normal form over the discrete valuation ring gives an integer
\(b\geq0\) such that
\[
       \mathcal F(c,Y)\in t^mW_1^r
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c\in t^{\max(m-b,0)}A^e. \tag{7}
\]
In detail, take \(b\) to be the maximum valuation of a nonzero invariant
factor of \(\mathcal F\), with \(b=0\) if there are none. In diagonal
coordinates an approximate kernel vector differs from a true kernel
vector by \(t^{\max(m-b,0)}\) times an integral vector; projecting to
\(A^e\) proves (7). Thus finite congruence torsion is kept in \(b\).

Every allowed primitive modulo \(t^m\) lifts to \(V\). Its coefficient
module is specified by coordinate zeros and skew symmetry, so one lifts
one entry from each skew pair and takes its negative in the other
entry. The diagonal is zero since the characteristic is odd. Therefore
if \(\sum_i c_i z_i\) is a boundary modulo \(t^m\), (7) applies.
Distinct coefficient vectors modulo \(t^{m-b}\), represented arbitrarily
in \(A^e\), give distinct classes in
\(\operatorname{Sel}^1_{\infty,m}/B^1_{\infty,m}\). For \(m\geq b\),
\[
                       q^{e(m-b)}\leq q^{mD+C_1}.          \tag{8}
\]
Letting \(m\) grow gives \(e\leq D\), proving `K1`.

This is a rank conclusion. The constants \(C_1,b\) are allowed to
depend on the fixed arithmetic point and chosen family. For example
\(A^D\oplus A/t^M\) has quotient size
\(q^{Dm+\min(m,M)}\). Its rank is \(D\) for every \(M\), while
its integral torsion length is unbounded as \(M\) varies. Accordingly
this passage does not silently prove the moving-point torsion estimate
still required by W04.

The source interface here has ordinary transpose, not a semilinear
coefficient involution: `PairedDualFirstOrderDualMatrixSpace` 222 and
246--247 define \(W_1\) and the boundary condition \(Y^T=-Y\).
Its actual surjective reduction is proved directly in
`CoefficientsBoundedOfSelmerBoundary` 64--99 by a coefficientwise
additive section. Thus no integral descent or norm equation is being
hidden in the primitive-lifting step.

## 3. The second-order lift by finite inverse limits

Fix the integral inputs \(p_{ij}\). For each \(m\geq1\), let \(X_m\)
be the set of all triples consisting of:

* a vector \(c\in A_m^{g+1}\) with at least one unit coordinate;
* a continuous original \(W_{2,m}\)-valued Selmer cocycle \(\Psi_m\)
  whose \(\epsilon'_j\)-coefficient is
  \(t^{C_2}\sum_i c_i p_{ij}\bmod t^m\);
* one original ordinary witness matrix at every ordinary place.

The set \(X_m\) is nonempty by the transferred B2 statement. It is
finite because the group is topologically finitely generated and all
coefficient and witness modules are finite. Reduction sends \(X_{m+1}\)
to \(X_m\), preserving primitivity, all original local equations and
the displayed coefficient identity. The maps need not be surjective.

An inverse sequence of nonempty finite sets has a compatible point.
Indeed, for any finite initial segment reduce one point from its last
set. The resulting finite compatibility conditions have the finite
intersection property in the compact product \(\prod_m X_m\).
Equivalently, apply the infinite-path lemma to the finite tree of
compatible initial segments. This does not assume that every modular
cocycle lifts individually.

A compatible point gives \(c\in A^{g+1}\), a cocycle
\(\Psi:\Gamma_\infty^+\to W_2\), and integral ordinary witnesses.
Every reduction of \(\Psi\) is continuous, so \(\Psi\) is continuous
for the \(t\)-adic topology. The cocycle, polarization, coefficient,
unramified and ordinary equations hold at every truncation; separatedness
of \(A\) makes them exact. A coordinate of \(c\bmod t\) is nonzero,
so that coordinate of \(c\) is a unit. With \(a_i=t^{C_2}c_i\),
equation (2) follows, and \(a\ne0\) because \(A\) is a domain.
This proves `K2` with the original loss and every original local condition.
When \(g=0\), the projection condition is empty and one may take
\(c_1=1\), \(\Psi=0\); the argument includes this case.

Finiteness of the ordinary-place index set is an explicit part of the
original arithmetic datum: `PairedDualFirstOrderDualMatrixSpace`
10--12 equips \(\mathrm{Vl}\) with a finite enumeration. Each local
witness is one matrix over the finite coefficient algebra, with the
zero conditions in lines 235--244. Thus retaining all witnesses still
gives a finite \(X_m\), even though each local group itself is infinite.

## 4. Odd descent and the bounded Laurent interface

The proof of `K3` is the already established resultant descent in
[the padded-relations note](selmer-integral-frontier-padded-relations.md).
The kernel of the limiting map is topologically normally generated
by the original auxiliary inertia elements \(\tau_j\). They commute
with the limiting Frobenius elements \(\varphi_j\). The odd value
\(z(\tau_j)\) therefore commutes with the prescribed block matrix
\(\rho(\varphi_j)\), whose nonzero block resultant makes that odd
commutant zero. Polarization gives normality also under complex
conjugation. Thus \(z\) descends to the original base group, where the
chosen \(\kappa_j\) generate after multiplication by a power of \(t\).
This is exactly (3), including the allowed skew boundary primitive.

For a finite family of **bounded continuous** Laurent cocycles, multiply
by one common sufficiently large power of \(t\) to make all values and
the finitely many ordinary witness matrices integral. Coordinate zeros,
the cocycle law, polarization and original local equations are preserved
by this common scaling. Apply `K1`, `K2`, or `K3` integrally and invert
the scaling factor in \(F\). Independence is preserved because
\(A\hookrightarrow F\) is injective. The coefficient vector in `K2`
remains nonzero; the witnesses in `K3` remain the same kind of skew
primitive. No assertion is made for arbitrary discontinuous or unbounded
Laurent cocycles.

## 5. Exact consumer and the next missing arithmetic statement

The source's `CohLim` theorem feeds these three conditions directly into
`AutomorphicDetection.GalOut`. The previously proved padded-coordinate
and incidence arguments then supply its local prime inclusion once the
actual complete local presentation and normalized-point identifications
are supplied. In rank three no new character-count assumption enters.

The next decisive statement is about the actual automorphic module:
the patched support must supply an irreducible prime \(Z\) of dimension
at least \(1+D\) at a seed and transport support through every prescribed
finite chain of nonsemisimple linking primes. The present result provides
the cohomological inputs of that transport but neither constructs this
module nor proves that dimension statement. Rational rigidity does not
replace it. The final uniform integral Selmer estimate and the W04 node
remain open.
