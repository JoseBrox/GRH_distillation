# The actual marked ring and characteristic-prime chain propagation

## Status and exact frontier

**Classification: closed marked-propagation subobligation inside W04, pending independent review.**
The theorem below constructs the original marked deformation ring and applies
the actual Taylor--Wiles modules, rather than adding a patched-support
hypothesis. It proves the complete source `Core`, `CoreBC`, and `Raw`
contracts. No nonzero starting prime, normalized characteristic-prime curve,

characteristic-zero point alone. W04 remains open.

### Frontier theorem

Let ell be odd. Retain the original complete coefficient DVR O, finite
residue field, rank n>=1, global/local frame, residual distinct characters,
ordinary determinant pair u=(D,psi) over the complete Noetherian local
O-algebra B, and ideal J. Assume B is topologically generated over O by
all characteristic-polynomial coefficients D_i(g) and the ordered
character values psi_vi(g). Retain an adapted original Taylor--Wiles
tower T with q auxiliary places and its actual TWAut modules, over the
original weight ring Lambda_0=O[[X_1,...,X_D]]. In particular its base
module M_0 is finite free over Lambda_0, and its support at a prime p
is exactly J subset p. Do not require M_0 to be nonzero.

Let x_0,...,x_h be any finite chain of the original normalized
characteristic-prime points. Write

    A_i=k_i[[t_i]],  F_i=k_i((t_i)),  p_i=ker(f_i:B -> A_i).

Keep their original normalized representations, ordinary bases, odd
Selmer families, ordered distinct eigenvalues at all q detecting
elements, and the two actual bounds B1 with D_0=D+nq and B2.
The finite fields k_i have the same cardinality, as supplied by the
simultaneous coefficient-extension theorem. Let e_i be arbitrary
primes satisfying

    e_i subset p_i and p_(i+1),
    the original ordinary Cayley--Hamilton equation algebra at e_i
    is nonsemisimple after some residue-field extension.

Suppose there is a prime q_0 subset p_0 such that

    J subset q_0,  DiscSt(D,q_0).

Then every prime q_* subset p_h satisfying DiscSt(D,q_*) or the same
original nonsemisimplicity condition contains J.

Here DiscSt is exactly the source's explicit discriminant predicate:
there are n^2 elements g_a and n^2 elements h_b for which

    det( tr D(g_a h_b) )_(a,b) is not in the prime.              (1)

The original equation ideal is the already closed Cayley--Hamilton,
ordinary-product, starred-product and prescribed-inertia ideal. Its
geometric nonsemisimplicity is retained; it is not replaced by
reducibility of the determinant. The quantifier is over every finite
chain and every displayed linking prime, with no bound on chain length.
This is exactly `Raw`, and the proof also supplies its marked `Core`
and coefficient-change `CoreBC` versions. The source allows rank zero
syntactically, but its normalized point has an integer `1 <= a <= n`;
therefore that case has no such point and its contracts are vacuous.

## 1. Construct the actual limiting group

First pass to the source's pro-ell quotient, as justified in Section 8.
The tower has a fixed open normal subgroup H in its base group, and
the preimages H_N are pro-ell with one bound b on
`dim Hom_cont(H_N,F_ell)`. Burnside's basis theorem makes every H_N
generated by at most b elements. Choose fixed lifts of all elements of
the finite quotient Gamma_0/H and the original complex conjugation.
The free profinite group P on these lifts and b further generators
therefore maps continuously and surjectively to every Gamma_N.
All maps agree modulo H, and a single generator maps to the original
complex conjugation at every level. No number-field degree or
auxiliary-prime cardinality is discarded in b.

Fix a free ultrafilter containing the cofinite sets. For each open
normal V of P, the finite subgroup
`(ker(pi_N)V)/V` stabilizes along the ultrafilter. These stabilized
subgroups are compatible under finite-quotient transition maps.
Their inverse limit is a closed normal subgroup K of P, and compactness
gives

    (ker(pi_N)V)/V = KV/V  for almost all N, for each fixed V.   (2)

Put Gamma_infinity=P/K. Because P is finitely generated, there are
only finitely many homomorphisms from P into any fixed finite group.
The base maps have unique compatible ultrafilter limits on all finite
quotients. They give the continuous surjection
Gamma_infinity -> Gamma_0, uniformly congruent on all of P at every
fixed finite quotient.

Here is the local-map construction without assuming that a homomorphism
lifts to the free group P. For an original local group Y, let J_Y be the
preimage of H under its fixed map to the base group. Every level map
sends J_Y into H_N, so it factors through
`Y_0=Y/PL(J_Y)`. Local Mazur finiteness gives finite H^1 of J_Y;
hence Y_0 has a finitely generated open pro-ell subgroup and finite
remaining quotient and is topologically finitely generated. Choose
one free profinite presentation `F_r ->> Y_0`.

At each N lift the images of its r generators individually to P,
using pi_N surjective. The tuple gives a homomorphism `a_N:F_r -> P`,
not an asserted lift `Y_0 -> P`. Take the ultrafilter limit of these
r-tuples in the compact space P^r and let a be the resulting map F_r -> P.
For every relator w in ker(F_r -> Y_0), `a_N(w)` lies in ker(pi_N).
Evaluation of any profinite word is continuous, so (2) implies
`a(w) in KV` for every open normal V and hence `a(w) in K`.
Thus a descends to a continuous map `Y_0 -> P/K`. For a fixed V,
congruence of the finitely many generator lifts holds almost everywhere
and makes the homomorphisms congruent on all of F_r. This gives the
uniform lifting congruence on every y in Y_0, and therefore on Y.
The base-map compatibility follows by its already uniform convergence.
Intersect over the finitely many original local groups. This constructs
all `phi_lim` and `phiu_lim` fields of Lim. Do the same for the finitely
many auxiliary Frobenius and inertia elements. The tame relation and Q_N congruent 1 mod ell^N imply that
these two limiting elements commute: in each finite pro-ell quotient,
the inertia element has bounded ell-power order. The inertia element
maps to one in the base group. The common conjugation generator has
square one in the limit. These are every group-theoretic field of S4;
the original local subgroups and maps are carried through the construction.

This is the construction in `SysCover` 8--80 and
`SelmerCocyclesLimitAndDescentProperties` 15--211. In particular the
common equation group P^+ is the open index-two subgroup of P and is
finitely generated by profinite Schreier, rather than an unrelated
product of the groups at separate levels.

Every "almost all" or "eventually" in this limiting construction and
Sections 2--6 means the same ultrafilter U. The exact source `Lim`
fields, `ThetaCoefficientMapEq` 273--294, use U, and S5 uses U in
`PatchedSupportCompletions` 58--67. The only comparison with atTop is
U<=atTop, so a cofinite bound such as N>=k+j implies U-eventuality.
No claim that the ultrafilter congruences are cofinite is made or needed.

## 2. Construct the universal marked coefficient ring

We first prove the marked version. Adjoin the original single curve
variable s to the weight ring, so

    Lambda=O[[s,X_1,...,X_D]],  S_infinity=Lambda[[Y_ji]]_(j,i).

For the moment assume O surjects onto every k_i and f_i(s)=t_i.
B is now topologically generated by its pair values and this one s.
Section 7 removes precisely these additional assumptions.

Let R_D represent continuous n-dimensional determinants of P^+
lifting the original residual determinant. Chenevier's Proposition 3.3
constructs it. It is Noetherian: P^+ is topologically finitely generated,
so continuous cocycles with values in any fixed finite module form a
finite set; Wang--Erickson's Proposition 3.2 applies, without an
assumption ell>n or division by n!.

For every original local ordered character, use its character deformation
ring, retaining the specified finite-order values on mu_v. Local
class-field theory identifies its pro-ell abelian group with a finite
group times a finite-rank Z_ell-module. Thus this ring is a quotient of
a finite-variable power-series ring with all finite torsion relations
retained. The completed tensor product R_1 of these finitely many rings
and R_D represents the determinant and all ordered local characters,
before imposing global compatibility. It is Noetherian and local with
the original finite residue field. Now put

    R=R_1[[s, Z_ji, Y_ji]],  U_ji=u^0_ji+Z_ji,                (3)

where u^0_ji are the original residual-root lifts in TWAut. This is the
actual `RRObj`; no generic deformation model is substituted for it.
Its universal property gives maps rho_N:R -> R_N into the actual
auxiliary Hecke coefficient rings, carrying the determinant, characters,
s, roots and diamond variables to their original values.

The original weight map S_infinity -> R sends s to s,

    X_k -> c_k psi_(v_k,i_k)(gamma_k)-1,  Y_ji -> Y_ji.         (4)

Every displayed value lies in m_R by the actual residual weight identity.
Completeness gives one continuous map. Its compositions with all rho_N
are exactly the original S_infinity-actions, since they agree on O and
all power-series variables. This proves the source RMaps compatibility,
not merely an isomorphism of abstract rings.

Let C be the original local splitting algebra over B for the q
polynomials `chi_D(sigma_j)`, with the prescribed residual ordered roots.
The full splitting algebra is obtained by successively adjoining a
root and dividing the monic polynomial; it is free of rank (n!)^q.
Its local factor at the prescribed residual tuple is a nonzero direct
summand because B is complete local. It is consequently finite free
and faithfully flat over B. The selected factor's rank is retained;
it need not equal (n!)^q. Equivalently it is exactly the original
power-series root presentation `Sp`, not a reduced or etale substitute.

The same universal property supplies a map

    pi:R -> C,  U_ji -> u_ji,  Y_ji -> 0,                     (5)

with determinant and characters pulled back from u along the limiting
map to the base group. It is surjective. Indeed its image contains every
pair coefficient and s in B and all roots generating C; the image is
closed because R is compact and C is Hausdorff. These elements generate
a dense O-subalgebra of C. All characteristic-polynomial coefficients
are used in this argument, including when ell<=n.

## 3. Impose exactly the original relations

Let I be the ideal of R generated by the following actual scalar
relations, with arbitrary group test element h whenever a trace occurs:

| Family | Relation |
|---|---|
| Limiting-group kernel | tr D(h(k-1)) for k in ker(P^+ -> Gamma_infinity^+) |
| Polarization | every characteristic-polynomial coefficient of the original conjugate inverse, with its prescribed epsilon^(1-n) factor, minus the original coefficient |
| Ordinary flag | tr D(h product_i(g_(v,i)-psi_vi(g_i))), in the original order |
| Prescribed tame inertia | tr D(h(g-1)) on the original subgroup I_p |
| Auxiliary roots | tr D(h product_i(phi_j-U_ji)) |
| Auxiliary inertia | tr D(h product_(a!=i)(phi_j-U_ja)(tau_j-1-Y_ji)) |

The group elements in the last four rows are arbitrary lifts of their
prescribed limiting local elements. This is precisely `Rel` and
`ii=span(Rel)`; there is no localization, radical, flat quotient or
saturation at this stage. As R is complete Noetherian, I is closed.
Every generator maps to zero under pi, by the original pair equations,
Cayley--Hamilton, the splitting-polynomial identity, and tau_j mapping
to one. Therefore pi descends to a surjection

                         pi_bar:R/I -> C.                   (6)

The determinant descends to the limiting group over R/I, as a full
polynomial law. To justify the trace-to-determinant step, use the actual
finite continuous Cayley--Hamilton algebra. Its original residual
characters are distinct, so it has a generalized matrix algebra with
rank-one diagonal corners. A two-sided trace-radical ideal has zero
intersection with each diagonal corner. In the canonical determinant,
every term containing an element of that ideal contains a cycle in such
a corner and vanishes, also after every coefficient extension. Hence
quotienting by the kernel relations preserves the whole determinant.
This is the same denominator-free argument proved in the polarized-model
package. The remaining rows give its original Pair, root and partial-root
relations. Their starred partners follow from the exact polarization.

For each i, lift p_i and the entire given prime chain to C. Lying over,
going down for the finite flat map B -> C, and going up for the integral
map give primes P_i and E_i with exactly the original contractions and
E_i subset P_i,P_(i+1). The initial and terminal primes can likewise be
lifted below P_0 and P_h. No separate prime choices break the chain.

Every P_i above p_i is the kernel of a map x'_i:C -> A_i extending f_i.
First, f_i is local and continuous. If an element of m_B mapped to a
unit of A_i, apply a monic polynomial over the finite residue field to
the inverse of its nonzero residue; lifting its coefficients from O
would make a unit of B map to a nonunit. Locality and finite generation
of m_B imply continuity. Now f_i is surjective: its compact image contains k_i and t_i and
therefore all k_i[[t_i]]. Every root polynomial splits with distinct
roots in F_i and all its roots lie in A_i. The residue-field extension
at P_i thus has fraction field F_i; normality of A_i forces the finite
integral image of C/P_i to be A_i. This gives the asserted lift and kernel.
Set q_i=pi^(-1)(P_i). Composing (6) with x'_i gives the original OoPt:
all ordered roots remain distinct, s maps to t_i, and all Y_ji map to zero.
Its equation ideal is precisely the already closed

    D.chBar(pairGens union qGens),                            (7)

not just the displayed generators. The entire nilpotent radical and
annihilator torsion of this algebra are retained.

## 4. Verify the actual S5 inputs with their quantifiers

Give the actual M_N its R-action through rho_N, and put

                         X=C tensor_B M_0.                  (8)

If C has rank r over B, X is the direct sum of r copies of M_0 as a
Lambda-module. It is finite free, with YX=0. This proves the actual
freeness and action requirements with the original rank, even if it is
zero.

The original diamond equations are `(1+Y_ji)^(ell^a_j(N))-1`, with
`a_j(N)>=N`. For every fixed k,j, if N>=k+j these equations lie in
`(Y)^k+m_Lambda^j`; the binomial coefficients give this exact containment.
Thus

    M_N/((Y)^k+m_Lambda^j)M_N

is finite free over the corresponding original power-series quotient.
Its rank may vary with N. There is no uniform-rank assumption.

For the comparison maps at coefficient order j, choose e such that

                    m_B^e M_0 subset m_Lambda^j M_0.         (9)

This e exists without a uniform integral bound: the finite set
M_0/m_Lambda^j M_0 has a finite descending m_B-filtration; stabilization
and Nakayama force its eventual term to be zero. The limiting congruences
then make the base determinants at the auxiliary Frobenius and the
selected detecting elements equal modulo m_B^e. Their full splitting
presentations and original ordered characters agree on (9). The actual
TWAut maps consequently induce R-linear maps

    X/m_Lambda^j X <--> M_N/((Y)+m_Lambda^j)M_N

for almost all N, with both composites the original limiting multiplier

    f=fEl(1,U)=product_j product_(i!=a)(U_ji-U_ja)^2.          (10)

Here `fEl(Q_N,U)` and f agree modulo ell^N; taking N>=j supplies the
required identity on these quotients. This keeps the comparison of the
original module actions, not only equality of dimensions.

For every relation r in Section 3 and every c, the limiting congruences
supply, for almost all N, an r_N in R with

    r-r_N in m_R^c,
    rho_N(r_N)=0, or (fEl(Q_N,U) r_N)^e_T M_N=0.              (11)

The first alternative covers the kernel, polarization, ordinary and
prescribed-inertia relations. For the kernel relation use (2) to replace
the kernel element by one in ker(pi_N). For ordinary and inertia relations
replace the finitely many local lifts by their level-N lifts. The fixed
finite determinant coefficients are uniformly continuous on the compact
common cover. Polarization uses its original cyclotomic coefficient,
whose finite quotients agree by the base-map convergence. The two
auxiliary rows are exactly the TWAut root/partial-root annihilation
identities, giving the second alternative.

The original uniform exponent is e_T=n! for the constructed modules.
Use the source exponent e_T+1 in S5, including its zero-exponent edge case.
The elementary difference-of-powers identity gives

    (fr)^(e_T+1)-a_N in m_R^c,   a_N in Ann_R M_N,            (12)

with `a_N=(fEl(Q_N,U) r_N)^(e_T+1)`. This is the exact
`forall r forall c eventually N exists a_N` statement. No common level
for infinitely many relations is assumed. No constant in (9) or (11)
is declared uniform in the arithmetic point.

At q_i, f is nonzero because the specialized roots are pairwise distinct.
The weight map Lambda -> A_i is onto and sends s to t_i. Since Lambda
is regular local of dimension D+2 and its quotient A_i has dimension one,
the completion at that curve prime is regular of dimension D+1.
Thus S5 applies to these actual data and yields ideals S_i in

                B_i=completion((R/I)_(q_i/I))

whose minimal support primes have dimension at least

                           1+D+nq = 1+D_0.                 (13)

It also gives the exact diamond-fibre support identity and the prime-moving
property. The order remains: localize and complete the original equation
quotient; use the original Y=0 fibre; retain its nilpotent/torsion structure.

## 5. Preserve the exact nonsemisimple equation algebra

The original ordinary equation algebra over B is finite, by the continuous
Cayley--Hamilton theorem. The limiting equation algebra (7) is also finite.
The group quotient and (6) induce a surjection from the latter, after
base change to C, onto the former after base change to C. Kernel relations
are killed; ordinary, tame and starred relations map to their originals;
the root relation maps to Cayley--Hamilton; the partial-root relation
maps to zero because tau_j=1 and Y=0.

This assertion includes the closed ideals. In a finite complete module,
the closure of an equation ideal maps into the closed equation ideal of
the target. Thus the source uses the algebraic span of the image of the
already closed ideal after residue-field base change, exactly as in (7).
No new topological closure or saturation is inserted after the field map.

If an original linking algebra is nonsemisimple over a field extension
of its residue field, it remains nonsemisimple after any further field
extension: a nonzero nilpotent two-sided radical remains nonzero by
faithful flatness. For two unrelated coefficient-field extensions, take
a common field over both. A semisimple algebra cannot surject onto a
nonsemisimple algebra. Consequently every prime of B_i lying over the
lifted linking prime satisfies exactly the NonSS predicate for (7).
This also handles purely inseparable residue-field issues: the predicate
is the source's existence of a field extension with nonsemisimple algebra.

Discriminant stability transfers exactly by (1), and is preserved under
generalization. The limiting-group surjection preserves it in both
directions because every group element has a lift. The actual B1/B2
bounds and the already proved common-cover comparison give K1,K2,K3
for each of these OoPt points, with D_0=D+nq. S6/GalOut therefore says:

    Z,Q prime in B_i, DiscSt(D,Z), dim(B_i/Z)>=1+D_0,
    DiscSt(D,Q) or NonSS((7),Q)   =>   Z subset Q.             (14)

This is applied to the actual complete local ring constructed above,
with its actual determinant, original sign, and full ordinary equations.

## 6. Nonzero support propagates along the actual chain

The finite-module support formula gives, for every prime P of C,

    Ann_R X subset pi^(-1)(P)
       iff J subset P intersect B.                         (15)

Indeed X=C tensor_B M_0 and support of a finite module commutes with
base change; pi is surjective. This is an identity for the original J,
not an auxiliary radical or rational Hecke ideal.

Lift the initial q_0 below P_0 and then to B_0 by faithful flatness of
completion. The diamond variables vanish there. Equations (15) and S5's
fibre identity give a prime Q containing S_0 whose contraction is stable.
Choose a minimal prime Z of S_0 below Q. Stability descends to Z by (1),
and (13) gives its required dimension.

At the first linking prime E_0, choose any prime Q' of B_0 over its
inverse image. Section 5 gives its exact nonsemisimplicity. Formula (14)
forces Z subset Q'. S5's moving property then transports a prime of
support to B_1 with exactly the same contraction to R/I; that contraction
is still stable. Choose a minimal support prime below it and repeat.
Induction transports stable nonzero support across every given link.

At the terminal q_*, lift below P_h and to B_h. It is stable or
nonsemisimple by Section 5. Apply (14) once more to force containment
of the stable minimal support prime. The fibre identity and (15) imply
J subset q_*. This proves the entire marked Core theorem. It also shows
why an initial prime is essential: zero modules meet every freeness
condition but do not supply the first Q.

## 7. Remove the residue-field and curve-variable assumptions

For the common cardinality of the finite fields k_i, take the unramified
finite extension O'/O whose residue field has that cardinality. It is
finite free and generated by one element with monic polynomial P; the
reduction of P splits in every k_i. Set

                 B'=(O' tensor_O B)[[s]].                   (16)

The original IsCO condition identifies residue(B) with residue(O):
its coefficient map is local, and every element of B is congruent to an
O-element modulo m_B (`ResMapBijective` 9--13, 27--33). Consequently
`(O' tensor_O B)/m_B(O' tensor_O B)=residue(O')`, a field. Since the
tensor product is finite over the complete local ring B, all its maximal
ideals lie over m_B, so this proves it is local and complete. The
original residue characters extend by this residue embedding. All actual modules and
maps are base changed and then extended in the single formal variable.
Their finite freeness, exact diamond quotient, compositions and uniform
relation exponent are preserved. The new original ideal is the extension
of J; faithful flatness preserves its prime-support test.

First lift the entire chain through the finite flat algebra O' tensor B
by going down and going up. Every lifted prime above p_i gives a map into
the same A_i: its coefficient generator is a root of P, which already
splits in k_i; hence it maps to a constant in k_i. This defines the
appropriate O'-algebra structure on that k_i and is surjective on the
residue field. Extend the map to (16) by s -> t_i. Its contraction to
the coefficient tensor ring is the chosen lifted prime.

Extend each linking prime and the chosen initial/terminal primes as
power-series ideals. They are prime because their quotients are power
series over domains, and they lie below the appropriate curve kernels.
The base change of the original finite equation algebra is still finite
and complete and kills the new defining equations continuously. Hence
the new already closed equation ideal maps to zero in that algebra,
giving the exact quotient map needed in Section 5. Nonsemisimplicity
and discriminants transfer without assuming an unproved equality of
closed ideals. Apply the marked theorem and
contract back to B. Faithful flatness and the original support identity
give precisely the desired containment J subset q_*. This proves CoreBC.
There is no replacement of any k_i by a different characteristic-prime
curve or change of its original kernel in B.

## 8. Remove the pro-ell quotient assumption

Let PL(H) be the kernel of the maximal pro-ell quotient of the fixed open
normal H, and likewise at each level. These kernels are characteristic
in H and normal in the full group. Quotient by them. The original maps,
complex conjugation, ordinary local maps, and auxiliary elements descend;
all finite H^1 bounds and prescribed local subgroups have their original
images. Surjectivity sends PL(H_N) onto PL(H), so the tower remains a
tower with the same q, Q_N and detecting elements.

This quotient loses none of the relevant coefficient data. The residual
characters are trivial on H. The finite H^1 hypothesis for H makes H^1 of the full group with
coefficients in the residual adjoint finite: restrict to H, on which
the residual action is trivial, and retain finitely many coset values.
Wang--Erickson therefore supplies a finite continuous Cayley--Hamilton
algebra before taking this quotient. In it, H maps, modulo the coefficient
maximal ideal, into the unipotent radical, an ell-group; higher coefficient
kernels are pro-ell as well. Thus its entire H-image is pro-ell and kills
PL(H). The full determinant and the original pair descend. The same is
true of every actual auxiliary determinant and every normalized point.
All ordered character values and root/diamond variables remain unchanged.

For the finite cocycle modules in B1/B2, a cocycle on H together with
its coefficient action defines a homomorphism into the finite semidirect
product of its additive ell-group and the pro-ell image of H. It too
kills PL(H). Thus inflation identifies the exact cocycles, ordinary
witnesses, inertia restrictions and allowed boundaries at every finite
level; the B1/B2 bounds and normalized odd families descend unchanged.
The same argument applies to the continuous integral limits.

Finally the finite equation algebras factor through these group quotients.
The original nonsemisimple, stable and pair-generation conditions are
preserved, including their closed ideals. Apply CoreBC to the quotient
tower and contract along the unchanged B. This is exactly Raw.

## 9. What this closes, and what remains

The actual marked ring, its finite-flat root cover, the exact equation
quotient, the support dimension, the approximate annihilation estimates,
and propagation through every supplied finite characteristic-prime chain
are now constructed. The previously closed local GalOut and S5 theorems
have been applied to their prescribed arithmetic objects.

The next direct target is the original arithmetic P1/P3 application:
construct all normalized points and the detecting prime towers for the
actual global frame, then use the large arithmetic seed and the global
component/curve argument to exclude the off-triangular special-fibre
curves. An arbitrary nonzero characteristic-zero automorphic eigenvector
does not imply the initial containment below a prescribed p_0. Neither
this theorem nor the existing rational rigidity discards that requirement.
The final two-sided rigidity and moving-family integral Selmer budget
remain open until those exact consumers are supplied.
