# The actual rank-three determinant section and thick components

## Frontier and status

**Classification: closed rank-three local `Sharp` construction,
independently reviewed.** Retain the original odd prime ell,
coefficient DVR O, frame F and all hypotheses of `F.Ok C0` and
`F.Big C0`. Write d=sum_v d_v. Let S be the actual normalized rank-three
space and L an original chart at a closed point x of the coefficient
fibre with a nonzero lower entry. Retain its specified finite unramified
O'/O and residue embedding. Put R=L.R and k'=k(O').

The exact frontier is an ideal Qsharp of R such that:

1. If x is outside the original odd-entry locus, Qsharp=0.
2. Every prime outside that odd-entry locus contains Qsharp.
3. Every minimal prime over Qsharp has quotient dimension at least
   3d+2, and their graph with edges of intersection dimension at least
   3d+1 is connected.
4. Each such minimal prime is either original type I or carries an
   original `Thick` modification, including both ordered products and
   nonsplitting after every injective field extension.

This is the literal `Sharp` contract. It is a local support result;
it does not by itself supply arithmetic P23 transfer, global AllGood,
or a quantitative integral Selmer estimate. The preceding type-E
note supplies the distinct finite-weight ideal needed on type-E loci.

## 1. The actual rank-three chart and its normalization

The chart represents the normalized deformation functor of its actual
residual lower-triangular representation. The rank-two Laurent argument
extends without changing the chart. For a lift over a complete local
O'-algebra C, form inside C[t,t^-1] the algebra generated by C and

    r(g)_(ij) t^(w_i-w_j),            w=(1,0,-1).       (RS1)

There are finitely many coefficient ideals, all finitely generated
because C is Noetherian. Every positive-degree entry has coefficient
in m_C. A balanced monomial decomposes into balanced atoms of length
at most three. The vertex ideal v consequently satisfies

    m_C^a subset v^a intersect C subset m_C^ceil(a/3).

Every fixed graded piece is a finite C-module with its original
complete module topology. Localization and completion are injective:
as in the rank-two Laurent algebra, an element outside the vertex
has a nonzero Laurent-polynomial reduction, which cannot annihilate
the first nonzero m_C-adic coefficient of another Laurent polynomial.
Thus C is closed. Conjugation by diag(t,1,t^-1) preserves J3 exactly,
as well as the marking and the ordered equations. The original
universal property, after the prescribed O' base change, therefore
maps B into closed C and A into this finite Laurent algebra. Evaluation
at t=1 is used only on that algebra. The original chart property
then gives the unique local map R->C. No new deformation ring or
residue identification is introduced.

The normalization itself adds no obstruction. After normalizing the
unitary form at c, put D=r(g0)-J3 r(g0)^t J3. It is skew-adjoint for
J3, has trace and determinant zero, and its residual eigenvalues are
zeta_0,0,-zeta_0 with zeta_0 a unit. Its characteristic polynomial is
T(T^2-zeta^2); Hensel gives the root zeta with the prescribed residue.
The three eigenvalues remain distinct in every Artinian lift. Their
spectral lines are direct summands; skew-adjointness pairs the plus
and minus lines and makes the middle line orthogonal to them.
Normalize their pairings by units, using a square root with residue
one for the middle line. Only 2 and zeta are inverted. A strict
orthogonal basis change now makes D=diag(zeta,0,-zeta).

The remaining normalized gauge group is the one-dimensional torus
diag(u,1,u^-1). In particular this argument includes ell=3 and does
not divide by 3. Its infinitesimal stabilizer is zero: an invariant
commutes with the marked diagonal matrix, and hence is diagonal.
If an adjacent lower entry is nonzero, polarization supplies its
opposite adjacent lower entry and all three diagonal entries agree.
If only the corner lower entry is nonzero, the first and third agree.
In either case c negates the scalar entries or paired diagonal
entries, forcing zero since ell is odd.

## 2. The parity presentation on the type-E locus

Suppose the original odd-entry ideal E is contained in x. Then the
residual representation is

    r0(g)=[[Psi(g),0,0],[0,1,0],[a(g),0,Psi(g)^-1]],  (RS2)

with a nonzero strict corner cocycle. Conjugation by
K=diag(1,-1,1) fixes this residual pair and acts on the actual chart
with order two. It fixes every coefficient in B and changes the sign
of precisely the odd entries. All actions fix the prescribed O'.

Let W=M3(k') be the full unitary adjoint, with c acting by
X |-> -J3 X^t J3. Decompose W=W_even plus W_odd for conjugation
by K; their dimensions are 5 and 4. The original ordinary cone
decomposes accordingly, because the unique local ordered flags are
K-invariant. Indeed a separating local element has three distinct
residual eigenvalues Psi,1,Psi^-1, and its spectral projectors give
the original unique ordered flag.

To justify that last implication over the nonreduced Artinian rings,
put all three elements equal to the separating s in the ordered
product. Chinese remainders give three orthogonal rank-one projectors
E_i for r(s). Next leave only its k-th factor variable. Multiplying
on the left by E_i and on the right by E_j shows
E_i(r(g)-psi_k(g))E_j=0 whenever i>=k and j<=k: all other factors
are unit eigenvalue differences. Taking i=j=k gives the diagonal
character; taking i>j with j<=k<=i kills every lower entry. Hence
the first i spectral lines give exactly the prescribed invariant
flag. This also proves uniqueness and avoids replacing the ordered
product equations by merely their characteristic polynomials.

The even local Borel subspace has dimension 4 (the corner Borel
and the middle scalar); the odd one has dimension 2. At each of the
d original real places, the adjoint invariants are the orthogonal
Lie algebra for a symmetric form respecting the corner/middle split.
Their even and odd dimensions are respectively 1 and 2.

The even adjoint constituents are those of the rank-two corner,
plus the middle delta:

    delta, Psi^-2 delta, 1, Psi^2 delta, delta.

The odd constituents are Psi^-1 delta, Psi^-1, Psi delta and Psi.
The original f2a/f2c exclude the cyclotomic quotient in the even
part; f2b excludes it on the restriction of the odd part to G0.
The full Euler inequality is therefore applicable to each part.
Writing h^i_e,h^i_o for the original cone dimensions, it gives

    h^1_e-h^2_e >= 3d,         h^1_o-h^2_o >= 0.       (RS3)

All residual images are finite and continuous and all local subspaces
are invariant, as required by that inequality. The H^0 groups vanish
by the invariant calculation above.

The strict first-order classes are the same cone H^1 groups. The
orthogonal gauge Lie algebra has even dimension 1 and odd dimension
2. The two off-diagonal marking equations remove exactly those two
odd gauge directions: infinitesimally the map is [X,D0], and its
coefficients are units zeta_0 or 2zeta_0. The even gauge line remains.
Hence the actual normalized tangent dimensions are

    a=h^1_e+1,                  g=h^1_o.             (RS4)

For a small extension, multiplication errors and errors of the ordered
local flags give precisely the original ordinary cone H^2 obstruction.
The form and marking normalizations above restore a corrected lift
without further obstructions. Everything commutes with K. Since 2 is
a unit, taking even and odd parts is exact, and the usual injection
of the dual minimal relation space into the obstruction space applies
separately in the two parities. Thus the minimal numbers of even and
odd relations are at most h^2_e and h^2_o.

Choose parity eigenvectors lifting a basis of the relative cotangent
space. They give a parity-equivariant surjection from O'[[X,Y]] to R.
The kernel is parity-stable; average chosen relation lifts with their
K-transforms and apply Nakayama to obtain homogeneous parity relation
generators. Minimality says that every linear coefficient is in m_O'.
Pad the even relation list with zero equations to length b=a-(3d+1),
and the odd list to length g. Equations (RS3)-(RS4) permit this.
We obtain the literal original presentation

    R=O'[[X_1,...,X_a,Y_1,...,Y_g]]/(F_1,...,F_b,G_1,...,G_g),
    a-b=3d+1,       F_i even,       G_i odd.           (RS5)

Every entry has its original parity and B maps to the even subring.
The image of (Y) is exactly E: the odd entries generate the odd
part as an ideal, because the ring is topologically generated by
entries and the even ordered characters. More explicitly, K acts
identically on R/E, so every anti-invariant element maps to its own
negative there and hence maps to zero. Conversely every odd series
in the presentation is divisible by an odd variable, which puts
every odd entry in (Y). Thus E=(Y) on the original ring, including
its nilpotents.

## 3. There is an odd variable

It is essential that g>0 in (RS5). This follows from the original
nonvanishing f4, not from a presumed geometric dimension.

Inside W_odd the lower odd root space

    U=span(E10,E21)

is Gamma-stable and splits as k'(Psi^-1 delta) plus k'(Psi^-1).
The quotient has G0-character Psi in both dimensions and therefore
has no global invariants, since Psi(g0)^2!=1. At each local place,
the ordinary odd Borel meets U in zero and maps isomorphically onto
W_odd/U. To check this without changing the local object, write its
first eigenline as e0+lambda e2. The odd stabilizer equations are

    X10+lambda X12=0,           X21=lambda X01.

Their intersection with span(E10,E21) is zero. The short exact
sequence of original ordinary cone complexes consequently injects

    H^1_C(Gamma,k'(Psi^-1 delta);0)
        into H^1_C(Gamma,W_odd;(b_v)_odd).            (RS6)

The possible preceding H^0 term vanishes by the quotient invariant
calculation. Original f4 makes the source nonzero, also after finite
residue extension. Thus g=h^1_o>0.

## 4. A determinant section with the exact connectedness bound

Every odd relation can be written

    G_i=sum_j M_ij Y_j,             M_ij even,        (RS7)

by selecting one odd variable from each monomial. Minimality makes
every constant coefficient of M lie in m_O'. Put Delta=det M and
Qsharp=(Delta) in R. The adjugate identity gives Delta Y_j=0 in R;
therefore every prime outside E contains Qsharp.

We prove the needed connectedness statement directly. Let
A0=O'[[X_1,...,X_a,Y_1,...,Y_g]], so dim A0=a+g+1. Add a generic
g-by-g matrix Z and a vector v of length g. The universal incidence
ideal

    H=(Z v, det Z)                                  (RS8)

in A0[Z,v] has prime radical and codimension g. Here is a proof
valid over A0, not just over its fraction field. On v_i!=0, its g
linear equations solve one whole column of Z; the resulting chart
is a polynomial domain with v_i inverted, and det Z is automatic.
These charts have nonempty pairwise intersections, so their union
is irreducible with prime closure. Every point of V(H) with v=0
also belongs to that closure: over its residue field choose a
nonzero kernel vector u for its singular matrix and use the family
(Z,t u), specializing at t=0. Thus the radical is precisely that
prime. The same charts give generic relative transcendence degree
g^2 over A0 and hence codimension g.

This prime radical remains prime after completing at the vertex.
Indeed it is homogeneous in the new Z,v variables. Its graded
quotient is a domain with finite homogeneous A0-modules, and A0
is complete. The vertex completion is their product by degree;
the first nonzero homogeneous terms of two nonzero series have
nonzero product. This proves it is a domain. A fixed power of the
polynomial radical lies in H, so the completed ideal has the same
prime radical. The closed-point dimension formula and completion
give dimension

    dim(A0[[Z,v]]/H)=a+g+1+g^2.

Now impose Z_ij=M_ij, v_j=Y_j and F_i=0. These are
g^2+g+b elements of the maximal ideal, and their quotient is exactly
R/(Delta). Krull's height theorem gives every component dimension
at least

    a+g+1+g^2-(g^2+g+b)=a-b+1=3d+2.                (RS9)

For connectedness, the complete ambient incidence ring has one
irreducible component of dimension a+g+1+g^2. Stacks Tag 0ECR,
with cohomological dimension bounded by the number g^2+g+b of
cuts, says that deleting a closed subset of dimension less than
3d+1 leaves the section connected. Equivalently the graph of its
minimal primes with intersection dimension at least 3d+1 is
connected. No regular-sequence hypothesis, smoothness, or generic
transversality is required. The radical was used only for this
dimension/connectivity statement; the actual ring R and the actual
element Delta remain unchanged.

## 5. Every type-E prime on this section is genuinely thick

Let P be a type-E prime of R containing Delta, and put Q=R/P.
The matrix M over the domain Q has zero determinant. Over its fraction
field choose a nonzero kernel vector and clear its finitely many
denominators, obtaining 0!=nu in Q^g. The derivation

    D0(f)=sum_j nu_j (partial f/partial Y_j) mod P    (RS10)

on the presentation ring kills every even F_i, because all Y vanish
modulo P. It kills G=M Y because M nu=0. It therefore descends to
an O'-derivation D:R->Q. It kills every even element, every ordered
local character and every even matrix entry, but not all Y_j.
Since E is generated by odd entries and E maps to zero in Q, some
odd entry has nonzero derivative.

Define the following actual Q-valued representation, rather than
a dual-number representation:

    rhat(g)=[[r00bar(g),D(r01(g)),r02bar(g)],
             [0,r11bar(g),0],
             [r20bar(g),D(r21(g)),r22bar(g)]].        (RS11)

The Leibniz rule applied to r(gh)=r(g)r(h), with odd entries zero
modulo P and even derivatives zero, proves rhat(gh)=rhat(g)rhat(h).
Thus it is an extension of the original middle character by the
original corner. Applying the same block/derivation operation to
the original ordered product gives exactly its ordinary product.
Applying it after J3-transpose gives the second original ordered
product on g* with reversed local characters. Both equations are
retained; polarization of rhat itself is not asserted or needed by
the original Thick contract.

This extension is nonsplit after every injective map Q->L to a field.
If it split, there would be an eigenvector (z0,1,z2) for the middle
character. Compare its equations at g0 and g0*. The original marking
makes the off-diagonal entries and their derivatives agree at these
two elements; the middle entries also agree. The corner diagonal
differences are zeta and -zeta, both units. Hence z0=z2=0.
The eigenvector equations then make D(r01(g))=D(r21(g))=0 for every g.
The original polarized identities relate these to the other two odd
entry derivatives, so all odd derivatives vanish, a contradiction.

Finally D(m_R^(N+1)) is contained in m_Q^N by Leibniz. The original
entry continuity therefore implies the original adic continuity of
every entry of rhat. Equations (RS11), both ordered products, and the
field-independent nonsplitting are precisely an original Thick
modification at P.

## 6. The triangular dimension bound needed for Sharp

We give the bound for the actual chart, rather than assuming that
a high-dimensional component is stable. Fixed homogeneous pieces
of A are finite B-modules and have their coefficient-adic topology:
use the weighted presentation and balanced atoms of length at most
three, exactly as in Section 1. Hence entry functions modulo m_B
are locally constant.

At every prime of the closed coefficient fibre, a nonzero lower entry
forces all upper entries to vanish. If its degree is -a and an upper
entry has degree b, their balanced product upper^a lower^b belongs
to m_B, so it is zero in the prime quotient. The lower entry is a
unit in the chart. The diagonal and local characters are residual.
The resulting matrix is

    [[Psi,0,0],[y,1,0],[a,y(g*),Psi^-1]].             (RS12)

Its exact equations are the original Low equations:

    y(gh)=Psi(h)y(g)+y(h),
    a(gh)=Psi(h)a(g)+y(g*)y(h)+Psi(g)^-1 a(h),
    a(g*)=a(g),       y(g0)=y(g0*),
    y(loc_v s)=(Psi(s)-1) alpha_v,
    y((loc_v s)*)=(1-Psi(s)^-1) beta_v,
    a(loc_v s)=(Psi(s)-Psi(s)^-1) gamma_v
                     -(1-Psi(s)^-1) alpha_v beta_v. (RS13)

For clarity, the upper entries may be nilpotent in the fibre itself;
we used prime quotients only to bound Krull dimension, and did not
silently identify the original fibre with its reduction.

The linear space of allowed y has dimension at most C0. The function
y/Psi is the original G0-cocycle with coefficient Psi^-1 and local
trivializations at both sets of places. It maps to the group bounded
by `F.Ok.b1`. Its possible kernel consists of y=t(Psi-1), and the
marking forces t(Psi(g0)-Psi(g0)^-1)=0, hence t=0. The local
trivializations are unique by f3. For a fixed y, differences of two
solutions a lie in the original strict Psi^-2 delta cocycle space,
of dimension at most C0 by `b1p`.

This bounds the actual coefficient fibre by 2C0. Here is a generic-
point justification of the affine-fibre argument. Every entry function
has finite image modulo m_B, so fix its finite partition of G0. Over
the field generated by the y values, the equations for the finitely
many a values are linear and their right sides are in that field.
If they have a solution in the prime quotient's fraction field, they
have a solution in the first field, by linear algebra; infinitely
many equations reduce to a finite row space. The homogeneous solution
space has dimension at most C0. Thus the entry-field transcendence
degree is at most C0+C0. The finite-type local dimension formula and
the prescribed completion give

    dim R/m_B R <= 2C0.                              (RS14)

Let T_- be the upper-entry ideal in R. On R/T_- the three diagonal
entries are characters. Polarization determines the last from the
first and makes the middle character anticyclotomic. Use the same
at most C0+1 global norm-character detectors as in the rank-two proof,
and d inertia generators for the first diagonal character. For the
middle character only d inertia generators are needed, because its
anticyclotomic identity is already given. Include the uniformizer.
There are at most 2d+C0+2 cuts. In any prime containing these cuts,
the original gen/cl argument on finite ell-group quotients makes
both residual-trivial characters one. The third is then residual
too. The local characteristic polynomial and the prescribed residual
ordering force the ordered local characters to be residual. Hence
the cuts kill m_B up to radical. By (RS14) and the height theorem,

    dim R/T_- <= 2d+3C0+2 < 3d.                      (RS15)

The strict inequality is within the original d>3C0+7 range; no
weaker arithmetic-frame hypothesis is substituted.

## 7. Completion of the Sharp construction

In the type-E case, (RS9) and (RS15) make every minimal prime over
Qsharp off both triangular loci: the lower-entry ideal is already
the unit ideal in the chart. A minimal prime outside E is original
type I. A minimal prime containing E is type E and is Thick by
Section 5. The determinant section therefore has exactly the required
dichotomy and connectedness, and it contains every prime outside E
by the adjugate identity.

If x is outside E, set Qsharp=0. The same full adjoint argument,
without splitting parity, gives a presentation with tangent dimension
h1+1 and at most h2 relations. The local ordinary Lie dimension is
6, the real invariant dimension is 3, and the same constituent
exclusions give h1-h2>=3d. Thus the presentation deficit is at least
3d+1. Grothendieck connectedness supplies the required graph and
component dimensions. No prime of this local chart contains E,
because an original odd entry is a unit. Equation (RS15) then makes
every component type I. This proves all four clauses of Sharp.

## Exact next obligation

The local determinant section and the type-E finite-weight ideal
are the two inputs for the original rank-three local propagation.
To obtain the full global theorem one must also supply actual Nice
curves at every required large intersection, connectedness of the
original closed coefficient-fibre open with density of its non-E
part, and the original P23 transfer along actual Thick links. These
are the next targets. The construction here does not omit them or
replace them by a rank-two statement.
