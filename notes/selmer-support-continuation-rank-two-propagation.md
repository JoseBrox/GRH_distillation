# Rank-two propagation through the original normalized components

## Exact frontier and scope

**Classification: proposed closed original rank-two component-propagation
subobligation inside W04; independently reviewed.** This note proves
the literal `PropagationTheoremGL2`, using the actual rank-two curve
theorem from `selmer-support-continuation-rank-two-curve.md`. It also
checks the rank-two transfer from the already established arithmetic
P1 contract to the original normalized P1 condition.

Keep the original odd prime ell, coefficient DVR O, frame F, normalized
space S, and integer C0. Retain every hypothesis of `F.Ok C0` and
`F.Big C0`; write d=sum_v d_v. For an ideal I of B_S retain the
original finite-weight condition and normalized propagation contract:

    sqrt(I+lambda_0)=m_(B_S);
    if q is an original Nice type-I curve in a chart and I subset q,
       then I subset P for every prime P subset q.        (RP1)

The second line is exactly `P1'`. Retain `SeedIn`: a stable prime of
A_S lies over a coefficient prime Z of dimension 1+2d containing I.
Precisely, Z need only be contained in that stable prime's coefficient
contraction, as treated in (RP11). The coefficient DVR may have equal
characteristic ell. Its uniformizer, including in equal characteristic,
is one of the generators of lambda_0. The abstract curve lemma used
below assumes only `FinLam`, `Ok` and `Big`; it does not assume
`ModIn`. The latter supplies `FinLam` only in the arithmetic application.

**Frontier theorem.** Then I is contained in every stable prime of A_S.
This is exactly `AllGood S I`, with the original prime quantifiers.

For the actual arithmetic application, take I=Phi(J), with J the same
ordinary Hecke ideal used in `ModIn`. The preceding two continuation
notes give `SeedIn`, its starting chart, and finite-weight transfer.
Section 4 below supplies the literal rank-two `P1'` from the proved
arithmetic P1 theorem and the original `ModIn.n4`. Thus no additional
normalized propagation hypothesis is silently added to that application.

The theorem controls support. It gives neither a uniform nilpotence
exponent nor a quantitative integral lattice estimate. Rank three still
requires its separate type-E and thick-link arguments. W04 remains open.

## 1. The local chart represents the actual normalized deformations

Fix a closed point x of the original coefficient fibre at which an
original lower entry is nonzero. Let L be any original chart, with
coefficient extension O'/O and its specified residue embedding, and
put R=L.R and k'=k(O'). Its residual representation has form

    r0(g)=[[Psi(g),0],[a(g),Psi(g)^-1]],             (RP2)

where a is a nonzero normalized strict cocycle. This is the actual
closed-fibre description proved in the rank-two curve note.

The literal local universal property is a property of maps from A_S.
We check that it represents precisely the normalized deformations of
(RP2), so that its tangent and obstruction theory below applies to R
itself.

Let C be a complete Noetherian local O'-algebra with residue k', and
let r_C,psi_C be a continuous normalized lift of the prescribed residual
pair. Let U be the ideal generated by all upper entries of r_C. Since
the residual pair is lower triangular, U<=m_C. A prescribed lower entry
is a unit. Introduce one Laurent variable t and form the actual algebra

    D_C=C[t^-1,U t]  subset C[t,t^-1].              (RP3)

Replace each upper entry by its product with t and each lower entry by
its product with t^-1. The diagonal entries and local characters are
unchanged. This is conjugation by diag(t,1); it preserves the
J_2-polarization because that diagonal matrix scales J_2 by the scalar
t. The rank-two marking and ordered ordinary equations are preserved.
The vertex ideal is

    v=m_C+(all nonzero-degree elements of D_C).

Its quotient is k'. A balanced nonconstant monomial contains a factor
from U, so

    m_C^r subset v^r cap C subset m_C^ceil(r/2).

Thus C is a closed subring of the vertex completion. This construction
does not require C to be a domain. To check injectivity before completing,
an element outside v has nonzero constant term modulo m_C; it is a
nonzero Laurent polynomial modulo m_C. If it killed a nonzero Laurent
polynomial, reduction at the first nonzero m_C-adic coefficient of the
latter would contradict injectivity of multiplication by a nonzero
polynomial over the residue field. Localization at v is therefore
injective on D_C, and Krull intersection makes completion injective.

Base change the original finite weighted power-series presentation to
the prescribed finite unramified O'/O. Its residue field is now k', so
the original normalized universal property applies to the vertex lift.
It gives a map from S.R to the vertex completion. The degree-zero ring
B_S maps into the closed subring C, and A_S maps into D_C. Only this
finite Laurent algebra is evaluated at t=1; no arbitrary completed
series is evaluated. The resulting A_S->C has precisely the original
residue point x. The original chart property gives a unique R->C.

Conversely every local O'-algebra map R->C gives such a normalized lift.
The maps are inverse because matrix entries and ordered characters
generate A_S over the adically closed coefficient ring. Continuity is
the fixed-degree module continuity proved in the preceding note. Hence
R represents the literal normalized deformation functor of (RP2), with
the original coefficient and residue data.

## 2. The decisive presentation deficit

Let W=M_2(k') be the full unitary adjoint representation. On G0 it acts
by conjugation by r0; on the distinguished complex conjugation c it acts
by

    X |-> -J_2 X^t J_2.

At each local group use the Lie algebra b_v of the original ordered
ordinary flag. That flag is unique. Indeed choose a local element s
whose two residual characters are distinct, as in F.Ok.f3. In every
Artinian lift their difference remains a unit, and

    E=(r(s)-psi_2(s))/(psi_1(s)-psi_2(s))

is an idempotent of rank one. The ordered two-factor equations give
r(g)E=psi_1(g)E and (1-E)r(g)=psi_2(g)(1-E) for every local g.
Thus its image is exactly the ordered ordinary line, with no new flag
choice or loss of infinitesimal equations. In particular dim b_v=3.

The global invariants W^Gamma vanish. An invariant commutes with
r0(g0)-r0(g0*)=diag(zeta,-zeta), where 2zeta is a unit. It is therefore
diagonal. Since at least one lower entry a(g) is nonzero, it must be a
scalar. The c-action on a scalar is multiplication by -1, so it is zero.
The original cone H^0 is a subspace of these invariants and is also zero.

The cyclotomic quotient hypothesis required by the original Euler
inequality is verified as well. The adjoint representation has a
filtration with one-dimensional constituents

    delta,   Psi^-2 delta,   1,   Psi^2 delta.       (RP4)

The trace splitting uses only 2^-1. The lower root, diagonal trace-zero
quotient, and upper root give the remaining three constituents. The
actual F.Ok.f2a and f2c conditions say that none is the cyclotomic
character omega. Hence Hom_Gamma(W,k'(omega))=0. This remains true after
the finite residue extension. No assumption of residual absolute
irreducibility is used.

Write h^i for the dimensions of the original ordinary mapping-cone
groups H^i_C(Gamma,W;(b_v)). The local flags are invariant under their
local groups and r0 has finite continuous image, so every hypothesis
of F.Ok.ec applies. At every original real place, the adjoint action
is the negative transpose for a nondegenerate symmetric form. Its fixed
space is the orthogonal Lie algebra, of dimension one. There are
exactly d such places. Consequently the exact Euler inequality is

    h^2+3d <= h^1+d,     hence h^1-h^2>=2d.          (RP5)

Here are the tangent and obstruction identifications needed to use
this inequality. An infinitesimal deformation of the unitary
representation and its ordered local lines is a global adjoint cocycle
with the local trivializations modulo b_v; strict changes of basis are
exactly the original cone coboundaries. Thus its strict equivalence
classes form H^1_C. Every such first-order class can be normalized at c:
the order-two relation gives a symmetric-form deformation, and 2 being
a unit lets a strict change of basis restore the original form J_2.
The remaining gauge group is O(J_2), whose Lie algebra is

    {diag(t,-t):t in k'},

of dimension one. Its infinitesimal stabilizer is W^Gamma=0.
For rank two the marking imposes no further equation: for every matrix
A, A-J_2 A^t J_2 is already diagonal. It follows that

    dim_k' Tan(R/O')=h^1+1.                         (RP6)

For a small extension, arbitrary lifts of the matrices and local lines
give the usual multiplication-error 2-cocycle and the local flag-error
1-cochains modulo b_v. Their compatibility is exactly the original
mapping-cone 2-cocycle relation. Changing lifts adds a cone coboundary.
Its class in H^2_C tensored with the small kernel is zero exactly when
the lift can be corrected. Once corrected, the same symmetric-form
normalization restores c; the local flags give the original ordered
ordinary equations, and the rank-two marking is automatic. Thus H^2_C
is an obstruction space for this very normalized chart functor.

For completeness, a minimal power-series presentation with N tangent
variables has at most h^2 relations. The dual of its minimal relation
space injects into an obstruction space: otherwise push the universal
small extension out along a nonzero relation functional with zero
obstruction. A lift would split that extension. Changing tangent
variables cannot change the selected relation modulo the maximal ideal
times the relation ideal, because the presentation is minimal and the
coefficient ring is fixed; this contradicts the functional being
nonzero. This is the standard tangent/obstruction relation-count argument,
and it applies also to a possible relation involving the coefficient
uniformizer.

Combining (RP5)-(RP6) gives an actual presentation

    R=O'[[X_1,...,X_N]]/(f_1,...,f_r),
    r<=h^2,                     N-r>=2d+1.         (RP7)

No regular-sequence assertion is made. Only the number of equations is
needed. In particular every irreducible component of Spec R has
dimension at least 2d+2.

## 3. Local component propagation

Grothendieck connectedness applied to (RP7) says that the graph of
minimal primes of R, with an edge when

    dim R/(P+Q)>=2d+1,                              (RP8)

is connected. Precisely, the regular complete power-series ring has
dimension N+1, and the cohomological dimension of an ideal generated by
r elements is at most r. The connectedness bound says that removing a
closed subset of dimension less than N-r leaves its zero locus
connected. A partition of the minimal-prime graph without an edge
would give a disconnection after removing the intersections of those
two unions, contradicting N-r>=2d+1. This is the complete-local-ring
form of Grothendieck connectedness, recorded in Stacks Project Tag
[0ECR](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0ECR).

Every minimal prime of R is stable. The chosen lower entry is a unit,
and the other triangular locus has dimension less than 2d by (RC8)
of the curve note. A component of dimension at least 2d+2 cannot be
contained in that locus. In rank two, off both triangular loci is
equivalent to absolute irreducibility by the two marked spectral
projectors and the two nonzero matrix units.

Suppose one minimal prime P of R contains I. If Q is adjacent to P,
choose a prime Z above P+Q for which dim R/Z>=2d+1. Then I<=Z. The
actual rank-two curve theorem supplies a Nice type-I curve q containing
Z. The original P1 condition (RP1) gives I<=Q. Connectedness of the
graph propagates this conclusion to every minimal prime of R.

The same conclusion follows if a starting support prime P0 containing I
has dimension at least 2d+1. First choose its Nice type-I curve q;
(RP1) gives a good minimal prime below q, and the preceding graph
argument applies. In particular the already constructed stable Hecke
support prime of dimension 2d+2 supplies a good initial chart.

For the literal abstract `SeedIn`, the prime Z need only be contained
in the coefficient contraction of its stable point xi. Here is why
that weaker input also supplies the required initial chart. Choose an
upper entry u and a lower entry v nonzero at xi and put a=uv in B_S.
Then a is outside Z. In rank two the original graded algebra has the
exact localization

    (A_S)_a=(B_S)_a[u,u^-1].                         (RP11)

Indeed each upper entry u' equals (u'v/a)u and each lower entry v'
equals (uv')u^-1; the parenthesized factors belong to (B_S)_a.
The distinct degrees of the Laurent powers of the unit u show that
there is no Laurent-polynomial relation over (B_S)_a. The prime
Z(B_S)_a[u,u^-1] therefore contracts to a homogeneous stable prime
eta of A_S with coefficient contraction exactly Z and relative
transcendence degree one. The graded Nakayama argument in Section 5
places eta below a closed point of BB. The closed-point dimension
formula, selected coefficient extension and completion argument from
(RC10) give a chart prime P0 with contraction eta and dimension
dim(B_S/Z)+1=2d+2. Thus the stated proof covers the original `SeedIn`
quantifiers, not just the equality of contractions available for the
constructed arithmetic seed.

## 4. The actual arithmetic P1 transfer in rank two

We verify (RP1) for the original I=Phi(J), using the arithmetic P1
theorem already proved for J. Normalize R/q for a Nice curve q. The
result is a finite complete equicharacteristic DVR k''[[t]], and the
inclusion is injective and local. The original TwLink twists the same
representation and ordered characters by kappa and descends along the
same quotient pi. The original Pair equations are preserved, the
residual determinant is the original prescribed one, and the ordered
local flags exist by the idempotent argument above. This gives the
original arithmetic Point and preserves the kernel q.
For the old inertia equations, the original prime-to-ell exponent
from TwLink and the residual unipotent characteristic polynomial give
the same unit-resultant argument as in the seed-transfer proof. It
kills the residually trivial inertia part before the prescribed scalar
kappa twist. Thus these are the actual original inertia equations,
not just equalities of residual characteristic polynomials.

On the original N_v the ratio of the two normalized characters has
residue one and is nontrivial, since q avoids the coincidence ideal.
The group 1+t k''[[t]] is torsion-free: ell-power torsion is excluded by
(1+u)^(ell^a)=1+u^(ell^a), and prime-to-ell torsion by the simple root
at 1. Thus this ratio has infinite order on some element. Raising that
element to a suitable positive power puts it in any prescribed open
subgroup. The exact TwLink local/cyclotomic identities put its image
inside the original G'=ker(epsilon), and kappa cancels in the ratio.
This proves both inequalities in the original N1 condition.

It also gives a short direct proof of the original N3 condition in
rank two. Given an open coset meeting G', choose its representative
gamma in G' and a sufficiently small open subgroup V. The preceding
argument gives h in V intersect G' with eigenvalues alpha,beta and
alpha/beta of infinite order. In an eigenbasis for h write the matrix
of gamma as M=(m_ij). Up to a nonzero scalar the discriminant of M h^a
is the value at z=(alpha/beta)^a of

    m_11^2 z^2 + (2m_11 m_22-4 det M)z + m_22^2.    (RP9)

This polynomial is not zero: if both square coefficients vanished,
its middle coefficient would be -4 det M, a unit. It therefore cannot
vanish at every one of the infinitely many distinct values z. Some
gamma h^a remains in the specified open coset and has separable
characteristic polynomial. This is N3 with its original open-set
quantifier; no appeal to Zariski density is needed.

We also check the original type-I requirement, namely irreducibility
on G'. More generally it holds on U intersect G' for every open
subgroup U. Replace U by its open normal core. Restriction of a
finite-dimensional irreducible representation to a normal subgroup
is semisimple: the radical of its finite-dimensional image algebra is
stable under the full group, and its image on the irreducible module
must vanish because it is nilpotent. In dimension two, a reducible
restriction is therefore either scalar or a sum of two different
characters permuted by the full group.

The scalar case is impossible by the element with infinite eigenvalue
ratio in U intersect G'. In the other case let H be the index-two
stabilizer of one line. The representation has a nontrivial quadratic
self-twist xi. Each local ordinary group preserves one of the two
global lines: its ordinary line is an eigenline of the separating
element in U intersect G'. Hence every local ordinary group lies in H.
Reducing the self-twist identity and using the original residual
characters kappa_bar Psi and kappa_bar Psi^-1 forces

    xi=Psi^2 composed with pi.

It follows that Psi^2 is trivial on every local ordinary group,
contrary to the exact F.Ok.f3 condition and the original local
surjectivity in TwLink. This excludes the induced alternative. The
same argument applies after the coefficient and fraction-field
extensions because all maps used are injective.

Thus the arithmetic curve satisfies N1, TypeI and N3. It satisfies N4
by the original `ModIn.n4`, which quantifies over these very arithmetic
Points. Its universal determinant/character map is the original one
and kills J. Apply the already established arithmetic P1 theorem to
the inverse image in B_det of any P<=q. It yields J contained in that
inverse image. By definition of I=Phi(J), this is I<=P. This proves
the exact normalized P1 transfer in rank two.

## 5. Propagation from one chart to every stable component

The original closed coefficient-fibre open BB consists of points with
a nonzero lower entry. Its representations are exactly the nonzero
normalized strict cocycles (RC6). Let S0 be their finite-dimensional
k-vector space. There is a surjective continuous map

    affine(S0) minus {0}  ->  BB.                    (RP10)

To construct it without assuming a new moduli interpretation, put a
generic linear combination of a basis of S0 in the lower entry and
use the original constant diagonal and local characters. Over the
formal power-series ring in those coefficients it is a normalized
lift of the original diagonal representation. Universality gives a
map from S.R. On B_S it is the residue map and on A_S all entries
are linear polynomials, so it restricts to the polynomial family.
Conversely every field-valued point of BB has entries in S0 after
scalar extension, by the already proved coefficient-fibre calculation;
its point map agrees on every matrix entry and on B_S. This proves
surjectivity on underlying points. The source is nonempty and
irreducible; the initial chart ensures nonemptiness even without
using the separate nonzero-cocycle existence hypothesis. Thus BB is
connected.

Every irreducible component of Spec A_S containing a stable point
meets BB. Its minimal prime eta is homogeneous. One direct proof of
this familiar graded fact is to take the ideal generated by its
homogeneous elements: its quotient has no product of nonzero
homogeneous elements equal to zero, hence is a domain by comparing
highest degrees. This homogeneous ideal is prime and is contained
in the minimal prime eta, so they coincide.

In the graded domain A_S/eta choose a nonzero lower entry. If the
ideal generated by all lower entries lay in the radical of the
coefficient maximal ideal, a power of that lower ideal would lie in
the coefficient maximal ideal. All sufficiently negative homogeneous
pieces would then equal that maximal ideal times themselves. They
are finite coefficient modules, so Nakayama would make them zero.
Nonzero powers of the chosen lower entry give a contradiction. Hence
the component has a lower-entry point in the closed coefficient
fibre. That finite-type fibre has a closed such point, belonging to BB.

There are finitely many components. Call a component good when its
minimal prime contains I. At any closed x in BB, if a good component
passes through x, then every component through x is good. Indeed the
original finite unramified coefficient change and local completion
are faithfully flat. A minimal prime in the chart over the good
component is good. Section 3 makes every chart minimal prime good;
contracting them proves the assertion for every original component
through x. This same reasoning, using (RP7) and the triangular bound,
shows that every component through BB is stable.

Intersect BB with the union of good components and with the union
of the remaining components. These are relatively closed subsets.
They are disjoint: otherwise their intersection, a nonempty locally
closed subset of the finite-type coefficient fibre, would have a
closed point, contradicting the preceding local argument. They cover
BB. The starting chart supplies a good component, so connectedness
of BB forces the second subset to be empty.

Every stable component meets BB and is therefore good. A stable
prime contains a minimal prime, which is itself stable since the
stable locus is open and closed under generalization. Consequently
I is contained in every stable prime. This proves the exact
rank-two propagation theorem and its stated arithmetic application.
