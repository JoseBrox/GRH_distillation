# Tangent comparison through one finite coefficient field

## Result and scope

Three of the remaining deformation-model comparisons admit short proofs:

1. every graded equation point becomes a local point of the completed
   equation ring after one permitted grading rescaling;
2. a derivation on the graded equation algebra lifts uniquely to the
   completed equation ring;
3. the resulting representation cocycle determines that derivation.

The first two statements use the **actual weighted presentation and finite
degree-zero quotient**. The third uses the **actual framed universal
property**. Neither statement constructs these objects. The proof keeps
the original equations and nilpotents; it never replaces an equation
ring by its reduced quotient. All coefficient extensions used below are
finite and depend on the point and tangent vector. No uniform bound on
their degrees, denominators, or integral Selmer lengths is inferred.

A general marking calculation also improves the previous base-point
calculation. At every marked point, a normalized coboundary has primitive
in the one-dimensional diagonal torus. Consequently two-sided
cohomological rigidity implies the required tangent dimension at most
one. At the base point the
torus acts trivially, so the preceding nonpositive-weight vanishing
gives the required cotangent spanning once the local finite condition
is identified. The global and local arithmetic suppliers remain open.

## 1. Algebraic tangents of a complete local ring are continuous

Let \(\mathcal O\) be the ring of integers of a finite extension of
\(\mathbb Q_\ell\), with uniformizer \(\pi\) and residue field
\(k\). Let \((R,\mathfrak m)\) be a complete Noetherian local
\(\mathcal O\)-algebra with residue field \(k\). Fix an
\(\mathcal O\)-algebra map
\[
 f:R\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell,
 \qquad |f(x)|<1\quad(x\in\mathfrak m),
 \tag{1}
\]
and put \(\mathfrak p=\ker f\).

**Finite-neighbourhood lemma.** The module \(R/\mathfrak p^2\)
is finite over \(\mathcal O\). Every \(\mathcal O\)-linear
derivation \(D\) at \(f\) has image in a finite extension of the
coefficient field and is continuous for the \(\mathfrak m\)-adic
topology on \(R\) and the \(\ell\)-adic topology on the target.

**Proof.** Every \(r\in R\) is an element of \(\mathcal O\)
plus an element of \(\mathfrak m\), so \(|f(r)|\le1\).
Thus \(R/\mathfrak p\), identified with its image under \(f\),
is integral over \(\mathcal O\). If a prime of \(R\) contains
\((\pi)+\mathfrak p^2\), it contains \(\mathfrak p\), and
its quotient is an integral domain algebraic over \(k\). It is
therefore a field, so that prime is \(\mathfrak m\). Hence
\[
 \mathfrak m^N\subseteq(\pi)+\mathfrak p^2
 \tag{2}
\]
for some \(N\). The quotient \(C=R/\mathfrak p^2\) is complete,
and its maximal-ideal topology equals its \(\pi\)-adic topology
by (2). The vector space \(C/\pi C\) is finite over \(k\).
Lifting finitely many generators modulo \(\pi\) and successively
correcting the error by multiples of \(\pi\) proves that these
lifts generate \(C\) over \(\mathcal O\): the coefficient
series converge because \(\mathcal O\) is complete. This proves
the asserted finiteness without a flatness assumption.

The derivation identity gives \(D(\mathfrak p^2)=0\), so \(D\)
factors through the finite module \(C\). Its values lie in the
\(\mathcal O\)-span of finitely many algebraic numbers, and hence
in one finite extension. In particular there is \(M_D<\infty\)
with \(|D(r)|\le M_D\) for all \(r\in R\). Raising (2) to the
\(n\)-th power gives
\[
 |D(r)|\le |\pi|^nM_D
       \qquad(r\in\mathfrak m^{Nn}),
 \tag{3}
\]
because every term containing \(\mathfrak p^2\) is killed by
\(D\). This proves continuity. The same finite-module argument
also places \(f(R)\) in a finite extension. \(\square\)

The constants and extension in this argument are **pointwise**, not
uniform in a family. Formula (3) supplies continuity, not the missing
arithmetic order bound.

## 2. Lifting from the graded algebra to its completion

Give the variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_d\) integer weights. Put
\(P=\mathcal O[[z_1,\ldots,z_d]]\), and let \(J\) be an
ideal stable under taking every weight component of a formal series.
Let \(R=P/J\). Write \(P_j\) for the formal series all of whose
monomials have weight \(j\), and define
\[
 A=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb Z}A_j\subset R,
 \qquad A_j=\operatorname{image}(P_j\to R).
 \tag{4}
\]
An element of \(A\) has finitely many weights; its individual
weight components need not be polynomials. The sum in (4) is direct
because \(J\) is stable under weight components.

Let \(Q\subset A\) be a homogeneous ideal generated by finitely
many elements, put \(S=R/QR\), and assume
\[
                  (A/Q)_0\text{ is finite over }\mathcal O.
 \tag{5}
\]
Let \(f:S\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) be a local point as
in (1). Let \(D:A\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) be an
\(\mathcal O\)-linear derivation at the restriction of \(f\)
such that \(D(Q)=0\).

**Lifting theorem.** There is a unique derivation
\(\widetilde D:S\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) at \(f\)
whose pullback to \(A\) is \(D\). It is continuous, and
\(f(S)\) and \(\widetilde D(S)\) lie in one finite coefficient
extension.

**Proof.** First,
\[
                         A=A_0[z_1,\ldots,z_d].
 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, for a fixed weight, the exponent vectors of that weight have
finitely many minimal elements under coordinatewise order (Dickson's
lemma). Any other such vector differs from one of the minimal ones
by a nonnegative vector of weight zero. Partitioning the monomials
of a homogeneous series accordingly expresses it as a finite sum of
monomials times weight-zero series. This proves (6) also after
passing to the homogeneous quotient.

The map
\[
 \tau:A\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2),
 \qquad \tau(a)=f(a)+\epsilon D(a),
 \tag{7}
\]
annihilates \(Q\). By (5), \(\tau(A_0)\) is a finite
\(\mathcal O\)-module. Choose one finite extension \(E\)
containing the two coordinates of its module generators and of all
\(\tau(z_i)\). Formula (6) puts the whole image in
\(E[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\).

The scalar coordinates of this image have absolute value at most
one. Choose an integer \(b\ge0\) large enough that the complete
\(\mathcal O_E\)-algebra
\[
             C_b=\mathcal O_E+\epsilon\pi_E^{-b}\mathcal O_E
 \tag{8}
\]
contains \(\tau(A_0)\) and every \(\tau(z_i)\). Equation (6)
then gives \(\tau(A)\subset C_b\). The ideal generated by the
\(\tau(z_i)\) is topologically nilpotent: their scalar
coordinates lie in \(\mathfrak m_E\), and a product can contain
at most one \(\epsilon\)-term. Thus the substitution
\(z_i\mapsto\tau(z_i)\) defines a continuous map
\(P\to C_b\).

This substitution agrees with \(\tau\) on every homogeneous
series. To see this without presupposing continuity of \(D\),
truncate at total degree \(n\). A homogeneous tail can be written
as a finite sum \(\sum_{|\alpha|=n}z^\alpha H_\alpha\), where
each \(H_\alpha\) is homogeneous. Their images under \(\tau\)
all belong to the bounded lattice (8), so the image of the tail
tends to zero. For an element of \(J\), every weight component
is in \(J\) and hence has image zero. A finite sum of weight
components captures all terms of degree less than \(n\); taking
\(n\to\infty\) shows that the substitution kills \(J\).
It therefore descends to \(R\), and then to \(S\) because it
kills the generators of \(Q\).

The scalar component equals \(f\): both maps are bounded,
continuous, and agree on the dense polynomial image. Its second
component is the required derivation. Every other derivation on
\(S\) at \(f\) is continuous by the finite-neighbourhood lemma,
so density proves uniqueness. \(\square\)

This proves the source's derivation-lifting interface from its weighted
presentation and its existing finite degree-zero condition.

### Every graded point can be moved into the local region

Let now \(\xi:A/Q\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) be an
arbitrary point, before imposing the local-point condition. The
degree-zero ring \(A_0\) is local, with maximal ideal
\(\mathfrak m_R\cap A_0\) and residue field \(k\). Indeed, a
weight-zero series with unit constant coefficient has a weight-zero
formal inverse. Every other weight-zero element lies in
\(\mathfrak m_R\) and cannot be a unit. Therefore
\(B=(A/Q)_0=A_0/(Q\cap A_0)\) is a finite **local**
\(\mathcal O\)-algebra whenever such a point exists.

Every element of \(\xi(B)\) is integral, and hence has nonnegative
valuation. Moreover \(B/\pi B\) is a finite-dimensional local
\(k\)-algebra, so for some \(n\)
\(\mathfrak m_B^n\subseteq\pi B\). Consequently
\[
                v(\xi(b))>0\qquad(b\in\mathfrak m_B),
 \tag{8a}
\]
where \(v(0)=+\infty\) and \(v(\pi)=1\). This proves the
strict assertion at every point, without assuming that a morphism
to the field \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) is local in the
ring-theoretic sense.

Set \(a_i=\xi(z_i)\), omitting indices with \(a_i=0\). If
\(w_i=0\), (8a) already gives \(v(a_i)>0\). For
\(w_i>0>w_j\), the monomial
\(z_i^{-w_j}z_j^{w_i}\) has weight zero and positive total degree.
It is in \(\mathfrak m_B\), so
\[
       -w_jv(a_i)+w_iv(a_j)>0,
       \qquad -\frac{v(a_i)}{w_i}<-\frac{v(a_j)}{w_j}.
 \tag{8b}
\]
Thus the open interval
\[
 \max_{w_i>0}\left(-\frac{v(a_i)}{w_i}\right)
       <r<
 \min_{w_j<0}\left(-\frac{v(a_j)}{w_j}\right)
 \tag{8c}
\]
is nonempty, interpreting an absent left or right constraint as
\(-\infty\) or \(+\infty\). Choose rational \(r\) in it
and \(t\in\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell^\times\) with
\(v(t)=r\), for example a suitable rational power of \(\pi\).
Then \(v(t^{w_i}a_i)>0\) for every coordinate. Zero coordinates
stay zero. If all nonzero weights have one sign, only one endpoint
constrains \(r\); if no such coordinate exists, take \(r=0\).
This includes no variables and weight-zero variables.

The grading action sends \(\xi\) to
\(\xi_t(a)=t^j\xi(a)\) for \(a\in A_j\). It preserves
\(Q\), because \(Q\) is homogeneous. Its degree-zero image is
unchanged and finite over \(\mathcal O\); with its finitely many
coordinate values it lies in one finite extension \(E\).
Formula (6) and the coordinate inequalities put \(\xi_t(A)\)
in \(\mathcal O_E\). The homogeneous-tail substitution already
proved, with no nilpotent part, now gives a map
\(f:S\to\mathcal O_E\) extending **exactly** \(\xi_t\).
The images of \(\pi,z_1,\ldots,z_d\) have positive valuation,
so \(f\) is the required local point.

The point is preserved up to precisely this grading action, which is
the action allowed by the pinned source contract. No unscaled
extension of the original \(\xi\) is asserted. Tangents are
transported by \(D_t(a)=t^jD(a)\) on \(A_j\); this is a linear
isomorphism between the tangent spaces at \(\xi\) and \(\xi_t\),
preserves the equation-annihilating condition, and preserves whether
the positive or negative coordinates all vanish. Hence the
one-dimensional tangent conclusion below applies to the original
two-sided graded point after this exact transport. Every degree-zero
trace element satisfies \(\xi_t(a)=\xi(a)\), so the trace values
and their valuations in the numerical length theorem are unchanged.
The rescaling compares generic points and tangents; the original
integral equation algebra and conormal module are retained.

## 3. The universal matrix entries determine the tangent

Suppose \(S\) represents the actual framed deformation problem,
with universal representation \(\rho\), fixed component character,
and fixed residual representation. Its matrix entries topologically
generate \(S\) over \(\mathcal O\). Here is a proof using only
the universal property at the residual dual numbers.

If the entry differences from their chosen residual lifts did not
span \(\mathfrak m_S/(\mathfrak m_S^2+\pi S)\), a nonzero
linear functional annihilating them would give two distinct maps
\(S\to k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\) with the same matrix
entries and hence the same framed representation. This contradicts
uniqueness in the representing property. Choose finitely many entry
differences spanning this finite-dimensional space. Nakayama gives
generators of \(\mathfrak m_S\) together with \(\pi\), and
successive approximation gives a surjection from the corresponding
formal power-series ring onto \(S\).

For a derivation \(\delta\) at \(f\), define
\[
 c_\delta(g)=\delta(\rho(g))\,f(\rho(g))^{-1},
 \tag{9}
\]
where \(\delta\) is applied entrywise to the matrix component.
Differentiating the representation identity gives the usual adjoint
cocycle, including the inverse-transpose component. It is continuous
by the finite-neighbourhood lemma. If \(c_\delta=0\), then
\(\delta\) kills every matrix entry. It kills their polynomial
algebra, and continuity and density give \(\delta=0\).
Thus (9) is a **linear injection**, so it preserves all linear
relations needed for the dimension comparison.

Differentiating the original marking and the equations \(\rho(g)=1\)
gives the actual normalized cocycle and the prescribed killed-element
conditions. The finite local cohomology condition still needs its
arithmetic local-model comparison; continuity alone does not give it.

## 4. One marking identity at every point

Let \(J_3\) be the antidiagonal permutation matrix and
\(R(X)=J_3X^tJ_3\). Consider
\(\mathrm{GL}_3\rtimes\{\pm1\}\), where \(-1\) acts by
\(M\mapsto J_3M^{-t}J_3\). Retain marks \(c,g_1\) with
\(c^2=1\), component signs \(-1,+1\), and matrix component
\(\rho(c)=I\). Set \(M=\rho(g_1)\) and
\(g_1^*=cg_1^{-1}c\). Then
\(\rho(g_1^*)=R(M)\). The actual marking is
\[
 (M-R(M))_{12}=(M-R(M))_{21}=0,
 \qquad \zeta=M_{11}-M_{33}\ne0.
 \tag{10}
\]
The other entries cancel automatically, giving the exact identity
\[
 M-R(M)=\zeta T,
 \qquad T=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,-1).
 \tag{11}
\]

For a coboundary \(c_X(g)=X-\operatorname{Ad}(\rho(g))X\),
the differentiated marking says
\[
 X+R(X)=0,\qquad [X,\zeta T]_{12}=[X,\zeta T]_{21}=0.
 \tag{12}
\]
Over a field of characteristic zero, the first equation gives
\[
 X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0\\ d&0&-b\\0&-d&-a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The next two equations are \(-\zeta b=\zeta d=0\), so
\(X=aT\). Conversely every \(aT\) satisfies (12). This proof
also holds over any commutative ring in which \(2\) and \(\zeta\)
are units. Neither hypothesis may be silently removed integrally.

Combining the lifting theorem, the faithful cocycle map and (12):

- at a two-sided point where all relevant cocycles are coboundaries,
  the tangent space has dimension at most one;
- at the distinguished toral point, the entire adjoint action fixes
  \(T\), so every normalized coboundary is zero;
- if the tangent cocycles satisfy the previously proved nonpositive
  finite-Selmer condition and entry-weight compatibility, all nonpositive equation-annihilating
  derivations at the base vanish. Cotangent duality gives the required
  spanning of the nonpositive equation classes.

These are implications under the stated arithmetic and presentation
hypotheses. The actual weighted model, its initial chart and finite
quotient, the local finite condition, non-diagonal
two-sided cohomological rigidity, and the uniform arithmetic budget
remain suppliers. The coefficient-field and matrix arguments are in
characteristic zero, including \(\ell=2\); the source model's
separate odd-prime hypothesis is retained.
