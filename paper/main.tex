\begin{abstract}
We develop a conventional mathematical reconstruction of the announced
proof of the Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet $L$-functions.
A one-sided Mellin argument reduces the conclusion to upper bounds for
fixed positive combinations of smoothed prime sums on arbitrarily large
multiplicative intervals. A uniform quasi-Riemann hypothesis supplies
simultaneous auxiliary quadratic characters. The arithmetic arguments
include a short algebraic $K$-theory bound, explicit Selmer comparisons,
integral polynomial duality, and normalized theta measures. The analytic
arguments establish local orbital identities, an automorphic multiplicity
comparison, radial differential identities, and Jacquet adjointness.

The central uniform arithmetic estimate remains unproved. In particular,
the complete integrated radial construction, global orbital-energy
bounds, auxiliary-level divisibility, and the remaining Selmer components
must still be assembled with compatible constants. Each conditional result
retains its stated hypotheses. A fixed zero-free region is never substituted
for the critical line. The exposition is arranged by mathematical subject;
the detailed arguments and alternative proofs are retained in separate
sections so that each dependency can be examined on its own.
\end{abstract}
