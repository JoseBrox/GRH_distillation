\begin{abstract}
We develop a conventional mathematical reconstruction of the announced
proof of the Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet $L$-functions.
A one-sided Mellin argument reduces the conclusion to upper bounds for
positive combinations of smoothed prime sums on arbitrarily large
multiplicative intervals. A uniform quasi-Riemann hypothesis supplies
simultaneous auxiliary quadratic characters. The arithmetic arguments
include a short algebraic $K$-theory bound, explicit Selmer comparisons,
integral polynomial duality, and normalized theta measures. The analytic
arguments establish local orbital identities, an automorphic multiplicity
comparison, radial differential and matrix restriction identities, and
Jacquet adjointness. Three commutators recover the full contracted matrix
from one scalar determinant and give its successive restrictions.
A matrix of Schwartz marginals justifies the integration of every finite
residue word. One ordered congruence gives positivity for every fixed
finite mixture of the two residue operations, and a single approximation argument extends it to explicit
classes with finitely many decay and differentiability bounds.
Only a total exponent loss tending to zero is needed at the analytic
endpoint; multiplicative constants, interval shapes and auxiliary positive
character combinations may vary with the accuracy, provided each
combination retains the target character. The local residue comparison retains one positive
normalization independent of the word and the original admissible
parameter conditions.
Finite character averaging isolates an averaged arithmetic discrepancy
and explicit sampled errors. An exact orthogonal splitting expresses
covolume losses through discarded degrees and integral indices.
Classical Galois theorems supply the required compatibility after the
weight and period conventions are fixed.
For ordinary RH, positivity of the complete primitive-character kernel
permits a direct comparison of the larger weights before averaging.
The remaining small weights are controlled by the uniform quasi-Riemann
hypothesis at larger arguments; no symmetry of the weights is needed.
Sharper determinant descent, two weighted height moments and correlated
frame averages reduce the remaining geometric budget.

The central uniform arithmetic estimate remains unproved. In particular,
geometric core lower-slope estimates, normalized global theta
identities, orbital-energy bounds, auxiliary-level divisibility, and the remaining Selmer components
must still be assembled with compatible constants. Each conditional result
retains its stated hypotheses. A fixed zero-free region is never substituted
for the critical line. The exposition is arranged by mathematical subject;
the detailed arguments and alternative proofs are retained in separate
sections so that each dependency can be examined on its own.
\end{abstract}

\part{Alternative methods and their limitations}

The preceding route uses a uniform quasi-Riemann hypothesis for its
Dirichlet character estimates and Theorem~\ref{kt-fourteen} for the coarse
cohomological bound.  The latter also supplies the bounded-order case in
Theorem~\ref{sv-exact-case}, so that application does not require the
finite-field homology and arithmetic-cycle construction below.

The following arguments provide independent routes and possible further
improvements.  They are retained with their precise hypotheses.  In
particular, the proposed improvement by a shorter mean-square estimate
remains conditional on that estimate.  The quadratic determinant method
provides a separate real-zero argument; its real-zero gap is already a
consequence of the uniform quasi-Riemann hypothesis used in the preceding
route.  None of these alternatives supplies the remaining uniform
construction of compatible theta maps.
