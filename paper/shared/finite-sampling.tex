\subsection{Finite comparisons at integer arguments}
\label{shared-finite-sampling}

The arithmetic construction need only be made at finitely many integer
arguments in each chosen interval. The corrected prime sums vary little
between consecutive integers, so no measurable choice of arithmetic
objects over a real interval is needed.

Let $H$ be a fixed finite linear combination of real parts of the
corrected prime sums. It is locally absolutely continuous and, for
some finite $C\ge0$ depending on that combination,
\begin{equation}\label{shared-sampling-derivative}
                         |H'(x)|\le C(1+\log x)^2
\end{equation}
almost everywhere. Indeed, the derivatives of the prime terms are
$\sum_{m\le x}\Lambda(m)\chi(m)/m$, whose absolute value is at most
$\log x(1+\log x)$. The correcting derivatives are a constant for
a nonprincipal character, and $-\log x+\gamma$ for zeta.
New summands vanish at their integer endpoints, giving continuity.

Fix $h\ge0$. For a finite real table $(B_k)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{shared-discrete-average}
 \begin{split}
 (\mathsf A_hB)(n)&=
  \frac1h\sum_{k=n}^{\lceil e^hn\rceil-1}
       B_k\log\frac{\min\{k+1,e^hn\}}k \quad(h>0),\\
 (\mathsf A_0B)(n)&=B_n.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The coefficients in the first line are positive and sum to one.
This is the exact logarithmic average of the function equal to $B_k$
on $[k,k+1)$. In particular, it retains signs before averaging.

\begin{lemma}[Finite tables suffice]\label{shared-integer-table}
Let $H$ be locally absolutely continuous and satisfy
\eqref{shared-sampling-derivative}. Fix $b>1$,
$h\ge0$ and $0<\theta<1$. Let $N\ge2$ be an integer. Suppose
$H(k)\le B_k$ at every integer
$N\le k\le\lceil e^hN^b\rceil$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{shared-integer-bound}
 \int_N^{N^b}(\mathcal A_hH(x))_+x^{-\theta}\,\frac{dx}{x}
 \le \sum_{n=N}^{\lfloor N^b\rfloor}
       \frac{(\mathsf A_hB(n))_+}{n^{1+\theta}}
       +O_{C,h,\theta}\!\left(N^{-\theta}(1+\log N)^2\right).
\end{equation}
The implicit constant is independent of $N$, $b$ and the table.
No bound on the absolute values of the $B_k$ is required.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $k\le t\le k+1$, integrating
\eqref{shared-sampling-derivative} gives
$H(t)\le B_k+C(1+\log(k+1))^2$.
Put $F=\mathcal A_hH$ and set $d_0=1$,
$d_h=(e^h-1)/h$ for $h>0$. Averaging the cell estimates gives
\[
 F(n)\le\mathsf A_hB(n)+C(1+h+\log(n+1))^2.
\]
This remains valid for $h=0$. Differentiation under the bounded
averaging integral gives
$|F'(x)|\le Cd_h(1+h+\log x)^2$ almost everywhere. Thus, on
$[n,n+1]$,
\[
 F(x)_+\le(\mathsf A_hB(n))_+
                  +C(1+d_h)(1+h+\log(n+1))^2.
\]
Integrate over the cells meeting $[N,N^b]$; each weight is at most
$n^{-1-\theta}$. Write $c=1+h+\log2$ and $\ell=c+\log N$.
The total error is at most $C(1+d_h)$ times
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{(c+\log n)^2}{n^{1+\theta}}
 &\le 2^{1+\theta}\int_N^\infty
                 (c+\log x)^2x^{-1-\theta}\,dx\\
 &=2^{1+\theta}N^{-\theta}
    \left(\frac{\ell^2}{\theta}
          +\frac{2\ell}{\theta^2}+\frac2{\theta^3}\right).
 \end{split}
\]
For the inequality use $x/n\le2$ on each cell and
$c+\log n\le c+\log x$. This proves the stated uniform error.
All averages at integers use only the supplied expanded table.
\end{proof}

Consequently, for each fixed accuracy $j$, one may choose a finite
table $B_{j,N}$ independently on every interval starting at an integer
$N$ in an unbounded set $\mathcal N_j$. It suffices that the actual
comparisons hold at the sampled integers and
\begin{equation}\label{shared-finite-criterion}
 \liminf_{\substack{N\to\infty\\N\in\mathcal N_j}}
 \frac{\log\left(1+\displaystyle
  \sum_{n=N}^{\lfloor N^{b_j}\rfloor}
  \frac{(\mathsf A_{h_j}B_{j,N}(n))_+}{n^{1+\theta_j}}\right)}
 {\log N}=0.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{shared-integer-table} then gives the averaged analytic
criterion. The analytic function, $b_j$, $h_j$ and $\theta_j$ stay
fixed while $N$ grows; the arithmetic tables need not agree where
two intervals overlap. For ordinary RH use the single function $G$.
For Dirichlet GRH use the fixed positive combinations containing the
target character, with $\theta_j\to1/2$.

The simplest version takes $h_j=0$. Its test is just a weighted sum
of positive parts at integers. If local averaging is useful, retain
\eqref{shared-discrete-average}: taking positive parts before it can
lose cancellation. The discrete alternative requires its comparisons
at the stated integers; an almost-everywhere construction does not
automatically provide them.
