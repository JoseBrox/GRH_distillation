\subsection{Congruence on the original row lattice}
\label{sec:flag-row-congruence}

\begin{proposition}[Equality of row labels]
\label{prop:flag-row-label-congruence}
For any two original terms with equal row label, their
target and source lattices agree, and for their rational
representatives \(\gamma,\gamma'\) one has
\[
 (\gamma'\gamma^{-1}-1)(\Lambda\cap X^\perp)
 \subset g\Lambda+(\Lambda\cap X).
\]
Here \(g=g_S(D)\) and \(X\) is the original target
isotropic space. This assertion includes the degenerate
terms; no nonzero-Gram or genericity condition is needed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We use the finite-adelic coordinates of the original
row system, not a newly chosen level. Identify its host
field with \(E\) by the given isomorphism. Its coefficient
ring is \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_E\): indeed the subring
generated by \(\mathcal O_E\) and
\(\widehat{\mathcal O}_F\) is the image of
\(\mathcal O_E\otimes_{\mathcal O_F}\widehat{\mathcal O}_F\),
which is the product of the completed rings of integers.
The reference lattice \(L_f\) is the extension of the
original self-dual rational lattice to this ring.
For any finite-adelic lattice \(M\), the completion of
\(M\cap E^n\) is \(M\). To see this, bound \(M\) between
two integer multiples of a fixed completed full lattice
and apply the Chinese remainder theorem to the resulting
finite quotient. In particular rational lattice membership
and local membership at every finite prime are equivalent.

Write \(A=h_\rho x_u\) and \(A'=h_{\rho'}x_{u'}\) for
the original finite-adelic base representatives of the
two row lattices; the row system gives
\[
 \widehat{\Lambda}=A L_f,\qquad
 \widehat{\Lambda'}=A'L_f
\]
in the given coordinates. The row label records the
target lattice, source lattice and the finite Levi matrix.
Consequently equal labels give the same \(A,A'\) for
both terms. Put
\(k_1=A^{-1}\gamma A'\), \(k_2=A^{-1}\gamma' A'\),
where rational isometries here denote their finite
adelic images. Each preserves \(L_f\) because it sends
the same source lattice onto the target lattice.

Work over \(R=\widehat{\mathcal O}_E/g\widehat{\mathcal O}_E\)
and \(M=L_f/gL_f\). In this paragraph use the source
hermitian convention \(h\) conjugate-linear in the
first variable. Write \(e_a,f_a,z,b_i,b_i^d\) for the
images of the source's \(e_0,f_0,z_0,\beta,\beta^d\)
reference vectors. Set \(U=\sum_a Re_a\). The original
Gram conditions give the following identities in \(M\):
\[
 h(e_a,e_c)=h(f_a,f_c)=0,\quad
 h(e_a,f_c)=\delta_{ac},\quad h(z,z)=-1,
\]
with \(z\) perpendicular to the hyperbolic pairs.
Both weight families are perpendicular to those pairs
and \(z\). With \(\epsilon\) the source's idempotent
\(e_w\) in \(R\), one has
\[
 \epsilon\bar\epsilon=0,\quad b_i=\epsilon b_i,\quad
 b_i^d=\bar\epsilon b_i^d,\quad
 h(b_i^d,b_l)=\delta_{il}\epsilon .
\]
Thus the two weight families are each isotropic and
\(h(b_i,b_l^d)=\delta_{il}\bar\epsilon\).
The original lattice-span hypothesis says that these
vectors generate \(M\). The idempotent-support and
Gram relations show that this is the orthogonal sum
of the hyperbolic pairs, \(Rz\), and the paired weight
modules
\[
 B_+=\bigoplus_i \epsilon R b_i,\qquad
 B_-=\bigoplus_i \bar\epsilon R b_i^d .
\]
For instance a relation between the weight vectors can
be tested against the opposite family to recover every
supported coefficient. The same argument with \(e,f,z\)
recovers their coefficients. The \(Rz\) summand is
faithful since \(h(z,z)=-1\).
The source's \(e_w+\bar e_w\) support condition on the
whole lattice also gives \(\epsilon+\bar\epsilon=1\)
in \(R\), by applying it to \(z\) and pairing with \(z\).
The argument works without choosing local field bases.

The original admissibility witness for each term is
an integral upper unitriangular matrix on its
\((r+1)\)-vector flag. Its congruences hold at every
prime over \(g\). Under \(A^{-1}\) and \((A')^{-1}\),
the rational isotropic vectors are the fixed rational
reference vectors, and the last vectors become the
prescribed \(x_u^{-1}z_u,x_{u'}^{-1}z_{u'}\).
The source's \texttt{basisApprox} and \texttt{xu\_z}
conditions identify their reductions with \(e_a,z\);
denominators prime to \(g\) are units in \(R\).
Thus reduction of the witness gives, for each \(k_i\),
\[
 (k_i-1)e_a\in\sum_{c<a}Re_c,\qquad
 (k_i-1)z\in U .
\]
All vectors on the left and the witness combination
are integral at the primes over \(g\), so this is a
congruence in \(M\) itself. This proves the needed
normalized unipotent congruence directly.
In particular \(k_i\) preserves \(U\), acts on
\(U^\perp/U\), and fixes the image of \(z\) there.
It preserves the orthogonal complement of \(z\) in
this quotient and its two idempotent parts
\(B_+,B_-\).

In the basis \(b_j\) the resulting invertible matrix
on \(B_+\) has entries \(h(b_i^d,k b_j)\), which are
supported on \(\epsilon R\). The original maps
\(\mathrm{res}_v\) induce an isomorphism
\[
 \epsilon R \longrightarrow \prod_{v\in\mathrm{Pl}}\mathbb F_{q_v} :
\]
joint surjectivity is a source hypothesis, and the
kernel on the supported component is zero by
\(\mathrm{res}_{\mathrm{ker}}\) and
\(\mathrm{res}_{e_w}\).
The original \(\mathrm{leviM}\) consists exactly of
these reduced matrix entries. Its determinants
are units because the induced action is invertible,
so the identity fallback in the definition of
\(\mathrm{levi}\) is never used here.
Equality of the two recorded Levi labels therefore
means that \(\delta=k_2 k_1^{-1}\) acts trivially on
\(B_+\). Since the pairing of \(B_+\) with \(B_-\)
is perfect on the supported coefficients and
\(\delta\) is unitary, it acts trivially on \(B_-\)
as well. It also fixes the \(z\)-class. We conclude
\[
 (\delta-1)U^\perp\subset U
 \quad\text{in } M.
\]

Finally take \(x\in\Lambda\cap X^\perp\).
Its coordinates under \(A^{-1}\) belong to \(L_f\),
and are orthogonal to the fixed rational isotropic
vectors because \(A\) fixes them.
Their reductions are the \(e_a\), hence the
reduction of \(A^{-1}x\) belongs to \(U^\perp\).
By the preceding congruence, the reduction of
\(A^{-1}(\gamma'\gamma^{-1}-1)x\) lies in \(U\).
For each prime dividing \(g\), the source's
\texttt{IsFixedConv} condition says that the
actual saturated lattice \(\Lambda\cap X\) is
locally generated by the rational flag vectors
without extra scalar factors. Their images under
\(A^{-1}\) reduce to \(e_a\). Hence
\((\gamma'\gamma^{-1}-1)x\) lies locally in
\(g\Lambda+(\Lambda\cap X)\) at every such prime.
At the other primes this inclusion holds because
\(g\) is a unit and \(\gamma'\gamma^{-1}\) preserves
\(\Lambda\). Local membership for the rational
lattice proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

This gives exactly (FC7), with the original modulus,
rather than only a congruence of abstract representatives.
Together with the rational normal forms and the
weighted term-class comparison it supplies the original
fixed-row inequality.
