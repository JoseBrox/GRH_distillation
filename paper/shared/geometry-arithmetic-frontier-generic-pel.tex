\section{The prescribed generic PEL moduli space}
\label{sec:generic-pel-frontier}

We construct the characteristic-zero moduli space required by the
geometric route, including its original line and finite level labels.
This construction does not yet supply its integral compactification.
Write $E/F$ for the source's CM extension, $B$ for its perfect
alternating form on the projective $\mathcal O_E$-lattice $\Lambda$,
and $w=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2$. In particular $w+\bar w=1$.
The signature is $(p,q)$ at each embedding above the chosen embedding
of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$, and $f=[F:\mathbb Q]$.
The level consists of odd $M\geq3$, an ideal $\mathfrak b$ with
\[
 \mathfrak b\overline{\mathfrak b}=(M),\qquad
 \mathfrak b+\overline{\mathfrak b}=\mathcal O_E,
\]
and an integer $A\geq1$. Set
$K_A=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d},e^{2\pi i/A})$ with the original embeddings.

\begin{proposition}[The exact primitive line]\label{prop:pel-primitive-line}
Let $(\mathcal A,\lambda,\iota)$ be a principally polarized abelian
scheme with $\mathcal O_E$-action satisfying
$\iota(a)^\vee\lambda=\lambda\iota(\bar a)$.
For the normalized Poincar\'e line $\mathcal P$ on
$\mathcal A\times\mathcal A^\vee$, put
\[
 \mathcal L_w=(1,\lambda\iota(w))^*\mathcal P.
\]
This is a rigidified symmetric cubical line, relatively ample and
principally polarizing. It satisfies the source's adjoint identities
and its exact primitive identity
$\mathcal L_w\simeq(1,\iota(w))^*\delta\mathcal L_w$.
Conversely every original primitive line inducing $\lambda$ is
uniquely isomorphic to $\mathcal L_w$ as a rigidified cubical line.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a homomorphism $h:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A^\vee$, the polarization
associated to $(1,h)^*\mathcal P$ is $h+h^\vee$. Thus
\[
 \lambda_{\mathcal L_w}
 =\lambda\iota(w)+\iota(w)^\vee\lambda
 =\lambda\iota(w+\bar w)=\lambda.
\]
Simultaneous inversion on the two factors preserves $\mathcal P$,
giving symmetry. The theorem of the cube gives its unique cubical
structure compatible with the rigidification. Relative ampleness
follows fibrewise from the polarization just computed. The normalized
biextension identity is
$\delta\mathcal L_w=(\operatorname{id}\times\lambda)^*\mathcal P$
on $\mathcal A\times\mathcal A$; the primitive identity then pulls
this line back along the graph $x\mapsto(x,\iota(w)x)$.
It gives both the adjoint identities and the primitive identity.
For an original line $\mathcal L$, the same biextension identity and
its stipulated primitive identity give $\mathcal L\simeq\mathcal L_w$.
Normalize this isomorphism at zero. Since
$\pi_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal A}=\mathcal O_S$, its normalized version
is unique, hence respects the unique cubical structures and commutes
with base change. No square root, tensor power, or replacement theta
line is involved. These standard Poincar\'e identities are recalled
in \cite[Sections 1.3.2, 3.2.2 and 5.2.4]{generic-pel-lan}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The half-level kills actual automorphisms]
\label{lem:pel-half-level-automorphisms}
Every automorphism of a geometric original object preserving its
polarization, coefficient action and half-level labels is the identity.
The auxiliary full level $A$ is not needed for this assertion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use any original full Tate marking. On
$\widehat\Lambda=\Lambda\otimes\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ the resulting
operator $g$ preserves the perfect form, and fixing the labels means
$(g-1)\overline{\mathfrak b}\widehat\Lambda\subset M\widehat\Lambda$.
Invertibility of the ideal $\overline{\mathfrak b}$ gives
$g-1\in\mathfrak b\operatorname{End}_{\widehat{\mathcal O}_E}
(\widehat\Lambda)$. The same holds for $g^{-1}-1$.
The adjoint is integral because $B$ is perfect; it conjugates
coefficient ideals and satisfies $g^*=g^{-1}$. Taking its adjoint
gives $g-1\in\overline{\mathfrak b}\operatorname{End}(\widehat\Lambda)$.
Their intersection is $M\operatorname{End}(\widehat\Lambda)$.
Thus the automorphism fixes every point of $\mathcal A[M]$.
The standard rigidity of polarized abelian varieties with full level
$M\geq3$ makes it the identity
\cite[Lemma 2.3.3.1]{generic-pel-lan}.
This is an assertion about polarization-preserving automorphisms;
it does not assert neatness of an adelic similitude subgroup.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Generic moduli supplier]\label{thm:original-generic-pel-moduli}
For every original host, datum and level there is a smooth
quasi-projective scheme $X/K_A$ of relative dimension $fpq$, with a
universal object, representing the original moduli functor. In
particular it supplies the source's \texttt{Moduli} object, including
its reduced-base representing property and its dual-number
unramified property. The universal line is the original primitive
line, and both entire label maps are retained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The case $p+q=0$ is $\operatorname{Spec}K_A$ with the trivial abelian
scheme and its rigidified trivial line; assume $p+q>0$.
First keep track of the pairing convention. For a complex structure
$J$ in the prescribed domain, the source assumes $B(Jx,x)>0$.
Lan's positivity convention is therefore satisfied by the
$\mathbb Z(1)$-valued form $-2\pi i B$ and $h(i)=J$
\cite[Definition 1.2.1.2]{generic-pel-lan}.
The Weil pairing of his polarization is the theta commutator for
$\mathcal L^{\otimes n}$, with lifts
$t_x^*\mathcal L^{\otimes n}\to\mathcal L^{\otimes n}$, by
\cite[Definition 5.2.4.6 and Proposition 5.2.4.9]{generic-pel-lan};
this is exactly the original commutator convention.

The source's unramified CM hypothesis supplies a unit
$u\in\widehat{\mathcal O}_E^\times$ with $u\bar u=-1$.
At a split place use $(1,-1)$; at an unramified quadratic place
the norm on units is onto, including residue characteristic two.
For completeness, surjectivity follows from the finite-field norm
and from the trace maps on successive principal-unit quotients,
followed by completeness. Thus an original coherent Tate marking
$\alpha$, with pairing $\zeta_n^{B(x,y)}$, gives the PEL marking
$\alpha\circ u$, with pairing $\zeta_n^{-B(x,y)}$.
The roots $\zeta_n$ and in particular the specified $\zeta_A$ do not
change. Recover the original labels by applying this marking to
$u^{-1}x$. This fixed integral adelic change of coordinates affects
neither the abelian variety nor its line or its labelled points.

The finite labels are precisely an integral finite-level orbit.
Indeed, $\Lambda=\mathfrak b\Lambda+
\overline{\mathfrak b}\Lambda$ and their intersection is $M\Lambda$.
Consequently the map killed by $\mathfrak b\Lambda$ is uniquely
determined by its restriction to $\overline{\mathfrak b}\Lambda$;
that restriction is the original full $M$-division marking there.
The other label map is the full $A$-division marking. Their simultaneous
stabilizer $H$ is an open compact subgroup of the integral PEL
similitude group, containing the principal congruence subgroup of
level $\operatorname{lcm}(M,A)$, with cyclotomic multiplier one modulo
$A$. The condition that a marking extend to the complete Tate module
is retained: this is Lan's integral level of the prescribed lattice
type, not merely a signature or a rational genus.
Take the open-and-closed cyclotomic fibre specified by
$\zeta_A=e^{2\pi i/A}$ after base change to $K_A$.

The determinant polynomial is defined over
$\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$: for $a\in E$ its multiplicities are $p$ at
embeddings over the chosen quadratic embedding and $q$ at their
conjugates. Hence the reflex field is contained in this quadratic
field. Because $\mathcal O_E$ is finite \'etale in characteristic zero,
the original geometric eigenspace dimensions give precisely the PEL
determinant condition, also over nonreduced bases: after an \'etale
splitting, its eigensubbundles have the prescribed locally constant
ranks. Likewise the finite label identities are identities of finite
\'etale sections, so their geometric-point verification holds over
such bases. This proves the equivalence of the original functor and
the indicated PEL moduli problem. Proposition~\ref{prop:pel-primitive-line}
recovers its exact cubical line and all line-preserving isomorphisms.

Apply \cite[Theorem 1.4.1.11]{generic-pel-lan} with the set of retained
good primes empty, so the base has characteristic zero. It gives a
separated smooth algebraic stack of finite type. The preceding lemma
makes its inertia trivial, hence it is an algebraic space.
By \cite[Corollary 7.2.3.10]{generic-pel-lan}, the coarse moduli space
for every such $H$ is a quasi-projective scheme. Since the stack is
already an algebraic space, it equals its coarse space. This yields
the required fine scheme and its universal object, without assuming
that $H$ is neat. At each conjugate pair the Hodge filtration has
Grassmannian dimension $pq$, giving the total dimension $fpq$.
Representability makes two maps with isomorphic pulled-back objects
equal, including a dual-number map and its constant reduction.
\end{proof}

This closes the generic moduli supplier in both routes. It does not
yet supply the source's normal flat projective integral model, its
semiabelian boundary with the same cubical line, the prescribed
uniformization charts, or any metric, section, cusp or global norm
estimate. In particular a compactification theorem requiring a neat
prime-to-residue-characteristic level cannot be invoked merely from
Lemma~\ref{lem:pel-half-level-automorphisms}.
The source interfaces are pinned separately\cite{generic-pel-source}.
