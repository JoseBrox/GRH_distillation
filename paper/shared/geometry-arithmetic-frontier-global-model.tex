\section{One arithmetic model dominating all places}
\label{sec:pel-global-model}

The global model requires coordination over all finite places.
Finitely many arbitrary local models can be incorporated by a
projective graph construction.  At the other places we use the
same arithmetic toroidal construction over one ring of integers
with finitely many primes inverted.

Fix the invariant projective rational cone data used for the
individual models, presented as the pullback of auxiliary Siegel
cone data on the original singleton symplectic lattice.
Use the corresponding canonical normalization models throughout.
Their independence of the auxiliary presentation is
\cite[Theorem 7.14]{global-model-lan-ramified}.

\begin{theorem}[The original global domination clause]
\label{thm:pel-global-domination}
The individual models can be chosen with these fixed cone data so
that they satisfy the source's \texttt{N6}.  Precisely, for every
original level $l$ with $A=1$ and $\mathrm{GoodAt}(p)$ for every
prime $p$, and for every original coefficient field $F=c.F$, there
is a normal projective flat model over $\mathcal O_F$, carrying
the original semiabelian family and primitive cubical line.  Its
generic compactification has the original descent to $K_A$, and
its localization dominates every prescribed local model, with
the exact original family comparisons in \texttt{Dominates}.
No extension of $F$ is needed.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Only finitely many exceptional localizations}

Let $O=\mathcal O_F$ and write $X_F$ for the fixed generic
toroidal compactification, with its original family and line.
Choose a positive integer $N$ containing the finitely many bad
primes of the integral PEL datum and its fixed auxiliary level.
There is a genuine arithmetic toroidal model $Y_U$ over
$U=\operatorname{Spec}O[1/N]$ whose generic fibre is $X_F$.

Here this statement does not rely on arbitrary spreading of a
generic compactification.  The set of good primes in
\cite[Definition 1.4.1.1]{global-model-lan} may be the entire
cofinite set away from $N$.  Theorems 6.4.1.1 and 7.3.3.4 there
give the projective auxiliary Siegel toroidal scheme over this
ring, with its actual semiabelian family.  Normalize the closure
of the original generic PEL locus in this scheme in its original
generic function fields, using all its original components.
This is the same normalization construction as
\cite[(7.7)--(7.10)]{global-model-lan-ramified}, now over $O[1/N]$.
Normalization is finite over the excellent arithmetic base.
The original singleton lattice makes the pulled-back Siegel
family the required underlying family.  The coefficient action
and the original symmetric cubical primitive line extend by
the normal-base extension theorems already used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:original-integral-pel-model}.

Normalization and schematic closure commute with localization.
Consequently, for each finite place $v\nmid N$, $(Y_U)_{O_v}$
is precisely the prescribed canonical local model, with its
tautological family.  At a place where a different auxiliary
Siegel presentation was used, Theorem 7.14 identifies the models
canonically because the induced rational cone data are the same.
This proves the necessary outside-$N$ agreement; a generic
isomorphism alone would not prove it.

There are only finitely many places $v\mid N$.  At each one,
use the actual local model $Y_v/O_v$ already constructed, with
the same generic compactification $X_F$.

\subsection*{A finite projective graph construction}

Choose a closed projective embedding of $Y_U$ over $O[1/N]$.
Take the closure of its generic embedded $X_F$ in the same
projective space over $O$, and normalize.  Denote the result by
$C_0$.  It is normal, projective and flat over $O$, has generic
fibre $X_F$, and restricts to $Y_U$ over $U$.

For each exceptional place $v$, choose a closed embedding
$Y_v\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^{n_v}_{O_v}$.  Let $C_v$ be the
normalization of the closure of its generic embedded $X_F$ in
$\mathbb P^{n_v}_O$.  Then
\[
                         (C_v)_{O_v}\simeq Y_v.
\]
Indeed $Y_v$ is flat and is the schematic closure of its generic
fibre in $\mathbb P^{n_v}_{O_v}$; it is already normal.
Localization of the closure and of normalization gives the stated
equality.  Every normalization here is finite, because the rings
are of finite type over the excellent Dedekind ring $O$.

Define $Z$ to be the normalization of the closure of the
diagonal copy of $X_F$ in
\[
                         C_0\times_O\prod_{v\mid N} C_v.
\]
This is a finite product.  The generic diagonal is closed since
$X_F$ is proper and separated, and is already normal.
Thus $Z_F=X_F$.  The scheme $Z$ is normal and projective over
$O$.  All its irreducible components dominate $\operatorname{Spec}O$,
so its structure sheaf is torsion-free as an $O$-module and
$Z\to\operatorname{Spec}O$ is flat.  This argument is applied
componentwise if $X_F$ is disconnected.

The projections give actual morphisms
\[
             Z_U\longrightarrow Y_U,
             \qquad Z_{O_v}\longrightarrow Y_v\quad(v\mid N),
\]
each equal to the identity on the common generic compactification.
For $v\nmid N$, the first projection gives
$Z_{O_v}\to(Y_U)_{O_v}=Y_v$.  Hence the base model already
dominates every local model.  No finiteness of these projection
morphisms is asserted or required by \texttt{Dominates}.

\subsection*{Descent of the exact family and line}

The finite collection
\[
 U\ \amalg\ \coprod_{v\mid N}\operatorname{Spec}O_v
                      \longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}O
\]
is faithfully flat and quasi-compact.  Two different members
intersect in $\operatorname{Spec}F$: both
$O_v\otimes_OO_w=F$ for $v\ne w$ and
$O[1/N]\otimes_OO_v=F$ for $v\mid N$.
Pull this cover back to $Z$.

On $Z_U$ and each $Z_{O_v}$, pull back the corresponding actual
semiabelian family and its rigidified primitive line.
On every overlap the two pullbacks are canonically the original
family and line over $X_F$.  The comparisons agree on the
original moduli interior and extend uniquely across the generic
boundary by normal-base full faithfulness.  Therefore they
satisfy the cocycle identity and preserve the coefficient action,
rigidification, symmetry, adjunction and primitive identities.

For completeness, scheme descent here is effective for a
specific reason.  The original symmetric cubical line on each
piece is relatively ample, by the generic ampleness criterion
already used above.  Let $\pi_i:G_i\to Z_i$ and $\mathscr L_i$
denote these pieces.  Flat base change gives descent data on the
quasi-coherent graded algebras
\[
                 \mathcal A_i=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}
                           \pi_{i*}\mathscr L_i^n.
\]
They descend to a graded algebra $\mathcal A$ on $Z$.
Relative ampleness identifies each $G_i$ with its canonical
open subscheme of $\operatorname{Proj}_{Z_i}\mathcal A_i$.
These opens agree on overlaps and hence descend to an open
subscheme $G$ of $\operatorname{Proj}_Z\mathcal A$.
This constructs the descended scheme directly; arbitrary
projective descent is not being assumed
\cite[Tags 02KH, 01VJ and 0D2P]{global-model-stacks}.
The line, group operations and all specified comparison maps
then descend by faithfully flat descent.  The resulting family
is smooth, separated and semiabelian, as can be checked on this
cover.

The generic fibre of $(Z,G,\mathscr L)$ is the unchanged
$(X_F,G_F,\mathscr L_F)$.  Its generic abelian locus is
therefore exactly the original moduli interior, retaining the
source's \texttt{ab\_gen}.  These data define the actual
global \texttt{Model}.  Since $A=1$ and the invariant cones
give the original $K_A$-descent of the generic compactification
and family, they also give \texttt{Descends} for this global
model.  Vertical modifications in the graph construction have
not changed that generic descent.

\subsection*{Every original valuation and every comparison}

It remains to check the source quantifier over valuation subrings
$W$ of $\mathbb C$.  Let $p$ be nonunit in $W$ and put
$V=F\cap W$, precisely the source's $c.V\,W$.
Every integer of $F$ is integral over $\mathbb Z\subset V$;
since a valuation ring is integrally closed, $O\subset V$.
The maximal ideal of $V$ contracts to a place $v\mid p$ of $O$.
Thus $O_v\subset V\subset F$.  A discrete valuation ring has
no proper overring in its fraction field other than the field:
an element of negative valuation would invert a power of its
uniformizer.  The condition that $p$ is nonunit excludes $V=F$.
Consequently
\[
                              c.V\,W=O_v.
\]
There are only finitely many such places above each rational
prime, and all $W$ with the same restriction give this same
original subring of $F$.

Choose the local systems using the canonical model for this
place, as above.  The projection $Z_{O_v}\to Y_v$ together
with the descended cartesian family comparison supplies the
map $f$ and the two \texttt{SABMap}s in \texttt{Dominates}.
Their restrictions to the original moduli interior are the
specified original comparisons, so its universal equalities
for every test scheme hold as equalities of these maps.
The base map $O\to c.V\,W$ in the source is forced to be this
inclusion by its prescribed equality inside $F$.  The generic
open lift is unique, because localization is a monomorphism.
This verifies all fields and all valuation quantifiers of
\texttt{N6}\cite{global-model-source}.

The theorem does not assert that any independently chosen
infinite family of local blowups has a global dominator.
Its outside-finitely-many-primes agreement is part of the
actual construction.  Coordinated finite level transitions,
the exact Siegel comparison and the numerical height and
section estimates remain separate obligations.
