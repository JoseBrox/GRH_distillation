\subsection{Absolute convergence of all original framed returns}
\label{global-gram-framed-returns}

The preceding arithmetic count also controls the finer return set
used by the raw kernel.  A positive dominating function avoids the
finite phases altogether at this convergence step.  It is used only
for domination; the original scalar seeds, level, maps and norms are
not replaced.

Retain an original setting in the gap range of
Theorem~\ref{actual-seed-decay-theorem}, and its original finite
sets $\mathcal L_u,\mathcal L_{u'}$ of framed lattices.  Put
$f_u(g)=\|W_u(g)\|$.  For $\Lambda\in\mathcal L_u$, the original
column data give
\[
 a_\Lambda=c_\Lambda x_u,\qquad
 \widehat\Lambda=a_\Lambda L_f,
 \qquad c_\Lambda\in H_u(\mathbb A_{F,f}).
\]
Here $L_f$ is the original finite adelic lattice.  Let
$K_0=\operatorname{Stab}_{G(\mathbb A_{F,f})}(L_f)$, the actual
compact open lattice stabilizer.  Its Haar volume is denoted
$v_0=\mu_f(K_0)>0$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-positive-seed}
 F_u^+(g,y_f)=f_u(g)1_{S_u(\mathbb A_{F,f})x_uK_0}(y_f),
 \qquad
 P_u^+(y)=\sum_{S_u(F)\backslash\Gamma}F_u^+(\delta y).
\end{equation}
These are explicitly auxiliary nonnegative functions, not new
arithmetic theta maps.  In particular $v_0$ will not be substituted
for the original level volume in any kernel formula.

For an original framed return $t=(\Lambda,\Lambda',[r])$, its
representative $r\in\Gamma$ satisfies $r\Lambda'=\Lambda$.
The equivalence is the original double relation by
$N_\Lambda$ and $N_{\Lambda'}$, not by the larger $S$ groups.
Write
\[
 R_t=N_\Lambda\cap rN_{\Lambda'}r^{-1},\qquad
 A_\Lambda=S_u(F)\cap\operatorname{Stab}(\Lambda),\qquad
 h_\Lambda=[A_\Lambda:N_\Lambda].
\]
All quotients at infinity below use counting measure on these
discrete groups and the original infinite-place Haar measure.
They are precisely the source's \texttt{ROinf} and
\texttt{FundOf} quotients.\cite{global-gram-framed-source}

\begin{theorem}[The full framed-return sum]
\label{global-gram-framed-sum}
For every such original setting and every pair of original words,
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-original-oolint}
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{u,u'}}
 \int_{R_t\backslash G(F_\infty)}
       f_u(x)f_{u'}(r_\infty^{-1}x)\,dx<\infty.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal T_{u,u'}$ is the entire original
\texttt{GTerm} set, including its original admissibility condition.
In particular this is the literal \texttt{OOLintSum} conclusion,
for the original fundamental domains, throughout the already
established gap regime.  It implies absolute convergence of the
corresponding orbital rank-one operators.  No finite phase
well-definedness or column lower-frame premise is needed for this
positive convergence result.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The auxiliary positive series has finite $L^2$ norm.}
The function $f_u$ is left $S_u(F_\infty)$-invariant by the original
unitary covariance of $W_u$.  The finite support in
\eqref{global-gram-positive-seed} is open, right $K_0$-invariant
and left $S_u(\mathbb A_{F,f})$-invariant.  Thus $F_u^+$ is Borel
and left $S_u(F)$-invariant, without using a finite character.

The proof of Theorem~\ref{global-gram-ifinite} applies directly
to this explicit function, with column bound $M_T=1$ and $K_0$
in place of the support set $K$.  Namely the compact arithmetic
quotient of $S_u$, the actual multiplicity theorem, and the
infimum over $S_u(F_\infty)$ give, for every compact $\Omega$,
\[
 \int_{S_u(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)}
                  m_\Omega(y)F_u^+(y)\,dy<\infty.
\]
This invocation does not use \texttt{PhWellDef}: $F_u^+$ is
defined by a norm and a support indicator, so there is no
choice of finite decomposition.

The original compact reproducer gives
$f_u(g)\le\int|\alpha(t)|f_u(gt)\,dt$.
Multiply by the finite support indicator and average over $K_0$
with kernel $1_{K_0}/v_0$.  This is a compact reproducing
\emph{inequality} for $F_u^+$, with bounded kernel and the original
adelic Haar measure.  The positive folding argument in
Corollary~\ref{global-gram-poincare} therefore proves that $P_u^+$
is finite everywhere and lies in $L^2(\Gamma\backslash G(\mathbb A_F))$.
The same holds for $u'$.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-positive-J}
 J_{u,u'}:=\int_{\Gamma\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)}P_u^+P_{u'}^+
   =\int_{S_u(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)}F_u^+P_{u'}^+
   <\infty.
\end{equation}
The equality is positive unfolding; finiteness follows from
Cauchy--Schwarz in the original quotient norm.

\emph{The exact finite-support slice.}
For each $\Lambda$ put
\[
 Q_\Lambda(x)=f_u(x)P_{u'}^+(x,a_\Lambda).
\]
The group $A_\Lambda$ acts discretely at infinity.  This follows
either from the lattice matrix entries or from the same fixed
denominator argument as Theorem~\ref{global-gram-multiplicity}.
The infinite projection is injective on rational matrices.
For $s\in S_u(F)$, the translates of
$G(F_\infty)\times a_\Lambda K_0$ meet if and only if
$s_f\in a_\Lambda K_0a_\Lambda^{-1}$, equivalently
$s\in A_\Lambda$.
The lattice equivalence uses the original completions:
a full finite adelic lattice is the closure of its intersection
with $V(F)$, by additive approximation.  No unipotent strong
approximation or changed level is needed here.

If $D_A$ is an infinite-place fundamental domain for $A_\Lambda$,
then $D_A\times a_\Lambda K_0$ is a fundamental domain for
this slice in $S_u(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)$.
The integrand in \eqref{global-gram-positive-J} is right $K_0$-invariant.
Integration over the finite factor is therefore exactly $v_0$.
The function $Q_\Lambda$ is $A_\Lambda$-invariant.  Since
$h_\Lambda<\infty$ by Theorem~\ref{global-gram-levi-fibres},
passing from $A_\Lambda$ to $N_\Lambda$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-slice}
 \int_{N_\Lambda\backslash G(F_\infty)}Q_\Lambda(x)\,dx
 =h_\Lambda\int_{A_\Lambda\backslash G(F_\infty)}Q_\Lambda(x)\,dx
 \le {h_\Lambda\over v_0}J_{u,u'}.
\end{equation}
This retains the finite stabilizer index and the Haar volume.
Different lattice slices need not be disjoint; the inequality
is used separately for the finite list of $\Lambda$.

\emph{Finite fibres of the actual return indexing.}
Fix $\Lambda$.  For each original $t$ beginning at $\Lambda$,
take representatives $E_t$ of $R_t\backslash N_\Lambda$.
Transversal unfolding gives
\begin{align*}
 &\sum_{t:\,t.\Lambda=\Lambda}
    \int_{R_t\backslash G(F_\infty)}
                         f_u(x)f_{u'}(r_\infty^{-1}x)\,dx\\
 &\quad=\int_{N_\Lambda\backslash G(F_\infty)}
      f_u(x)\sum_{t:\,t.\Lambda=\Lambda}\sum_{m\in E_t}
                         f_{u'}(r_\infty^{-1}m_\infty x)\,dx .
\end{align*}
Everything is nonnegative, so Tonelli requires no prior
convergence.  The integrands are invariant under their stated
groups, since those groups lie in the original unipotent
stabilizers of the two norm seeds.

Send $i=(t,m)$ to the rational coset
\[
                 \varphi(i)=S_{u'}(F)r^{-1}m.
\]
There is a finite bound $M$ on all its fibres.  Here is the
explicit arithmetic proof, rather than an assumed counting
hypothesis.  Choose the finite sets $F_{\Lambda_1',\Lambda_2'}$
from Theorem~\ref{global-gram-levi-fibres}, and put
\[
 M=\sum_{\Lambda_1',\Lambda_2'\in\mathcal L_{u'}}
                              |F_{\Lambda_1',\Lambda_2'}|.
\]
In a nonempty fibre choose $i_0=(t_0,m_0)$ and write
$g_i=r_i^{-1}m_i$.  Then
$\sigma_i=g_{i_0}g_i^{-1}\in S_{u'}(F)$ carries
$\Lambda_i'$ to $\Lambda_{i_0}'$.  Choose
$f_i\in F_{\Lambda_{i_0}',\Lambda_i'}$ with
$f_i^{-1}\sigma_i\in N_{\Lambda_i'}$.
The map $i\mapsto(\Lambda_i',f_i)$ is injective.
Indeed equality for $i,j$ gives $g_jg_i^{-1}\in N_{\Lambda_i'}$,
so
\[
 r_j\in N_\Lambda r_iN_{\Lambda_i'}.
\]
The original double-coset relation therefore gives $t_i=t_j$.
Their fixed representatives now agree, and
$m_jm_i^{-1}\in R_{t_i}$; the chosen transversals give $m_i=m_j$.
This proves the fibre bound, also when the admissibility condition
selects only part of the possible double-coset set.

Finally $m$ preserves $\Lambda$ and $r\Lambda'=\Lambda$ imply
\[
                   r_f^{-1}m_fa_\Lambda\in a_{\Lambda'}K_0.
\]
Thus the relevant value of $F_{u'}^+$ equals
$f_{u'}(r_\infty^{-1}m_\infty x)$ exactly.
Its left rational $S_{u'}$-invariance identifies this value with
the term of $P_{u'}^+(x,a_\Lambda)$ indexed by $\varphi(i)$.
The fibre bound therefore majorizes the preceding double sum
by $M P_{u'}^+(x,a_\Lambda)$.
Using \eqref{global-gram-slice} and summing over the original
finite source lattices gives the explicit finite bound
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-finite-ool-bound}
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{u,u'}}\int_{R_t\backslash G(F_\infty)}
                       f_u(x)f_{u'}(r_\infty^{-1}x)\,dx
 \le {M\over v_0}\Bigl(\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathcal L_u}h_\Lambda\Bigr)
                                                       J_{u,u'}<\infty.
\end{equation}
The norm of each rank-one integrand is exactly the product in
\eqref{global-gram-original-oolint}.  This also proves integrability
of every original orbital operator and summability of their norms.
\end{proof}

This proves the entire original framed-return summability statement
in the arithmetic gap range already used by the construction.
The source's unrestricted predicate \texttt{OOLintSumC} is phrased
with \texttt{FoldOK} premises alone; no stronger quantifier is
silently substituted for the statement above.  Its downstream
\texttt{OOIntAll} and orbital absolute-convergence conclusions apply
to these very settings and representatives.  There is still no
uniform estimate for $M$, $h_\Lambda$, $v_0^{-1}$ or $J_{u,u'}$.
The complex raw-kernel identity must separately match its phases,
original $K_2$ volume, class frames and final restrictions; a
positive majorant does not identify that operator.
