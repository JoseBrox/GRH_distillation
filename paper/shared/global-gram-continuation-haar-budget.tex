\subsection{The actual unipotent covolume and a Haar-independent seed budget}
\label{global-gram-haar-budget}

The remaining Haar ratio in the preceding bound is not uniformly
bounded by the source's measure predicates.  Its subgroup measures
are arbitrary Haar measures, and \texttt{IsTextNorm} imposes product
identities without fixing their scale.  The actual period normalizer
cancels these scales.  We compute the arithmetic covolume needed
after that cancellation, including the original fractional flag
ideals.\cite{global-gram-haar-source}

Put $f=[F:\mathbb Q]$, $D=|\operatorname{disc}F|$ and retain
$n=2r+k+1$, $k\ge1$.  The actual CM extension $E/F$ is unramified
at every finite place, so $|\operatorname{disc}E|=D^2$ and the
ordinary complex-embedding covolume of $\mathcal O_E$ is
$c_E=2^{-f}D$.  This is the literal CM hypothesis: at every
prime of $E$ some integer differs from its conjugate modulo
that prime.  A ramified prime in the quadratic extension would
put conjugation in inertia and contradict this condition.
The specified fixed field is $F$, so no different real subfield
is used.  Let
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-haar-exponents}
 \begin{aligned}
 d_N&=r(2n-2r-1),\qquad
 \beta=r(r+1)(n-r)+\frac{r(r^2-1)}3,\\
 e_N&=\frac{d_N}{2}-\beta
       =-\frac{8r^3+6r^2+6kr^2+r}{6}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The group $N$ has real dimension $d_N$ at each real place.
All its tangent volumes below use the Hilbert--Schmidt metric.

\begin{theorem}[The literal original \texttt{CovolN}]
\label{global-gram-haar-covolume}
For the actual setting and word $u$, let $\Gamma_{N,u}$ be the
source's \texttt{NOinf} and let $F_{N,u}$ be any specified
fundamental domain.  In the original source coordinate box
$\operatorname{box}_N(u)$, every original Haar measure satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-haar-source-covolume}
 \mu_N(F_{N,u})
  =T_u^{fr}\,2^{-fr(k+r)}D^{e_N}
                               \mu_N(\operatorname{box}_N(u)).
\end{equation}
This is exactly the source's \texttt{CovolN}, including its
additional factor $2^{-fr(r+1)/2}$ outside \texttt{covolN}.
After the frame and character transport
\eqref{global-gram-quantitative-character-scale}, the covolume
for product Hilbert--Schmidt Haar measure is
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-haar-transported-covolume}
 \operatorname{covol}_{\rm HS}(t^{-1}\Gamma_{N,u}t)
                        =D^{e_N}c^{-f\beta},\qquad c=g/(2\pi).
\end{equation}
No finite-prime index or lattice class has been removed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First identify the actual discrete group.  The original
$x_u$ belongs to the full integral stabilizer $K_0$.
The membership part of the proved finite-phase theorem gives
$x_u^{-1}(N_f\cap K_0)x_u\subseteq K_2$.
Conversely $K_2\subseteq K_0$, and $x_u$ normalizes $K_0$.
Thus
\[
              \Gamma_{N,u}=N(F)\cap\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal O_E}(L).
\]
This uses the proved membership argument, not an assertion that a
principal congruence group has index one.  That membership argument
does not require an additional character-law hypothesis.

The original splitting is the direct sum
\[
 L=P\oplus L_b\oplus P^\vee,\qquad
 P=\bigoplus_{a=0}^{r-1}\mathfrak d^{a-r}e_a,
 \qquad N_E\mathfrak d=D^2.
\]
The Hermitian form pairs $P$ and $P^\vee$ perfectly.  Since $L$
is self-dual, $L_b$ is self-dual.  Its rank is $b=n-2r=k+1$.
Before the diagonal character scaling, the standard-frame
positive metric makes the $e_a,f_a$ Euclidean unit vectors.
Consequently the normalized covolume of $P$ is
\[
                    H(P)=D^{-r(r+1)}.
\]

Decompose $N$ into upper unitriangular automorphisms of $P$ and
the radical of the parabolic fixing $P$.  This decomposition
also holds on integral points: the upper factor and its inverse
adjoint preserve $P\oplus P^\vee$, and the remaining kernel is
the integral parabolic radical.  All multiplication Jacobians
are triangular with diagonal one.
For the upper factor the $(a,b)$ coordinate, $a<b$, lies in
$\mathfrak d^{a-b}$.  Its two embedded matrix entries have
combined squared norm twice the squared complex coordinate.
The product covolume of these coordinates is therefore
\[
 \prod_{a<b}(2^f c_E D^{2(a-b)})
       =D^{r(r-1)/2-r(r^2-1)/3}.
\]

For clarity, the radical calculation used here is the established
``Primitive isotropic sublattices and the unipotent radical''
calculation.\cite{current-construction-record}
Its coordinates are $Z\in\operatorname{Hom}(\overline P^{\,*},L_b)$
and a skew-Hermitian $r\times r$ coordinate $C$, with tangent
norm $2\|Z\|_{\rm HS}^2+\|C\|_{\rm HS}^2$.
The integral projection onto $Z$ is surjective: the finite
unramified extension has an integer $\theta$ with
$\theta+\bar\theta=1$, and the upper-right block
$-\theta Z^*J_bZ$ supplies a lift.
The $Z$ covolume is $D^{br}H(P)^b$.
The diagonal skew coordinates have covolume $D^{1/2}$,
and each off-diagonal pair has covolume $D$.
For the actual direct sum of fractional ideals their total
determinant factor is $H(P)^r$.  Thus the center contributes
$D^{r^2/2}H(P)^r$.
These statements include all dyadic places; trace surjectivity
uses unramifiedness there as well.
Multiplication of the radical and upper covolumes yields exactly
$D^{e_N}$.  Reduction first in the upper coordinates, then in
$Z$, and then in the center gives a genuine fundamental domain,
so the calculation is a covolume calculation rather than a
count of formal coordinates.

We check the literal source box.  Its coordinates are the
strict upper entries of $x$, the entries
$y_a=h(f_a,nz)$ and $\phi_{aj}=h(f_a,nw_j)$, and the strict
upper and imaginary diagonal entries of
$C=(x^{-1}a-(x^{-1}a)^*)/2$.
The remaining quadratic Hermitian part is determined by
unitarity.  They are global triangular coordinates of Jacobian
one after choosing their tangent densities.  Replacing the
negative unit vector by $z=\sqrt{T_u}e_-$ multiplies the
$r$ complex $y$ coordinates by $\sqrt{T_u}$.  The two upper
matrix blocks and the off-diagonal skew pairs supply respectively
the factors
$2^{fr(r-1)/2}$, $2^{fr(k+1)}$ and
$2^{fr(r-1)/2}$.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_{\rm HS}(\operatorname{box}_N(u))
                         =2^{fr(k+r)}T_u^{-fr}.
\]
Divide $D^{e_N}$ by this exact box volume.  Haar uniqueness
proves \eqref{global-gram-haar-source-covolume} for the given
measure, with the stated source powers.

Finally conjugation by $D_\eta^{-1}$ has real determinant
$c^{-\beta}$ on the Lie algebra of $N$ at each place.
The three exponent contributions are
\[
 2\sum_{a<b}(b-a)=\frac{r(r^2-1)}3,\qquad
 2b\sum_a(r-a)=br(r+1),\qquad
 2r\sum_a(r-a)=r^2(r+1).
\]
Their sum is $\beta$.  The phases of $\eta_a$ have unit
modulus.  Multiplication over all $f$ embeddings proves
\eqref{global-gram-haar-transported-covolume}.
When $r=0$, every group in this calculation is trivial and
both assertions mean equality with factor one.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The intrinsic small-ball ratio.}
Let $B_N=\prod_v\{n_v:\|n_v\|_{\rm op}\le2\}$ in the
transported group.  Nilpotence makes the exponential Jacobian
one for Hilbert--Schmidt Haar measure.  The cube of side
$2\log2/\sqrt{d_N}$ in an orthonormal Lie basis maps into
this ball: its Hilbert--Schmidt radius is at most $\log2$,
and $\|\exp X\|_{\rm op}\le e^{\|X\|_{\rm HS}}$.
Thus, with the factor interpreted as one when $r=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-haar-ratio}
 R_N:=\frac{\mu_N(F_{N,u})}{(t^{-1}_*\mu_N)(B_N)}
 \le D^{e_N}c^{-f\beta}
           \left(\frac{\sqrt{d_N}}{2\log2}\right)^{fd_N}
 \le2^{f\beta}n^{fd_N}.
\end{equation}
Here $t^{-1}_*\mu_N$ denotes conjugation pushforward.
The last bound uses $e_N\le0$, $g\ge6$, $\pi<4$,
$2\log2>1$, $d_N\le n^2$ and $\beta\le n^3$.
It is independent of the arbitrary Haar scale, the word and
the sizes of the actual frame vectors.

\paragraph{Formal degree cancels the ambient Haar scalar.}
For one place put $s_a=\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}j!$.
The exact Cartan scalar used in
\eqref{global-gram-quantitative-L1} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-haar-formal-degree}
 d_\lambda c_G
   =\left(\prod_{a<b}|\lambda_a-\lambda_b|\right)
                         \frac{s_{p-q}}{s_p^2s_q^2}
   \le(2B)^{n(n-1)/2}.
\end{equation}
Here $p=k+r\ge q=r+1$ and both measures are precisely the
ones defining $d_\lambda$ and $c_G$.

We include the normalization check.  Hilbert--Schmidt volume of
$U(a)$ is $(2\pi)^{a(a+1)/2}/s_a$.
Indeed the successive quotients are spheres; their horizontal
metric at a unit vector is $t^2+2\|z\|^2$, so their volume
is $2^{a-1}$ times that of the ordinary sphere.  The resulting
successive volume ratio is $(2\pi)^a/(a-1)!$.
Complex singular-value coordinates on $M_{p,q}(\mathbb C)$,
with ordered singular values, have density
\[
 \frac{2^q\pi^{pq}}{\prod_{j=1}^q(j-1)!(p-q+j-1)!}
 \prod_{i<j}(t_i^2-t_j^2)^2\prod_i t_i^{2(p-q)+1}dt.
\]
The differential of the singular-value map gives the displayed
Vandermonde and powers.  Its scalar is fixed by integrating
$e^{-\operatorname{tr}(Z^*Z)}$: after $x_i=t_i^2$, expansion
of the two Vandermonde determinants reduces the integral to
the determinant of $(p-q+i+j-2)!$; factoring each row and
using the monic rising-factorial Vandermonde evaluates it as
$\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}j!(p-q+j)!$.

Near zero the quotient Hilbert--Schmidt density is $2^{pq}dZ$,
whereas the hyperbolic radial Jacobian has the additional
factor $2^q$ from $\sinh(2t_i)$.
Multiplying by the compact-group volume therefore gives
\[
        c_{G,\rm HS}=(2\pi)^{n(n+1)/2}
                                \frac{s_{p-q}}{s_p^2s_q^2}.
\]
The proved formal-degree formula with compact-dual normalization
is $\prod_{a<b}|\lambda_a-\lambda_b|/s_n$.
The compact-dual measure is Hilbert--Schmidt Haar measure divided
by the volume of $U(n)$, so the Hilbert--Schmidt formal degree
is the Vandermonde divided by $(2\pi)^{n(n+1)/2}$.
\cite{current-construction-record}
This proves the equality in \eqref{global-gram-haar-formal-degree}.
Rescaling Haar measure changes $d_\lambda$ and $c_G$ inversely,
so the equality holds for the literal original measure as well.
Finally $s_{p-q}\le s_p$ and every root difference is at most
$2B$, proving its upper bound.

\begin{theorem}[A uniform bound with the actual period normalizer]
\label{global-gram-haar-normalized-budget}
Retain the preceding actual seed theorem and put
\[
 {\cal W}_u^{\rm st}
  =\int\left(\prod_v\nu_v(g_v)\right)^{4n^2}
                      \|\widetilde W_u(g)\|dq(g),\qquad
 \mathcal M_\phi=(\prod_vd_v)e(1).
\]
Then the original $c_{0,u}=\texttt{c0X}_u$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-haar-normalized-result}
 \mathcal M_\phi c_{0,u}({\cal W}_u^{\rm st})^2
 \le\frac1{\dim_{\mathbb C}V_{\pi_u}}
                  \prod_{v\mid\infty}(2T_u H_v^{7n})^{5n^2}.
\end{equation}
Every moving embedding is present, and no Haar or lattice-index
constant remains unspecified.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the standard frame the compact $H$ is Euclidean unitary.
Consequently $\|nh\|_{\rm op}=\|n\|_{\rm op}$ and the
product of the local subgroup-ball masses is exactly
$\mu_H(H)\,(t^{-1}_*\mu_N)(B_N)$.
Use the literal normalizers
\[
 c_{0,u}=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(K_2)\mu_N(F_{N,u})\mu_H(H)}
                    {\dim V_{\pi_u}},\qquad
 e(1)=\operatorname{vol}(K_2)^{-1}.
\]
Squaring \eqref{global-gram-quantitative-global-L1} cancels
all these measure factors and gives the exact useful bound
\[
 \mathcal M_\phi c_{0,u}({\cal W}_u^{\rm st})^2
 \le\frac{R_N}{\dim V_{\pi_u}}\prod_v
   \left[2m\,4^{2pq+1}(d_vc_{G,v})
                          (2T_u H_v^{6n})^{5n^2}\right].
\]
Apply \eqref{global-gram-haar-ratio} and
\eqref{global-gram-haar-formal-degree}.
For each place, $H_v\ge\max\{4,n,2B_v,2m\}$,
$d_N\le n^2$, $\beta\le n^3$ and $2pq+1\le n^2$.
The extra factors are at most
$H_v^{n^3+(5/2)n^2+1}\le H_v^{5n^3}$.
Absorb them by replacing $6n$ with $7n$ in the displayed
power $5n^2$.  This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

Under the unchanged cited \texttt{Deep.FarBound}, let $I_u$ be
the actual weighted far-return integral on the left of
\eqref{global-gram-quantitative-far}.  The same normalization
therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal M_\phi c_{0,u} I_u^2
 \le\frac{(C_u^{\rm st})^2e^{-h\varepsilon^2/8}}
                {\dim V_{\pi_u}}
              \prod_v(2T_u H_v^{7n})^{5n^2}.
\]
This removes the arbitrary Haar ratio from the actual weighted
period budget and supplies the literal unipotent covolume.
Absorbing the remaining explicit field, weight and word terms
in the original growing-family parameters, controlling the
near returns, and passing to the final restricted chain trace
remain separate obligations.  No ambient average has been
substituted for the weighted period integral.
