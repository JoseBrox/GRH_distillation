\subsection{Far returns between two actual period cells}
\label{global-gram-two-point-far-section}

A diagonal kernel estimate is not, by itself, a bound for the
off-diagonal terms in a period pairing.  The required two-point
estimate follows directly from the same lattice separation and
local matrix-coefficient decay.  We give that argument before
integrating over the actual period measures.

Let $G$ be the original unitary group and let
$\phi(g_\infty,g_f)=p(g_\infty)e(g_f)$ be its given projector
test function.  Write
$\mathcal M_\phi=(\prod_vd_v)e(1)>0$.
For $x,y\in G(\mathbb A_F)$ and $\gamma\in G(F)$, set
\[
 d_v(\gamma;x,y)=
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^n(\log s_i(x_v^{-1}\gamma y_v))^2\right)^{1/2},
\]
where $s_i$ are the singular values in the original positive metric.
Let $\Gamma_{\rm far}(x,y)$ consist of those $\gamma$ for which
$\phi(x^{-1}\gamma y)\ne0$ and $d_v(\gamma;x,y)>\varepsilon$
at at least one real place.  Define the nonnegative absolute sum
\[
 K^{\rm abs}_{\rm far}(x,y)
  =\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\rm far}(x,y)}
                         |\phi(x^{-1}\gamma y)|.
\]
Every rational return, finite column and finite phase is included
before taking this absolute value.

\begin{lemma}[Two-point far-return estimate]
\label{global-gram-two-point-far}
Retain the original local matrix-coefficient estimate, the given
finite idempotent, and their lattice support.  Let $n\geq3$ and
$1\leq q\leq n/2$, where $(n-q,q)$ is the original real
signature.  Suppose
\[
 0<\varepsilon\leq(2q)^{-1/2},\quad
 2n^2\varepsilon\leq\log2,\quad
 64n^2\leq h\varepsilon,\quad
 h\varepsilon^2/16\geq1.
\]
Then the far sum converges absolutely and
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-two-point-far-bound}
 K^{\rm abs}_{\rm far}(x,y)
 \leq\mathcal M_\phi\,2f3^f
 \left(\frac{3\sqrt n}{\tau(x)\tau(y)}\right)^{2n^2f}
                           e^{-h\varepsilon^2/16},
\end{equation}
where $\tau(x)=\min\{1/2,\operatorname{least}(x)\}$ is the
actual lattice minimum.  The points $x$ and $y$ need not be equal,
or lie in the same period cell.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two-point lattice estimate already proved in
the automorphic projection argument~\cite{global-gram-two-point-inputs} is
\[
 \#\{\gamma:\phi(x^{-1}\gamma y)\ne0,
                   \ d_v(\gamma;x,y)\leq t_v\ (\forall v)\}
 \leq A_{x,y}\exp(2n^2\sum_vt_v),\qquad
 A_{x,y}=\left(\frac{3\sqrt n}{\tau(x)\tau(y)}\right)^{2n^2f}.
\]
Its arithmetic separation concerns maps from the lattice at $y$
to the lattice at $x$; it does not identify those two lattices.
The actual finite idempotent has $|e(g_f)|\leq e(1)$, and its
support is contained in the original full lattice stabilizer.
The local lowest-compact-type estimate gives
\[
 \frac{|\phi(x^{-1}\gamma y)|}{\mathcal M_\phi}
 \leq\prod_v\exp\left[-\frac h8
                \min\{d_v^2,d_v/\sqrt{2q}\}\right].
\]
Apply the already proved shell estimate
from the same argument~\cite{global-gram-two-point-inputs} with $A=A_{x,y}$, $\kappa=2n^2$ and
$c=\sqrt{2q}$.  Every one of its hypotheses is displayed above.
It proves both absolute convergence and
\eqref{global-gram-two-point-far-bound}.
This is a direct application of the proved lattice and local
decay arguments; no inference from the diagonal bound is used.
\end{proof}

For an original word $u$ and finite column $\rho$, write
\[
 x_{u,\rho}(g)=(t_ug,a_{u,\rho}),
\]
where $t_u$ is the exact character transport of the quantitative
seed theorem, $a_{u,\rho}$ is the original finite representative,
and $g\in G_\infty$.  Let $d\widetilde\nu_u$ be the transported
original representative measure, and let $\widetilde W_u$ be the
transported unit seed.  Define the actual nonnegative double integral
\[
 \mathcal J_{u\rho,u'\rho'}=
 \int\!\!\int
 K^{\rm abs}_{\rm far}(x_{u,\rho}(g),x_{u',\rho'}(g'))
 \|\widetilde W_u(g)\|\|\widetilde W_{u'}(g')\|
               \,d\widetilde\nu_u(g)d\widetilde\nu_{u'}(g').
\]
The kernel factor need not descend to either Bessel quotient, so
both representative measures remain in this definition.

\begin{theorem}[An exponentially small far contribution to period pairings]
\label{global-gram-two-point-period-bound}
Assume the hypotheses and notation of
Theorem~\ref{global-gram-far-absorption}, and strengthen its fixed
threshold to
\[
 C_\ell\geq16\cdot10^{12}(Z+1).
\]
The actual signature index is $q=r+1$; thus $2q\leq n$
because $n=2r+k+1$ and $k\geq1$.
At the same literal cutoff $\varepsilon=10^{-6}n^{-8}f^{-2}$,
every original pair of words and finite columns satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{global-gram-two-point-period-estimate}
 \sqrt{c_{0,u}c_{0,u'}}\,\mathcal J_{u\rho,u'\rho'}
 \leq
 \frac{e^{-n^3fL}}{
          \sqrt{\dim V_{\pi_u}\,\dim V_{\pi_{u'}}}}.
\end{equation}
In particular the double integral is finite.  The same estimate
holds with fixed right translates by elements of the original
finite level subgroup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\tau_u$ and $C^{\rm st}_u$ be the exact quantities in the
preceding absorption proof.  The arithmetic minimum on the
transported actual cell gives
$\tau(x_{u,\rho}(g))\geq\tau_u\nu_u(g)^{-1/f}$.
Consequently \eqref{global-gram-two-point-far-bound} is at most
\[
 \mathcal M_\phi\sqrt{C^{\rm st}_uC^{\rm st}_{u'}}
 e^{-h\varepsilon^2/16}\,
          \nu_u(g)^{2n^2}\nu_{u'}(g')^{2n^2}.
\]
The powers are exactly $2n^2$, since the field degree in the
counting exponent cancels the denominator in the minimum bound.
Since $\nu_u\geq1$, the existing moments of order $4n^2$ bound
both factors after integration.  Their majorants descend to the
appropriate quotients.  The original Weil formulas and Tonelli's
theorem therefore give
\[
 \mathcal J_{u\rho,u'\rho'}\leq
 \mathcal M_\phi\sqrt{C^{\rm st}_uC^{\rm st}_{u'}}
 e^{-h\varepsilon^2/16}\,
             \mathcal W^{\rm st}_u\mathcal W^{\rm st}_{u'}.
\]
The given finite representatives preserve the lattices used by the
minimum theorem, which proves uniformity in the columns and finite
right translates.

The normalized moment theorem, applied separately and then multiplied,
gives
\[
 \mathcal M_\phi\sqrt{c_{0,u}c_{0,u'}}
 \mathcal W^{\rm st}_u\mathcal W^{\rm st}_{u'}
 \leq\frac{\exp(Sn^3fL)}{
                  \sqrt{\dim V_{\pi_u}\dim V_{\pi_{u'}}}},
 \quad S=5(1+C_T+7(U+13)).
\]
The absorption proof gives
$\log C^{\rm st}_u\leq Cn^3fL$ for every $u$, where
$C=9+4(2C_g+6+C_T/2)>0$, and $Z=2C+S$.
Thus the exponent of the resulting bound is at most
\[
 \left(C+S-\frac{C_\ell}{16\cdot10^{12}}\right)n^3fL
 \leq(C+S-Z-1)n^3fL\leq-n^3fL.
\]
This proves \eqref{global-gram-two-point-period-estimate}.
\end{proof}

The estimate controls the full far part of these two-point weighted
kernel integrals with the actual measures and normalizers.  It does
not identify those integrals with a prescribed final arithmetic Gram
operator, and summing over the finite columns still requires their
actual ranks.  The near part is not bounded by this theorem.  In
particular, no positive-trace estimate follows by discarding it.
