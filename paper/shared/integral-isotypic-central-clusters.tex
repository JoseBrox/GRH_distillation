\subsubsection{Central separation before the integral AST estimate}
\label{sec:adriazola-projector-clusters}
Keep the admissible lattice \(\Lambda\) and algebraic
\(\mathrm{GL}_r\)-representation from the preceding improvement of
Adriazola's integral projector argument \cite{adriazola-reconstruction}.
The quadratic central operator
\[
 C=\sum_{a,b=1}^r E_{ab}E_{ba}
\]
preserves \(\Lambda\): off-diagonal root operators preserve it
by admissibility and diagonal operators act by integral weights.
It commutes with all \(E_{uv}\), as follows by expanding the
commutator and cancelling the two sums with opposite index shifts.
On the irreducible of highest weight
\(\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)\), its scalar is
\[
 c_\lambda=\sum_{a=1}^r\lambda_a(\lambda_a+r+1-2a).
\]
Indeed the strictly raising operators kill a highest vector;
each pair \(a<b\) contributes \(\lambda_a-\lambda_b\)
after commuting \(E_{ab}\) past \(E_{ba}\).

\begin{theorem}[Integral spectral clusters and reduced projector cost]
\label{thm:projector-central-clusters}
Partition the occurring types by \(c_\lambda\bmod p\) in the
residue field. The projector onto each full cluster preserves
\(\Lambda\). For a cluster \(B\) write \(V_B,\Lambda_B\) for the
cluster subspace and its intersection lattice.
For \(\lambda\in B\), let \(D_{\lambda,B}(p)\) be the
root-dependent AST/partial-trace bound of the preceding theorem
computed on \(V_B\), rather than \(V\).
For any subset \(\Sigma\) of types, a sufficient denominator
exponent of its projector is
\[
 \max_B\min\left\{
   \max_{\lambda\in B\cap\Sigma}D_{\lambda,B}(p),
   \max_{\lambda\in B\setminus\Sigma}D_{\lambda,B}(p)\right\}.
                                                               \tag{MP1}
\]
Empty maxima are zero. For a single type with scalar distinct
from every other type in its cluster, the polynomial separator
gives the additional sufficient exponent
\[
       \sum_{c'\in\{c_\mu:\mu\in B,\ \mu\ne\lambda\}}
                                      v_p(c_\lambda-c'),       \tag{MP2}
\]
where the sum is over distinct scalars. One may take the
smaller of this and (MP1) for that type.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each cluster take the monic product of \(X-c_\lambda\)
over its distinct scalars. These polynomials for different
clusters have pairwise unit resultants; hence are comaximal
over \(O[X]\) (the determinant of the Sylvester matrix gives
an integral Bezout identity). The Chinese remainder polynomials
which are one modulo one cluster polynomial and zero modulo the
others evaluate at \(C\) to integral projectors onto precisely
the cluster spaces. This works because \(C\) acts semisimply by
the given scalars. The resulting direct-sum lattice
decomposition is exact. Each cluster lattice is admissible:
weight projections and divided powers preserve the intersection
of the original lattice and the invariant subspace.
The previous AST estimate now uses only weights of
\(\operatorname{Hom}(W_\lambda,V_B)\), a subset of the former
weight support. Summing over the types in \(B\cap\Sigma\)
costs the maximum of the individual exponents, and the
complementary sum gives the same projector as identity minus
that sum. Take the smaller cost; combine clusters using their
integral projectors. This proves (MP1). Finally
\(\prod_{c'\ne c_\lambda}(C-c')/(c_\lambda-c')\)
on the specified cluster equals the single-type projector
when no other type has its scalar. The numerator is integral
and the displayed denominator gives (MP2).
\end{proof}

The same proof partitions by joint residual eigenvalues of
any finite family of integral central operators: apply the
individual integral cluster projections successively.
For example any additional integral central element of the
enveloping algebra can be used, provided its scalar values are
known. No separation of equal central characters is assumed.
In a cluster consisting of only one type, (MP1) is zero.
The estimate holds at every prime, and gives the balanced Fock
version by exactly the scalar-extension/descent argument of the
preceding theorem. It can also control the integral cost of
retaining a union of types without splitting it into individual
types. It is relevant to the original loss engines only after
verifying admissibility of the actual retained lattice.
