\subsection{Uniform weighted assembly for ordinary RH}
\label{rh-weighted-selmer-assembly}

This numerical implication is conditional on the individual length
estimate (SL3) and the previously reconstructed aggregate estimates.
In particular it does not constitute a further proof of (SL3).

\begin{proposition}[Weighted assembly from the individual estimates]
\label{rh-weighted-selmer-assembly-prop}
Retain the original RH host
\[
 E=\mathbf Q(\mu_{2^a})^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d}),\qquad
 N=2^a qd,\qquad [E:\mathbf Q]=2^a,\qquad a=h+2\ge2.
\]
Retain its exceptional set \(P\), the projectors
\eqref{shared-rh-selmer-projectors}, its local conditions, the
twist cutoff \(t\ge2\), and \(q,d>\max(P\cup\{2t+1\})\).
Assume the aggregate nonresonant estimate (RN3), the entire
exponent-one estimate (DC10), the aggregate high-dyadic-conductor
estimate of Corollary~\ref{cor:rh-selmer-low-dyadic}, and the individual
remaining Case II estimate (SL3), with uniform absolute constants.
Then there exist absolute constants \(A_*,C_*>0\) such that
\[
 \log\mathcal P(w)\le C_*(Nt)^{A_*}
\]
for every finite supported family \(0\le w_\ell\le1\).
No bound on the number of selected places or on the largest
nonresonant coefficient prime in the support is needed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the integral Fourier comparison, the contribution of an odd
coefficient prime and a twist is exactly the sum of the
ramification-normalized lengths of its retained characters,
multiplied by \(\log\ell\) and \(w_\ell\). All summands are
nonnegative. First remove all exponent-one terms using (DC10),
at a cost at most \((2N)^{B_1}\). For every \(2\le e<t\) use
(RN3) on the entire finite subset of nonresonant primes in the
support, removing a sum at most \(C_1N[N(e+1)]^{A_1}\).
It is essential that (RN3) is independent of that subset.

In the remaining sum \(\ell-1\mid2e\), so
\(\ell\le2e+1\le2t-1\). Thus \(\ell\nmid N\).
The trivial character is either omitted by the projector
or has zero length by the Case Z vanishing: outside \(P\) the
prime is at least five and the trivial character has even
\(e\). Every retained character is therefore a nontrivial
even-order character. Remove the high-dyadic-conductor part
using Corollary~\ref{cor:rh-selmer-low-dyadic}, at a cost at most
\([N(e+1)]^{B_2}\) for each \(e\).

For the low-conductor part put
\[
 a_\ell=\min\{a,\lfloor\log_2(8\ell^2)\rfloor\},
 \qquad E_{\ell,\mathrm{low}}=
 \mathbf Q(\mu_{2^{a_\ell}})^+(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d}).
\]
The invariants are those of the original Selmer group under
\(\operatorname{Gal}(E/E_{\ell,\mathrm{low}})\).
The index is a power of two and hence a unit at \(\ell\);
restriction and corestriction give the integral comparison,
including the strict local conditions. The number of characters
of the reduced field is \(2^{a_\ell}\le8\ell^2\).
Its conductor parameter is \(N_\ell=2^{a_\ell}qd\), not \(2^{a_\ell}\).
We apply (SL3) on the original host \(N\), without any
unstated size hypothesis on a new reduced host. With \(C\) its
absolute exponent, for each \(e\) the remaining sum is at most
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\ell\ \mathrm{odd\ prime}\\\ell-1\mid2e}}
 8\ell^2[\ell N(e+1)]^C
 \ \le\
 8(2e+1)^3[(2e+1)N(e+1)]^C.
\]
Both quadratic factors have been charged in every
individual bound, and in the count of the reduced characters.
There are at most \(t-1\) twist indices; \(e+1\le t\).
Adding the four nonnegative bounds proves the proposition,
for example with
\[
 A_*=\max\{B_1,A_1+2,B_2+1,2C+4\}
\]
and a sufficiently large absolute prefactor \(C_*\).
Weights can only decrease these bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Original allowance on the same windows]
\label{rh-weighted-selmer-allowance}
Under the preceding proposition and the supplied-case hypotheses
\eqref{shared-rh-supplied-cases}, on the original common windows,
\[
 \log\mathcal P(w)\le\tfrac12 C_V y(B_0+N_c),
 \qquad
 \mathcal V\le C_V y(B_0+N_c)
\]
eventually, for every fixed \(C_V>0\), uniformly in the permitted
finite prime weights, where \(y=x^\varepsilon\),
\(B_0=fm e^y y\), \(f,m\ge1\), and \(N_c\ge0\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(0<\varepsilon<1/52\), and all constants, before increasing
the window parameter. The same windows supply \(t\le x^{400}\),
\([E:\mathbf Q]\le8x^{2+8\varepsilon}\), and
\(\log(Nt)\le2x^{\varepsilon/4}\).
To see directly why the last estimate retains the two varying
quadratic factors, use the selected common pair at the left
endpoint \(U\), with
\[
 \log q\le U^{\varepsilon/5},\qquad
 \log d\le U^{\varepsilon/5}(\log U)^2
\]
(up to fixed multiplicative constants in these inequalities).
At every \(x\) of the prescribed window \(U\le x\le U^J\),
\[
 \log(Nt)=a\log2+\log q+\log d+\log t
 \le \log8+(402+8\varepsilon)\log x
       +O(U^{\varepsilon/5}(1+(\log U)^2))
 =o(x^{\varepsilon/4}).
\]
The estimate is uniform: the quadratic terms divided by
\(x^{\varepsilon/4}\) are bounded by a constant times
\(U^{-\varepsilon/20}(1+(\log U)^2)\).
The stipulated exponential lower bounds for the same pair
also imply \(q,d>2t+1\) eventually on this fixed-power window.
There is no reselection of either quadratic field for the
Selmer estimate.

The proposition gives \(\log\mathcal P(w)\le
 C_*\exp(2A_* x^{\varepsilon/4})=o(e^y)\).
The complementary terms of Proposition~\ref{shared-rh-residual-budget}
are \(o(e^y)\) as well. Since \(y(B_0+N_c)\ge e^y y^2\),
each is eventually at most half the specified allowance.
This proves the two inequalities. The proof is uniform in
the weights and so permits the original rank-weighted average
with no change in its weights or field data.
\end{proof}

The proposition supplies the numerical implication needed in
the RH specialization, subject to the preceding individual estimates.
It neither constructs the rest of the compatible arithmetic data
nor supplies the nontrivial-character version required for GRH.


\begin{theorem}[RH weighted Selmer supplier]\label{thm:rh-weighted-selmer-supplier}
For the conductor-one construction with the original host, twists,
projectors and size restrictions of
Proposition~\ref{rh-weighted-selmer-assembly-prop},
there are absolute constants \(A_*,C_*>0\) such that for every
finitely supported family \(0\le w_\ell\le1\),
\[
              \log\mathcal P(w)\le C_*(Nt)^{A_*}.
\]
On the original common windows, with the supplied complementary cases
in \eqref{shared-rh-supplied-cases}, this implies the half-allowance
and total allowance of Corollary~\ref{rh-weighted-selmer-allowance}.
There is no additional rank-four automorphy, rigidity, finiteness or
uniform individual length hypothesis in this statement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-four-point-premises} supplies (F), (E) and (A)
for the actual remaining Case II data. In its seed construction the
quadratic-field Eisenstein multiplier replaces the general Hasse
descent; the direct irreducible propagation theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:selmer-p1-direct}) supplies the required
characteristic-prime implication for the actual ordinary Hecke ideal.
Together with the ordinary-family theorem and the abstract integral
length theorem, these give (SL3) by
Theorem~\ref{thm:rh-sp4-conditional-length}.
The other three estimates in
Proposition~\ref{rh-weighted-selmer-assembly-prop}
are the previously proved nonresonant, exponent-one and high-dyadic
bounds. Apply that proposition and its corollary without changing
the host or the selected places. This is an implication on the
specified common windows, not the construction of the compatible
geometric or norm data there.
\end{proof}
