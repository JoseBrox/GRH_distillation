\paragraph{Three bounds for the odd obstruction.}
Write $A=k[[t]]$, $q=|k|$, $n=a+b$, and let $O_m$ be the odd
matrices over $A/(t^m)$. Fix the normalized point, its original
odd family $\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_g$ and its ordinary bases.
Retain the original even matrix span, pairwise distinct ordinary
characters and supplied arithmetic finiteness.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform odd bounds]
\label{prop:selmer-second-bound-suppliers}
There are constants $C_0,C_h,C_d$, independent of $m$ and of
the compatible auxiliary group, with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The base odd Selmer group has order at most $q^{gm+C_0}$.
\item Each original ordinary local quotient has at most $q^{C_h}$
invariants.
\item The additive maps $f:O_m\to\mathbb F_\ell$ satisfying
$f(\gamma Y)=\bar\chi(\gamma)f(Y)$ have order at most $q^{C_d}$.
\end{enumerate}
The compatible group acts through its given surjection to the
base group, and the coefficient twist is pulled back along that
same map.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the first assertion, list all the linear equations defining
the original polarized odd Selmer cocycles, retaining one local
witness at every ordinary place. Their complete solution module
has finite reduction modulo $t$: the residual cocycles and the
finite witness coordinates are finite. Let $K$ be the integral
kernel and let $B\subseteq K$ consist of solutions whose cocycle
part belongs to $\sum_iA\kappa_i$ plus integral skew boundaries.
The original spanning property of the odd family gives $K/B$
torsion.

Apply the complete-equation count to this $B$, then project away
the witnesses. It bounds every finite-level cocycle set by a
fixed factor times the projected image of $B$. That image has
at most $q^{gm}$ times the number of allowed modular boundaries.
Projection cannot increase the number of cosets. Normalization
at complex conjugation identifies the quotient with the odd
cohomological Selmer group. This proves the first assertion,
including classes that do not lift to integral cocycles.

At an ordinary place $v$, the actual quotient embeds in
\[
 T_m=M_n(A/(t^m))/\{\text{upper triangular matrices}\}
\]
through the lower coordinates in the given ordinary basis.
The kernel and surjectivity of its given quotient map prove this
injection, without assuming that the basis preserves the ordering
of the two blocks. Filter $T_m$ by distance below the diagonal.
Conjugation by an upper triangular matrix preserves that filtration,
whose successive characters are $\psi_{v,i}\psi_{v,j}^{-1}$,
one for each $i>j$. Choose $g_{v,ij}$ where these two characters
differ and put
\[
 C_{h,v}=\sum_{i>j}v_t\!\left(
       \psi_{v,i}(g_{v,ij})\psi_{v,j}(g_{v,ij})^{-1}-1\right).
\]
Each rank-one invariant module has at most the corresponding
$q$-power elements. Left exactness of invariants bounds the full
order by $q^{C_{h,v}}$. Use $C_h=\max_vC_{h,v}$, or zero if
there are no ordinary places. The local data are unchanged in
the compatible auxiliary groups.

For the last assertion, let $Q=\operatorname{diag}(I_a,0_b)$.
Choose the integer $s$ supplied by the original span:
\[
                       t^sQ=\sum_r u_r\rho(h_r),
 \qquad u_r\in A,\quad h_r\in\ker\chi.
\]
Choose preimages of the $h_r$ in the compatible group.
Equivariance on its cyclotomic kernel gives
$f(\rho(h_r)Y)=f(Y\rho(h_r))$. Replacing $Y$ by $u_rY$
and adding yields $f(t^s[Q,Y])=0$. This step does not require
$A$-linearity of $f$.
The commutator with $Q$ fixes the upper rectangular block and
negates the lower one, so it is an automorphism of $O_m$.
Thus $f$ factors through $O_m/t^sO_m$. Its full additive dual
has order $q^{2ab\min(m,s)}$, since additive maps are linear
over the prime field. Take $C_d=2ab\,s$. Empty blocks give
the zero module and $C_d=0$.
\end{proof}

Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:selmer-block-resultant} and its
original tame descent, the first assertion gives
$|\operatorname{Sel}_{N,e}|\leq q^{ge+C_0+C_{\rm aux}}$
simultaneously for $e\leq m$ at sufficiently large level depending
on $m$. The four constants in the second estimate are therefore
supplied, with
\[
          C_2=C_0+C_{\rm aux}
                     +|\{v:v\mid\ell\}|C_h+2C_d.
\]
Passing from these counts to the second obstruction module requires
its coefficient exact sequences and local-condition comparison;
identifying a vanishing obstruction with the prescribed second-order
lift is a further step \cite{alpo-second-bound-suppliers}.
