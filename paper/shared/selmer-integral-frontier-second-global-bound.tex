\paragraph{The second-order lift.}
Assume that $\ell$ is odd. Write $E_m$ and $O_m$ for the even and
odd matrices over $A_m$. In the original commutative coefficient
algebra
\[
 D_m=A_m\oplus\bigoplus_j A_m\epsilon_j
       \oplus\bigoplus_j A_m\epsilon'_j\oplus A_m\eta,\qquad
 \epsilon_i\epsilon'_j=\epsilon'_j\epsilon_i=\delta_{ij}\eta,
\]
all other products of nilpotent generators are zero. Put
$R=(1+\sum_j\epsilon_j\kappa_j)\rho$ and
$W_{2,m}=\bigoplus_j\epsilon'_jE_m\oplus\eta O_m$.

\begin{proposition}[Second Selmer estimate]
\label{prop:selmer-second-global-bound}
Retain the original arithmetic system, normalized point, odd family
and auxiliary tower. With the constant $C_2$ of Proposition
\ref{prop:selmer-obstruction-quotients}, every $m$ and all sufficiently
large $N$ have the following property. For any $g+1$ tuples
$p_{ij}$ of original even Selmer cocycles, there are $c_i\in A$,
at least one a unit, and a cocycle $\Psi$ in the original
second-order Selmer set such that
\[
                   (\Psi)_{\epsilon'_j}
                      =t^{C_2}\sum_i c_i p_{ij}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $(P_j;Z)\in W_{2,m}$, multiplication gives
\[
 \gamma(P_j;Z)=
 \left(\operatorname{Ad}\rho(\gamma)P_j;\
       \operatorname{Ad}\rho(\gamma)Z+
       \sum_j[\kappa_j(\gamma),\operatorname{Ad}\rho(\gamma)P_j]\right)
\]
on the index-two subgroup. Complex conjugation acts by minus
transpose. The original polarization is
$R(c\gamma c)=\chi(\gamma)^{1-n}R(\gamma)^{-T}$, with $\chi$
the reduced cyclotomic character; its scalar cancels in conjugation.
Thus the actual representations form an exact sequence
\[
                 0\to O_m\xrightarrow{\iota}W_{2,m}
                        \xrightarrow{\mathrm{pr}}E_m^g\to0,
 \qquad \iota(Z)=\eta Z.
\]
Its linear section is $(P_j)\mapsto(P_j;0)$.

For an ordinary place, let $Y_{j,v}$ be the original witnesses
for $\kappa_j$ and $w_{1,v}=(1-\sum_j\epsilon_jY_{j,v})w_v$.
The exact basis-change formula is
\[
 w_{1,v}^{-1}(P_j;Z)w_{1,v}
  =\left(w_v^{-1}P_jw_v;\
     w_v^{-1}\left(Z+\sum_j[Y_{j,v},P_j]\right)w_v\right).
\]
Let $N_v$ impose upper triangularity in this basis. It is locally
stable, and $\iota(Z)\in N_v$ if and only if $\pi_v(Z)=0$ for
the original odd local quotient. If all the even components
are upper triangular, subtracting
$\iota(Z+\sum_j[Y_{j,v},P_j])$ puts the element in $N_v$.
The transformed odd coefficient always has zero diagonal:
it joins distinct block types in the ordinary basis.
At old unramified places put $N_u=0$; the original $R$ is
trivial on inertia.

Extend each prescribed polarized cocycle $p_{ij}$ uniquely to the
full group by $p_{ij}(\gamma c)=p_{ij}(\gamma)$ for $\gamma$
in the index-two subgroup. This gives value zero at $c$ and
keeps the prescribed restriction unchanged.
Let $x_i=(p_{i1},\ldots,p_{ig};0)$ be its section lift.
Then $dx_i=\iota z_i$ with $z_i$ a global
two-cocycle. On the index-two subgroup,
\[
 z_i(\gamma,\delta)=
       \sum_j[\kappa_j(\gamma),
                    \operatorname{Ad}\rho(\gamma)p_{ij}(\delta)].
\]
At an ordinary place use the boundary $b_{i,v}=dS_{i,v}$ of
the section of its original even local witnesses. The basis-change
formula provides a continuous $\tau_{i,v}$ with
$x_i-b_{i,v}-\iota\tau_{i,v}\in N_v$.
At an old place the actual unramified lifting property supplies
a local cocycle $b_{i,u}$ lifting the projected tuple and vanishing
on inertia; then $x_i-b_{i,u}=\iota\tau_{i,u}$.
Set $y_{i,w}=\pi_w\tau_{i,w}$. Differentiating gives
$dy_{i,w}=\pi_w\operatorname{res}_wz_i$, so
$\xi_i=[z_i,(y_{i,w})_w]\in H_{N,m}$.

Suppose $\sum_i a_i\xi_i=0$. The definition of its boundary gives
a global one-cochain $u$ such that
$\Psi_0=\sum_i a_ix_i-\iota u$ is a cocycle, and local primitives
$Y_w\in O_m$ such that
\[
          \Psi_0-\sum_i a_i b_{i,w}-d(\iota Y_w)\in N_w
                     \quad\text{on }J_w.
\]
Since $\Psi_0(c)\in\iota(O_m)$ and $c\Psi_0(c)=-\Psi_0(c)$,
put $v=\iota^{-1}(\Psi_0(c))/2$ and
$\Psi=\Psi_0+d(\iota v)$. The plus sign makes $\Psi(c)=0$,
without changing its projection. Replace $Y_w$ by $Y_w+v$.

At old inertia, every term except $\Psi$ in the local condition
vanishes, so $\Psi$ vanishes there. At an ordinary place the
primitive is $\sum_i a_iS_{i,v}+\iota(Y_v+v)$, which gives the
required upper triangularity. On the original subgroup $\mu_v$,
the even diagonal coefficients vanish by the input local conditions;
the transformed odd coefficient has zero diagonal identically.
Thus the full original ordinary condition holds. Restriction to
the index-two subgroup gives the original polarization, and all
cochains are continuous over the finite coefficients.

Proposition~\ref{prop:selmer-obstruction-quotients} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:selmer-finite-obstruction} give $c_i$, at least one
a unit, with $t^{C_2}\sum_i c_i\xi_i=0$. Take $a_i=t^{C_2}c_i$
in the construction. This proves the assertion with precisely the
original module, local conditions and quantifiers
\cite{alpo-second-global-selmer}.
\end{proof}

Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:selmer-first-global-bound},
this supplies both finite-level Selmer estimates under the retained
arithmetic inputs. Their arithmetic realization and the patched
automorphic modules remain separate steps.
