\subsection{The original finite theta system}
\label{actual-finite-theta-system}

Let $X$ be the prescribed Hermitian frame space of rank $r$ over
the CM field $E$, with its integral alternating trace pairing $e$,
reference self-dual lattice $L_*$, and original Schr\"odinger
operators $H(y)$ and $W(\gamma)$. Fix original level data
\[
 \mathfrak m\ne0,\quad\mathfrak m\ne\mathcal O_E,
 \quad(\mathfrak m,2\overline{\mathfrak m})=1,
 \quad J\le\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m),
 \quad\lambda:J\longrightarrow\mathbb C^\times.
\]
The marked vectors $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_r$ form the prescribed
basis of $L_*/\mathfrak mL_*$. Retain the integral $w\in E$ with
$w+\bar w=1$. Let $\mathcal L$ be the original self-dual lattices
equal to $L_*$ at the primes of $\mathfrak m\overline{\mathfrak m}$,
and let $\Delta$ be the original rational unitary group with level
reduction in $J$. For $L\in\mathcal L$ set
\[
 S_L=\mathfrak m\overline{\mathfrak m}^{-1}L,
 \qquad A_{\Lambda,L}=\Lambda\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}S_L.
\]
The tensor lattice is embedded in $n$-by-$r$ matrices, with the
original alternating pairing and semicharacter
$\epsilon_w(y)=\exp(\pi i\sum_k e(y_k,wy_k))$.

\begin{theorem}\label{actual-finite-theta-system-theorem}
Let $n$ be even, $n\ge4[E:\mathbb Q]r+4$, and let $M>0$ with
$M\in\mathfrak m$. Choose unit vacua $v_{0,L}$ for
$\mathcal O_E^n\otimes S_L$ with the prescribed semicharacter.
For every original $\Lambda\in\mathrm{Vset}(E,n,M)$ define
\[
 v_{\Lambda,L}
  ={\operatorname{proj}_{A_{\Lambda,L}}v_{0,L}
       \over\|\operatorname{proj}_{A_{\Lambda,L}}v_{0,L}\|},
 \qquad
 \Phi_{L,\Lambda}=\sum_{k\in J}\lambda(k)H(c_k)v_{\Lambda,L},
\]
where $c_k\in\Lambda\otimes2\overline{\mathfrak m}L$ has first
$r$ rows congruent to $k\beta$ modulo $\mathfrak m$, and remaining
rows congruent to zero. The original quotient sum is precisely this
finite sum and is independent of the allowed representatives.
These vectors, with the original vacuum multiplier $\mu$, satisfy
every clause of the original \texttt{IsSystem}. In particular,
\begin{align}
 \langle\Phi_{L,\Lambda},\Phi_{L,\Lambda}\rangle&=|J|,
                                                        \label{finite-system-norm}\\
 |\langle\Phi_{L,\Lambda},\Phi_{L',\Lambda}\rangle|
   &\le |J|[L':L\cap L']^{-n/2},                         \label{finite-system-overlap}\\
 \sum_{L'\in\mathcal L,\ L'\ne L}
       [L':L\cap L']^{-n/2}&<0.65.                      \label{finite-system-tail}
\end{align}
The projections in the definition are nonzero. No coefficient
ring, level, lattice or Haar normalization is changed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Finite vacuum identities, including the prime two.}
The fractional ideal $\mathfrak m\overline{\mathfrak m}^{-1}$
has norm ideal one, so $S_L$ is self-dual. Localizing at each
prime shows that $A_{\Lambda,L}$ is self-dual and that
\begin{equation}\label{finite-system-tensor-index}
 [A_{\Lambda,L'}:A_{\Lambda,L}\cap A_{\Lambda,L'}]
                           =[L':L\cap L']^n.
\end{equation}
This uses lengths of projective modules over the local discrete
valuation rings, and does not presume that $\Lambda$ is globally
free. The identity $w+\bar w=1$ gives the required semicharacter
law on each of these integral alternating lattices.

All calculations take place in a finite Schr\"odinger quotient:
choose a sufficiently small common lattice $B$, and use the finite
symplectic module $B^\#/B$. For a self-dual lattice $A\supset B$,
the average of $\epsilon_w(a)H(a)$ over $A/B$ is an orthogonal
idempotent. Only the identity translation has nonzero trace;
the finite Schr\"odinger representation has dimension
$|A/B|$. The idempotent thus has rank one. Two such idempotents
have product trace
\[
 |\langle v_A,v_{A'}\rangle|^2=[A':A\cap A']^{-1}.
\]
Indeed the two semicharacters agree on $A\cap A'$; in the double
average only pairs with sum zero contribute. This proves existence,
uniqueness up to phase and nonvanishing of the projected vacua.
It also proves that translated vacuum lines are orthogonal unless
their displacement lies in $A+A'$, by testing the characters on
$A\cap A'$. These arguments keep the semicharacter at two and
never divide an integral equation by two.

\textbf{The exact finite sum and the level action.}
At primes of $\mathfrak m$, the original $\Lambda$ is standard
and $L$ equals $L_*$. Since $2\overline{\mathfrak m}$ is a unit
there, the Chinese remainder theorem supplies one representative
$c_k$ for each $k\in J$ in the stated tensor lattice. Representatives
for $L,L'$ can simultaneously be chosen in
$\Lambda\otimes2\overline{\mathfrak m}(L\cap L')$.
Changing an allowed representative changes it by the original
denominator lattice
$\Lambda\otimes2\overline{\mathfrak m}\mathfrak m L$;
the Heisenberg law and $\epsilon_w$ make its action identical.
The original quotient sum has exactly these $|J|$ nonzero terms,
with coefficient $\lambda(k)$. Finite order gives $|\lambda(k)|=1$.

Different labels are orthogonal even between $L$ and $L'$:
a nonzero overlap would force their displacement into
$A_{\Lambda,L}+A_{\Lambda,L'}$ and hence equality of their
$\mathfrak m$-residues. For equal labels the common translation
is unitary, so the overlap is the original vacuum overlap. Equations
\eqref{finite-system-norm} and \eqref{finite-system-overlap}
follow from \eqref{finite-system-tensor-index}.

Vacuum uniqueness and unitarity give the original phase
\[
 W(\gamma)v_{0,L}=\mu(\gamma,L)\lambda(\bar\gamma)
                          v_{0,\gamma L},\qquad |\mu(\gamma,L)|=1.
\]
Conjugating the finite projector transports its semicharacter
exactly. Reindexing the level labels therefore gives
$W(\gamma)\Phi_{L,\Lambda}=\mu(\gamma,L)\Phi_{\gamma L,\Lambda}$
for $\gamma\in\Delta$. If a rational unitary $\gamma$ has a
nonzero overlap between a level vector and $W(\gamma)$ of another,
the displacement test gives two marked row bases congruent under
$\gamma$. They imply integrality and invertibility at the level
primes and reduction $kk'^{-1}\in J$. Thus $\gamma\in\Delta$.
This proves the exact outside-level vanishing, without enlarging
the group. Finite sums and finite Fourier transforms preserve
the original Schwartz space.

\textbf{The strict overlap sum.}
Put $d=[E:\mathbb Q]r$. For fixed $L$, the number of self-dual
integral alternating lattices $L'$ with $[L':L\cap L']=m$ is at
most $m^{2d}$. To see this, first choose $I=L\cap L'$, a subgroup
of index $m$ in $L$. Then $I\subset L'\subset I^\#$ and
$L'/I$ has order $m$. Each choice has at most $m^d$ possibilities.
For the second assertion use annihilator duality in the finite
group $I^\#/I$, which is generated by at most $d$ elements; its
subgroups of order $m$ inject, after duality and pullback, into
the index-$m$ subgroups of $\mathbb Z^d$. The elementary divisor
count for those subgroups is multiplicative, with prime-power
factor
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^e{p^{d+j-1}-1\over p^j-1}\le p^{de}.
\]
Distinct self-dual lattices have $m\ge2$, since a proper inclusion
between self-dual lattices is impossible. The stated range gives
\[
 \sum_{L'\ne L}[L':L\cap L']^{-n/2}
 \le\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}m^{2d-n/2}
 \le\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}m^{-2}<0.65.
\]
The last strict inequality follows, for example, by summing
$m=2,\ldots,20$ and bounding the remaining tail by $1/20$.
Restricting to the original $\mathcal O_E$-lattices only decreases
this count. Rank zero has one lattice and an empty tail.

\textbf{Vanishing of the isotropic coinvariants.}
Fix $0\ne\xi\in E^r$ with $h(\xi,\xi)=0$ and set
$B_\xi=\{v\in E^n:v\otimes\xi\in A_{\Lambda,L}\}$.
Each term $H(c_k)v_{\Lambda,L}$ is an eigenvector for the commuting
operators $H(v\otimes\xi)$, $v\in B_\xi$, with character
\[
 \chi_k(v)=\exp\!\left(-2\pi i\sum_j e(v_j\xi,(c_k)_j)\right).
\]
This character is nontrivial. A local verification retains the
original markings: choose a prime $\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak m$.
It differs from $\overline{\mathfrak p}$, so the two completions
are paired perfectly by the Hermitian trace form. If
$v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak m)=a>0$, the two parts of $S_L$ are
$\mathfrak p^aL_{\mathfrak p}$ and
$\overline{\mathfrak p}^{-a}L_{\overline{\mathfrak p}}$.
The first $r$ rows of $c_k$ form a basis over
$\mathcal O_{E,\mathfrak p}$, since their reductions are a basis
modulo $\mathfrak p$. Scale the nonzero
$\xi_{\overline{\mathfrak p}}$ into
$\overline{\mathfrak p}^{-a}L_{\overline{\mathfrak p}}
\setminus L_{\overline{\mathfrak p}}$.
The annihilator of $L_{\mathfrak p}$ for the original alternating
trace pairing modulo $\mathbb Z_p$ is exactly
$L_{\overline{\mathfrak p}}$. Thus some integral multiple of
one marked row has nonintegral trace pairing with this vector;
absorb that multiplier into the chosen scale in the corresponding
coordinate of $v$. This argument uses the trace-dual module,
including the local codifferent and the original $\xi_0$;
it does not identify an unweighted Hermitian dual with a trace
dual at a ramified prime. Set the other local coordinates to zero.
The Chinese remainder theorem in the full lattice $B_\xi$
realizes this local character by a global $v$. Hence
$\chi_k(v)\ne1$ for some $v=v_k$.

The finite group-algebra element
\[
                 \prod_{k\in J}(H(v_k\otimes\xi)-\chi_k(v_k))
\]
kills every summand of $\Phi_{L,\Lambda}$. Its value at the
trivial character is $\prod_k(1-\chi_k(v_k))\ne0$. Expanding
it gives exactly the finite relation with nonzero coefficient sum
required by the original isotropic-coinvariant clause.

\textbf{The admitted doubled family and its phase.}
Put $S=S_L$, $S'=S_{L'}$, $K=S+S'$. The projective
$\mathcal O_E$-module $K$ permits a linear section of
$S\oplus S'\to K$, $(u,v)\mapsto u-v$. Write this section as
$(dA,dA-d)$. With the original skew matrix
$T=(\kappa_0/2)G$, set
\[
 \beta_0=(A-\tfrac12 I)T,
 \qquad\beta=(1-w)\beta_0+w\beta_0^*.
\]
Then $\beta=\beta^*$. Self-duality shows that
$d(\beta-\beta_0)$ pairs integrally with $K$ for every $d\in K$:
expand
$\beta_0^*-\beta_0=(A-I)T(A-I)^*-ATA^*$ and use the two
integral lattices. The integral $w$ preserves that property.
Consequently the original doubled lattice is the graph of
$\beta$ over $K$, modulo its trace-dual lattice. This proof retains
the rational halves in the original coordinates; it does not
assert that they are integral.

The finite Fourier transform for the original doubling map gives
fixed scalars $\kappa\ne0$ and $b>0$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{dbl}(f,g)(0)=\kappa\langle f,g\rangle,
 \qquad
 \langle\operatorname{dbl}(f,g),\operatorname{dbl}(f',g')\rangle
       =b\langle f,f'\rangle\overline{\langle g,g'\rangle}.
\]
These identities follow by expanding in the characteristic functions
of one common finite Schr\"odinger quotient and applying the original
finite Fourier orthogonality relation. Its measure factors are the
displayed scalars, not set equal to one. The graph description and
Heisenberg covariance now give
\[
 \operatorname{dbl}(v_{\Lambda,L},v_{\Lambda,L'})(x)
   =t\,1_{\Lambda\otimes K}(x)
         \exp\!\left(\pi i\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb Q}
                                      \operatorname{tr}(x\beta x^*)\right),
 \quad t=\kappa\langle v_{0,L},v_{0,L'}\rangle.             \tag{*}
\]
The independence of $t$ from $\Lambda$ is essential. To check it,
start with arbitrary unit vacua $u_1,u_2$ at $\Lambda$. Their
doubling constant has the same modulus as $t$ by the tensor-index
formula. Pair their doubled functions with the standard-lattice
doubled functions. Both have the same quadratic character on
their overlapping supports, whose measure is positive. The second
Fourier identity above therefore says that their relative phase
is precisely the inverse of the phases of
$\langle v_{0,L},u_1\rangle$ and
$\langle v_{0,L'},u_2\rangle$. Normalizing the two projected
vacua cancels those phases and proves $(*)$ exactly.

The double of the two finite level sums is thus a sum over
$J\times J$ of the original Hermitian Heisenberg translates of
$(*)$, with coefficients $\lambda(k)\overline{\lambda(k')}$,
displacement $c_k-c_{k'}$ and momentum
$(c_k+c_{k'})(\kappa_0G/4)$. Representatives can be chosen in
$\Lambda\otimes K_0$, where
$K_0=2\overline{\mathfrak m}(L\cap L')$.
Choose integers $N,N'>0$ with $N'\mathcal O_E^r\subset K$
and with $N$ carrying
\[
 K,\ K_0,\ K_0(\kappa_0G/4),\ K\beta,\ K\beta^*,
                         \mathcal O_E^r\beta
\]
into $\mathcal O_E^r$. Then $R=2NN'$ supplies the original
support and periodicity bounds, uniformly in $\Lambda$.
The original principal-$M$ rational unitary transport preserves
all row labels and the quadratic trace. Local equality of
lattices at the primes of $R$ preserves the same finite expression;
if a scalar prime to $R$ relates two lattice tests, Bezout's identity
and $R$-periodicity give their equality. These are precisely the
rational-equivariance, support, periodicity and locality clauses
of \texttt{admitted}. The integer $R$ may depend on $L,L'$,
as allowed in that contract. We have therefore supplied every
field of \texttt{IsSystem} for the original vectors.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The resulting finite theta span.}
The exact Hermitian class-sum theorem, the heat comparison and the
original Gaussian reproducer now give the actual
\texttt{LevelThetaSpan} conclusion: every original bounded
holomorphic family of the stated weight and $\mu$-covariance is
a finite linear combination of the prescribed theta functions
$\theta_{\Phi_{L,\Lambda},P}$, simultaneously for all
$L\in\mathcal L$. For clarity, the remaining analytic argument is
given here with its constants.

Let $m_M=\sum_{[\Lambda]}w_M(\Lambda)>0$ and define
$C_{LL'}=\sum_{[\Lambda]}w_M(\Lambda)
\langle\Phi_{L,\Lambda},\Phi_{L',\Lambda}\rangle$.
Its diagonal is $a=|J|m_M$, and its off-diagonal row norm is at
most $0.65a$. The Neumann series in the original weighted
supremum norm gives $F'=C^{-1}F$, with
\[
                  \|F'\|\le{\|F\|\over0.35a}.
\]
Covariance and local uniform convergence preserve holomorphy and
the original Borel weight. Choose a nonzero invariant family of
row-highest harmonic polynomials by taking an orthonormal basis
in that finite-dimensional representation. Its existence in
$n\ge r$ follows from products of leading minors in disjoint
column blocks, which all cross-contractions annihilate.

In the doubled Eisenstein expansion, a maximal isotropic subspace
which is not a graph has a nonzero isotropic kernel in one factor.
The coinvariant relation just proved kills its value at zero.
Graphs are indexed by rational unitary maps; outside-level
orthogonality retains exactly $\Delta$. Differentiation of the
locally normally convergent series and
Theorem~\ref{actual-theta-heat-theorem} therefore give the original
kernel identity, with its factor $\kappa$ unchanged.
Integrate that identity against $F'$ on a common thin slab and
unfold the $\Delta$ action. The row-sum bound and the already
proved full-slab absolute integrability justify this unfolding.
The reproducing integral is exactly $\rho C F'=\rho F$,
where the original $\rho>0$ remains in the resulting coefficients.

There are finitely many positive-definite lattice classes and
finitely many polynomials in the chosen invariant family. Thus
the unfolded integrand takes values in a finite-dimensional theta
span. Pointwise integrals remain in that span: select a basis of
evaluation functionals on it and integrate its coordinates, whose
absolute integrability follows from the same kernel bound.
This also avoids presuming separate integrability of a redundant
list of theta coefficients. Dividing by $\rho$ gives a finite
span, not merely its closure.

This statement proves the original finite-system and level-spanning
suppliers \cite{actual-sw-source}. The conversion from these level
families to the chosen global component readings must retain its
free-row, marking and coefficient-field comparisons. The estimate
does not bound $\kappa^{-1}$, $\rho^{-1}$, the resulting coefficients
or the archimedean norms uniformly over growing arithmetic data.
